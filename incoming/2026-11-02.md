## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 1

### Lifting the mixed to the pure: quantum phases of matter from canonical purification
**Xiaoyang Huang, Shengqi Sang, Chong Wang, Yijian Zou — arXiv:2610.41926**

Can mixed-state phases be studied through an associated pure state without losing the distinction between reversible noise and irreversible destruction of information? An arbitrary purification is unsuitable: introducing an environment makes every local channel look like unitary evolution. The canonical purification offers a more constrained construction because it is fixed by the density matrix.

The authors prove that locally reversible channels lift to local isometries between canonical purifications. Applying this construction gate by gate connects mixed-state phase equivalence to ordinary pure-state phase equivalence. Their explicit construction uses an amplitude-level version of the Petz recovery map, with quantitative error bounds. The locality assumption applies at intermediate circuit steps; the result does not follow merely from recoverability of the final state.

For the toric code under bit-flip noise, the doubled pure state changes from the topological order of two toric codes to that of one across the decoding transition. Numerical calculations support this correspondence, with a dephased Chern insulator providing another illustration.

**Background.** Canonical purification vectorizes $\sqrt{\rho}$ in a doubled Hilbert space: matrix entries become amplitudes connecting the system and its conjugate copy. The square root supplies the correct Schmidt weights. The ordinary Petz recovery map acts on a density matrix from both sides; its “half” acts on these amplitudes. Showing that this amplitude operation remains local is the essential bridge to a circuit relating pure states.

**Why it matters?** This supplies a structural justification for using canonical purification to diagnose noisy many-body phases. Its particular value for QEC and decoherence is that it ties a doubled-state construction to operational recovery. The implication is directional: the theorem gives a necessary pure-state equivalence for mixed-state equivalence, rather than a complete classification by purification alone.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 2

### Theory of Convective Diffusion
**Žiga Krajnik, Takato Yoshimura, Alvise Bastianello, Enej Ilievski — arXiv:2610.40862**

A Gaussian spreading peak need not imply ordinary fluctuating diffusion. In an integrable system, collisions can randomly displace a propagating mode while preserving much more of its underlying configuration than a noisy diffusion equation would suggest. Two-point functions can conceal that distinction.

The authors derive a stochastic hydrodynamic description in which a mode, viewed in its moving frame, obeys $[\partial_{t}+\eta_{a}(t)\partial_{y}]\phi_{a}(y,t)=0$. Here the field is the mode's density fluctuation, and the random velocity is uniform in space but fluctuates in time. Each realization translates the initial profile; averaging these translations produces diffusive broadening.

This construction yields closed higher-point correlations and a non-Gaussian distribution of transferred charge. The latter combines fluctuations of the displacement with fluctuations of the initial charge inside the swept interval. Rule-54 simulations quantitatively test the resulting nested Gaussian distribution. The framework also distinguishes this convective mechanism from two additional noise channels available in chaotic systems, under the stated restrictions on mode couplings.

**Background.** In generalized hydrodynamics, stable quasiparticles acquire effective velocities through collision-induced delays. Thermal fluctuations in the surrounding quasiparticle densities therefore fluctuate a chosen mode's velocity. Integrating out those surrounding modes produces multiplicative temporal noise. Because the same displacement moves an entire profile, several observations share correlated histories; ordinary local diffusion misses those correlations even when it reproduces the spreading peak.

**Why it matters?** This changes what should count as a diagnostic of diffusion in exactly solvable dynamics. A diffusion constant and Gaussian structure factor may leave the fluctuation mechanism unresolved. Higher-point measurements and charge-transfer statistics become substantive tests of integrable hydrodynamics, connecting exact transport theory to experimentally accessible non-equilibrium fluctuations.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 3

### A Universal Approach to Hydrodynamics in Higher Dimensions via Tensor Networks
**Sajant Anand, Christopher D. White, Jack Kemp, Julia Wei, Norman Y. Yao, Michael P. Zaletel — arXiv:2610.40776**

Late-time transport should depend mainly on conserved densities and currents, yet conventional tensor-network evolution spends resources following correlations that rapidly become irrelevant to those observables. In higher dimensions, mapping the lattice onto a chain introduces another problem: physically neighboring sites can become distant along the tensor network.

Lattice density matrix truncation addresses both difficulties by protecting geometrically local operator information during compression. The evolution still uses a one-dimensional tensor-network representation, but truncation preserves selected physical densities and currents regardless of their separation along that representation.

The authors benchmark against uncoupled interacting chains arranged in an unfavorable tensor-network ordering, then calculate energy diffusion on two- and three-dimensional Ising cylinders. They also resolve a crossover from superdiffusive to diffusive spin transport in the Heisenberg setting. These are quantitative high-temperature calculations, with convergence and transverse-size extrapolations carrying part of the uncertainty.

**Background.** A matrix product operator represents an evolving observable through a chain of tensors. Ordinary singular-value truncation minimizes a global representation error, which can alter small but hydrodynamically essential operator overlaps. Here, directions encoding protected observables are separated before the remaining block is compressed. For example, a current between adjacent lattice sites remains protected even when the chain ordering places those sites far apart. The retained information follows the physical geometry.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful advance in classical access to non-equilibrium many-body physics, particularly for benchmarking quantum simulators. It suggests that the difficulty of reproducing a full evolving state can substantially exceed that of extracting transport. Broader applicability will depend on convergence beyond the demonstrated high-temperature models and geometries; the method supplies numerical evidence rather than a general efficiency theorem.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 4

### Gapless and Non-Equilibrium Topology in Blockaded Ladders
**Ramanjit Sohal, Hannes Bernien, Ruben Verresen — arXiv:2610.40771**

Can one simple constraint explain both unusual equilibrium topology and persistent order far from equilibrium? The authors study particles hopping on ladders subject to the Rydberg blockade, which forbids occupation of neighboring sites. A particle's leg label then becomes intertwined with its motion along the ladder.

Their organizing idea is to examine the sequence of occupied particles after deleting empty positions. The blockade favors correlations in this squeezed space. On a two-leg ladder, these correlations produce an intrinsically gapless symmetry-protected topological phase. A related effective model also reaches a supersymmetric multicritical point. Three legs instead allow an emergent continuous symmetry and two gapless components.

After a quench, ordinary local correlations can disappear while string order persists much longer. Tensor-network calculations and a classical stochastic approximation support this separation of timescales. The order eventually decays; the claim is parametrically slow relaxation, rather than indefinite preservation of a high-energy topological state.

**Background.** Squeezed space labels particles by their order along the ladder, retaining their leg labels while removing holes. Motion of holes obscures this ordering in physical-space correlators. A particle-parity string compensates for the fluctuating number of intervening particles, exposing the hidden leg correlations. “Intrinsically gapless” means the protected structure requires the gapless bulk: the same symmetry cannot protect a corresponding gapped phase.

**Why it matters?** The paper provides a common microscopic mechanism for equilibrium string order and slow non-equilibrium memory. This is particularly appealing for constrained quantum simulation because the relevant Hamiltonians arise from simple blockade limits. Establishing the longevity and stability of the effect with realistic interaction tails would turn the conceptual mechanism into a stronger simulator target.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 5

### Proof of the entropic $c$-theorem
**Jonah Kudler-Flam — arXiv:2610.40874**

The entropic argument for irreversibility of two-dimensional renormalization-group flow is physically persuasive, but its familiar formulation manipulates divergent subregion entropies. Can the argument be carried out entirely within continuum QFT using finite quantities?

This paper replaces the entropy construction with mutual information defined through relative entropy. Its central inequality says that the correlation loss caused by shrinking one region increases when the other region grows. Combined with Lorentz symmetry, this gives nonnegative, nonincreasing functions probing the two chiral directions separately.

The treatment also handles possible infrared divergences of infinite wedges by first considering finite regions and controlled differences. Assumptions include the split property, finite mutual information across a gap, and finite ultraviolet bounds. Fixed-point constancy follows, while general identification with conventional stress-tensor central charges remains a separate issue that is not completely resolved.

**Background.** The split property supplies an intermediate algebra behaving like the operators on a tensor factor when regions are separated by a finite corridor. It permits a product reference state and finite relative-entropy comparisons without assigning a density matrix to every sharply bounded continuum region. The paper's “increasing differences” inequality recovers the useful content of the Casini–Huerta entropy argument through these comparisons.

**Why it matters?** The advance is a firmer continuum foundation for information-theoretic RG monotonicity. It clarifies which properties do the work and which endpoint identifications require additional input. For interests spanning QFT and quantum information, this is valuable precisely because it separates a robust monotonicity mechanism from regulator-dependent intuition.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 6

### Monopole-Fermion Scattering from Twisted Conformal Boundary States
**Clay Córdova, Diego García-Sepúlveda, Lian-Tao Wang, Yen-Chieh Wang — arXiv:2610.40779**

Low-energy fermion scattering from a monopole presents an apparent unitarity puzzle: conserved charges can demand outgoing excitations absent from the ordinary fermion Fock space. The radial lowest-partial-wave problem can be described by free fermions on a half-line, but its boundary condition must know about those additional channels.

The authors construct twisted conformal boundary states for current-preserving boundary conditions. Topological symmetry lines shift the allowed charge lattices, supplying sectors containing the required outgoing operators. Cluster decomposition and open-closed consistency fix the relative normalization.

For the Callan–Rubakov boundary condition, an incoming fermion has a nonzero two-point function with an outgoing defect operator, with coefficient of unit modulus, even when ordinary fermion correlators vanish. Matching to gauge-covariant four-dimensional partial waves identifies the missing contribution and supports the argument that this channel restores the required unitarity normalization. The treatment uses the massless, weak-coupling reduction.

**Background.** A twisted-sector operator sits at the endpoint of a symmetry line. Circling that endpoint imposes a symmetry twist, so its charges need not belong to the lattice of ordinary local fermions. A boundary state encodes reflection from the monopole core; allowing the symmetry line to meet that boundary adds precisely the sectors excluded by an ordinary local-operator calculation. Open-closed consistency compares two descriptions of the same cylinder amplitude and fixes their normalization.

**Why it matters?** This makes a generalized-symmetry construction do concrete scattering work. The important contribution is an explicit amplitude and normalization for a channel required by conservation laws. It connects familiar boundary CFT tools to nonperturbative particle physics, while leaving extensions beyond the controlled radial approximation as further work.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 7

### Faster-than-power-law decay of order parameter correlations in the two-dimensional RP$^2$ model: a rigorous result by vibe-proving
**Masaki Oshikawa — arXiv:2610.41256**

Point vortices can exist in a two-dimensional director model even though its local spin-wave geometry resembles that of the Heisenberg model. Whether those defects can support algebraic order has therefore remained a meaningful question beyond perturbative sigma-model intuition.

The paper reports a rigorous bound on quadrupolar correlations with envelope $C_{\beta}\exp\{-c_{\beta}[\log(2+r)]^{2}\}$ at every finite inverse temperature. Here the separation is measured on the lattice, and the two positive constants depend on temperature. The bound holds for specified finite-volume free-boundary measures and their local limits, with an extension to the four-component projective model.

The proof accumulates correlation contractions across nested annuli, using estimates for noncommuting rotations. It is accompanied by a reported Lean formalization. The conclusion excludes asymptotic power-law quadrupolar order in the covered models; it neither proves exponential decay at all temperatures nor settles vortex binding or every possible thermodynamic transition.

**Background.** A director identifies opposite spin directions, so its physical local order parameter is quadrupolar. Rotationally averaging an annulus surrounding one observation point annihilates that tensor correlation. The technical step controls the difference between an annular kernel and its average uniformly in boundary spins. Sufficiently many thin annuli fit across successive scales that their contraction factors produce the squared-logarithm decay exponent.

**Why it matters?** This would sharply constrain a long-debated route to two-dimensional criticality without relying on perturbative flow at strong coupling. The physical distinction is between algebraic orientational order, which is excluded, and transitions visible in other observables, which remain possible. That distinction makes the result relevant to field-theoretic reasoning about defects and emergent criticality.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 8

### Resolving the spinon hierarchy of the Heisenberg antiferromagnet
**Ian P. McCulloch, Jesse J. Osborne — arXiv:2610.42127**

A local spin probe creates an entire hierarchy of spinon states in the Heisenberg chain. Knowing the exact excitation spectrum does not by itself tell us how much observable spectral weight lies in each sector, especially when finite-size calculations converge slowly.

The authors evaluate thermodynamic-limit form-factor contributions through six spinons, revisiting the four-spinon normalization and integration. Two spinons carry 72.89% of the integrated intensity, four contribute about 21.90%, and six add roughly 4.3%. Together they account for approximately 99% of both intensity and first-frequency-moment sum rules, with numerical integration and soft-tail uncertainties distinguished.

They also derive an upper-edge exponent $\alpha_{n}=\frac{n^{2}-3}{2}$ for even spinon number $n$. Higher sectors thus have broader kinematic support but rapidly suppressed outer edges. Their appreciable weight largely overlaps the lower-sector continua, rather than forming a conspicuous high-energy extension.

**Background.** Spinon form factors are matrix elements of the local spin operator between the ground state and multiparticle excitations. Spectral intensity combines their squared magnitude with available phase space. Near an upper edge, spinon momenta coalesce; zeros in the form factor suppress the response much more strongly than phase-space counting alone predicts. This explains why bandwidth is a poor guide to the visible contribution of higher sectors.

**Why it matters?** This improves quantitative control of a central exactly solvable model and corrects the apparent near-exhaustion of spectral weight by two and four spinons. The combination of absolute normalization, sum rules, and tensor-network comparisons provides a demanding benchmark for dynamical many-body methods and quantum simulation.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 9

### All Symmetries and Boundaries of the Two-Dimensional Free Scalar from Continuous Flat-Gauging
**Yichul Choi, Ho Tat Lam, Brandon C. Rayhaun — arXiv:2610.40818**

Even the free scalar has a richer boundary and symmetry structure than its elementary Lagrangian suggests. The difficult question is completeness: after constructing many conformal boundaries and topological lines, how can one know whether others remain?

The authors approach the non-compact boson through the $SU(2)_1$ Wess–Zumino–Witten model, flat-gauging its $U(1)$ winding symmetry. They construct a four-dimensional space of topological lines, largely extending previously known examples, together with boundary states, fusion rules, and interval and twisted-sector spectra. Annulus amplitudes provide detailed consistency checks.

Their completeness argument invokes a proposed remote detectability principle and its converse: a proper closed collection of lines should fail to detect some non-Virasoro local operator. The paper explicitly treats this as a physical proposal requiring sharper formulation. The concrete constructions and checks are consequently firmer than an unconditional completeness theorem.

**Background.** Continuous flat-gauging sums only over gauge fields with zero curvature. It retains global twists and their associated sectors while preserving the conformal central charge. A gauging interface separates the original and gauged theories; moving it onto a boundary transfers boundary data between them, sometimes producing a sum that must be decomposed into elementary boundaries. A topological line detects an operator when moving the line through it changes a correlator.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantial extension of the working toolkit for a basic CFT, with concrete realizations of continuous and non-invertible symmetry structures. Its broader value lies in turning classification questions into constructions that can be checked through spectra and amplitudes. The proposed completeness criterion is an interesting additional direction, with its present logical status kept explicit.

### Charge Pumping Leptogenesis from the Symmetric Mass Generation Phase on a Four-Leg Ladder Lattice
**Runze Chi, Meng Zeng, Tao Xiang, Yi-Zhuang You, Bertrand I. Halperin, Juven Wang — arXiv:2610.42103**

Quantized Hall pumping is usually pictured as spectral flow through gapless edge modes. What becomes of that picture when interactions gap an anomaly-free boundary while preserving its continuous symmetries?

The authors investigate a four-leg realization of the chiral 3-4-5-0 fermion model using density-matrix renormalization. Six-fermion interactions produce an intermediate symmetric mass generation regime, between weak- and strong-coupling regimes with gapless modes near the interacting edge. At stronger coupling, an additional discrete symmetry breaks.

A flux path interpolating an exact discrete twist through a direction with mixed anomalies produces the same quantized charge depletion at the opposite edge across these regimes. The receiving edge and neighboring bulk redistribute that charge differently. An interaction-induced flavor-conversion current further distinguishes the symmetric gapped regime. These are finite-ladder results; some transition boundaries remain unresolved. The proposed connection to leptogenesis is an analogy, rather than a cosmological calculation.

**Background.** Symmetric mass generation gaps a set of fermions through interactions without a symmetry-breaking bilinear condensate. Anomaly cancellation makes such gapping possible. In the 3-4-5-0 model, opposite chiralities carry charge assignments whose squared-charge contributions cancel for the preserved symmetry. The pumping path probes a different direction with a mixed anomaly, so a symmetric gapped edge can coexist with the bulk response under that specified twist.

**Why it matters?** The interesting result is a concrete account of how anomalous transport is accommodated near an interacting, symmetrically gapped boundary. It connects lattice chiral fermions, anomaly constraints, and non-equilibrium response in a numerically accessible setting. Wider systems would help separate the proposed boundary mechanism from residual coupling between the two edges.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 11

### Almost-Quantum Correlations Violate Information Causality
**Anandamay Das Bhowmik, Manik Banik — arXiv:2610.41444**

Why are physically allowed nonlocal correlations restricted to the quantum set rather than the larger set compatible with no signalling? Almost-quantum correlations are a particularly demanding test because they satisfy several proposed physical principles and previously appeared compatible with information causality.

This paper constructs an unbounded violation using one classical bit and at most one use of an almost-quantum box. Its mechanism uses zero-error certificates of equality: for $N$ possible settings, an explicit box certifies matching settings with probability $1/17$, whereas quantum resources permit at most $1/N$.

Alice uses the box to identify an all-ones block in a random bit string. A successful certificate guarantees every bit in the requested block. Summing the information available for alternative bit questions then exceeds the communication budget, with no discarded runs in the final guessing protocol. Increasing the construction's size makes the violation unbounded.

**Background.** The Navascués–Pironio–Acín hierarchy approximates allowed quantum correlations through positive moment matrices. At the almost-quantum level used here, Alice's and Bob's measurement projectors need commute on the shared state, rather than as operators everywhere. Information causality bounds a sum of mutual informations over alternative requested bits; it does not assert that Bob learns those bits simultaneously. Rare, perfectly reliable certificates contribute information linearly in their probability.

**Why it matters?** This gives a sharp physical separation between quantum and almost-quantum correlations in a setting where several other principles fail to distinguish them. The significance is foundational rather than a practical communication protocol. It strengthens information causality as a discriminating principle without establishing that it uniquely characterizes quantum theory.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 12

### Guaranteed Slater Conditions for the Numerical Conformal Bootstrap: Fixing Instabilities in SDPB, Navigator Methods, and Skydiving
**Marten Reehorst — arXiv:2610.40764**

A numerical bootstrap bound is useful only if a solver's verdict reflects the mathematical problem rather than a degeneracy in its representation. Some familiar stalls and ambiguous outcomes arise because the semidefinite programs lack strictly feasible points, violating assumptions behind the interior-point algorithm.

Reehorst introduces diagnostics for the primal and dual Slater conditions, together with two remedies compatible with SDPB's structured input. Transformations restore strict feasibility while bounding the original optimum; facial reduction removes the offending degeneracies. The tools explain and repair several failures in mixed-correlator Ising problems and a published current-scalar setup.

A useful consequence concerns the customary twist-gap remedy. It can remove endpoint degeneracies, but cannot cure every failure: unequal polynomial degrees can create a degeneracy at infinity. The paper therefore replaces a sometimes effective empirical adjustment with a diagnosis of which constraint geometry actually causes the instability.

**Background.** Slater conditions require feasible positive-definite matrices on both sides of a semidefinite program. A matrix forced to have a zero direction can satisfy positivity while never entering the cone's interior. An interior-point solver then tries to follow a central path that does not have the assumed form. An exposing certificate identifies the forced directions; facial reduction restricts the problem to the smaller cone where a meaningful interior can be recovered.

**Why it matters?** This is a practical methodological contribution to nonperturbative QFT. Reliable failure diagnosis matters as much as speed when excluded regions and optimization trajectories depend on solver outcomes. The value extends to other bootstrap settings sharing the same optimization structure, although each application still requires its own numerical implementation and checks.

### Airy distribution of the quenched spectral form factor in non-interacting fermions
**Oscar Martin, Satya N. Majumdar, Laura Foini — arXiv:2610.41393**

Long-time spectral statistics can conceal the difference between interacting integrability and free-particle structure. The question here concerns the full fluctuations of the spectral form factor's logarithm, rather than its average ramp or local level spacings.

For free fermions at fixed particle density and infinite temperature, with rationally independent single-particle energies, the authors obtain an Airy distribution after rescaling the logarithm by $\sqrt{L}$, where $L$ counts single-particle levels. Its mean and standard deviation have the same scaling, so relative fluctuations persist. An interacting integrable-chain example instead follows reflected-Gumbel statistics, as in the chaotic comparison.

The mechanism comes from projection onto fixed particle number. The generating partition function defines a random landscape in a conjugate phase; long-time sampling makes this landscape Brownian-like with exactly zero average. Its maximum controls the projected partition function and produces the Airy law. The derivation is supported by finite-size numerical tests.

**Background.** A free many-body spectrum contains exponentially many levels built from only a linear number of single-particle energies. Consequently, independent single-particle phases do not yield independent many-body phases. Here the Airy distribution is the probability law for the maximum height of a periodic Brownian bridge measured relative to its spatial average. The number projection makes this extreme-value problem relevant to a spectral observable.

**Why it matters?** This identifies a diagnostic of additive free-particle structure that ordinary local spectral statistics can miss. The connection between a quantum spectral quantity and interface fluctuations is also conceptually useful. The result's restrictions—fixed particle number, infinite temperature, and incommensurability—matter when deciding where the proposed diagnostic should survive.

### A systematic study of hydrodynamic attractors in periodically driven systems at weak and strong couplings
**Aleksas Mazeliauskas, Toshali Mitra, Sören Schlichting, Simon Schneider, Louis Nwakile Onwuka, Martin Vrdoljak — arXiv:2610.40877**

A periodically driven fluid need not settle near an instantaneous constitutive relation: the drive continually excites its relaxation modes. This paper asks which aspects of the resulting cyclic response are hydrodynamic and which retain information about microscopic coupling.

The authors compare kinetic theories, relativistic hydrodynamics, and strongly coupled holography under periodic expansion and contraction. Linear response separates a persistent driven cycle from transients. The control parameter $\omega\tau_{\pi}$ multiplies drive frequency by shear-relaxation time. The kinetic responses are well described by Müller–Israel–Stewart theory, interpolating between slow hydrodynamic response and fast free-streaming behavior.

Holography has a different transient spectrum and exhibits damped ringing, together with sensitivity to curvature terms. At longer times, viscous heating changes the temperature and relaxation time, so the apparent cycle drifts. Nonlinear calculations in relaxation-time kinetic theory track the eventual approach toward the Navier–Stokes regime.

**Background.** Müller–Israel–Stewart theory promotes shear stress to a relaxing dynamical variable instead of making it respond instantaneously to gradients. In frequency space this introduces a damped pole. Holographic quasinormal modes supply a richer set of complex-frequency poles: their real parts generate oscillations, and their imaginary parts set decay rates. The driven cycle samples the response at the driving frequency; transients expose the poles away from it.

**Why it matters?** This provides a controlled comparison of hydrodynamic universality and microscopic memory under sustained driving. It is a useful QFT and non-equilibrium benchmark because the steady response and its approach can be studied separately. The cross-theory linear comparison is broader than the paper's nonlinear evidence, which is concentrated in the relaxation-time model.

### Real-time BP decoding of a high-rate QEC code
**Andreas Kell, Daniel Herr, Daniel Bascones García, Alexandru Paler, Davide Orsucci, Francisco Lázaro, Francisco García Herrero — arXiv:2610.41413**

A high-rate code saves physical qubits only if its decoder can keep pace without an expensive post-processing bottleneck. This paper studies that joint algorithm-and-hardware problem for a $[[512,174,8]]$ three-dimensional product code.

The decoder uses row-wise serial min-sum updates with cumulative memory, combined in a small parallel ensemble. Circuit-level simulations yield a logical error rate around $5\times10^{-7}$ per logical qubit and round at physical error probability $10^{-3}$, with observed effective distance six. Three ensemble members achieve accuracy comparable to the reported Relay-BP benchmark.

An FPGA implementation has average core latency of 5.1 microseconds at $10^{-4}$ and a 97.9-microsecond bound when iterations are capped at thirty. The implementation tests use a simpler one-round configuration than the repeated-round accuracy study. Superconducting-timescale operation is an ASIC projection, whereas the FPGA latency fits the stated neutral-atom and trapped-ion budgets.

**Background.** Cumulative memory carries the previous iteration's bias forward while integrating new evidence, rather than repeatedly resetting the bias to its original prior. Different check-processing orders give ensemble members different routes through the same inference problem. Serial processing of check rows retains substantial parallel work within a row while avoiding the routing burden of updating the entire graph simultaneously.

**Why it matters?** This is an implementation-oriented contribution showing how a modest change in iterative decoding can replace heavier post-processing for a useful high-rate example. It complements learned-decoder work by making latency and memory architecture explicit. Repeated-round hardware integration remains necessary to establish the complete operating capability suggested by the separate accuracy and timing results.

## arXiv-2026-11-02 — Item 17

### Quantum Popcorn: the shape of a phase transition bubble
**Kfir Blum, Ipak Fadakar, Omri Rosner, Guilherme L. Pimentel — arXiv:2610.41504**

Vacuum decay is usually drawn as the appearance of a perfectly spherical bubble. That picture describes the leading semiclassical saddle, but the field state produced by tunneling also contains quantum fluctuations. What shape should a typical individual bubble have at nucleation?

The authors derive its wave functional from a half-bounce path integral, fixing the field on the turning slice and integrating over the preceding Euclidean evolution. The quadratic action becomes a Gaussian functional of the slice fluctuations. Its covariance determines the root-mean-square quadrupole of the bubble, while the ensemble average retains spherical symmetry.

The characteristic relative wall distortion scales as $\delta R/R\sim(\hbar/S_{b})^{1/2}$, with the bounce action setting the semiclassical suppression. Thus $S_{b}/\hbar\sim100$ suggests distortions of order ten percent, with profile-dependent coefficients. Thin-wall and thick-wall examples clarify which fluctuations contribute. Internal wall excitations are suppressed in an appropriate thin-wall limit, but a light false vacuum can enhance their contribution.

The treatment also distinguishes shape fluctuations from collective translations and the escape direction associated with bubble growth. These distinctions are necessary before reading the quadratic functional as a probability distribution.

**Background.** The Dirichlet-to-Neumann map converts a prescribed field fluctuation on the nucleation slice into the normal derivative of its extension through the Euclidean half-space. Evaluating the on-shell quadratic action then gives a boundary quadratic form; its inverse determines the slice covariance. Translation modes move the whole bubble, whereas the quadrupole changes its shape. Fixing the nucleation point and turning slice prevents those distinct effects from being counted as shape noise.

**Why it matters?** This gives a systematic initial fluctuation ensemble for real-time vacuum-decay dynamics, beyond the spherical saddle used to calculate the decay exponent. It is a clear connection between semiclassical QFT and non-equilibrium initial conditions. Consequences for later bubble collisions or gravitational radiation require propagation of this ensemble through the subsequent dynamics; the nucleation distortion estimate alone does not determine those signals.

### DiscKosky: Efficient Genealogical Constraints from Sector-By-Sector Critical Point Counting
**Giulio Crisanti, Luke Lippstreu, Andrew J. McLeod, Maria Polackova — arXiv:2610.40772**

Knowing every possible singularity of a Feynman integral still leaves a large question: which singularities can occur after earlier discontinuities have already constrained the integration contour? Answering this efficiently would reduce the space of functions and analytic structures needed in multiloop calculations.

DiscKosky identifies the on-shell sectors associated with a candidate singularity by tracking losses of critical points of the Lee–Pomeransky polynomial.
