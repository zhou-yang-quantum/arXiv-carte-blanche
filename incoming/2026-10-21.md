# arXiv-2026-10-21

This digest covers the newly announced batch for 2026-10-21. Ten papers span thermal information, driven quantum order, logical operations, gauge-theory simulation, and exact many-body methods. A recurring question is what structure survives beyond an idealized limit: weak perturbations, periodic driving, noncommuting interactions, or fluctuations neglected by mean field.

## arXiv-2026-10-21 — Item 1

### Local-Channel Stability of Classical $\mathbb Z_2$ Lattice Gauge Models
**Guanxi Xun, Weiguang Cao, Haruki Watanabe — arXiv:2610.27939**

Can a thermal state retain robust nonlocal information without an exact protecting symmetry? Ordinary symmetry breaking offers limited reassurance: a small magnetic field can select an Ising orientation and remove the distinction between ordered and disordered regimes. Lattice gauge models suggest another possibility, because information carried by winding sectors can be invisible to every bounded region.

The authors establish an operational stability result for classical gauge models with spins on $m$-dimensional cells of a $d$-dimensional lattice. In the range $1\le m\le d-2$, sufficiently low positive temperatures and sufficiently weak local perturbations preserve two-way conversion between the Gibbs state and the canonical flat ensemble, which contains all defect-free winding sectors. The perturbations may break the gauge and internal symmetries. Noncommuting quantum perturbations are also covered, within a narrower temperature-dependent window.

The mechanism combines local indistinguishability of flat sectors with suppression of large extended defects. Small defects can be removed locally; a reverse construction restores their thermal distribution. Quantum perturbations require an additional step that recovers coherences after reducing the diagonal probabilities to a classical problem. The conversion channels have polylogarithmic causal radius and vanishing whole-system trace error. At sufficiently high temperature, the states instead become equivalent to a product state. An optimized correlation between distant winding readers distinguishes these two domains.

**Background.** The flat ensemble is a classical mixture, so its nontriviality does not require entanglement. For link spins, two distant loops winding around the same periodic direction can read the same random sign even though local regions cannot determine it. Product ancillary inputs cannot supply that shared sign in advance.

Two-way conversion is essential because local quantum channels can discard information. Erasing a correlated state is easy; recreating its winding information using operations whose causal reach remains much smaller than the system is the substantive requirement. Here the conversions concern specified state families, without requiring that their composition undo arbitrary inputs.

**Why it matters?** This gives a concrete, perturbatively stable example of thermal information organized beyond microscopic symmetry protection. It connects statistical mechanics and error correction through the geometry of correctable defects, while distinguishing state-level phase structure from dynamical memory performance. The protected information here is classical: a quantum-memory lifetime and recovery of a noncommuting logical algebra are not established. Extending the conversions beyond the dilute-defect regime would be an important next step toward locating the actual phase boundaries.

## arXiv-2026-10-21 — Item 2

### Floquet ground state order
**Suman Mondal, Paul M. Schindler, Marin Bukov — arXiv:2610.28336**

Periodic driving usually heats a clean interacting system toward infinite temperature. This makes ground-state reasoning difficult to transplant out of equilibrium: quasienergy wraps around a finite interval, leaving no distinguished lowest level. The paper asks whether ordering Floquet eigenstates by their actual average energy can reveal structured states hidden within the dense quasienergy spectrum.

The average-energy operator assigns each Floquet eigenstate the period-averaged expectation of the laboratory Hamiltonian along its full motion. Its eigenvalues avoid the folding ambiguity of quasienergy. The authors derive a variational construction of this operator by projecting the Hamiltonian onto dynamical invariants, then develop a matrix product state method for finding its lowest state.

For a driven nonintegrable Ising chain, calculations reaching 140 spins support parameter regions with a finite average-energy gap and low entanglement. The associated states exhibit antiferromagnetic order or a ground-state time-crystal response. An exactly solvable driven chain supplies a complementary example. The proposal therefore concerns exceptional structured Floquet states; generic initial states can still heat.

The paper also constructs nonintegrable quasilocal drives with exact ordered Floquet eigenstates using counterdiabatic driving. This establishes an explicit stabilization mechanism, but it does not prove thermodynamic stability of the numerically studied Ising-chain states, nor does that construction alone guarantee an average-energy gap to every other Floquet state.

**Background.** A dynamical invariant has fixed eigenvalues while its eigenprojectors move with the evolution. Separating that motion from phase accumulation exposes a geometric operation over one drive cycle, called the drive holonomy. A cycle can rotate an ordered manifold into itself while exchanging its orientations, producing a time-crystal response.

Counterdiabatic driving adds a generator that cancels transitions caused by the moving eigenbasis. For a gapped local parent Hamiltonian, quasiadiabatic continuation supplies such transport with spatially decaying tails. Locality is the crucial bridge: formally transporting a chosen state is straightforward, but realizing that transport with physically local interactions is what makes the construction meaningful for many-body matter.

**Why it matters?** The contribution combines a useful computational principle with a provocative way to organize driven phases around special eigenstates. Its relevance to non-equilibrium physics lies in making the structured part of the Floquet spectrum accessible without resolving the exponentially larger thermal bulk. The numerical phase evidence and the exact stabilization construction should be assessed separately. Establishing their connection for simple experimentally available drives would substantially strengthen the case for robust Floquet ground-state phases.

## arXiv-2026-10-21 — Item 3

### Hybrid Lattice Surgery between Smooth Boundaries
**Grace Ding, Yanzhu Chen, Sheng-Jie Huang — arXiv:2610.27556**

Generalized surface codes offer richer logical degrees of freedom, but using them requires explicit operations between code patches. When patches are built from different finite groups, the familiar interpretation of surgery as measuring a joint binary parity needs a broader replacement. This paper identifies that replacement for smooth boundaries and develops measurement procedures that implement it.

The central operation is a logical $K$-coset measurement, with $K$ specifying the subgroup that defines the interface between the two code groups. The measurement resolves a collective relation between logical labels while preserving coherence within the selected coset. Ordinary surface-code parity measurement appears as the binary special case.

Using a tensor-network description, the authors derive physical measurement circuits and correction procedures for nonidentity outcomes. They then combine smooth surgery with other available operations to construct generalized controlled multiplication and prepare encoded T, controlled-Z, Toffoli, and controlled-S resource states. The outputs can reside in ordinary binary surface-code patches, giving an explicit route from richer group codes to familiar encoded resources.

An operational qualification matters here: general interfaces can impose a subgroup restriction that requires finite-probability postselection. When that restriction is trivial, the corresponding postselection is unnecessary. Parallel correction procedures also depend on suitable group structure. The paper provides logical constructions and circuits, without a circuit-level noise threshold or a complete resource comparison.

**Background.** A group surface code replaces binary edge labels with basis states labeled by elements of a finite group. Multiplication order becomes significant for non-Abelian groups, so one cannot infer the logical action merely by replacing addition modulo two with a larger alphabet.

A coset collects labels related by multiplication with elements of a subgroup. At an interface, the relevant labels are pairs, one from each patch. Measuring their coset reveals which collective class the pair occupies, without resolving the individual labels inside that class. In the binary example, this is the distinction between equal and unequal logical labels, with superpositions preserved within each parity sector. The subgroup defining the interface therefore specifies both what is measured and what coherence survives.

**Why it matters?** This is a constructive extension of the logical-operation toolkit for group surface codes. It turns boundary data into an interpretable measurement primitive and shows how that primitive can produce resources useful to conventional surface-code computation. For error-correction interests, the main opportunity is heterogeneous encoded processing. Its practical value will depend on whether the extra code structure, correction circuitry, and occasional postselection outperform existing resource-preparation routes under realistic noise.

## arXiv-2026-10-21 — Item 4

### Nambu-Goldstone Bosons and Quantum Fisher Information
**Eugene Chen, Daniel Green — arXiv:2610.27054**

Quantum Fisher information quantifies how distinguishable nearby quantum states are, but its formal definition can obscure the underlying physics. For states related by a continuous symmetry, a more physical description should be possible: the same conserved charges that move the state also create its low-energy Goldstone excitations. The paper develops this connection, including the obstruction to simultaneously estimating several symmetry directions.

The authors relate the quantum Fisher matrix to charge fluctuations and match its low-momentum geometry to the Goldstone effective theory. The metric encoded by Goldstone correlators determines the information metric on symmetry-related vacua. In relativistic systems this connects directly to the coset metric; in nonrelativistic systems distinct temporal and spatial kinetic coefficients both enter.

The imaginary part of the information geometry carries complementary content. Its Uhlmann curvature is controlled by the charge-commutator density that also distinguishes type-A and type-B Goldstone modes. Thus the failure of separate parameter-estimation bounds to be simultaneously attainable has the same physical origin as the pairing of broken generators into a single canonical degree of freedom. Examples include a relativistic symmetry-breaking model and a ferromagnetic condensate. The paper also discusses mixed states and finite temperature, where the pure-state relations require modification.

**Background.** The symmetric logarithmic derivative represents the infinitesimal change of a density matrix along a parameter direction. Its state-weighted symmetric correlations give the Fisher matrix; the corresponding commutators give the Uhlmann curvature. A nonzero curvature means that optimal measurements for different directions remain incompatible in the relevant statistical sense.

For type-A modes, broken symmetry directions supply independent Goldstone coordinates. For a type-B mode, two broken generators act as a conjugate pair, as in transverse fluctuations of a ferromagnet. The same pairing obstructs unrestricted simultaneous precision in the associated parameters. The Zamolodchikov metric appearing here describes the geometry encoded in Goldstone two-point functions on the vacuum manifold; it should not be read automatically as a metric on a conformal manifold.

**Why it matters?** This is a conceptual bridge between effective field theory and quantum estimation. It gives information-geometric quantities a physical organization in terms of currents, soft modes, and canonical pairing, making clear which part measures distinguishability and which part measures incompatibility. For broader QFT and quantum-information interests, the useful direction is to extract measurement constraints from low-energy structure. Applying the correspondence quantitatively still requires attention to momentum, volume, normalization, and the distinction between pure and thermal states.

## arXiv-2026-10-21 — Item 5

### Cavity models of non-Abelian gauge theories
**Lucia Valor, Jacquelin Luneau, Klaus Liegener, Stefan Filipp, Peter Rabl — arXiv:2610.27070**

Cavity QED succeeds by reducing a field theory to a few experimentally accessible modes while retaining a useful interaction mechanism. Can a comparable reduction preserve interesting pure-gauge physics in a non-Abelian theory? The obstacle is that gauge fields interact with themselves, so even a minimal cavity can have composite excitations and nontrivial internal dynamics.

Starting from SU(2) Yang–Mills theory in Coulomb gauge, the authors retain one confined spatial mode for each of the three gauge components. The resulting bosonic Hamiltonian contains density-density interactions and two-boson exchange. Its symmetry permits the compact form

$$H_{\rm cav}=\omega_{0}N+UL^{2}$$

Here $N$ counts excitations, $\omega_0$ is the mode frequency, $L$ is the gauge-flavor angular momentum, and $U$ is a positive interaction strength. Singlet pairs minimize the energy in sectors with even excitation number, producing an exactly characterized ladder of cavity glueball states.

The authors study driven excitation of these composites and extend the model to coupled cavities. When on-site interactions dominate inter-cavity hopping, virtual processes generate composite motion and interactions, including bound states of multiple glueballs. They also propose a superconducting-circuit implementation of the required couplings.

The reduction has a controlled scope: interactions must remain small compared with the spacing to discarded spatial modes. Strong coupling in the array refers to the comparison with the still smaller inter-cavity hopping. These are compatible limits, but they describe a cavity model rather than the full nonperturbative continuum theory.

**Background.** The angular momentum in the Hamiltonian generates rotations among gauge flavors, rather than spatial rotations of a particle. A coherent sum of same-flavor boson pairs creates a singlet under these rotations. The positive angular-momentum penalty makes that composite energetically favorable at fixed particle number.

The composite ladder operators have occupation-dependent commutators. Consequently, a glueball is not simply an elementary harmonic-oscillator excitation: its algebra affects hopping matrix elements and the interactions between composites. This is part of the mechanism behind the array’s clustering behavior.

**Why it matters?** The paper offers a compact meeting point of gauge theory, exact solvability, and analog quantum simulation. Its strength is the explicit chain from a continuum starting point to a solvable few-mode Hamiltonian and implementable interactions. Such models could make composite gauge excitations and their driven dynamics accessible with modest hardware. The value of a realization would be to test those mechanisms within the derived regime; connecting them to broader confinement physics would require restoring additional spatial structure and modes.

## arXiv-2026-10-21 — Item 6

### Field theories and critical excitations at anyon condensation transitions
**Luisa Eck, Xie Chen — arXiv:2610.28148**

Knowing which anyons condense can identify the topological phases on either side of a transition, but it usually does not determine the critical theory between them. The problem is especially difficult for non-Abelian anyons, where fusion channels carry structure that an ordinary scalar order parameter misses. This paper asks how much critical information can nevertheless be extracted directly from the parent topological order and the condensate.

The first proposal constructs Ginzburg–Landau field theories when the condensing anyons have integer quantum dimensions. The anyons determine field components, while allowed fusion trees ending in the vacuum determine invariant interaction terms. The construction is illustrated for doubled Ising and a non-Abelian quantum-double theory. It supplies candidate field theories, without guaranteeing that every allowed transition is continuous.

The second proposal identifies critical point and loop excitations, assuming a direct continuous transition. One first determines which anyon loops remain condensed across it. The other loops become critical, while point excitations that can be defined as endpoints of the persistent condensed loops are tested by their braiding with that entire set. Trivial braiding identifies the critical point excitations. In the electric-charge condensation transition out of the toric code, this predicts critical electric points together with magnetic-flux loops, illustrating why point and extended excitations must be tracked separately.

**Background.** A condensable algebra records more than a list of bosons: it specifies consistent ways for them to fuse within the condensate, including the selected fusion channels. These multiplication data constrain the field interactions. A fusion tree represents successive combinations of anyons; requiring its final charge to be the vacuum is the topological counterpart of demanding an invariant interaction.

Quantum dimension measures the asymptotic growth of fusion-state spaces. An integer value permits the proposed assignment of finitely many field components, with reality conditions determined by antiparticle and exchange properties. The loop criterion uses a different notion of condensation: inserting a noncontractible condensed loop changes the energy only exponentially weakly with system size. A critical loop instead produces an algebraically vanishing splitting.

**Why it matters?** The useful advance is a route from gapped topological data to concrete questions about gapless physics: candidate interactions and the sectors in which gaps should close. This connects field-theory construction with diagnostics accessible in lattice models and finite-size spectra. It is particularly worthwhile beyond Abelian examples, where familiar order-parameter intuition becomes unreliable. Determining stable fixed points, transition orders, and quantitative exponents still requires dynamical analysis; the categorical input constrains that task without completing it.

## arXiv-2026-10-21 — Item 7

### Complete Modified Logarithmic Sobolev Inequalities for one-dimensional quantum Spin Chain
**Li Gao, Jingyu Guo, Sihao Wang — arXiv:2610.28600**

A dissipative process can have the desired Gibbs state as its stationary state and still take too long to prepare it. For noncommuting spin chains, proving fast convergence requires controlling correlations that cross every boundary used to divide the problem. A spectral gap alone also gives weaker worst-case preparation bounds than an estimate that directly controls entropy decay.

The authors prove a complete modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality with a constant independent of chain length for Chen–Kastoryano–Gilyén Gibbs samplers. The result holds at every fixed positive temperature for bounded finite-range interactions, allowing noncommuting and spatially nonuniform terms on open chains or cycles.

The resulting mixing time scales as $O(\log N+\log(1/\epsilon))$ for $N$ spins and target accuracy $\epsilon$. The estimate holds in diamond norm as well as trace distance, so convergence remains controlled when the initial chain is entangled with an arbitrary retained quantum memory. This system-size dependence is optimal over the class considered. Temperature-dependent constants remain, and the bound concerns the generator’s evolution time with local update rates fixed; it does not equate that time with total implementation cost.

The proof organizes global relative entropy into conditional contributions associated with individual sites. One-dimensional Gibbs locality controls the errors from comparing regions, and these errors are absorbed into the entropy terms themselves. The static estimate also gives size-independent entropy-decay constants for related heat-bath dynamics.

**Background.** An entropy-decay inequality lower-bounds instantaneous entropy production by the current relative entropy from equilibrium. It therefore guarantees exponential relaxation throughout the trajectory. “Complete” means that the same rate works after adjoining any finite reference system, with the reference marginal left unchanged. This stronger statement is what enables the diamond-norm conclusion.

The sampler constructs jumps by frequency-filtering time-evolved on-site operators and choosing thermal transition weights, together with a coherent correction that ensures exact Gibbs stationarity. For noncommuting Hamiltonians, a region’s reduced Gibbs state retains boundary effects from its surroundings. Spatial entropy factorization must control these effects instead of replacing every marginal by an isolated-region Gibbs state.

**Why it matters?** This strengthens rapid thermalization from a property of uncorrelated inputs to a uniform statement about a channel acting inside a larger quantum system. It provides a rigorous benchmark for dissipative quantum algorithms and for the removal of correlations with quantum memory. The conceptual connection to non-equilibrium physics is the conversion of equilibrium locality into a global relaxation guarantee. Extending comparable control to higher dimensions or obtaining useful low-temperature constants would address substantially harder preparation problems.

## arXiv-2026-10-21 — Item 8

### Exact spectral integrability of a finite-channel random Schr\"odinger operator: from fermionization of the Dyson--Riccati gas to Kontsevich integral
**Yan V. Fyodorov, Pierre Le Doussal — arXiv:2610.27154**

Rare disorder configurations create spectral weight beyond edges that appear sharp in a large-channel approximation. Describing that weight together with typical states is difficult, because coupling several one-dimensional channels turns the scalar random Schrödinger problem into a matrix problem. The paper finds an exact solution that retains an arbitrary finite number of channels.

For a complex Hermitian white-noise potential in the unitary symmetry class, $\beta=2$, the authors transform the matrix wave equation into stochastic Riccati dynamics. Its eigenvalues form a repelling Dyson–Riccati gas. Two cancellations make the problem solvable: the unitary-class repulsion can be removed by fermionization, and the particular quadratic drift turns the remaining apparent many-body contribution into a sum of one-body terms.

This yields a free-fermion representation at finite sample length. In the long-wire limit, an Airy Hankel determinant encodes both the density of states and the sum of Lyapunov growth exponents. Its equivalent Kontsevich integral representation supports asymptotic analysis of the rare-state Lifshitz tail and the large-channel spectral edge. The edge is rounded over an $N^{-4/5}$ window, where $N$ is the channel number, with a profile governed by a particular Painlevé I solution. Extension of that profile to a broader class of random band matrices remains a universality conjecture.

**Background.** The Riccati matrix is a matrix logarithmic derivative of a fundamental wavefunction solution. Its eigenvalues develop poles when the wavefunction acquires nodes. Identifying escape to negative infinity with reinjection at positive infinity turns these poles into windings; their stationary current counts energy levels. The relevant stationary process therefore carries probability current and is not an equilibrium gas.

Fermionization rewrites its evolution as antisymmetric independent-particle dynamics. The resulting determinant reduces the many-variable problem to one-variable functions. The Kontsevich integral is a cubic matrix integral on a suitable complex contour; its contour implements the physical boundary condition and makes the large-channel critical limit accessible.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantial exact-solvability result for a disordered spectral problem, with a useful bridge between stochastic dynamics, localization, and matrix-integral methods. It resolves finite-channel information that a sharp large-channel edge suppresses, while separating an exact model calculation from a proposed broader universality class. The Lyapunov result concerns their sum, so a complete set of channel-dependent localization scales requires further work. Testing the conjectured edge profile outside the white-noise model would clarify how much of the integrable structure survives microscopically.

## arXiv-2026-10-21 — Item 9

### Systematic Truncated Wigner Approximation for Open Spin Systems Reveals Long-Range Order Far from Equilibrium
**Igor Timofeev, Dawid A. Hryniuk, Marzena H. Szymańska — arXiv:2610.27462**

Long-range interactions can stabilize order against dissipative fluctuations, but deciding where this happens in two dimensions is computationally demanding. Mean field omits the fluctuations that may destroy order, while long-range connectivity limits many methods that otherwise treat correlations well. This paper develops a scalable spin phase-space approximation and uses it to examine that competition.

The construction starts from the Stratonovich–Weyl correspondence, representing each spin by a function on a Bloch sphere. Expanding the exact spin star product through second order produces, for the models studied, a Fokker–Planck equation and associated stochastic trajectories. Both deterministic drift and reservoir noise follow from the Lindblad dynamics. The expansion parameter is $1/(2S+1)$, with $S$ the local spin quantum number.

Applied to dissipative anisotropic XY lattices containing 400 spins, the method strongly suppresses the staggered order predicted by nearest-neighbor mean field. Individual trajectories reveal vortex–antivortex pairs, supporting the proposed fluctuation-driven destruction mechanism. With algebraically decaying interactions, the authors find an extended region interpreted as $\mathbb Z_2$ order, including a return to disorder at sufficiently strong coupling. No ordered region is resolved beyond approximately $\alpha\approx 3.5$, where $\alpha$ is the interaction-decay exponent.

The evidence includes exact and tensor-network benchmarks, but quantitative discrepancies remain. In particular, the large-system phase diagram comes from a finite-size semiclassical approximation, and its long-distance correlations retain finite-size ambiguity. The quoted range boundary should therefore be treated as a numerical estimate within that method.

**Background.** The spherical representation keeps spin geometry explicit. Operator multiplication becomes a star product: ordinary multiplication supplemented by derivative corrections that retain quantum noncommutativity. Keeping terms through second derivative order converts phase-space evolution into a diffusion problem, which can be sampled using noisy trajectories with two coordinates per spin.

This explains both scalability and the approximation’s limitation. Sampling many trajectories controls statistical error, while discarded derivative terms produce a separate systematic error. Large spin supplies a formal expansion parameter; applying the same truncation to spin one half relies more heavily on benchmarks. A trajectory is a tool for reconstructing quantum observables, rather than an exact microscopic spin history.

**Why it matters?** The main contribution is a systematic route to noise-inclusive dynamics in geometries where exact methods become expensive. Its ability to expose defects along individual trajectories also helps connect numerical loss of order to a physical mechanism. This makes it useful for non-equilibrium theory and quantum-simulation benchmarks. Stronger finite-size scaling and independent checks of the long-range ordered region would be needed to turn the proposed phase boundaries into reliable thermodynamic conclusions.

## arXiv-2026-10-21 — Item 10

### Boundary One-Point Functions in the Open XXZ Spin Chain with Generic Integrable Boundaries
**Till Bargheer, Tengis Batmunkh, Paul Ryan — arXiv:2610.27062**

Solving an integrable model’s spectrum does not automatically make its observables accessible. This gap becomes pronounced for an open XXZ chain with generic boundary fields: the boundaries break conservation of total longitudinal spin, removing the simple reference-state structure used by familiar Bethe-ansatz constructions. Even boundary magnetization then becomes a nontrivial exact-calculation problem.

The authors extend Functional Separation of Variables to this setting and obtain determinant formulas for longitudinal and transverse boundary-spin expectation values in arbitrary finite-chain Hamiltonian eigenstates. The two ends need not satisfy a relation that restores a special boundary configuration, and no reference state is required. Direct diagonalization supplies finite-volume checks.

The central simplification concerns the inhomogeneous Baxter equation encoding the spectrum. Generic boundaries introduce an additional source term, but its zeros make it vanish under the contour functionals used to build the scalar product. The determinant construction therefore survives the loss of spin conservation. Differentiating the spectral equations with respect to boundary parameters then produces a finite linear system; its determinant ratios give derivatives of conserved eigenvalues and, through the Hellmann–Feynman relation, the boundary observables.

The demonstrated result concerns diagonal matrix elements of boundary spins. General off-diagonal form factors and full spatial or dynamical correlation functions remain further targets.

**Background.** A Baxter Q-function packages the Bethe-root data into a single function satisfying a finite-difference equation. Functional Separation of Variables works directly with such functions and carefully chosen contour integrals, allowing overlaps and derivatives to be organized without constructing the full many-body wavefunction.

The important point here is why the extra source is harmless. Its zeros cancel the poles that would otherwise contribute to the contour integrals. Consequently, the same functional machinery can establish orthogonality and extract eigenvalue derivatives even though the spectral equation itself has changed. Boundary fields are especially convenient probes because differentiating the Hamiltonian with respect to them inserts the corresponding boundary spin.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful methodological advance beyond spectral integrability. It makes exact boundary response accessible in a setting where generic integrable boundaries had obstructed existing observable calculations. For interests in solvable models, the broader lesson is that the functional structure needed for observables can survive the loss of a conventional reference state. Extending the construction to non-diagonal matrix elements would be the next substantive step toward boundary dynamics and correlation functions.
