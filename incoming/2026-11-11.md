## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 1

### Surface codes with coherent errors: optimal decoding and finite-size crossovers
**Xu-Dong Dai, Zhou-Quan Wan, Xi Dai, Guo-Yi Zhu — arXiv:2611.13098**

Why do coherent-error simulations give such unstable threshold estimates as code distance increases? Uniform phase rotations followed by perfect syndrome measurements retain interference between error strings, and the accessible sizes may remain far from their asymptotic regime.

The authors derive a replicated Majorana description with an interaction quadratic in the detuning from the ballistic SWAP point. Its marginal renormalization-group flow produces an exponentially large crossover length, separated from a much smaller algebraic scale. A broad intermediate regime can therefore resemble a stable decoding plateau. Free-fermion simulations approximately collapse in the proposed scaling variable, supporting a thermodynamic Pauli-decoding threshold at $\theta_c=\pi/4$; this threshold value is a field-theoretic and numerical conclusion, rather than an exact theorem.

A separate exact result establishes that, for even distances, maximum-likelihood Pauli recovery maximizes entanglement fidelity among deterministic syndrome-conditioned quantum recoveries. Odd distances behave differently: their conditional logical rotations admit exact quantum reversal.

**Background.** The important distinction is between a conditional rotation and a conditional weak measurement of the logical qubit. A rotation can be undone if its angle is known. A weak measurement has unequal singular values and has already transferred information to the syndrome record. Distance parity fixes the relative phases of the two logical-sector amplitudes and determines which situation occurs.

**Why it matters?** This connects microscopic coherent interference to both continuum scaling and recovery optimality. It supplies a concrete explanation for misleading finite-size behavior, while making clear why Pauli-decoding failure and irreversible information loss need not coincide.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 2

### Dynamical GKP codes
**Jonathan Pelletier, Baptiste Royer — arXiv:2611.14081**

Can oscillator codes gain the flexibility of Floquet codes, where a sequence of measurements defines the protected information? Static GKP lattices provide a geometric language for displacement errors, but changing the measured translations requires tracking which constraints and logical operators survive through time.

The authors construct subsystem GKP codes and derive measurement update rules using scaled symplectic lattices. They then turn measurement histories into detectors and give a finite-window method for computing syndromes and distance. Their two-mode square-hexagonal example repeatedly teleports one logical qubit between two lattice geometries. It lowers measurement weight and combines their available logical gates, simplifying access to the single-qubit Clifford group.

**Background.** A phase-space translation acquires a commutation phase determined by the symplectic area between its displacement vector and another translation. Measuring a new translation retains the commuting part of the previous stabilizer lattice and adds the measured vector. “Ancestry” expresses a present stabilizer in terms of earlier measurements; comparing its inferred and observed phases produces a detector. A persistent lattice supplies constraints that can repeatedly be inferred within a bounded number of rounds, making an indefinitely running protocol analyzable with finite data.

**Why it matters?** The advance is a framework for designing measurement-driven bosonic codes, with a worked example showing an operational benefit. It extends familiar dynamical-code reasoning into a setting where geometry and continuous measurement introduce genuinely new possibilities; realistic finite-energy noise performance remains a further task.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 3

### Frustration-free control for quantum-state preparation: an exact first-passage theory
**T. Dörstel, T. C. Lang, T. Iadecola, J. H. Wilson, M. Buchhold — arXiv:2611.13094**

Local measurement and feedback can remove violations of a target state's constraints, but what actually limits the preparation speed? The difficulty is usually an exponentially large open-system evolution.

For preparation of the permutation-symmetric Dicke manifold, the authors find an exact closure on singlet-pair projectors. Measurements transport their endpoints, while feedback removes pairs when the endpoints occupy a corrected bond. The many-body problem becomes a classical walk with absorption in pair-configuration space. On a periodic nearest-neighbor chain, its slowest rate is $2[1-\cos(\pi/N_q)]$, in units of the measurement rate per bond, giving quadratic relaxation-time scaling in the number of qubits. This rate is an exact Liouvillian eigenvalue; bounds place the full Liouvillian gap within a factor of two.

**Background.** The walkers are pairs of sites, not particles moving on the original chain. There are quadratically many pair configurations, whereas local feedback absorbs only linearly many. Transport must bring a violation into that small absorbing set. This geometric mismatch explains why faster transport eventually encounters an absorption bottleneck. A second closure tracks the final state selected from the initial condition, so reaching the Dicke manifold does not imply preparing an arbitrary chosen Dicke state.

**Why it matters?** This is an unusually transparent exactly solvable theory of measurement-assisted preparation. It replaces qualitative speed arguments with spectra, preparation bounds, and a mechanism for deciding whether improved transport or expanded feedback can help.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 4

### Categorical RMT
**Chuanxin Cui, Kristan Jensen, Sridip Pal, Moshe Rozali — arXiv:2611.13093**

Random matrix theory describes chaotic spectra only after symmetry-enforced structure has been removed. With non-invertible symmetries, even identifying the independent spectral blocks becomes subtle: different boundary twists can share exactly the same dynamical spectrum.

The authors propose organizing the combined twisted Hilbert spaces by irreducible representations of the tube algebra. Symmetry fixes degeneracies and shared spectral pieces; ordinary random matrix statistics govern the remaining multiplicity spaces. Tests in chaotic Fibonacci anyon and ordinary symmetry chains support this prescription. Extensions to conformal field theories incorporate additional correlations required by modular covariance, with holographic wormhole calculations reproducing the corresponding spectral ramps.

**Background.** A topological symmetry line running along Euclidean time imposes a twisted spatial boundary condition. Wrapping another line around it, with allowed junctions, gives an operation that can connect different twists. These cylinder operations form the tube algebra. Its irreducible sectors can occur in several twisted Hilbert spaces, forcing those appearances to share one Hamiltonian block. Treating each twist as an independent random matrix would erase these exact identifications.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual gain is a practical rule for separating generalized symmetry from chaos. It makes non-invertible symmetry visible in spectral correlations and connects modern QFT symmetry structures to concrete many-body diagnostics. The random-matrix description remains a universality proposal supported by examples, rather than a general theorem of chaotic dynamics.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 5

### Dynamics of R\'enyi entanglement entropies and Eigenstate Thermalization
**Riccardo Senese, Pasquale Calabrese — arXiv:2611.13141**

How can stationary Floquet eigenstates encode the growth of entanglement after a quench? Ordinary eigenstate thermalization describes local observables, whereas subsystem purity is nonlinear in the state and depends on correlations among several eigenstate amplitudes.

The authors express purity as an expectation value of a subsystem swap in two replicas. Its evolution then has a spectral representation, allowing dephasing to describe entanglement growth. Exact random-matrix calculations establish a reference structure, which is dressed by smooth functions to formulate ETH-like ansätze for deterministic local dynamics. Crucially, the construction also retains correlations with initial-state overlaps. Numerical tests use one-dimensional Floquet models. For large subsystems, locality and bulk dephasing simplify the spectral dependence to one fundamental frequency.

**Background.** The swap exchanges the subsystem between two copies while leaving its complement untouched. Its expectation equals purity, so an apparently nonlinear observable becomes linear in the replicated space. However, randomizing swap matrix elements independently of the initial state would lose the correlations responsible for quench dynamics. The paper's spectral weights keep those dependencies together.

**Why it matters?** This offers a bridge between eigenstate statistics and entanglement dynamics beyond fully random circuits. Its value is the structure of the proposed effective description, including extensive subsystems. Turning that structure into an efficient spectral-filter calculation is a prospective application, not an algorithm already demonstrated here.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 6

### Solving the SU(2) BFSS model
**Kabir Bajaj, Henry W. Lin — arXiv:2611.14512**

Even the smallest nontrivial BFSS matrix quantum mechanics has a strongly coupled ground state and scattering along flat directions. Knowing that its supersymmetric ground state exists does not provide its wavefunction or nonperturbative scattering data.

The authors exploit gauge and rotational invariance to reduce the ground-state supercharge equations to coupled differential equations in three bosonic variables, then solve them numerically. Ground-state correlators agree with matrix-bootstrap constraints to five significant figures. The wavefunction's power-law tail reproduces an independently computed three-point amplitude. Finite-box spectra also yield nonperturbative scattering phase shifts, agreeing in the appropriate regime with the familiar velocity-dependent Born–Oppenheimer interaction. Positive-energy bound states and metastable resonances enrich the resulting spectral picture.

**Background.** Lüscher's method extracts scattering information from how a finite boundary shifts energy levels. At large separation, an interacting radial wave has a phase offset relative to a free wave. Requiring that wave to satisfy the box boundary condition converts the discrete spectrum into phase shifts. Supersymmetry constrains polarization dependence, leaving scalar partial-wave data to determine numerically.

**Why it matters?** This makes a strongly coupled matrix model quantitatively accessible at the wavefunction and scattering levels, supplying benchmarks for bootstrap and future simulation methods. The result is substantial without extrapolating it to large matrix size, where the gravitational interpretation requires a very different limit.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 7

### Reading long-range information from a separable quantum record requires entangled measurements
**Qingtian Miao — arXiv:2611.14295**

Does an environment need entanglement to store information that only collective quantum measurements can recover? This paper constructs a spatially local recording process whose output is fully separable, yet whose distant information becomes inaccessible to every local readout strategy.

Each bond of initially uncorrelated Ising spins writes its relative orientation into a probe qubit using two nonorthogonal states. The receiver must infer whether two distant spins agree. On the square lattice, the paper proves an interval of recording strengths where measurements with separable effects lose all asymptotic advantage, while a joint measurement retains a positive advantage independent of distance. The upper bound extends to effects positive under partial transposition across every cut. The proven separation uses overlapping bounds, rather than an exact determination of the collective-readout transition.

**Background.** The record is a mixture of probe product states because the spins serve as shared classical labels. Nevertheless, the bond states are nonorthogonal. Combining uncertain labels along long paths is fundamentally different from reading classical bits. A percolation connection bounds restricted readout, while a separate classical-inference mapping lower-bounds the joint measurement's performance.

**Why it matters?** This separates the quantum character of information storage from the entanglement needed to retrieve it. It gives measurement-induced information transitions an operational task and a locality-based construction, offering a useful direction beyond asking only whether a state itself is entangled.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 8

### Tripartite nonlocal magic from reflected and negativity spectra
**Sriram Akella — arXiv:2611.13071**

Which nonstabilizer resources genuinely belong to correlations among three regions, rather than to a removable choice of basis within each region? Directly minimizing magic over local unitaries is difficult, and marginal entanglement spectra alone do not settle the tripartite problem.

The paper gives a spectral characterization: a tripartite pure state is locally unitary equivalent to a stabilizer state precisely when its marginal and reflected spectra are flat and its negativity spectrum is flat in absolute value. Spectral anti-flatness therefore supplies a faithful diagnostic. Applications distinguish a lattice-corner contribution in non-Abelian quantum doubles from a connectivity transition in random tensor networks.

**Background.** The reflected spectrum comes from canonically purifying a two-party density matrix using its positive square root, then grouping each region with its copied partner. The negativity spectrum instead consists of eigenvalues after partial transposition; their signs are discarded for the flatness test. These constructions probe correlation structure absent from ordinary marginals. Here “flat” means that all nonzero eigenvalues are equal, with the absolute-value qualification for negativity.

**Why it matters?** This replaces a difficult optimization with computable spectral conditions and potentially measurable moments. The quantum-double example also sharpens interpretation: the diagnostic can depend on how a tripartition meets the lattice, so a nonzero value need not be a robust invariant of a topological phase.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 9

### More physics at the entangling surface: boundary conditions and entanglement in 2d TQFT
**Jackson R. Fliss, Alan Rios Fukelman, Stathis Vitouladitis — arXiv:2611.13078**

Entanglement in a topological field theory depends on how one cuts its physical Hilbert space into regional factors. A path integral with boundary conditions can implement a cut, but it need not preserve state norms, undermining its interpretation as an embedding of the original quantum system.

The authors repair this by dressing the cut with edge operators. In the compact semisimple setting they study, this gives norm-preserving maps and entanglement formulas for general states, cuts, and boundary choices. Further results include noninteger effective boundary degeneracies, reconstruction of higher-genus partition functions from multi-interval Rényi entropies, and diagnostics of gapped phases and symmetry breaking.

**Background.** Different vacuum sectors acquire different normalization factors under a bare cut. Edge dressing compensates these factors sector by sector, so the cut state retains the original inner product before either region is traced out. More general allowed dressings also change the edge entanglement. Thus choosing boundary conditions and edge operators specifies physical information about factorization; it is not merely a normalization convention applied after computing entropy.

**Why it matters?** This clarifies what entanglement actually measures in an effective topological theory and provides a constructive alternative when a simple shrinkable boundary is unavailable. It is valuable for connecting QFT factorization, generalized symmetry, and entropic phase diagnostics, while extensions to noncompact theories and higher dimensions require additional work.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 10

### Quantized memory of measurement records near the light cone of quantum circuits
**Jamal Slim — arXiv:2611.13175**

Can measurement records from a scrambling circuit have an exact classical reference after the full device becomes too large to simulate? Near a circuit's light cone, causality removes most of the degrees of freedom that might otherwise make this impossible.

For a brickwork circuit with one polarized qubit in a maximally mixed background, the edge record is a two-state Markov chain for arbitrary two-qubit gates. Moving the readout inward requires a finite set of co-moving qubits, whose size depends on displacement from the edge but not chain length. The resulting bounds on record memory are generically attained. Hardware demonstrations test this structure and its noise sensitivity.

**Background.** Along circuit diagonals, information passes through a cascade and cannot propagate back toward the leading edge. A finite cut therefore separates all relevant past and future information. Record memory is defined through the rank of the matrix of joint past–future probabilities, rather than by the number of previous outcomes appearing in a classical Markov rule. Random Pauli operations immediately before incoming sites meet the edge restore the maximally mixed input needed by the exact law.

**Why it matters?** The theoretical contribution is an exact measurement-record description based on causal geometry rather than gate fine-tuning. It gives monitored dynamics a scalable benchmark and realizes finite-memory open-system models inside a larger unitary circuit, under explicit initial-state and readout conditions.

### Planckian Charge Equilibration in Fast Scramblers
**Jaewon Kim, Eduardo Fradkin — arXiv:2611.13081**

Does maximal scrambling determine how quickly an ordinary density imbalance relaxes? Both processes can occur on a thermal timescale, but they involve different correlation functions and need not have identical rates.

The authors divide complex Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev models and Yukawa generalizations into two sets of fermion flavors, prepare a weak relative chemical-potential bias, and study its removal in linear response. In the low-temperature conformal regime, the slow relative-charge response has rate $\Gamma=2\pi(2\Delta-d)k_BT/\hbar$, where $\Delta$ is the fermion scaling dimension and $d$ is spatial dimension. The dimension-dependent result concerns the specified imbalance protocol; spatially localized preparation in one dimension also distinguishes fixed-position and propagating-front behavior.

**Background.** Total charge is conserved, whereas the difference between flavor subsets can decay. In the large-size collective action, interactions depend on the flavor-averaged propagator. They therefore generate no separate interaction vertex in the relative fluctuation channel. Its response reduces to a bubble built from fully interacting equilibrium propagators, allowing conformal scaling to determine the relaxation law.

**Why it matters?** This gives a controlled comparison between scrambling and equilibration using an ordinary retarded response. It supports a thermal relaxation scale while showing that maximal scrambling does not fix its numerical coefficient. The scope is these conformal large-size models and weak imbalances, rather than a universal relaxation bound.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 12

### Tunneling from an oscillating state in quantum field theory
**Oliver Janssen, Matthew Kleban, Mehrdad Mirbabayi, Cameron Norton — arXiv:2611.14022**

A scalar field can oscillate well below a potential barrier and still decay by bubble nucleation. The obstacle is that tunneling depends on the wavefunction's under-barrier tail, which is not determined just by its instantaneous mean position.

Using a Euclidean source construction for a gradually prepared coherent state, the authors obtain two complementary limits. When $mR\ll1$, with mass $m$ and bubble radius $R$, the background is effectively frozen during nucleation, but the relevant shifted potential includes subtraction of its linear force term. At small oscillation amplitude, without requiring a small mass-radius product, the leading correction to the tunneling exponent is linear in the amplitude and changes with oscillation phase. For thin walls, its coefficient grows exponentially with the bubble radius in mass units.

**Background.** Euclidean sources specify the prepared quantum state rather than imposing a classical trajectory alone. The small coherent excitation grows along the Euclidean continuation near the bounce tips, so its overlap with the bounce tail can strongly affect the decay exponent. This explains why a minute real-time oscillation can matter appreciably. The calculation determines the exponent, leaving the semiclassical prefactor open.

**Why it matters?** This supplies a state-sensitive method for false-vacuum decay away from equilibrium and resolves a qualitative question about phase dependence. It connects semiclassical QFT with driven or quenched metastable systems without assuming that all time-dependent states share one instantaneous decay prescription.

### Rational quantum spectral filtering by repeat-until-success protocol
**Maksim A. Gavreev, Aleksey K. Fedorov, Evgeniy O. Kiktenko — arXiv:2611.13154**

Spectroscopy and state filtering often call for sharply structured functions of a Hamiltonian. Fixed coherent query sequences naturally generate polynomials, which can make rational responses such as broadened resolvents expensive to approximate.

This paper uses finite controller memory and intermediate measurements with three outcomes: accept, reject, or continue. Continuing preserves the current quantum state. Summing over arbitrarily many possible stopping times produces rational acceptance responses. Under termination and symmetry assumptions, the authors characterize all realizable real rational probabilities on the normalized spectral interval and provide a constructive compiler. Resolvent and Lorentzian examples improve expected query cost relative to the specified fixed-depth polynomial-postselection baseline, including restarts.

**Background.** A block encoding embeds a normalized Hermitian operator in a subblock of a unitary, giving circuit access to its spectrum. Here repeated continuation creates a geometric-series-like sum, evaluated through a finite Lyapunov equation. Importantly, the acceptance probability $R(X)$ does not by itself specify the accepted state transformation: distinguishable stopping histories can alter coherence between different eigenvalues. The paper computes a separate coherence kernel to track that effect.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful algorithmic mechanism with direct physical applications in spectral resolution. Its strongest contribution is exact rational measurement response with finite memory. Broader advantages require comparing full state preparation, coherence requirements, and runtime distributions against alternatives beyond the chosen baseline.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 14

### Error Resilience and Unitary 2-Designs
**Zheng-Hang Sun, Fabian Ballar Trigueros, Xhek Turkeshi, Piotr Sierant — arXiv:2611.14314**

Why do some scrambling encoders protect information in circuit-inversion experiments while others fail, even when their dynamics thermalize? Familiar chaotic behavior does not guarantee the moment structure needed for recovery.

The authors analyze encoding, a unitary error layer, inverse decoding, and ancilla postselection. They derive an inequality relating recovery fidelity to the encoder ensemble's excess second frame potential. Undoped matchgate encoders lack a stable protecting phase in the thermodynamic limit. Adding non-Gaussian gates drives their relevant statistics toward the appropriate random ensemble and restores protection. Hamiltonian encoders provide further examples of the same distinction.

**Background.** Matchgates act as fermionic Gaussian transformations: they rotate Majorana operators linearly and retain substantial structure despite creating entanglement. Non-Gaussian gates mix operator sectors that Gaussian dynamics cannot adequately randomize. The frame potential measures the ensemble's second-moment discrepancy; the fidelity probes only part of that discrepancy and must also be interpreted alongside the postselection probability.

**Why it matters?** This turns recovery into an operational probe of randomness beyond thermalization and links information protection to a controlled departure from solvable Gaussian dynamics. It is a conceptual contribution to noisy many-body circuits. Because the protocol uses inverse decoding and postselection, its protecting phase does not directly establish a fault-tolerance threshold for an implementable error-correcting architecture.

### Quantum Control Geometry as a Metric of Chaos
**Mahmoud Abdelshafy, Joaquin F. Rodriguez-Nieva — arXiv:2611.13101**

Level repulsion can identify chaos while leaving large differences in relaxation and eigenstate structure unresolved. Can one quantify those differences using the Hamiltonian changes that a physical system actually permits?

The authors build a geometric susceptibility from eigenstate responses to a manifold of allowed controls, respecting locality and conservation laws. In the studied chaotic models, small susceptibility coincides with strong eigenstate randomness, broad operator spectral response, and reduced nonhydrodynamic slow structure. This suggests a shared organization behind several diagnostics that are usually considered separately.

**Background.** The adiabatic gauge potential generates the rotation of an eigenbasis under a Hamiltonian deformation. Its off-diagonal matrix elements divide perturbation matrix elements by energy differences, making its squared norm sensitive to low-frequency spectral weight. A regulated denominator supplies a finite frequency resolution. Extending this response to several control directions identifies especially sensitive combinations of perturbations and avoids tying the diagnosis to one arbitrarily chosen probe.

**Why it matters?** The method gives a physically constrained way to compare chaotic regimes and potentially choose controls that suppress slow dynamics. Its significance lies in linking geometry to spectral response, rather than simply adding another chaos indicator. The observed alignment of diagnostics is evidence from model studies; it does not yet establish a universal scalar ordering of all chaotic systems or their scrambling rates.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 16

### Time Direction Entanglement Entropy of Free Fermion Ground States
**Xinyu Sun, Bowei Liu, Joshua Yi, Biao Lian — arXiv:2611.13085**

What does a time interval mean as a quantum subsystem? Operators observed at different times are not independent tensor factors, so treating successive instants as separate sites would introduce artificial degrees of freedom.

This paper studies the minimal fermionic mode space supporting operators sampled along a temporal interval. The resulting entropy distinguishes clean gapless systems, band insulators, and Anderson-localized systems. Analytical Toeplitz methods organize clean-system scaling, including sampling-dependent volume laws. In the continuous-sampling limit, the respective long-interval behaviors are logarithmic, saturating, and sub-area-law. Localized systems numerically exhibit initial growth followed by a power-law decrease, described as a Page-curve analogy.

**Background.** Unequal-time fermion operators have a nontrivial anticommutation matrix. Diagonalizing and whitening its nonzero subspace constructs independent canonical modes, whose ordinary correlation matrix defines the reduced state. The temporal subsystem therefore grows by adding linearly independent operator directions, not by appending a fresh Hilbert space at every time. Localization makes the accessible mode space increasingly confined, helping explain the unusual late-time decrease.

**Why it matters?** This offers a concrete, positive-state definition of temporal entanglement in solvable systems and exposes the role of sampling and localization. It is useful for comparing information stored across time with conventional spatial entanglement. The Page-curve shape should be understood through this operator-space construction, without importing a black-hole evaporation mechanism.

### Lattice Hamiltonians for Chiral Gauge Theories
**Dorota M. Grabowska — arXiv:2611.13886**

A nonperturbative chiral gauge theory needs more than a Euclidean prescription if one wants real-time evolution or quantum circuits. Extending boundary gauge fields through an auxiliary dimension can introduce dependence on other times, obstructing a Hamiltonian formulation.

The author constructs a gauge-fixed slab description whose bulk configuration depends on instantaneous boundary variables and an integer topological register. Gauss's law is built into the physical variables, and boundary anomaly conditions become tests of whether the boundary can satisfy it independently. Background-field calculations support mirror decoupling and degeneracy between adjacent topological sectors after Pauli–Villars subtraction.

A consequential qualification appears in the treatment of dynamical gauge variables: integrating out the regulator alone can spoil kinetic positivity. A regulated functional combining regulator and fermion seas with the required properties is left for future construction.

**Background.** The integer register remembers boundary windings through a localized bulk instanton. It supplements the instantaneous boundary configuration with topological information that a smooth local extension cannot discard. Regulator subtraction must remove bulk vacuum effects without introducing an unphysical kinetic term when the boundary itself moves; static-background cancellation is therefore insufficient.

**Why it matters?** This is a concrete Hamiltonian-oriented approach to an important QFT obstruction, with clear relevance to eventual quantum simulation. Its value is the construction and exposed consistency conditions. The unresolved dynamical regularization prevents treating the paper as a completed, generally usable lattice realization of chiral gauge dynamics.

## arXiv-2026-11-11 — Item 18

### The KLN theorem meets collinear factorisation: Local Unitarity for hadronic cross-sections
**Zeno Capatti, Lucien Huber — arXiv:2611.13184**

Infrared cancellation is physically inclusive, yet hadronic calculations usually treat incoming and outgoing radiation asymmetrically. Final-state singularities cancel across degenerate states; initial-state collinear singularities are absorbed into parton distributions. Can initial-state inclusiveness be restored while retaining factorisation?

The authors define partonic cross-sections by summing over incoming as well as outgoing multiplicities. A clustering prescription relates these incoming configurations to effective partonic objects. At next-to-leading order and leading power, the resulting quantities are infrared finite and admit a universal conversion to the conventional factorisation scheme. Analytic deep-inelastic and Drell–Yan calculations, together with numerical Drell–Yan and top-pair production, test the construction.

**Background.** Local Unitarity arranges different cuts of forward-scattering diagrams in common integration variables. Real and virtual singular contributions then cancel locally, before Monte Carlo integration, rather than only after separately divergent integrals are evaluated. Extending this organization to clustered incoming states is the paper's central step; the parton-distribution conversion preserves the connection to standard hadronic predictions.

**Why it matters?** This gives infrared cancellation a more symmetric formulation and a route to direct four-dimensional numerical calculations. It is a substantive methodological development in perturbative QFT. General higher-order automation and proposed applications to subleading powers require further demonstration beyond the leading-power, next-to-leading-order results established here.

### Inverse Scattering & Isospectrality in (Analog) Cosmology
**Christian F. Schmidt, Stefan Floerchinger — arXiv:2611.13871**

Can the particles created by a changing background reveal the history that created them? Particle counts alone lose phase information, allowing different expansion histories to produce identical excitation spectra.

For a minimally coupled massless scalar in the studied asymptotically static backgrounds, the authors map mode evolution to one-dimensional scattering. Inverting the scale factor exchanges expansion and contraction while preserving particle numbers and shifting phase. Including phase-sensitive spectral data permits unique reconstruction within the regular class considered. Exact scattering examples and numerical logistic histories demonstrate the procedure, with tests of finite momentum resolution and noise.

**Background.** After rescaling the field and using conformal time, the mode equation becomes a Schrödinger scattering equation whose potential depends on the scale factor. The relevant power of that scale factor is a zero-energy resonant solution. Inverse-scattering integral equations reconstruct a transformation kernel from the complex reflection amplitude; the zero-energy solution then recovers the background. This step avoids differentiating noisy reconstructed data to infer the potential first.

**Why it matters?** The useful advance is an explicit account of which information makes reconstruction possible and which ambiguities particle numbers retain. It connects exactly solvable scattering theory to analog quantum simulation and QFT in changing backgrounds. Application to actual cosmology would need assumptions and observables beyond the controlled asymptotically static setting used here.

**Sanziana Stelea, Anthony Gandon, Francesco Tacchino, Jad C. Halimeh, Ivano Tavernelli — arXiv:2611.14484**

In two spatial dimensions, an electric flux string can bend as well as break. Observing that competition requires both dynamical matter and a magnetic plaquette interaction, while noisy simulation makes it difficult to distinguish genuine spreading from error-induced mixing.

Using 63 superconducting qubits, including 12 ancillas, the authors evolve strings in a discrete two-valued gauge theory for up to fifteen second-order Trotter steps. Static and dynamical endpoints help separate pair creation from charge displacement. Spatial occupations and string-path measurements probe deconfined spreading, enhanced interior matter near resonance, and suppressed spreading at strong electric fields. Explicit matter and gauge registers preserve measurable Gauss constraints for error detection.

**Background.** A magnetic plaquette operation flips flux around one elementary square, connecting strings with different transverse paths. Gauge constraints identify allowed charge–flux combinations; retaining redundant variables makes these constraints usable as experimental checks. Postselection and noise rescaling improve observables but do not remove all errors. The source reports late-time mixing and growing mitigation uncertainties, while tensor-network agreement is assessed only where convergence is checked.

**Why it matters?** This experiment merits inclusion because it demonstrates a theoretical design principle: gauge redundancy can help protect access to genuinely two-dimensional dynamics. It supplies useful evidence for short-time nonperturbative simulation, with a clear route toward richer string phenomena. The data do not establish reliable arbitrary-time evolution or a general computational advantage.
