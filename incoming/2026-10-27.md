## arXiv-2026-10-27 — Item 1

### Self-Correcting 3D Surface Code with Growing Local Dimension
**Jinkang Guo — arXiv:2610.35099**

A passive quantum memory must protect both conjugate logical observables. In the ordinary 3D surface code, the membrane sector has thermal protection, while a string-like logical operator provides an inexpensive route to failure. This paper changes the resource balance by allowing the local Hilbert-space dimension to grow.

The construction preserves stabilizer supports and bulk translation invariance, but changes Pauli powers along one direction. The vulnerable logical string consequently carries successively doubled exponents. Under elementary unit shifts, building this operator becomes difficult even though its spatial support remains string-like. For $N$ physical qudits with odd local dimension $q=\Theta(N)$, the author proves, at sufficiently low temperature,

$$t_{\mathrm{mem}}\geq\exp[\Omega(N^{1/3})].$$

The theorem concerns separate error-sector memory times under local Metropolis dynamics; one sector uses a thermal encoding. Numerical first-passage results support the predicted exponential growth with linear size.

**Background.** The relevant error geometry is the Lee metric: the distance of a cyclic Pauli shift counts the smallest number of unit steps needed to produce it. This distinguishes a large shift from a single small displacement. The Hamiltonian likewise assigns energy through syndrome displacement magnitudes. An error can therefore have small spatial support but require many elementary thermal moves. Protection depends on these dynamics and energy assignments, as well as on the code.

**Why it matters?** This is an interesting conceptual tradeoff between spatial dimension, local dimension, and physically allowed noise. It gives a comparatively simple setting in which statistical-mechanical bottlenecks protect both sectors. Its practical relevance depends on realizing the weighted interactions and strongly favoring small shifts; robustness against more general local errors remains a separate question.

## arXiv-2026-10-27 — Item 2

### Multipartite dynamics beyond bipartite entanglement equilibration
**Yan Liu, Ya-Wen Sun, Ren-Jie Ying, Jun-Yuan Zeng — arXiv:2610.35377**

When entanglement entropy reaches a plateau, how much information dynamics has actually ended? Even stationarity of every Schmidt spectrum associated with a fixed tripartition leaves a stronger possibility: correlations among all three parties can continue to reorganize.

The authors demonstrate this separation using local-unitary invariants, quantities unchanged by independent basis rotations within each party. In self-dual kicked-Ising dynamics, all three one-party density matrices are exactly stationary during a finite time window, while a multipartite invariant changes. Haar-random circuits provide a complementary asymptotic construction at large local dimension.

They then engineer an independent dynamical clock using quantum error correction. A five-qubit code distributes logical information among three shares, each individually insensitive to the logical state. Coupling the encoded degree of freedom weakly to a locally scrambling bath allows ordinary entanglement to settle rapidly while multipartite structure responds on the slower logical-coupling timescale. This construction supplies a physical separation of channels, beyond a comparison of plateau times.

**Background.** The diagnostic contracts four copies of the state with different copy permutations in different parties. Ordinary purity uses the same swap pattern to recover a marginal spectrum; more intricate permutation patterns retain information about how the parties fit together. Such invariants can distinguish states with identical marginal spectra, although an individual invariant is not automatically an entanglement measure. In the code construction, erasure correction explains why logical motion can remain invisible to every individual share.

**Why it matters?** The paper sharpens what entanglement equilibration can establish about thermalization. Its combination of exact dynamics and protected logical information makes the distinction especially useful for thinking about hidden information channels. The independently tunable clock is demonstrated in designed models; whether comparable mechanisms arise naturally in broader Hamiltonian systems is an appealing next question.

## arXiv-2026-10-27 — Item 3

### Sublinear thermal state and ground state preparation for free fermions via system-bath interaction
**Zhiyan Ding, Lin Lin, Ke Wang — arXiv:2610.35370**

Fast relaxation does not by itself imply an inexpensive state-preparation algorithm: implementing dissipative transitions throughout a system can still require extensive Hamiltonian simulation time. This paper asks whether an extensive engineered bath can overcome that cost while retaining local system-bath couplings.

The authors couple free fermions to a proportional number of bath modes, evolve jointly, and reset the bath between rounds. Carefully chosen interaction envelopes filter energy transfers. Starting from arbitrary initial states, thermal preparation to trace-norm error $\epsilon$ requires total simulation time $\widetilde O(\sqrt{N}/\epsilon)$ at fixed temperature and bounded single-particle bandwidth. Gapped ground-state preparation has $\operatorname{polylog}(N/\epsilon)$ dependence at fixed gap.

The analysis also identifies why the two tasks differ. At finite temperature, fourth-order corrections can spoil the desired Gibbs state. Randomizing a frequency shared across the bath introduces another problem: correct single-mode thermal populations can coexist with unwanted correlations between modes. For a pure ground-state target, the corresponding obstruction is absent.

**Background.** An energy filter is the Fourier transform of the time-dependent interaction envelope. It controls which system transitions exchange excitations with the bath. The ratio of upward and downward transition rates determines the stationary occupations, but matching that ratio only at leading perturbative order need not control the full many-body state. The paper analyzes the joint channel beyond the usual weak-coupling Lindblad approximation where necessary.

**Why it matters?** This establishes a useful space-time tradeoff in dissipative quantum algorithms. Free fermions provide a controlled benchmark for understanding bath design, including mechanisms that can defeat an apparently favorable mixing-time estimate. The resource bounded here is Hamiltonian simulation time; an extensive bath and its implementation still carry costs. Extension to interacting systems would be the major further advance.

### The Free Scalar as a Thermal Envelope for Three-Dimensional CFTs
**Christopher P. Herzog, Andreas Stergiou — arXiv:2610.33179**

In two-dimensional CFT, the central charge fixes the leading thermal entropy. In three dimensions, thermal entropy and vacuum stress-tensor fluctuations are independent data. Can unitarity nevertheless constrain their ratio?

Writing the entropy density as $s=c_ST^2$, and denoting the stress-tensor two-point coefficient by $C_T$, the authors conjecture

$$c_S/C_T\leq16\pi\zeta(3),$$

with saturation by free scalars. Their stronger proposal bounds the normalized heat capacity on hyperbolic space by the free-scalar answer at every temperature above $T_0=(2\pi R)^{-1}$, where R is the hyperbolic curvature radius. The flat-space inequality follows from its high-temperature limit.

The evidence combines free theories, interacting large-N examples, holography, and available Monte Carlo thermodynamics. A local result near the conformal temperature follows from conformal-collider positivity, provided the thermal response is sufficiently smooth and the modular Hamiltonian has no entangling-surface boundary contribution. That local argument does not establish the global conjecture.

**Background.** The reduced vacuum state of a disk is conformally equivalent to a thermal state on hyperbolic space at the conformal temperature. Varying that temperature packages disk Rényi data and ordinary high-temperature thermodynamics into one function. Its derivatives near the vacuum point involve stress-tensor correlators. Conformal-collider positivity constrains the energy flux created by a local excitation, thereby restricting the stress-tensor three-point data that enter those derivatives. This supplies a bridge between vacuum consistency conditions and thermal response.

**Why it matters?** A universal bound would constrain how many thermal degrees of freedom a CFT can have relative to its vacuum stress fluctuations. The hyperbolic formulation gives a sharper target than a comparison of isolated numerical ratios. For field-theory reading, its value is both the proposed constraint and the concrete route connecting entanglement geometry, positivity, and thermodynamics; a global proof or a well-controlled counterexample would be decisive.

### Large spin large deviations for interacting particle systems
**Cristian Giardinà, Tomohiro Sasamoto — arXiv:2610.33523**

Rare density and current histories reveal more than average diffusion, but deriving their probabilities in a spatial continuum can be difficult for systems with unbounded occupation. The authors instead suppress fluctuations while keeping the lattice fixed, using a large-spin limit $s\to\infty$.

They derive a lattice diffusion law and the nonlinear Hamiltonian governing deviations from it. For the symmetric harmonic process, which permits transfers of variable particle number, this Hamiltonian becomes the classical non-compact Ishimori spin chain. The continuum macroscopic fluctuation theory emerges subsequently from a smooth spatial limit. Related constructions cover exclusion, inclusion, and energy-exchange processes, although their resulting spin Hamiltonians need not be integrable.

**Background.** A large-deviation Hamiltonian generates the cost of atypical histories. Its momentum-like field biases particle transfers; Hamiltonian trajectories describe the most likely way to realize an imposed rare event. Here the spin parameter controls the weak-fluctuation limit, rather than the lattice spacing. Rewriting density and its conjugate field as non-compact spin variables places them on a hyperboloid and exposes an existing integrable structure. The logarithmic Hamiltonian retains information about variable-size jumps that a Gaussian noise approximation would discard.

**Why it matters?** This provides a useful meeting point between non-equilibrium fluctuations and exactly solvable models. Keeping space discrete preserves a microscopic regulator while allowing controlled rare-event analysis. The integrable identification opens a route to solving optimal histories; the present paper establishes the framework and correspondence rather than completing that program for arbitrary boundary conditions.

## arXiv-2026-10-27 — Item 6

### When is a bosonic operator a Hamiltonian?
**Felix Fischer, Hermann Schulz-Baldes, Daniel Burgarth, Davide Lonigro — arXiv:2610.33545**

Engineering a Hermitian polynomial in oscillator quadratures does not always specify a unique closed quantum system. At sufficiently high occupation, additional boundary information can be required, and a finite Fock-space simulation may silently choose that information for you.

The authors turn this issue into a practical calculation for single-mode polynomial operators. In the number basis, the operator becomes a block Jacobi recurrence. Matrix-valued Weyl theory then supplies shrinking sets of possible Green functions, called Weyl disks. The ranks of their limiting radius matrices determine the deficiency indices: whether the operator has a unique self-adjoint realization, a family of realizations, or no self-adjoint extension on the given Hilbert space.

They implement the method and apply it to operators appearing in continuous-variable quantum information. The mathematical reduction is exact, while the numerical step distinguishes eigenvalues tending to zero from those approaching a nonzero limit. That distinction becomes delicate near parameter boundaries where the indices change. General multimode polynomials are outside the method's unrestricted scope.

**Background.** For an unbounded operator, its domain is part of its definition. Symmetry on a convenient dense set does not ensure that it generates unitary evolution. Deficiency indices count the missing boundary data needed for self-adjointness. Equal positive indices permit choices of boundary condition; unequal indices obstruct a self-adjoint extension. The Weyl-disk construction tracks whether sensitivity to a distant occupation cutoff disappears as that cutoff is removed.

**Why it matters?** This is a valuable model-validation tool for bosonic quantum simulation. It addresses a physical ambiguity that ordinary convergence checks can miss, and clarifies when a high-occupation regularization supplies essential dynamics. Its strongest practical contribution is making a specialized functional-analysis question accessible before simulation or gate compilation begins.

### Equilibrium propagation in non-equilibrium quantum systems
**Aditya Gandotra, David D. Noachtar, Aashish A. Clerk — arXiv:2610.35585**

A physical learning device can obtain many parameter gradients by comparing an unperturbed state with one weakly nudged toward the desired output. For quantum ground-state implementations, however, repeatedly preparing those states can dominate the cost. Could naturally relaxing, driven open systems support the same strategy?

The authors show that equilibrium propagation requires an appropriate susceptibility symmetry, which can survive far from thermal equilibrium. In an all-to-all transverse-field Ising model with local decay, they derive

$$\frac{\partial\langle\sigma_y^i\rangle}{\partial\Omega_j}=\frac{\partial\langle\sigma_y^j\rangle}{\partial\Omega_i}.$$

Here the drive amplitudes are the trainable parameters and the measured quantities are transverse coherences. This reciprocity allows an output-directed perturbation to reveal gradients throughout the device. The paper develops training examples for this model and for a parametrically driven bosonic lattice.

**Background.** Equilibrium propagation uses two steady-state preparations per update: a free state, then a state with all output fields nudged according to the output error. Reciprocal response converts the resulting changes in internal observables into the desired parameter gradients. The specialized ingredient here is hidden time-reversal symmetry, a quantum detailed-balance condition relating jump operators and the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian to their adjoints through a steady-state-dependent transformation. It constrains selected responses without requiring a Gibbs state. Together with the models' parameter covariance, it yields the needed susceptibility relation.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual result is a non-equilibrium response principle with an operational use. It connects exactly structured open-system dynamics to physical learning while supplying the relaxation mechanism within the model itself. The advantage concerns gradient acquisition and state preparation structure; useful scaling still depends on relaxation times, measurement precision, and the persistence of the required symmetry under imperfections.

### Revisiting monodromy defects in $\text{O}(N)$ conformal field theories
**Jiechao Feng, Nayan Myerson-Jain, G. Shankar, Taige Wang, Meng Cheng — arXiv:2610.33972**

A symmetry twist specifies what happens when a field encircles a defect, but does it uniquely determine the defect's long-distance physics? This question becomes particularly interesting when only some components of an order parameter are twisted: the remaining components can develop their own defect dynamics.

The authors study these partial-monodromy defects in interacting vector CFTs using renormalization-group analysis near four dimensions and a large-N expansion. They identify several defect fixed points and follow their evolution toward three dimensions. At large N, a stable three-dimensional solution has enhanced untwisted-field correlations and suppressed twisted-field correlations. The two sectors select different radial boundary conditions.

Classical Monte Carlo simulations in the three-component theory give qualitative support. The finite-N interpretation remains qualified by scaling-dimension drift as defect pinning is varied and evidence for defect ordering in part of the parameter range.

**Background.** A monodromy defect is analogous to a localized symmetry flux: transporting a field around it applies a prescribed symmetry transformation. Partial twisting leaves some components invariant. Near the defect, radial field equations admit different allowed scaling behaviors. Standard boundary conditions select the less enhanced branch, while alternate boundary conditions select a branch with stronger defect correlations. Local mass terms on the defect act as pinning couplings, and their RG flows determine which behavior is realized. Thus a fixed bulk CFT can support distinct defect universality classes.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a more complete physical account of symmetry defects, including the active role of components untouched by the twist. It is worthwhile QFT reading because extended observables can reveal structure absent from bulk critical exponents. The combination of perturbative, large-N, and numerical approaches is persuasive about the richness of the problem, while leaving quantitative finite-N identification open.

### Distillable entanglement is continuous
**Lukas Schmitt, David Sutter — arXiv:2610.35722**

Can an arbitrarily small change in a mixed quantum state cause a finite jump in its asymptotic Bell-pair yield? General continuity of distillable entanglement remained unresolved, particularly at boundaries between distillable and nondistillable states.

For states with trace distance $\epsilon<1/16$, the authors prove

$$|E_D(\rho)-E_D(\sigma)|\leq4\sqrt{\epsilon}\log_2 d+4h_2(\sqrt{\epsilon}).$$

Here $E_D$ is the distillable entanglement, $d$ is the smaller local Hilbert-space dimension, and the last term uses binary entropy. The proof constructs asymptotic conversion protocols between nearby states using local operations and classical communication, with an entanglement cost per copy that vanishes with their separation. Combining conversion with distillation controls the difference between optimal yields.

**Background.** Closeness of individual states does not give uniform closeness of arbitrarily many tensor copies. Consequently, continuity of a finite-copy quantity cannot simply be passed through the asymptotic optimization defining distillation. The conversion argument instead controls a resource rate: the additional shared entanglement needed per converted copy. This allows comparison of protocols even as their block lengths grow.

**Why it matters?** This closes a foundational stability question about an operational entanglement resource. It strengthens the justification for drawing conclusions about asymptotic quantum communication from approximate state descriptions. The result supplies continuity without making the distillation rate easy to calculate or resolving which states have bound entanglement.

### Twist-controlled imaginary-time Mpemba effect in quantum spin systems
**Zhi-Yao Ning, Zi-Xiang Li, Naoki Kawashima — arXiv:2610.35784**

An initially higher-energy state can approach a ground state faster in imaginary time if it avoids the slowest excited modes. Demonstrations become harder to interpret when changing the initial state simultaneously changes its entanglement and correlations. This paper constructs a controlled comparison.

Spatially varying on-site rotations preserve every real-space bipartite entanglement spectrum and the relevant correlation magnitudes. In the compact-boson description, a sinusoidal twist excites a selected standing-wave oscillator. Higher harmonics bypass low-energy modes and can overtake initially closer states during relaxation. The authors derive crossing times, show that varying amplitude at fixed harmonic gives no reversal, and delay the reversal by deliberately restoring slow-mode weight. Calculations on XXZ chains and ladders support the mechanism, alongside noiseless twelve-qubit simulations of variational quantum imaginary-time evolution.

**Background.** The twist $U_f=\exp(i\sum_j f_jS_j^z)$ factors into separate unitaries on either side of any spatial cut, explaining preservation of the initial Schmidt spectra. In the bosonized theory it displaces a Fourier oscillator. Imaginary-time evolution preferentially suppresses components with larger excitation gaps, so redistributing weight among modes can reverse the ordering of distances to the target.

**Why it matters?** This gives a particularly clean explanation of anomalous relaxation through controlled spectral weights. It connects bosonization to state-preparation dynamics without attributing the effect to changing initial entanglement. Algorithmic benefits require an additional step: the construction begins by twisting a known ground state, so it does not by itself solve preparation of an unknown target.

### Fast and accurate rare-event simulation by Markov chain Monte Carlo for general quantum codes
**Junichi Haruna, Kotaro Murakami — arXiv:2610.35498**

Estimating extremely small logical failure rates requires sampling methods that deliberately visit failures. Such methods become biased if their Markov chains remain trapped in only part of the failure ensemble. Single-bit proposals can encounter precisely this problem when logical sectors are separated by high-weight error configurations.

The authors combine locally balanced single-bit proposals with a cluster-exchange move that relocates errors while preserving their number. The decoder accepts an exchange when the new configuration still causes failure. Equal-weight rearrangements can therefore connect sectors without a noise-probability penalty for adding errors. Benchmarks on twelve codes under code-capacity bit-flip noise agree with an independent mean-fault-weight reference, including cases where ordinary proposals are trapped. At the lowest tested error rate, some timing improvements over direct sampling exceed four orders of magnitude.

**Background.** The splitting method expresses a rare failure probability through a ladder of ratios linking neighboring physical error rates. Each ratio uses samples conditioned on decoding failure. Keeping the chain inside this conditional ensemble makes geometry important: a locally accessible region can look equilibrated while other sectors remain unexplored. Weight-preserving exchange attacks that barrier; weight-changing moves remain necessary to explore the full distribution.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful methodological contribution at the QEC–statistical-mechanics interface. Reliable rare-event estimates affect comparisons between codes and decoders deep below threshold. The benchmarks establish substantial practical value, while efficient mixing for arbitrary codes and general circuit noise is not proved.

### Geometric aspects of fault tolerance
**Jonathan Conrad — arXiv:2610.35513**

Logical operations can be implemented by moving between code spaces, but a general account of when that motion preserves protected information is less developed than individual constructions. This paper formulates the problem geometrically for quantum channels, extending earlier treatments centered on unitary evolution.

The author represents encodings through their logical operator algebras and derives generalized Knill-Laflamme conditions for transport between them. Joint correctability induces a flat connection on suitable smooth families: locally, the transported logical information is insensitive to small path deformations. The framework is then developed for GKP codes, including the mathematical care required because ideal oscillator code states are distributions rather than normalizable vectors.

A further application constructs logical magic states by projecting Gaussian states into specially chosen GKP code spaces or syndrome sectors. Codes obtained from algebraic curves supply explicit examples, including a CCZ magic state associated with an octahedral curve.

**Background.** A bundle organizes a family of codes by attaching a logical frame to each code space. A connection specifies how to compare those frames during motion. Flatness removes local path dependence, while global loops can still implement a nontrivial logical transformation through holonomy.

GKP, or Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill, codes are specified by commuting displacement operators arranged on an oscillator phase-space lattice. A Jacobian lattice is built by integrating differential forms around the independent cycles of a compact Riemann surface; the intersection pairing supplies the symplectic structure needed for the displacement lattice. A code automorphism is a physical transformation preserving that lattice and hence the code space, while acting on its logical states. Symmetries of the underlying curve generate useful examples. When a symmetric Gaussian input is projected into such a code, its inherited logical symmetries can select a magic state. The projection is an essential resource in this mechanism.

**Why it matters?** The strongest contribution is a common language for correctable code deformation, oscillator geometry, and non-Clifford resources. It suggests systematic ways to discover protected operations and structured magic states. Turning the ideal constructions into competitive protocols requires finite-energy states, implementable projections, and an accounting of noise and preparation costs; the paper's geometric results do not supply those performance guarantees.

### Krylov geometry and optimal recovery under uncertain coherent noise
**Abhishek Chowdhury — arXiv:2610.33607**

Correcting an unknown coherent rotation depends on both the encoding and the information available about its angle distribution. Designing recovery for a guessed distribution can obscure what calibration actually guarantees. This paper analyzes their combined limit for a known finite-dimensional generator and one final recovery.

Two orthogonalization problems organize the result. A physical Krylov chain identifies the first error amplitude that distorts logical states differently. A second projection, over angle-dependent polynomials, determines how much of that amplitude a coherent recovery can cancel. Under explicit rank and moment conditions, the author obtains the optimal leading weak-noise entanglement infidelity and constructs an attaining recovery.

With incomplete calibration, one decoder is optimized against every compatible angle distribution. Commensurate spectral and calibration frequencies permit an exact finite moment formulation. In a four-photon example, population data at two durations retain a quartic error guarantee for a specified symmetric narrow-noise family, whereas one duration permits quadratic loss.

**Background.** If V is the encoding isometry and H the noise generator, the sequence $V,HV,H^2V,\ldots$ describes the error images reached by expanding the rotation. Orthogonalizing whole logical blocks retains whether each image is an undistorted embedding of the logical register. A scalar Krylov chain can lose this information. Separately, lower powers of the random angle can be used to cancel part of the first harmful amplitude. The uncancelled polynomial residual depends on distributional moments, explaining why calibration affects the optimal coefficient.

**Why it matters?** The paper makes calibration quality an explicit resource in coherent-error correction. Its useful lesson is that small measured averages need not exclude rare damaging rotations. The framework provides attainable guarantees within a precise model, while extension to several noncommuting generators or repeated fault-tolerant correction requires further work.

### Direct tests of conformal symmetry with Rydberg atoms: assessment and proposals
**Junchen Rong, Slava Rychkov — arXiv:2610.33156**

Measuring a critical exponent or a power-law two-point function does not directly test the full conformal symmetry. The authors identify a more discriminating observable that remains experimentally simple: the spatial dependence of equal-time correlations on a ring.

For angular separation $\phi$, a primary of scaling dimension $\Delta$ has a correlator proportional to $|2\sin(\phi/2)|^{-2\Delta}$. The paper studies its measurement in critical Rydberg-atom arrays, estimates snapshot requirements, and optimizes state-preparation ramps to reduce decoherence exposure. Numerical examples with twenty-four atoms indicate that a few thousand snapshots can resolve the shape, provided preparation errors are sufficiently controlled. **Background.** The experimental occupation variable contains several continuum operators. Staggering it isolates the ordering pattern, while subtracting its mean removes the identity contribution. The desired Ising order field then dominates at long distance, with higher-dimension operators producing lattice corrections. The practical question is therefore how well an accessible microscopic correlator follows the conformal shape over the available range. Faster optimized ramps address a separate systematic: correlations can be distorted before measurement by imperfect preparation and decoherence.

**Why it matters?** This is a particularly well-motivated quantum-simulation proposal because the observable tests an important theoretical principle. It converts familiar CFT geometry into an experimentally assessable criterion and analyzes the preparation obstacle rather than assuming an ideal critical state. A successful implementation would make emergent symmetry visible in a stronger sense than agreement with selected exponents.

### Exact Stress Tensor Flows in Causal Electrodynamics: Auxiliary Field Formalism
**Zahra Pezhman, H. Babaei-Aghbolagh, Komeil Babaei Velni — arXiv:2610.35135**

Stress-tensor deformations can generate nonlinear field theories, but a formal flow equation is only useful if its branches, causality, and relation to the underlying action remain controlled. This paper develops that control for a class of causal, electric-magnetic self-dual nonlinear electrodynamics theories.

An auxiliary potential encodes the nonlinear dynamics. The irrelevant coupling rescales this potential, while a marginal deformation shifts its argument. Eliminating the auxiliary coordinate produces an autonomous flow,

$$\partial_\lambda\mathcal L=X F(r),$$

where $X=T_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}$, $Y=(T^\mu{}_\mu)^2$, and $r=-T^\mu{}_\mu/\sqrt{4X-Y}$. The authors also reconstruct the potential from the kernel, express causality and characteristic propagation in these variables, and obtain explicit examples including Born-Infeld and logarithmic theories.

An important qualification is global: causality can give a unique auxiliary solution without making the flow kernel globally single-valued. Monotonicity of the invariant ratio along the selected branch is an additional requirement.

**Background.** The auxiliary field is algebraic and introduces no independent propagating mode. Solving its equation recovers the nonlinear electromagnetic action, while keeping it temporarily makes parameter deformations simple. Electric-magnetic duality here concerns rotations of the electromagnetic equations and their constitutive relation. The $T\bar T$-like terminology describes flows built from stress-tensor invariants; it does not by itself import the special quantum solvability properties of two-dimensional deformations.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful constructive framework for exploring irrelevant deformations while checking their physical propagation. Its value lies in the reversible dictionary between an action, a potential, and a stress-tensor kernel. It broadens the toolkit for solvable classical field theories, with quantum definition and ultraviolet completion remaining additional questions.

### Information geometry of continuum limits in lattice field theory: The Gross-Neveu model with Wilson fermions
**Akio Tomiya — arXiv:2610.34277**

Universality says that many microscopic actions produce the same long-distance field theory. It does not imply that their full microscopic probability distributions become indistinguishable. This paper separates those statements using the Fisher metric in a controlled lattice model.

The author studies the two-dimensional Gross-Neveu model after integrating out Wilson fermions, using the positive distribution of the auxiliary scalar field. In the specified large-N Gaussian theory, followed by infinite-volume and continuum limits, irrelevant kinetic deformations lose their relative Fisher length when only fixed physical momenta are retained. Combining those long-distance modes leaves the two physical directions associated with the mass gap and explicit mass.

The metric of the full auxiliary-field distribution behaves differently: lattice-scale correlations retain information about the regulator. A contact-term deformation is an exception, becoming redundant in both descriptions. The calculation also finds logarithm-squared curvature divergence rather than the frequently assumed correlation-volume behavior.

**Background.** Fisher distance measures how distinguishable nearby probability distributions are. For action parameters it involves fluctuations of derivatives of the log probability, closely connecting it to connected correlators of deformation operators. Retaining all modes lets a measurement inspect the cutoff scale. Marginalizing to physical long wavelengths removes that information. The paper calculates this distinction rather than identifying the microscopic and coarse-grained metrics from the outset.

**Why it matters?** This gives an operationally concrete interpretation of RG universality: distinguishability disappears relative to a specified resolution and set of observables. The bridge between field-theory tuning and information geometry is useful beyond this model. The detailed rates and curvature statements belong to its ordered limits and Gaussian large-N description, rather than constituting a general theorem about interacting theory space.

### Junction Hook Errors in the Color Code
**Salahedeen Issa, Margaret Martonosi — arXiv:2610.33347**

A syndrome schedule can avoid correlated faults that shorten each individual anyon string and still miss a dangerous fault at the junction of several strings. This paper identifies that omission in a proposed color-dependent extraction schedule for triangular color codes.

A junction hook places errors on endpoints of two differently colored strings belonging to one minimum-weight logical operator. For $d=7,9,11$, the authors check every non-corner plaquette and find such a scheduled fault. Together with $d-2$ single-data faults, it produces an undetectable logical error using $d-1$ faults. Circuit checks confirm that protecting only boundary stabilizers does not restore full distance: bulk hooks remain relevant.
