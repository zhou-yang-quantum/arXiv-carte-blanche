### An Exact QFT/Lattice Map for Fermions
**Shu-Heng Shao, Nikita Sopenko — arXiv:2611.05329**

Putting fermions on a lattice while retaining their chiral symmetries usually entails a compromise: unwanted low-energy species, modified symmetry transformations, or an additional spatial direction. This paper explores a different resource. It retains an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space at each lattice site and constructs an exact rewriting of continuum fermions in one spatial dimension.

The construction first folds neighboring pieces of left- and right-moving fields together, then rotates them so that each interval has independent boundary conditions. Expanding each interval in a complete set of modes produces a lattice site with an infinite tower of fermion flavors. The free Hamiltonian has nearest-neighbor couplings, while the map spreads local operators over only a bounded spatial neighborhood. The exact spectrum, chiral symmetries, and anomalies survive even with finitely many lattice sites. The authors also construct a Schwinger-model realization and numerically recover its mass gap $e/\sqrt{\pi}$, where e is the gauge coupling.

Finite truncations are useful approximations to this exact construction. They introduce no additional massless species, although particular truncations can slightly gap the desired modes. The exact symmetry and spectrum statements belong to the untruncated theory.

**Background.** Simply assigning continuum fields to adjacent intervals fails because their endpoint values remain connected. The fold-and-rotate step transfers those connections into interactions between independent interval systems. A second subtlety concerns locality: the lattice algebra for a region sits between continuum algebras for slightly smaller and larger regions. This realizes the split property—the possibility of inserting an ordinary tensor-factor algebra between suitably separated continuum regions. It explains how lattice subsystems can have finite entanglement entropy despite retaining infinitely many on-site modes.

**Why it matters?** This provides an unusually explicit bridge between continuum QFT and Hamiltonian lattice systems. Its conceptual value is already substantial: it identifies precisely where infinite local dimension changes familiar lattice obstructions. For quantum simulation, the next question is how efficiently finite truncations reproduce interacting observables and anomalous symmetries at useful accuracy.

### Long-range entangled criticality from coherent errors
**Amir-Reza Negari, Subhayan Sahu, Jan Behrends, Benjamin Béri, Timothy H. Hsieh — arXiv:2611.03811**

For stochastic errors in the toric code, losing the quantum memory, leaving the topological mixed-state phase, and losing long-range entanglement can occur together. Coherent errors make this correspondence much less straightforward. The relevant state is produced by rotations followed by syndrome dephasing, and changing the rotation angle does not directly furnish a Lindblad evolution between those states.

The authors develop two general tools to address this difficulty. A preserved strong higher-form symmetry, together with positive surviving coherent information, certifies long-range entanglement for generic encoded inputs. Separately, they extend finite-Markov-length recovery arguments to locally parameterized families, including the rotation-and-dephasing construction.

Applied to the honeycomb surface code, these tools reveal an extended critical regime above the threshold for reliable Pauli decoding. Partial quantum information survives there, and the state remains long-range entangled, while conditional mutual information grows logarithmically with system size. Erasing a suitable bulk region destroys the surviving information in this regime, supporting the conclusion that recovering it requires a global operation. A Majorana network description connects the conditional correlations to transport; the authors prove a bound between Markov and localization lengths and argue for their equality with numerical support.

**Background.** Long-range entanglement of a mixed state here means that it cannot be approximated by a convex mixture of pure states prepared by shallow local circuits. Classical mixing therefore does not automatically qualify. A strong one-form symmetry requires every noise Kraus operator to commute with the relevant loop operators, preserving the constraint at the level of individual noise processes. The new recovery construction uses a different locality condition: changing one local parameter leaves sufficiently distant reduced states unchanged. This permits local recovery along a path without first identifying that path as Lindbladian evolution.

**Why it matters?** The paper separates several notions of robustness that coincide in simpler noise models. An extended regime can retain entanglement and partial memory while losing both reliable Pauli decoding and local recoverability. This is a substantial conceptual advance in the relation between coherent errors, mixed-state phases, and localization, with general tools that extend beyond the particular code studied.

### Exponential decay for the two-dimensional Heisenberg model
**Jian Ding, Fenglin Huang, Peng Yang — arXiv:2611.04908**

The absence of spontaneous magnetization in a two-dimensional continuous-spin model does not determine whether correlations decay algebraically or exponentially. The XY model makes this distinction unavoidable: its low-temperature phase has algebraic correlations. Establishing finite correlation length at every positive temperature for the classical Heisenberg model requires a stronger argument.

The authors prove exponential decay for the square-lattice classical $O(N)$ model, including the Heisenberg case $N=3$, at every finite inverse temperature. Their method extends to a loop-based continuation with real $N>2$. This gives an independent rigorous route to the massive behavior predicted by the nonlinear sigma model. The theorem establishes finite correlation length at each positive temperature; it does not supply the expected precise low-temperature asymptotics of that length.

The proof converts spins into colored collections of random loops. It then exploits the competition between the colors: loops using the extra component reduce the ability of the remaining two components to maintain long-distance connections. Strengthened spin-wave estimates and coarse graining turn this mechanism into exponential decay.

**Background.** A loop soup is a random collection of closed paths. Here the paths live on a metric graph, obtained by replacing lattice bonds with continuous intervals. Conditioning their total occupation time at each vertex encodes the fixed spin length. Dividing loops among colors reconstructs spin components through occupation amplitudes and cluster signs. The fixed total occupation couples the colors, so adding a component changes the connectivity available to the two-color, XY-like sector. This geometric competition is the paper-specific bridge between the spin model and the decay theorem.

**Why it matters?** This is a foundational result for two-dimensional statistical field theory. Its interest extends beyond confirming the Heisenberg prediction: the continuation through real component number exposes a mechanism that appears immediately beyond the XY case. It offers a concrete probabilistic counterpart to the field-theoretic distinction between algebraic order and dynamically generated finite correlation length.

## arXiv-2026-11-04 — Item 4

### Quantum Error Correction in Nearly Critical Toric Codes
**Zack Weinstein, Samuel J. Garratt — arXiv:2611.05375**

A topological ground-state manifold can encode quantum information even when its Hamiltonian has moved far from a commuting stabilizer point. The operational obstacle is extracting that information: measuring the original stabilizers also measures the strong quantum fluctuations already present in the deformed ground state. Near a quantum critical point, it is especially unclear whether such measurements leave a useful memory.

For the transverse-field toric code, the authors show that bare stabilizer measurements preserve the encoded information with exponentially small errors throughout the topological phase, under their stated physical assumptions. They construct optimal decoders conditioned on having made those measurements, using classical lattice gauge theories whose worldlines are constrained by the observed syndrome.

Their simulations support finite thresholds to both bit-flip and phase-flip decoherence arbitrarily near criticality. For phase flips, they additionally prove that the optimal postmeasurement threshold is independent of transverse field and equals the stabilizer-code value, approximately 10.9%. The numerics use an isotropic spacetime gauge model, so their quantitative threshold values should not be read as precision estimates for the continuous-time Hamiltonian. Optimality among unrestricted recovery channels is supported analytically for bit flips but remains unproved.

**Background.** Stoquasticity supplies the crucial extra structure: the relevant ground-state amplitudes can be chosen nonnegative. Consequently, the decoding calculation becomes a comparison of positive statistical weights rather than interfering amplitudes. A measured syndrome fixes where anyon worldlines cross one imaginary-time slice; different winding classes represent different logical corrections. Positivity also lets the authors control postmeasurement overlaps between logical sectors, explaining why the measurements need not reveal the encoded state despite producing abundant syndromes.

**Why it matters?** This makes the persistence of a nearly critical quantum memory operationally concrete. It connects deformed topological order to an explicit measurement-and-decoding procedure rather than only an existence criterion for recovery. The special role of stoquasticity is equally informative: extending the construction to genuinely signful ground states is a clear next challenge.

## arXiv-2026-11-04 — Item 5

### Chaos on the mass shell
**Alexandre Homrich, David Simmons-Duffin, George Yuzhe Zhou — arXiv:2611.03787**

A high-energy excitation can produce many massive particles even when its short-distance creation is described by a simple ultraviolet operator. Following the complete final state rapidly becomes impractical. The authors instead ask whether the energy dependence of particle distributions can be determined by matching the measuring apparatus to ultraviolet observables.

Their detector-matching framework expresses infrared particle counters in terms of ultraviolet light-ray operators. The scaling dimensions and boost weights of those operators determine evolution with logarithmic energy on the massive-particle momentum hyperboloid. In two-dimensional examples, the leading evolution takes the schematic wave-equation form

$$\left(\partial_t^2-\partial_\theta^2\right)n(\theta,t)\simeq0.$$

Here $t$ is logarithmic energy, $\theta$ is rapidity, and $n$ is the particle density. Subleading trajectories and the ultraviolet deformation supply corrections. The authors test the picture in integrable thermal and magnetic deformations of the Ising CFT. They also relate the evolution kernel to the kernel controlling out-of-time-order correlations in hyperbolic space, and discuss its implications for gauge-theory particle production.

**Background.** Light-ray operators are extended observables associated with null propagation. Their Regge trajectories give analytic relations between scaling dimension and spin; these relations become differential or integral operators governing detector evolution. The integrable examples provide a second interpretation. Squared form factors act as Boltzmann weights for a Feynman gas in rapidity space. Increasing the physical energy expands the effective box containing that gas, producing propagating density profiles. The shared kernel explains the connection to chaos without requiring the integrable massive theory itself to be chaotic.

**Why it matters?** This proposes a nonperturbative organizing principle for real-time particle production, linking ultraviolet conformal data to massive infrared observables. The examples make the mechanism tangible. Its general scope remains limited by an unresolved issue identified in the paper: sufficiently spread-out detectors can require composite ultraviolet detectors beyond the ordinary single-operator Regge spectrum.

### Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz for the Toda chain from inverse scattering transform
**Alberto Brollo — arXiv:2611.03857**

Knowing how an integrable wave field evolves from one initial condition does not automatically tell us how to count random initial conditions thermodynamically. Soliton gases capture localized excitations, but dispersive radiation also carries conserved quantities and interacts with solitons. A complete statistical description must account for both.

Brollo derives a coupled thermodynamic Bethe ansatz for the Toda chain directly from its inverse scattering transform. The derivation changes the classical phase-space measure to scattering variables, determines the available phase space after scattering shifts, and coarse grains both soliton and radiation sectors. Minimizing the resulting free energy gives coupled equilibrium equations; the same scattering data determine dressed velocities and generalized hydrodynamics.

The result is checked against the established Toda-fluid description, including equilibrium quantities and Euler-scale conserved-charge correlators. These are two descriptions of the same mechanics with different notions of space: particle position in the fluid picture and lattice-site label in the chain picture. Extensions to other integrable wave equations are discussed, while models containing breathers present additional unresolved difficulties.

**Background.** Inverse scattering replaces a nonlinear field by spectral data of an auxiliary linear operator. Discrete spectral data describe solitons, while continuous scattering data encode radiation. The thermodynamic difficulty is that continuous radiation amplitudes need their own extensive statistical measure. Here random initial conditions make the auxiliary Toda operator a random one-dimensional operator. Its localization properties imply exponentially small transmission through a long sample, making the logarithmic radiation action extensive. This is a statement about the auxiliary spectral problem, rather than physical localization caused by quenched disorder in the chain.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a microscopic statistical foundation for a soliton-plus-radiation description. It strengthens the connection between integrability and non-equilibrium hydrodynamics by identifying what must be counted, rather than importing that counting from a quantum limit. Successful extensions could clarify when soliton-only hydrodynamics omits an essential transport sector.

### Reconstruction of the conformal algebra from a critical groundstate on the (fuzzy) sphere
**Rolando Ramirez Camasca, Xiang Li, Ting-Chun Lin, John McGreevy — arXiv:2611.03817**

A critical wavefunction should contain the structure of its continuum conformal theory, but numerical access usually begins with a particular microscopic Hamiltonian. Extracting conformal generators from its energy density introduces choices that can obscure the universal information. Can the ground state itself supply those generators?

Building on earlier reconstruction of the dilatation operator, the authors extract the global conformal algebra from modular Hamiltonians of spherical regions. In the continuum, these operators are weighted integrals of energy density. Suitable combinations of cap sizes and positions isolate the desired generators while canceling leading short-distance contributions at the entangling boundaries.

Tests using fuzzy sphere regularization show improved low-energy algebra relations relative to constructions from the microscopic Hamiltonian density, without additional tuning. The authors also use the reconstructed special conformal generators to identify candidate primary states. The separation improves with system size and works best at low energy; some descendants also have small diagnostic values, so primary identification retains finite-size ambiguities.

**Background.** The modular Hamiltonian $K_A=-\log\rho_A$ is determined by a region's reduced density operator. Its geometric weighting in a CFT makes it a useful probe of spacetime generators. A regulator adds local boundary contributions with characteristic dependence on cap size and curvature. Combining several caps cancels those terms while retaining the continuum contribution. An alternative uses $K_A-K_{\bar A}$, the full modular Hamiltonian of a region and its complement. Fuzzy sphere regularization supplies finite spatial resolution while preserving rotations, making it particularly suitable for testing this reconstruction on a sphere.

**Why it matters?** This advances the program of extracting field-theory structure from entanglement rather than from a preferred microscopic dynamics. Reconstructing generators provides more information than fitting a spectrum alone, especially for separating primary operators from descendants. Its broader usefulness will depend on controlling the boundary expansion and computational cost in larger systems and other regulators.

### A Note On Lattice Definition of Gauge Theories With Chiral Fermions
**Edward Witten — arXiv:2611.05051**

A lattice formulation of a chiral gauge theory must do more than reproduce a chiral Dirac operator: it must define the quantum fermion measure consistently over gauge-field configurations. The latter is where anomalies and global phase choices become central. This note presents a continuum-assisted route to that measure, revisiting recent constructions.

The gauge variables remain lattice link variables, restricted to admissible configurations with sufficiently small plaquette curvature. Such configurations can be interpolated to smooth continuum gauge fields. One then couples continuum chiral fermions to the interpolation and evaluates their anomaly-free path integral, including its phase. The formulation applies to general anomaly-free representations and extends beyond four-dimensional tori to more general spacetime settings.

Witten also explains how the required continuum quantities can be approximated computationally without reintroducing fermion doubling. Squared, Laplace-like Dirac operators provide the spectral data, with additional matrix elements recovering signs where necessary. The result is computability in principle. The fermion weight remains complex, leaving the practical Monte Carlo obstacle intact.

**Background.** Admissibility prevents lattice instantons from shrinking invisibly through gaps between sites and makes continuum interpolation topologically meaningful. The fermion determinant has both a magnitude and a phase. The latter can be expressed using an eta invariant, a regulated imbalance between positive and negative eigenvalues of a Dirac operator on an auxiliary higher-dimensional space. Anomaly cancellation makes the resulting physical quantity independent of the auxiliary choices. This incorporates global consistency conditions that a perturbative triangle-anomaly calculation alone cannot settle.

**Why it matters?** The note clarifies which ingredients are sufficient for a nonperturbative definition and how they can be made constructive. It is valuable alongside the exact Hamiltonian map in Item 1 because it addresses a different setting: continuum fermions coupled to interpolated lattice gauge fields. Turning either conceptual control into efficient calculations remains a separate task.

### Liouvillian Kramers Degeneracy
**Kohei Kawabata — arXiv:2611.03881**

Kramers degeneracy is a basic consequence of time reversal in closed quantum systems. Its extension to dissipative dynamics has seemed obstructed because a Liouvillian acts on operators, with a doubled Hilbert-space structure. This paper shows that the doubling argument misses a possibility supplied by parity.

The relevant symmetry is an antiunitary adjoint relation, $\mathcal T\mathcal L^\dagger\mathcal T^{-1}=\mathcal L$. Instead of requiring its square to have one fixed sign throughout operator space, Kawabata constructs cases with $\mathcal T^2=\mathcal P$, where the right-hand side is parity acting on operators. In the negative-parity sector, the antiunitary therefore squares to minus one and enforces pairs at the same complex Liouvillian eigenvalue.

Explicit interacting-fermion and spin-chain Lindblad models realize this algebra. Their symmetry-resolved spectra exhibit the corresponding symplectic statistics of non-Hermitian random matrices. The construction establishes a degeneracy theorem and physical realizations of symmetry classes previously argued to be unavailable in many-body Liouvillians.

**Background.** For a non-Hermitian generator, ordinary time-reversal covariance and the adjoint relation above are different conditions. The adjoint relation maps a left eigenvector into a right eigenvector at the same complex eigenvalue. A negative antiunitary square ensures an independent partner. Parity matters because an operator can connect Hilbert-space sectors of different parity, giving the induced transformation on that operator a negative square even when other sectors have a positive one. Spectral statistics must therefore be evaluated after resolving these invariant sectors.

**Why it matters?** This changes the symmetry classification available to dissipative many-body physics and supplies concrete models for testing it. The connection to non-equilibrium quantum chaos is particularly useful: the theorem determines which spectral degeneracies and correlations should survive interactions. Degenerate decay modes can still decay, so the result alone does not provide a protected quantum memory.

### Substantially Improved Rate and Distance for Addressable Transversal Phase Gates
**Bujiao Wu, Tongyin Lin, Bin Cheng — arXiv:2611.04964**

A transversal non-Clifford gate becomes more useful when it can address individual logical qubits inside a densely encoded block. Existing asymptotically good constructions establish this possibility, but their explicit simultaneous guarantees for rate and relative distance can be very small. The obstacle is quantitative as well as structural.

The authors introduce a cyclic encoding over $\mathbb Z/2^h\mathbb Z$, with $h\ge3$ specifying the phase-gate hierarchy level. An inverse encoding matrix extracts each logical bit as a linear modular function of physical coordinates. Physical phase rotations can therefore accumulate precisely the phase intended for one logical qubit, while leaving the others unchanged. This separates logical addressing from much of the distance design.

A deterministic polynomial-time construction based on algebraic-geometric codes gives a common asymptotic lower bound $52/(675h)$ for rate and relative distance. For T gates, corresponding to $h=3$, this is approximately 2.57%. A counting construction raises the common guarantee to approximately 7.34%, with exponential construction cost and exact verification. The paper also gives upper bounds and finite examples with 48 and 90 physical qubits.

**Background.** A ring coordinate is stored in the binary digits of several physical qubits. For the T-gate case, $y=y_0+2y_1+4y_2$: a phase linear in the integer coordinate decomposes into appropriately weighted T, S, and Z powers on those digits. The inverse matrix chooses combinations whose total phase reads a selected logical bit throughout its encoded superposition. Additional freedom, including quadratic phases, can then improve error detection without altering that addressing identity. This replaces restrictive binary overlap conditions with a linear coordinate construction.

**Why it matters?** The improvement brings the certified constants into a substantially more useful range and makes addressability a flexible design ingredient. It is a significant code-construction result, rather than a complete low-overhead computing architecture. Efficient decoding, sparse checks, and the cost of preparing or moving between these encodings remain necessary considerations for practical use.

### Closed- and open-system characterization of the Yukawa-SYK model in real time
**Alex Windey, Christian H. Johansen, Philipp Hauke, Andrea Legramandi — arXiv:2611.04704**

Realizing a strongly interacting critical state experimentally requires both maintaining it against environmental coupling and recognizing it in accessible measurements. In the Yukawa–Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev model, even a softened boson does not uniquely identify the conformal regime: a different, effectively quadratic regime can look superficially similar.

The authors solve the disorder-averaged model directly in real time in the large-number limit at fixed fermion-to-boson ratio. Their Schwinger–Keldysh treatment maps thermal stationary regimes and their spectral functions, including high-temperature regimes where an almost bare boson drives fermions with resolved or overlapping sidebands. They then study thermal stationary states coupled to a bosonic environment.

A useful organizing result is a bound on fermion-induced bosonic softening,

$$|\Pi^R(0)|\leq\frac{g^2}{4T}\frac{N}{M}.$$

The left side is the static bosonic self-energy, $g$ is the Yukawa coupling, $T$ is temperature, and $N/M$ is the fermion-to-boson ratio. This criticality budget limits the mass cancellation needed for self-tuned criticality. Environmental mass shifts can exhaust it. The authors also identify response-based diagnostics that distinguish the regimes without fitting conformal power laws.

**Background.** Self-tuned criticality arises because fermionic fluctuations soften the boson, which in turn strongly scatters the fermions. The budget follows from spectral normalization and the bounded slope of the thermal Fermi distribution, rather than a particular numerical saddle. The diagnostics probe complementary information: static susceptibility measures bosonic softening, while curvature of the inverse response distinguishes conformal dynamics from the quadratic impurity regime. A small gap alone misses that distinction.

**Why it matters?** This supplies both a constraint on realizing critical quantum matter and a practical strategy for diagnosing it. The work connects solvable strong-coupling physics to linear-response measurements while keeping the environmental modification explicit. Its conclusions concern the specified thermal stationary setting; applying the diagnostics to a particular simulator requires matching its bath and finite-size dynamics to the model.

### The integer spectrum of a superintegrable quantum field theory
**Marine De Clerck, Oleg Evnin — arXiv:2611.05015**

Classical integrability can coexist with transfer of excitation toward progressively shorter wavelengths. Quantizing such a system raises a sharper question than whether conserved quantities survive: can its interacting quantum spectrum be found explicitly? For the quantum Gérard–Grellier model, numerical diagonalization had revealed the striking additional feature that all energies are integers in the chosen normalization.

The authors prove this property and obtain explicit eigenvalue formulas. The Hamiltonian describes quartic scattering among bosonic modes with nonnegative momenta, constrained to conserve total momentum. Fixing particle number and momentum gives finite-dimensional sectors labeled by integer partitions.

The central construction is a basis inspired by classical multisoliton coherent states. Ordering its elements using successive hook reductions of partitions makes the Hamiltonian and a second conserved quartic operator upper triangular. Their diagonal entries give the joint integer spectrum, while orthogonalization recovers an eigenbasis. This replaces a numerical pattern with an exact solution of the interacting spectral problem.

**Background.** The underlying classical cubic Szegő equation retains only nonnegative Fourier modes of a nonlinear wave on a circle. Its soliton solutions are encoded by moving poles of a meromorphic function. The quantum basis takes products of states associated with simpler soliton sectors. A Young diagram records a momentum partition as rows of boxes; a hook consists of a box together with those to its right and below. Removing diagonal hooks determines how to organize those products so that Hamiltonian action produces only the same basis element and elements earlier in the ordering. That triangular structure is what makes the spectrum accessible.

**Why it matters?** This gives an unusually explicit interacting quantum model connected to classical energy cascades. Integer spectra are more restrictive than generic integrability, and the constructive eigenbasis offers a useful benchmark for studying quantum dynamics in this setting. The contribution is specific to this resonant bosonic field theory; its broader promise lies in whether the soliton-inspired triangularization can be adapted elsewhere.

### Autonomous cooling of Kerr-cat qubits to minutes-long bit-flip times
**Sergey Hazanov, Mikhail Svintsov, Uri Goldblatt, Barkay Guttel, Fabien Lafont, Serge Rosenblum — arXiv:2611.04868**

Kerr-cat qubits combine Hamiltonian confinement with fast coherent control, but an energy barrier alone cannot remove excitations. Noise can populate excited states inside either logical well, from which switching between wells becomes much easier. This mechanism has limited the expected exponential suppression of bit flips.

The experiment turns the existing nonlinear coupling to the readout circuitry into an autonomous cooling channel. When an intra-well excitation becomes resonant with the lossy readout mode or its filter, the excitation is converted into photons that escape. Spectroscopy and relaxation measurements identify this mechanism, and the measured bit-flip time grows exponentially with photon number, reaching 450 seconds.

At operating points with seconds-long bit-flip times, phase-flip rates remain consistent with intrinsic photon loss. The authors also demonstrate fast Z control without measurable degradation of bit-flip protection and readout-induced bit-flip probabilities below one in a million. The longest bit-flip time characterizes one error component; it should be distinguished from the lifetime of an arbitrary quantum superposition.

**Background.** The two logical wells have opposite oscillator amplitudes. Near either well, the oscillator separates approximately into a logical sign and a small intra-well fluctuation. The existing two-photon exchange depends on the square of the oscillator operator, so that logical sign cancels to leading order. It can therefore transfer an intra-well excitation to the readout circuit without learning which logical well was occupied. Resonance selects the excitation-removal process, and readout loss makes the cooling irreversible.

**Why it matters?** This is a direct demonstration of a useful theoretical principle: engineered dissipation can remove dangerous excitations while preserving the information that distinguishes logical states. It also closes a large protection gap between Hamiltonian-confined and dissipatively stabilized cat qubits. For error correction, the relevant next step is maintaining the bias through multi-qubit operations and repeated correction cycles, alongside control of the remaining phase noise.

### Designing turbulence through hierarchies
**Flavio Tuteri, André Freitas — arXiv:2611.04706**

Turbulent cascades, intermittency, and persistent dissipation occur together with complicated fluid geometry, making it difficult to identify their minimal physical ingredients. Shell models separate scales effectively, but their couplings are usually chosen to reproduce desired conservation laws. This paper asks whether comparable behavior can emerge from a mechanically specified hierarchy.

The authors study a planar chain of rigid links whose lengths and masses decrease geometrically. Gravity is neglected, so the nonlinearity comes from the configuration-dependent inertia imposed by the constraints. Large links are driven, relative motion of neighboring links is damped, and thermal noise can be included consistently with fluctuation–dissipation balance.

Simulations show a constant-flux energy cascade, intermittent fluctuations, and statistically local transfer despite the nonlocal inertia coupling. Over ten decades of decreasing viscosity, the mean dissipation approaches a nonzero plateau. Small-scale noise also propagates toward large scales within a finite fraction of a large-scale period. These are numerical signatures of a dissipative anomaly and spontaneous stochasticity. Mechanical and electrical realizations are proposed; the reported evidence comes from simulations.

**Background.** A dissipative anomaly occurs when increasingly sharp small-scale motion compensates a vanishing viscosity, leaving finite energy loss. Spontaneous stochasticity concerns a different limit: macroscopic uncertainty can remain at fixed time as microscopic noise vanishes and the hierarchy extends to arbitrarily small scales. Ordinary finite-dimensional chaos amplifies a smaller perturbation only after a progressively longer delay. Here local turnover times decrease geometrically down the chain, so uncertainty can climb all scales in a convergent sum of times. This explains why the hierarchy changes the predictability problem.

**Why it matters?** The work offers a physically explicit laboratory for several universal features of far-from-equilibrium dynamics. Its strongest conceptual contribution is separating those features from vortex dynamics and incompressibility. Establishing controlled limiting behavior and demonstrating the proposed realizations would determine how far this mechanical analogue can sharpen our understanding of fluid turbulence.

### Crossover duality for the quantum long-range Ising chain: perturbation theory around the short-range endpoint and a DMRG test
**Junchen Rong — arXiv:2611.05284**

The long-range quantum Ising chain has a window of interacting critical points between its mean-field and short-range limits. An expansion around an almost Gaussian order parameter controls one end, but loses its small parameter near the short-range crossover. A complementary description is needed there.

For interactions decaying as $r^{-1-s}$, Rong proposes coupling the short-range Ising CFT to an auxiliary Gaussian field. The coupling reproduces the long-range spatial interaction, while its strength becomes small near $s_*=7/4$. Unlike the classical long-range duality, the quantum construction is instantaneous in imaginary time and generates a running velocity. This yields a nontrivial dynamical exponent $z$.

Writing $\delta=s_*-s$, the leading crossover result is

$$z=1-0.720\,\delta+O(\delta^2).$$

The leading irrelevant exponent also vanishes toward the crossover, explaining unusually slow finite-size corrections. Density matrix renormalization group calculations on periodic chains up to 128 sites compare dynamical-gap and static-correlation estimates. They support consistency with the proposed description after corrections are included. The fits use theoretical input for other exponents, so this is a theory-informed consistency test rather than an independent determination of the entire critical theory.

**Background.** The auxiliary field carries a spatial power-law covariance but a delta function in imaginary time. Integrating it out reconstructs the original equal-time interaction. The important new step beyond a standard Hubbard–Stratonovich rewriting is treating the coupling as a perturbation of the interacting Ising CFT. Its short-distance products generate a stress-tensor deformation that renormalizes the relative scaling of space and time. An isotropic classical auxiliary field would miss this velocity flow.

**Why it matters?** This provides a controlled foothold at a previously difficult endpoint and explains why numerical estimates drift so slowly there. It connects conformal perturbation theory to long-range quantum criticality in a form relevant to tunable quantum simulators. The proposed infrared identification and interpolation away from the endpoint still require tests beyond the leading expansion.

### Analog quantum simulation of paraparticle statistics
**Gyunghun Kim, Cora N. Barrett, Sarah E. Muschinske, Junyoung An, Helin Zhang, Ilan T. Rosen, Rabindra Das, Michael A. Gingras, David K. Kim, Bethany M. Niedzielski, Meghan Schuldt, Jonilyn L. Yoder, Mollie E. Schwartz, Kyle Serniak, Jeffrey A. Grover, Max Hays, William D. Oliver — arXiv:2611.03908**

Generalized particle statistics become physically meaningful only when their exchange rules lead to distinguishable operations and measurements. The recently proposed R-paraparticle framework introduces internal labels that are encoded nonlocally, avoiding their interpretation as ordinary local spins. This experiment tests a particular two-color species through several complementary observables.

Pairs of superconducting qubits supply three effective states per site: vacuum and the two colors. A strong interaction excludes simultaneous occupation by both colors. A generalized Jordan–Wigner mapping turns the resulting ladder dynamics into a chain of free paraparticles.

The authors measure an exchange phase of 180 degrees, color-independent antibunching in two-particle interference, and a thermal occupation different from Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac distributions. The exchange and interference experiments use short chains; the thermal experiment couples one paraparticle site to a finite emulated bath. Thermal data are postselected on conservation of total excitation number. Together these measurements support the intended simulated statistics, without constituting discovery of a new elementary particle.

**Background.** For ordinary spinful fermions, opposite spins can occupy the same spatial mode. In the simulated species, exclusion applies across both colors. The labels are tied to the globally ordered many-particle state: exchange acts nontrivially on this structure rather than simply carrying a local spin along with a particle. The nonlocal strings in the generalized Jordan–Wigner map are therefore essential. ### On the universality of entanglement entropy in quantum field theory
**Orr Sela — arXiv:2611.04238**

Calling part of a field-theory entanglement entropy universal usually means it survives changes of cutoff. There is another choice to confront first: defining spatial subsystems can require introducing a regulating boundary at the entangling surface. Why should changing its boundary condition leave the same universal term?

Sela formulates this question using shrinkable boundaries—regulating boundaries that disappear into the identity defect, up to removable local terms. In topological theories, with three-dimensional Chern–Simons theory as the explicit example, the paper shows under stated assumptions that changing between such boundaries changes non-universal contributions while preserving the topological constant. Boundary conditions that fail this criterion can change that constant.

An analogous argument protects the interval logarithm in two-dimensional CFT, with additional assumptions about replica continuation and the shrinking limit. The paper explains why the same reasoning does not immediately establish universality in general higher-dimensional theories: smoothing the replica geometry can generate curvature that becomes singular as the regulator shrinks.

**Background.** Shrinkability is an admissibility requirement on the subsystem construction. If a tiny regulating tube leaves a nontrivial line operator behind, the path integral has effectively inserted something into the original state. Its entropy need not agree with that of the intended state. When the tube instead leaves only the identity and local geometric counterterms, those terms can modify boundary-sensitive contributions without changing the protected quantity in the demonstrated settings. Replica calculations add a further subtlety: taking the shrinking limit before differentiating in replica number must be justified, rather than assumed to commute automatically.

**Why it matters?** This sharpens the conditions under which universal entanglement data are genuinely properties of the QFT and state. The value is conceptual clarity, particularly for gauge theories and comparisons between different factorizations. It also delineates a substantive open problem: a general criterion ensuring that admissible subsystem boundaries remain admissible in the replica construction.

Strong localization noise can make gravity-generated entanglement essentially invisible without making the underlying dynamics separable. Can strictly local error correction expose that residual entangling resource while preserving gravity as the only interaction between the two parties?

The authors analyze two trapped masses with Newtonian interaction and several noise models. Perfectly correcting the leading position errors also eliminates the leading position-product interaction, creating a signal-versus-noise obstruction. Their construction instead uses imperfect local encodings that retain higher-order terms of the gravitational potential while suppressing the relevant noise differently.

For Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber noise and continuous spontaneous localization, the effective interaction-to-noise ratio can become arbitrarily large in the analyzed scaling limit, including controlled scalings that keep the masses' contact probability small. **Background.** Each mass is encoded in a pair of motional Fock states, $\{|0\rangle,|2N+1\rangle\}$, with integer $N$ controlling their separation. Low powers of position cannot connect the two codewords, while sufficiently high odd powers can. Recovery can therefore suppress low-order logical transitions while leaving a higher-order gravitational coupling. Unequal diagonal moments also generate phase noise, requiring additional local concatenation. The analysis uses the Zeno limit of very frequent recovery. Increasing the encoding separation suppresses the absolute interaction as well as noise, so an improving ratio does not imply a fast entangling operation.

**Why it matters?** This introduces recoverability as a sharper operational question than whether a noisy gravitational channel is separable. It connects error-correction structure to proposed tests of gravity-mediated quantum information and distinguishes noise models through their functional form. The result establishes an in-principle resource; practical recovery rates, state preparation, and usable interaction times remain essential obstacles to an experiment.
