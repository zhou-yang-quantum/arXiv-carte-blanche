## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 1

### Instanton Structure from 325-Loop 3D $\phi^3$ Perturbation Theory
**Ian Moult, Bo Wang — arXiv:2610.36025**

Perturbative coefficients contain information about nonperturbative field configurations, but extracting that information requires reaching orders where conventional diagram enumeration becomes hopeless. This paper makes that connection quantitatively accessible in an interacting three-dimensional field theory.

The authors combine global sampling of Feynman graphs and their Schwinger parameters with annealed importance sampling. Rather than reweighting directly between poorly overlapping distributions, they pass through intermediate distributions that gradually introduce the physical weight. They compute the two-point self-energy through 325 loops and infer the instanton action to about 0.03% precision, the exponent associated with collective coordinates, and fluctuation corrections through four loops. Independent semiclassical calculations provide a demanding comparison.

**Background.** In tropical field theory, a graph polynomial is replaced by its dominant monomial to obtain a tractable sampling distribution; a compensating weight restores the original integral. Global sampling also explores graph topology, avoiding an explicit list of diagrams. The remaining obstacle is the enormous variation of these weights. Annealing addresses this statistical overlap problem. At large order, the factorial growth, its power-law correction, and its normalization encode different pieces of the instanton saddle and its fluctuations.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantial methodological advance: perturbation theory becomes a numerical probe of nonperturbative structure. The demonstration uses a super-renormalizable scalar theory without running-coupling renormalons. Extending it to gauge theories requires additional control of subdivergences, numerators, and signs, but the achieved bridge between high orders and semiclassics is already compelling.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 2

### Typicality of entanglement growth and transport fluctuations in quantum symmetric exclusion processes
**Ewan McCulloch — arXiv:2610.36048**

A noise-averaged evolution can look classical while individual realizations retain quantum coherence. Consequently, knowing the averaged transport statistics does not establish what happens to entanglement in a typical realization. This paper resolves that distinction for a diffusive model of stochastically hopping free fermions.

The key calculation compares noise averages of a charge-transfer generating function and its reciprocal. The first becomes the classical symmetric simple exclusion process; the second becomes a Brownian energy process. Their hydrodynamic saddle problems have opposite actions, giving concentration around the classical transport prediction. Free-fermion identities then establish typical subsystem spectra and entropy growth. For domain-wall and Néel product states, the paper obtains explicit coefficients of the $\sqrt{t}$ entanglement growth and checks them numerically.

**Background.** Macroscopic fluctuation theory assigns an action to coarse-grained density and current histories, including rare transport fluctuations. Here the auxiliary Brownian energy process exchanges continuous positive quantities between neighbouring sites. Mapping its saddle problem to exclusion dynamics controls fluctuations that a forward average alone cannot constrain. For Gaussian fermionic states, the subsystem covariance eigenvalues determine both particle-number statistics and Rényi and von Neumann entropies, providing the final bridge to entanglement.

**Why it matters?** This supplies a concrete route from hydrodynamic fluctuations to typical quantum entanglement dynamics. The argument assumes the relevant hydrodynamic saddles remain smoothly connected as the counting field changes. Within that scope, it strengthens a useful connection between exactly tractable stochastic systems and non-equilibrium quantum information.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 3

### Measurement-induced entanglement phases in hyperbolic gauge-Higgs models
**Cole Kelson-Packer, Hiroki Sukeno, Akimasa Miyake — arXiv:2610.36528**

Hyperbolic lattices have exponentially growing volume and an extensive boundary. That geometry can change how entangled regions connect, making it natural to ask whether familiar topological, trivial, and Higgs phases exhaust the possibilities under measurement-only gauge-Higgs dynamics.

The authors find an intermediate “archipelago” regime containing separated large clusters with symmetry-protected topological order. They represent the measurement history through a three-dimensional resource state and relate surviving entanglement structures to generalized percolation. Duality helps assemble the phase diagrams, while ordinary percolation appears on special boundaries. Numerical transition estimates support mean-field behaviour. The intermediate phase is persuasive evidence of a geometric effect, although its detailed boundaries and effective description remain less settled.

**Background.** The construction borrows from measurement-based quantum computation: stacking spatial layers turns circuit time into a third direction, and single-qubit measurements reproduce the original dynamics. Products of resource-state stabilizers form spacetime sponges whose surviving boundary operators become Wilson lines or loops in the output state. Their compatibility with the measurement history becomes a connectivity question. Hyperbolic percolation can support multiple infinite clusters, giving a mechanism for separated extensive entangled regions.

**Why it matters?** Geometry here changes the organization of measurement-induced order, extending the usual Euclidean picture. This is a worthwhile connection among gauge theory, monitored dynamics, and quantum information. Accessible hyperbolic radii are small, and some diagnostics are heuristic, so a sharper thermodynamic characterization would substantially strengthen the proposed phase structure.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 4

### Universal Scaling of Conditional Mutual Information in Classical Stochastic Dynamics
**Songlei Wang, Chenguang Liang, Zhi-Cheng Yang — arXiv:2610.36058**

A growing Markov length need not mean that conditional dependence between small regions becomes stronger. This paper asks how its spatial range and amplitude evolve together, using classical stochastic dynamics where the underlying mechanism can be resolved.

For fixed microscopic endpoint regions, the proposed scaling is

$$I_t(A:C|B)=\ell(t)^{-2}f\!\left(|B|/\ell(t)\right).$$

The growing length $\ell(t)$ sets both the range and the diminishing amplitude. The authors derive this structure from domain-wall coarsening in inactive parity-conserving directed percolation and find numerical support at its critical point. There the growing dynamical correlation length replaces the mean domain-wall spacing. They also develop a temporal counterpart relating conditional dependence to memory time.

**Background.** The power of two follows from a useful cancellation: CMI is a finite second difference of interval entropies. Under self-similar coarsening, the extensive entropy contribution cancels, leaving two derivatives of a function of rescaled interval size. The numerical advance is a Hankel matrix compression: it ranks virtual directions by their contribution to probabilities of concatenated configuration strings, preserving the small probability differences needed for CMI more effectively than conventional state-vector compression.

**Why it matters?** The paper gives a mechanism behind scaling collapses and distinguishes growing inferential range from growing local information. Its universality proposal has analytical support in coarsening and numerical support elsewhere, rather than a theorem for arbitrary stochastic channels. Both the scaling argument and probability-aware compression merit attention for mixed-state dynamics.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 5

### The 't Hooft model on (A)dS$_2$
**Panos Betzios — arXiv:2610.37667**

Confinement on an expanding or confining curved background is difficult to separate from the complexity of strong interactions themselves. The two-dimensional ’t Hooft model offers a controlled starting point, but its familiar solution relies on light-front symmetries whose curved-space replacement is not obvious.

The author constructs that replacement on anti-de Sitter and de Sitter backgrounds. In an affine null coordinate system, the Yang–Mills area form and instantaneous Coulomb interaction retain their flat-space form. Curvature enters through a special-conformal contribution to the quark kinetic term. The isometry Casimir then produces a meson equation consisting of the flat-space integral operator plus a curvature-dependent Legendre differential operator.

This opens a substantial spectral analysis, combining exact identities, solvable limits, and numerical calculations. In anti-de Sitter space, sufficiently excited meson dimensions acquire a linear growth with a logarithmic interaction correction after leaving the flat-space Regge regime. In de Sitter space, mesons become resonances even at $N\to\infty$: expansion enables their constituents to escape confinement. For narrow resonances, the semiclassical widths exhibit thermal activation at the de Sitter temperature, with the approximation checked against the spectral calculation.

**Background.** Light-front quantization eliminates propagating gauge modes in two dimensions, leaving an instantaneous interaction between quarks. At large numbers of colours, the meson problem closes on a quark–antiquark wavefunction. Curved backgrounds replace the invariant mass by an isometry Casimir. The affine coordinate choice is decisive because it preserves the simple gauge interaction while isolating curvature in a known one-body generator; the resulting equation remains connected to the flat model’s integrable spectral structure.

**Why it matters?** This supplies an unusually detailed laboratory for strongly coupled gauge dynamics on rigid curved spacetime. Its scientific value lies in separating geometric confinement, flux-tube binding, and cosmological dissociation within one controlled framework. The result concerns the planar two-dimensional theory on prescribed backgrounds; extensions beyond that limit would be needed to assess how its mechanisms generalize.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 6

### Correlation-sensitive magic-state distillation from spin-squeezed neutral atoms
**Ximo Wang, Ruyue Guo, Xinbo Li, Min Feng, Haozhe Wang, Zhenqi Bai, Yichi Zhang — arXiv:2610.36963**

A resource factory can produce individually good inputs that become worse after distillation. The issue is that accepting a joint syndrome conditions on correlations among inputs, so the independent-state threshold alone need not predict whether the output improves.

The authors derive an exact correlated-input map for the five-to-one Bravyi–Kitaev protocol based on the $[[5,1,3]]$ code. Independent Clifford twirling leaves a classical joint distribution of signs along the target Bloch axis. Denoting the sum of all kth-order target-axis moments by $A_k$, the acceptance probability and output polarization are

$$p_{\rm acc}=\frac{3+A_4}{48},\qquad t_{\rm out}=\frac{A_3-2A_5}{3+A_4}.$$

Thus fourth-order correlations determine acceptance, while third- and fifth-order correlations determine the accepted resource quality. Explicit families with identical one- and two-qubit reduced states have opposite distillation gains. The paper connects this mechanism to spin-squeezed neutral-atom states prepared through finite-range Ising interactions, showing how block separation and addressed Clifford phases improve the preparation-and-distillation process.

**Background.** Twirling removes coherences between target-axis eigenstates while retaining correlations between their signs. Those correlations enter because the code projector contains weight-four stabilizers, whereas logical Pauli cosets contain weight-three and weight-five operators. Postselection therefore probes precisely the higher moments absent from local and pair characterization. Addressed Clifford phases have a separate role during preparation: they change the symmetry selected by code projection, allowing the interaction-generated resource to survive that projection as magic.

**Why it matters?** The strongest contribution is an operational criterion for correlated resources, rather than another threshold for independent noise. It identifies measurable information that a factory must characterize and explains why pairwise diagnostics can miss failure. The neutral-atom calculations are a concrete implementation study; broader practical impact requires controlling the relevant higher moments together with acceptance, storage, and circuit errors.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 7

### The TEMPO network can be made independent of the system size
**Valentin Link — arXiv:2610.37012**

Simulating a quantum system with a memoryful environment is often limited by carrying system-dependent indices throughout the entire environmental tensor network. This paper identifies a small polynomial subspace that removes that dependence from the network interior.

For $m$ independent coupling channels to a Gaussian bosonic environment, all internal bond dimensions can be reduced to $(2m+1)(m+1)$. A single channel therefore needs at most six internal components, regardless of the number of distinct coupling-operator eigenvalues. The system dimension survives in the final boundary layer. Crucially, this reduction is accurate to the usual second order in the time step; it is not an exact finite-step identity.

The construction is tested against a damped oscillator with an exact Gaussian reference and applied to several emitters coupled to a structured two-dimensional reservoir. These examples show both the removal of an avoidable dimension dependence and access to previously costly multichannel calculations.

**Background.** TEMPO, the time-evolving matrix product operator method, contracts a network representing the Feynman–Vernon influence functional. Forward and backward system histories couple through bath correlations. Expanding an elementary influence factor reveals that monomials of the coupling eigenvalues through quadratic order span a small subspace. Projecting the whole network into that subspace requires accounting for errors accumulated across all time pairs. The paper shows that the resulting global error has the same time-step order as the standard symmetric splitting already used by TEMPO.

**Why it matters?** This is a broadly useful structural simplification for non-Markovian simulation. It separates the complexity of environmental memory from one source of unnecessary dependence on system size. Contraction complexity and the final system evolution can still be substantial, particularly as coupling channels multiply, but the reduction removes a clear algorithmic obstruction without changing the method’s standard convergence order.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 8

### Entanglement Bounds on Cardy Growth in Higher Dimensions
**Yuya Kusuki, Yin Tang — arXiv:2610.37449**

In two-dimensional CFT, the same central charge controls vacuum entanglement and high-temperature thermodynamics. Higher-dimensional conformal symmetry leaves these data independent, raising a sharp question: can vacuum information nevertheless bound the number of high-energy states?

The authors propose $f\le4\pi^2a$ in four dimensions and $f\le2.99830949\ldots\,F$ in three dimensions. The thermal normalization is $s=dfT^{d-1}$, with d the spacetime dimension and T the temperature; $a$ is the Euler anomaly coefficient and $F$ the sphere free energy. Both proposed bounds are saturated by a free real scalar.

A stronger conjecture compares the thermal coefficient with $f_{\rm vac}$, a linear combination of vacuum stress-tensor three-point data chosen to reproduce every free theory. The paper establishes $f\le f_{\rm vac}$ in specified controlled regimes, including weakly coupled gauge theories, leading large-number vector models, and classical gravitational duals, and supplies additional numerical tests. These are substantial checks, but the general bounds remain conjectural. In three dimensions, deriving the sphere-free-energy bound through this route also uses an independent conjecture concerning stress-tensor normalization.

**Background.** Spherical Rényi entropy connects a vacuum reduced state to thermal physics on hyperbolic space. Its ordinary entanglement limit measures vacuum data, while its small-index limit probes the high-temperature coefficient. The proposed bounds extend a comparison with the free scalar across these limits. Conformal collider bounds constrain the energy flux created by stress-tensor insertions and restrict the coefficients of the stress-tensor three-point function. Those restrictions help turn the stronger thermal conjecture into the four-dimensional anomaly bound.

**Why it matters?** The proposal would constrain thermodynamic state counting using vacuum observables, offering a higher-dimensional counterpart to part of the Cardy relation. The value lies in the sharp conjecture and the range of mechanisms supporting it. A general proof, or an instructive counterexample, would materially change our understanding of which thermal data conformal consistency can control.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 9

### The Harmonic Structure of Multiparticle Scattering: Amplitude Projection and Non-Renormalization
**Nathaniel Craig, Brian Henning, Michael Stadlbauer — arXiv:2610.36052**

Large anomalous-dimension matrices contain many zeros whose origin is difficult to see diagram by diagram. On-shell methods suggest a cleaner question: can the amplitudes on opposite sides of a unitarity cut occupy compatible kinematic sectors at all?

This paper constructs a multiparticle generalization of partial-wave decomposition for four-dimensional massless scattering. Projecting amplitudes onto an orthonormal channel basis turns phase-space cut integrals into sums of products of coefficients. If the two sides share no allowed harmonic component, the cut vanishes. A local contact amplitude has finite harmonic support, so only finitely many projections of the other amplitude are required.

The authors apply this structure to EFT operator mixing, recovering familiar one-loop helicity restrictions and deriving higher-loop restrictions. A particularly definite result concerns mixing from operators with more fields into those with fewer fields at the first loop order allowed by operator length. In that setting, length counting removes competing cut topologies and relevant iteration terms, allowing cut-level selection rules to imply zeros in the full anomalous dimension. Exceptional Yukawa contributions require separate treatment.

**Background.** Ordinary partial waves organize two-body states by angular momentum. Multiparticle states have additional kinematic degeneracies even at fixed total angular momentum. Stiefel harmonics provide orthogonal functions on the corresponding fixed-total-momentum phase space; Young tableaux organize their polynomial and helicity structure. Combining these labels with angular momentum resolves the degeneracy and makes orthogonality usable inside unitarity cuts. Establishing a full renormalization statement also requires consistent infrared subtraction and control of lower-order terms.

**Why it matters?** This offers both a computational representation of cuts and a structural explanation of operator-mixing zeros. Its appeal extends beyond a particular EFT basis: it organizes renormalization through physical intermediate-state overlaps. The strongest higher-loop conclusions have carefully specified massless and operator-length assumptions, while a systematic infrared treatment remains important for broader applications.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 10

### Measurement incompatibility and noiseless quantum codes
**Yu-Xuan Zhang, Jing-Ling Chen — arXiv:2610.37347**

Surviving measurement incompatibility is evidence that a channel retains quantum structure. How strong must that survival be before it guarantees an actual quantum code? This paper answers the question for universal preservation, meaning preservation of every finite incompatible measurement family.

The authors prove that this property holds exactly when the input contains a noiseless code subspace as large as the entire output space. They construct an isometric encoding satisfying

$$\mathcal N(W\rho W^\dagger)=\rho.$$

The same encoding works for every logical state, and the channel directly returns that state without a subsequent recovery. The converse is enforced by a finite family of binary measurements: preserving its precise compatibility threshold already guarantees the code. A related construction gives, in every nontrivial dimension, a measurement family whose incompatibility disappears under compression to any proper subspace.

**Background.** Measurements are compatible when their outcome statistics can all be obtained by classical processing of one parent measurement. The Heisenberg adjoint pulls output measurement effects back to the channel input and always maps a parent measurement to another parent. The proof constructs a rank-one minimal informationally complete measurement whose effects span operator space. Their rank-one structure strongly restricts how a parent can be split. Preserving the boundary between possible and impossible splittings then constrains the channel’s dilation enough to produce the noiseless encoding.

**Why it matters?** This gives an exact bridge between an operational measurement property and error correction, resolving the dimensional mismatch left open by equal-dimension results. The universal requirement is essential: preservation of an arbitrary selected incompatible pair does not establish such a code. The finite threshold witness makes the equivalence more concrete and suggests how approximate incompatibility preservation can constrain transmission quality.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 11

### Many-body entanglement quantification in arbitrary states through a few partial-transpose moments
**Sudip Chakrabarty, A. S. Majumdar — arXiv:2610.36156**

Detecting mixed-state entanglement is often easier than quantifying how much survives. Logarithmic negativity is particularly useful, but evaluating its trace norm normally requires far more spectral information than a many-body experiment can reconstruct. This paper asks for the strongest lower bound obtainable from a finite set of even partial-transpose moments.

Writing these moments as $p_k=\operatorname{tr}[(\rho^{T_B})^k]$, the authors turn the problem into an optimization over positive measures on a compact interval. Gauss and Gauss–Radau quadrature solve that relaxed moment problem exactly. The resulting bounds improve with additional moments and eventually recover the exact negativity in finite dimension. The first member is

$$\mathcal E\ge\frac12\ln\!\left(\frac{p_2^3}{p_4}\right).$$

Here logarithmic negativity uses natural logarithms, and a negative lower estimate can be replaced by zero. Spin-model, thermal-state, and tensor-network examples examine the useful low orders and the effects of finite statistical errors.

**Background.** Square the partial-transpose eigenvalues and normalize them by the purity. These normalized squares sum to one. Placing the corresponding weight at each normalized square makes the even moments into ordinary moments of a positive measure; the trace norm becomes an integral of an inverse square root. Quadrature replaces that unknown measure by a few weighted nodes reproducing the measured moments. A polynomial lying below the inverse square root certifies that the quadrature value is a lower bound. Optimality applies to this moment relaxation, which does not enforce every condition of a physical density matrix.

**Why it matters?** This turns partial spectral information into a systematic quantitative certificate, with a clear explanation of what another moment buys. Exact convergence does not make generic many-body negativity cheaply measurable: high-order moments remain expensive and statistically delicate. The practical contribution is the optimized low-order hierarchy, especially for numerical or experimental settings where such moments are already accessible.

### Spin Hamiltonian for Quantum Simulation of Minimally Truncated $3+1$D SU(2) Lattice Gauge Theory and Testing Eigenstate Thermalization
**Ziyuan Li, Xiaojun Yao — arXiv:2610.36608**

Real-time non-Abelian gauge dynamics requires a representation that respects Gauss’s law without spending most computational resources on unphysical states. Moving beyond a single spatial cell is also necessary before questions about thermalization become meaningful.

The authors construct an explicit Pauli Hamiltonian for minimally truncated SU(2) gauge theory on a three-dimensional hyperhoneycomb lattice. With $j_{\rm max}=1/2$, each link retains only the spin-zero and spin-one-half electric representations. They solve the vertex constraints and remove redundancies among flux-loop generators, obtaining a bijection between physical states and qubits. In the bulk, the encoding needs two qubits per unit cell, with boundary corrections.

A lattice of two by three by three cells becomes a sixteen-qubit problem that can be exactly diagonalized. Level statistics identify an ergodic coupling regime. There, local energy-density matrix elements and eigenstate entanglement are consistent with the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis, including approximately random-matrix fluctuations and highly excited states approaching the Page curve.

**Background.** The trivalent geometry is doing useful work. Three SU(2) representations meeting at a vertex have a uniquely specified singlet when allowed. Under the minimal truncation, a vertex has either no occupied flux links or two, so physical configurations form closed loops. Independent binary loop variables can therefore label the gauge-singlet Hilbert space. Plaquette operations flip combinations of these variables with representation-dependent amplitudes, producing the explicit spin Hamiltonian derived from the Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian.

**Why it matters?** The main advance is a reusable physical-space encoding for three spatial dimensions, accompanied by a non-equilibrium benchmark. It creates a concrete starting point for quantum simulation of gauge thermalization. The ETH evidence belongs to a small, severely truncated lattice model; approaching continuum SU(2) dynamics requires larger systems and systematic enlargement of the electric-representation cutoff.

### Quantum Fisher Information Deficit as a Geometric Signature of Strong-to-Weak Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking
**Zhang-Chi Yin, Wei Zheng — arXiv:2610.37092**

Mixed-state order can survive even when ordinary charged two-point functions lose their long-distance signal. Existing nonlinear overlap diagnostics reveal this order, but their connection to an infinitesimal physical response is less transparent. This paper supplies such a connection through a deficit in quantum Fisher information.

For strongly $\mathbb Z_2$-symmetric states, the probe is a neutral rotation generated by two separated charged Hermitian operators that square to the identity. Its QFI, called $Q$, has algebraic maximum four. Defining $D=1-Q/4$, the authors prove

$F^2\le D\le R_1\le F$,

where $F$ is the root-fidelity correlator and $R_1$ the Wightman correlator. Their nonzero long-distance limits are therefore equivalent, although their finite-distance values differ. For symmetry-projected Gibbs states, a further lower bound on the deficit depends on the mean energy injected by the pair operation. Locality bounds that energy independently of endpoint separation, leaving a persistent deficit at every fixed nonzero temperature.

**Background.** Strong symmetry restricts the density matrix to a definite charge sector; weak symmetry requires invariance under conjugation. The neutral pair rotation stays within the sector while probing correlations between charged endpoints. QFI measures the local Bures geometry along this orbit. Its convex-roof representation expresses the deficit through squared pair expectations in pure-state decompositions: opposite signs can cancel in the ordinary correlator while their squares remain finite. This explains why hidden order can reduce the response rather than enhance it.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a rigorous response-based characterization of an established mixed-state ordering criterion. The thermal energy bound adds a particularly clear locality mechanism. A persistent deficit diagnoses strong-to-weak symmetry breaking when conventional charged long-range order is absent; that qualification is necessary. Whether this viewpoint leads to easier measurements remains a separate practical question, but the conceptual bridge to information geometry is useful.

### Phase Transitions in Mismatched Bayesian Inference for the Ising Model
**Xinyu Sun, Zhou-Quan Wan, Shao-Kai Jian, Hong Yao — arXiv:2610.36041**

Agreement among inferred configurations is not sufficient evidence that they reconstruct the underlying system. This distinction becomes a thermodynamic phase question when noisy local measurements are interpreted using an incorrect prior or noise model.

The authors study Gaussian-noisy bond observations of a paramagnetic Ising model. A replica field theory distinguishes truth–posterior overlap from posterior–posterior overlap. Underestimating measurement noise or assuming a colder prior preferentially orders the latter, allowing an overconfident phase with persistent internal overlap but no long-range reconstruction of the true relative spin orientations.

An exact change of variables maps the inference problem to a correlated random-bond Ising model. Informative, uninformative, and overconfident inference become ferromagnetic, paramagnetic, and spin-glass order, respectively. Three-dimensional Monte Carlo calculations support a finite intervening overconfident region for both types of mismatch. The paper also shows how measurement-record statistics can calibrate the true parameters within the specified generative model.

**Background.** At Bayes matching, the true configuration and posterior samples enter the replicated theory symmetrically. Nishimori identities then tie reconstruction to internal posterior agreement. Mismatch breaks that enlarged symmetry: the effective couplings governing the two overlap fields need no longer reach their ordering thresholds together. Multiplying an inferred spin by the corresponding true spin makes reconstruction into ordinary ferromagnetic order, while overlap between two inferred configurations becomes the spin-glass diagnostic. The transformed disorder inherits correlations from the original Ising prior.

**Why it matters?** This makes overconfidence a distinct statistical-mechanical phase rather than a vague failure of uncertainty estimates. It is conceptually relevant to inference transitions and decoding with imperfect noise calibration, without establishing the same phase diagram for arbitrary decoders. The combination of an exact mapping, a mechanism for splitting the ordering channels, and finite-dimensional numerical evidence gives the result substance beyond mean-field analogy.

### Resurgence of Black Holes in Thermal OPE
**Alba Grassi, Hewei Frederic Jia — arXiv:2610.37554**

A high-frequency thermal correlator is governed perturbatively by short-distance field-theory data. Its full response also knows about relaxation and, in holographic theories, a black-hole horizon. How can the asymptotic short-distance expansion encode information about regions far from the bulk boundary?

The authors study scalar thermal correlators dual to planar and spherical anti-de Sitter black holes. The stress-tensor sector of the operator product expansion determines an all-orders high-energy series. Exact Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin analysis associates its Borel singularities with complex radial trajectories connecting the boundary to turning points and, in specified kinematic regimes, the black-hole singularity.

The paper goes beyond identifying large-order scales. In a particular complex-frequency chamber within the timelike regime, Borel resummation reconstructs the full retarded correlator. Crossing the chamber boundary requires additional nonperturbative terms fixed by Stokes jumps. The derivation is made in the holographic bulk description, with the stated even-dimensional and nonresonant scalar setup.

**Background.** A Borel transform divides out factorial growth, exposing singularities whose positions set exponentially small corrections to the original expansion. In the radial wave equation, exact WKB organizes these corrections through trajectories of complex classical momentum. Their connectivity changes as the frequency phase varies. A Stokes jump records the corresponding change in how perturbative and exponential contributions combine. Retarded and advanced correlators can share their algebraic high-energy expansion while differing in precisely this completion, because their horizon boundary conditions differ.

**Why it matters?** This provides a concrete reconstruction mechanism linking asymptotic QFT data to global bulk response. Its significance is sharper than the general statement that large orders contain nonperturbative information: the paper identifies when resummation suffices and how continuation changes the answer. A direct CFT derivation would clarify which parts survive beyond a classical gravitational dual and how additional thermal operator sectors supply the completion.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 16

### Universal Critical Jump for Inverse-Square Ising Ferromagnets
**Dyuman Bhattacharya — arXiv:2610.36131**

The inverse-square Ising chain orders with a discontinuous magnetization, yet retains critical fluctuations. The jump has long been expected to satisfy a universal relation. The issue addressed here is whether that equality holds for each microscopic ferromagnetic interaction, without restrictive assumptions about its short-distance core or approach to the inverse-square tail.

For translation-invariant nonnegative couplings with $J_n\sim B/n^2$, the author proves

$$2\beta_c Bm_c^2=1.$$

Here $B$ is the tail amplitude, $\beta_c$ the critical inverse temperature, and $m_c$ the magnetization at criticality. Arbitrary finite changes of the ferromagnetic core are permitted, as are slowly converging or oscillating corrections to the tail.

The paper also proves that connected susceptibility diverges at criticality and throughout some neighbouring ordered interval. At the critical coupling, the excess magnetization in a small positive field decays more slowly than every positive power of that field. These statements constrain the unusual mixed character of the transition, without selecting a particular logarithmic law. A continuous-time extension covers a specified class of Ohmic spin-boson models.

**Background.** Inverse-square interactions give large domains a logarithmic interface cost, allowing an order-parameter jump to coexist with very soft fluctuations. The proof recovers the physical magnetization in that cost by measuring the pressure change when weak, long-distance interactions are removed. If the critical product exceeded one, interfaces would be sufficiently suppressed to produce rigid ordered blocks. A finite-block ordering criterion would then remain valid slightly below the supposed critical temperature parameter, contradicting criticality. Integrating out an Ohmic bath produces the related inverse-square interaction in imaginary time.

**Why it matters?** This establishes a sharp universality statement under unusually weak microscopic assumptions and ties the jump to rigorous fluctuation bounds. It is valuable for exactly tractable statistical mechanics and dissipative quantum systems alike. The remaining challenge is a quantitatively sharper description of correlation decay, field response, and the slow finite-size crossover that obscures this transition numerically.

## arXiv-2026-10-28 — Item 17

### Exact $T\bar{T}$-Like flows in Integrable Sigma Models
**H. Babaei-Aghbolagh — arXiv:2610.37458**

Knowing a family of classically integrable Lagrangians does not automatically identify the stress-tensor operator that moves along it. Beyond the standard $T\bar{T}$ example, that generator can become complicated and difficult to control away from a perturbative expansion.

For a class of one-scale sigma-model families, this paper derives an autonomous flow of the form

$$\partial_\lambda\mathcal L=X\mathcal F\!\left(\sqrt{Y/X}\right),\qquad X=T_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu},\quad Y=(T^\mu{}_{\mu})^2.$$

The change of the Lagrangian is expressed entirely through its own stress tensor; the generating function has no explicit dependence on the deformation coupling. The author obtains parametric expressions, perturbative expansions, and regularity domains, with explicit Born–Infeld, deformed, and logarithmic examples. The irrelevant flow commutes with the marginal root deformation and preserves the classical integrable structure. A common formulation also connects the two-dimensional construction with self-dual nonlinear electrodynamics in four dimensions.
