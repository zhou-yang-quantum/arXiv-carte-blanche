### Algebraic strong-to-weak symmetry breaking and partial decodability in the surface code
**Stephen W. Yan, Yimu Bao, Sagar Vijay — arXiv:2610.40567**

Interference between error configurations can change the long-distance physics of error correction, even after syndrome measurements have removed coherence between different syndrome sectors. The important question is whether the remaining interference merely shifts a threshold or supports a qualitatively different recovery regime. This paper finds the latter in a triangular-lattice surface code subjected to coherent single-qubit Z rotations followed by syndrome measurements.

The authors relate optimal Pauli recovery to a disordered statistical model and derive an effective $\mathrm{SO}(n)$ nonlinear sigma model in the replica limit $n\to1$. Its stable weak-coupling regime produces an extended partially decodable phase: at fixed aspect ratio, recovery fidelity approaches a universal geometry-dependent value rather than improving to unity with increasing size. Through Wegner duality, the same regime becomes algebraic strong-to-weak symmetry breaking in a decohered paramagnet. Its fidelity correlator obeys $C_F(r)\sim r^{-1/16}$, with logarithmic corrections to the asymptotic scaling.

Large-scale simulations support the field-theory predictions. The analysis also identifies substantial crossover effects near special rotation angles, where boundary termination can dominate accessible sizes. A separate flux-insertion diagnostic shows persistent sensitivity to interference at long distances, supporting the claim that stochastic Pauli dephasing cannot reproduce this phase. The recovery conclusion specifically concerns the syndrome-conditioned Pauli recovery task studied here.

**Background.** Strong symmetry constrains the ket and bra charge sectors separately; weak symmetry retains their simultaneous action. Strong-to-weak ordering therefore requires nonlinear diagnostics rather than ordinary magnetization. The fidelity correlator compares a state with the state obtained by inserting a distant charge pair. In the replicated description, such insertions become twist operators: crossing an associated defect changes the boundary condition of selected sigma-model fields. Their correlations connect a field-theory scaling dimension to both mixed-state order and logical recovery.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a quantitative theory of an extended recovery regime sustained by coherent errors. Universal aspect-ratio dependence gives a sharper prediction than the existence of an unusual threshold alone. It connects error correction, disorder field theory, and mixed-state phases while identifying a concrete obstruction to replacing coherent noise by a classical error distribution.

## arXiv-2026-10-30 — Item 2

### Mixed-State Topological Transition and Emergent Fractionalized Supersymmetry in the Non-Abelian Kitaev Spin Liquid
**Zhou-Quan Wan, Hong Yao, Shao-Kai Jian — arXiv:2610.38741**

Decoherence can reorganize topological order, but the universality classes of these transformations remain much less understood than equilibrium topological transitions. The Kitaev spin liquid offers a controlled setting because its fractionalized fermions and gauge fluxes can be tracked explicitly. Here, bond dephasing generates interactions between two time-reversed Majorana systems in the doubled description of the density matrix.

The authors first find an inter-copy fermion-pair condensate that changes doubled Ising topological order into toric-code order. Interpreting the doubled state back as a density matrix yields an intrinsically mixed topological structure, rather than simply another pure-state spin liquid.

A distinct construction produces the supersymmetry result. The authors form a temporal interface between topological and trivial reference states. Their Chern-number mismatch supplies a gapless Majorana field, while dephasing tunes its interaction with an inter-copy scalar. The endpoint is described by tricritical Ising theory with emergent $\mathcal N=1$ supersymmetry. Sign-problem-free Monte Carlo gives $\nu=1.27(3)$ and $\eta_b\simeq\eta_f\simeq0.40$, consistent with the predictions for this topology-constrained tuning path. Matching bosonic and fermionic spatial anisotropies provides an additional check. This interface transition and the bulk doubled-state transition are separate results.

**Background.** Choi vectorization turns the ket and bra indices of a density matrix into two Hilbert-space copies. A dephasing operation then becomes an interaction between them. A temporal interface is accessed through a normalized overlap with a different reference state; it is a boundary in the state-preparation path integral, not an ordinary physical edge evolving in real time. Fractionalized Majorana operators require gauge strings to become physical observables. Consequently, the proposed supersymmetric partners appear as different gauge-invariant spin strings. Their scaling dimensions differ by half a unit, as supersymmetry requires; equal anomalous exponents do not mean equal operator dimensions.

**Why it matters?** This supplies a concrete route from decoherence to an interacting supersymmetric critical theory with identifiable physical diagnostics. The combination of an effective theory, numerical exponents, and anisotropy matching makes the evidence substantive. The broader opportunity is to classify mixed-state transitions through topology-constrained interfaces, while keeping their relation to directly measurable density-matrix observables explicit.

## arXiv-2026-10-30 — Item 3

### Long-Range Toric Code: Finite-Temperature Topological Order in Two Dimensions
**Jacopo Gliozzi, Taylor L. Hughes, Kyung-Su Kim — arXiv:2610.37765**

A two-dimensional toric-code memory fails thermally because separating an anyon pair costs only bounded energy while the number of possible error paths grows. A passive memory needs an energetic penalty that continues to resist this proliferation. The authors construct one by adding interactions supported on large stabilizer loops, with strengths that decrease as a power of loop perimeter.

These terms commute with the original stabilizers and preserve the toric-code ground states, but substantially alter the excitation energies. For square-loop interactions with decay exponent $1<\alpha\le2$, isolated dipoles have energies that diverge with system size. The finite-energy thermal excitations are instead neutral four-anyon clusters. The authors argue that these clusters do not screen the confinement responsible for protection, and present analytical and Monte Carlo evidence for finite-temperature topological order and self-correction.

The distinction from the regime $2<\alpha\le3$ is essential. There, anyons are confined above the ground state, but thermally excited dipoles screen that confinement beyond a large finite length. Memory protection can improve strongly over accessible sizes without surviving indefinitely as size increases. The paper studies thermal behavior, decoding failures, memory lifetimes, and long-range entanglement to distinguish these possibilities.

**Background.** A large contractible loop is a product of elementary stabilizers, so adding it need not change any ground-state wavefunction. Its eigenvalue nevertheless detects the parity of the anyons enclosed. An error string can therefore violate many additional loop terms as its endpoints separate. Screening asks whether a thermal background randomizes those loop eigenvalues enough to remove the growing energy cost. Dipoles cross long loop boundaries extensively; compact quadrupoles affect square loops differently, producing the mechanism behind the two regimes. These are many-qubit loop interactions, not ordinary pairwise long-range couplings.

**Why it matters?** The work separates ground-state topological order from excitation deconfinement and gives a concrete two-dimensional model of passive protection beyond local-Hamiltonian restrictions. Its significance is primarily conceptual: scalable implementation of the required loop interactions remains a substantial additional problem. The screening analysis is especially useful for distinguishing genuine self-correction from exceptionally favorable finite-size behavior.

## arXiv-2026-10-30 — Item 4

### Thermodynamic cost of locally maintaining a steady state
**Sarang Gopalakrishnan — arXiv:2610.38925**

What must a local environment spend to maintain a state with long-range conditional correlations? Detailed balance might seem to settle the question: a reversible process should be an equilibrium process with no entropy production. This paper shows why that inference can fail when the physical implementation is required to remain local.

The author constructs local Gaussian processes with unique stationary states and arbitrarily long conditional correlations, despite detailed balance in the reduced stochastic dynamics. A simple example combines local damping with spatially differentiated noise. Its ordinary correlations are short ranged, while its inverse covariance has a long-range Coulomb form.

The thermodynamic resolution comes from specifying the implementation. With bounded local quadratic Hamiltonians and finite-range couplings to rapidly relaxing equilibrium baths, maintaining these states requires nonequilibrium heat flow. For the regulated counterexample at fixed interaction range, entropy produced per site per characteristic relaxation time grows at least quadratically with the Markov length. The paper also derives range-versus-dissipation restrictions. Discrete-state jump processes behave differently: under stated support and irreducibility assumptions, detailed balance already excludes the analogous long-range conditional structure. Stronger claims about rapid mixing are presented as conjectures, not general theorems.

**Background.** In a Gaussian state, ordinary correlations are controlled by the covariance, whereas conditional correlations are controlled by its inverse, the precision matrix. A covariance can be very local while possessing a small-momentum zero whose inverse is singular. This makes conditional information long ranged without an ordinary critical covariance. The same reduced stochastic equation can admit different physical realizations: an equilibrium realization may require a nonlocal Hamiltonian, while a local realization uses multiple baths. Response measurements distinguish these implementations through mode-dependent effective temperatures even when passive fluctuation measurements agree.

**Why it matters?** The paper makes locality an explicit thermodynamic resource constraint and shows why reduced-process entropy production can miss its cost. This is a useful connection between nonequilibrium physics and information-theoretic descriptions of mixed states. The result is strongest within its Gaussian implementation class; extending comparable bounds to interacting quantum systems would turn the underlying question into a much broader resource theory.

### The Effective Field Theory Origin of the Fuzzy Sphere Method
**Mykola Dedushenko — arXiv:2610.38702**

The fuzzy sphere method extracts continuum critical physics from finite Hamiltonians while preserving spatial rotations. Its success raises a field-theory question: why should a Hamiltonian built from lowest-Landau-level orbitals flow to the desired local theory, and which constructions should exhibit relativistic scaling?

The author introduces local mother theories on $S^2\times\mathbb R$ containing massive charged fermions and neutral mediator fields. Integrating out the fermions gives an ordinary effective field theory for the mediators. Integrating out the mediators and higher Landau levels instead produces a fuzzy Hamiltonian. These two routes organize the relationship between microscopic interactions, critical tuning, and continuum observables.

For a real mediator, the framework recovers the Ising construction and describes routes toward Gaussian and Lee–Yang theories. A complex mediator produces an easy-plane family whose magnetic-flux background permits a first-order time-derivative term. Its critical point has dynamical exponent $z=2$, meaning frequency scales quadratically with momentum. Exact one-magnon solutions locate that point analytically. Thus rotational invariance of a fuzzy Hamiltonian does not by itself enforce emergent Lorentz invariance.

**Background.** Magnetic flux through a sphere creates a finite set of lowest-Landau-level orbitals that carries exact rotation symmetry. Projecting into this set replaces spatial functions by finite matrices. The mediator construction explains how continuum fields can emerge as collective fermion bilinears. It also identifies which terms survive the projection. In the complex-field example, a Berry term linear in time derivatives changes the critical dynamics; in the broken phase, removing the massive amplitude mode can leave relativistic phase fluctuations. The two regimes therefore follow from different low-energy actions of the same family.

**Why it matters?** This provides a constructive field-theory interpretation of a numerical method that has often relied on informed model building. The main value is guidance: it helps identify continuum targets, symmetry restrictions, and sources of nonrelativistic behavior before expensive diagonalization. The controlled scale separation and approximations in each route remain important when translating this framework into quantitative finite-size predictions.

### Quasi-Phases of Quantum Matter
**Nick G. Jones, Paul Fendley, Nathanan Tantivasadakarn, Ruben Verresen — arXiv:2610.40526**

Two gapped regimes can lie in the same conventional phase yet be separated nearby by a robust continuous transition. If the critical line eventually terminates, global phase classification alone does not explain what distinguishes its two sides or why their interface can support modes. This paper introduces quasi-phases to characterize that local structure of a phase diagram.

The distinction is made relative to a critical theory. Relevant perturbations lead to different gapped regimes, but no sufficiently small allowed perturbation creates an invertible interface between them. A gapped route connecting the regimes exists elsewhere in the microscopic phase diagram, beyond the perturbative neighborhood of that theory.

The authors develop spin-chain examples with central charge $c=1$ critical lines. Describing both the critical line and the connecting gapped path requires larger parent theories, including orbifold theories with $c=3/2$ and $c=9/5$. Interface modes can carry higher-dimensional ordinary representations even when conventional projective-representation labels do not distinguish the neighboring regimes. The framework therefore identifies additional structure within a single phase without changing the usual global equivalence relation.

**Background.** An invertible interface can be paired with its reverse and removed without closing the gap. For the short-range-entangled chains considered here, a protected interface degeneracy obstructs this process. Crucially, the definition allows spatially varying perturbations, including terms irrelevant in the homogeneous bulk that might lift an interface mode. It is consequently stronger than observing a critical line in a restricted Hamiltonian slice. The parent orbifold construction combines critical sectors by gauging a shared discrete symmetry; different perturbations of the additional sector make the otherwise inaccessible gapped connection possible.

**Why it matters?** The paper supplies a useful intermediate description between a local critical field theory and a global phase diagram. Its interface criterion gives physical content to distinctions that ordinary phase labels erase. For interests in conformal field theory and exactly solvable chains, the constructive consequence is particularly appealing: apparently unnecessary criticality can point toward a larger multicritical theory whose extra degrees of freedom explain how the transition ends.

## arXiv-2026-10-30 — Item 7

### Generating Multipartite Entanglement via Structured-Bath Engineering
**Baptiste Debecker, Philipp Westhoff, Visal So, Sebastian Paeckel, Guido Pagano, John Martin, Mattia Moroder, François Damanet — arXiv:2610.38807**

Independent local reservoirs provide no shared noise through which distant spins could directly coordinate. Can they nevertheless stabilize entanglement across an entire interacting chain? The authors show that temporal bath structure and a conserved global $\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry can work together to do so.

In an Ising chain, the energy of a local spin flip depends on its neighbors. A frequency-selective reservoir therefore turns temporal correlations into configuration-dependent transition rates and spatial correlations. Flat reservoirs erase the relevant Ising-axis correlations for the coupling class studied. Structured reservoirs overcome that obstruction by selecting a nonuniform classical stationary distribution.

Strong symmetry supplies the second ingredient. It makes coherences between globally reversed spin configurations follow the same evolution as their populations. This yields exact stationary states within weak-coupling theory by solving a classical rate problem, including at the Redfield level without secularizing the generator. Within a fixed symmetry sector, bipartite negativity becomes proportional to the distinguishability between the stationary classical distribution and a partially spin-reversed version. Suitable bath spectra produce GHZ-like states in finite chains. Explicit reservoir embeddings and tensor-network simulations test the mechanism beyond the analytical approximation.

**Background.** Frequency selectivity matters because an interacting spin can flip at different energies depending on its surroundings; a local bath can distinguish those transitions without coupling directly to other sites. Strong symmetry means the Hamiltonian and individual coupling operators commute with global parity, preserving its sector. This protects the coherent pairing of each configuration with its globally reversed partner. Tracing out any spin removes that coherence, so proper reduced states can be separable even when the full state is entangled. Generating genuine multipartite entanglement additionally requires enough classical probability to concentrate on the appropriate coherent configuration pair.

**Why it matters?** The paper gives a transparent mechanism through which reservoir memory and symmetry become complementary resources for quantum state preparation. Its classical reduction makes an interacting open-system problem unusually tractable. Scaling highly coherent states to larger chains will require maintaining the symmetry sector and increasingly favorable bath-controlled correlations, but the theoretical design principle is already useful beyond a particular hardware proposal.

## arXiv-2026-10-30 — Item 8

### Locality in Dissipative Ground-State Preparation
**Qicheng Tang, Ruhanshi Barad, Xueda Wen — arXiv:2610.40628**

A local Hamiltonian need not have a ground state that can be prepared by equally local dissipation. Cooling requires selecting energy-lowering processes, and that spectral selection may be nonlocal in space. This paper determines the obstruction for free fermions prepared by quadratic Lindblad dynamics with linear jump operators.

For translation-invariant targets, exponentially decaying jump coefficients correspond to analytic functions of momentum. The authors turn this observation into a necessary and sufficient spectral factorization criterion. A sign-changing crossing of the Fermi level makes the required separation of positive and nonpositive energies nonanalytic, ruling out exponentially local linear jumps that leave the ground-state subspace dark and attract all other states.

Such crossings are the complete obstruction in one dimension. Higher dimensions allow additional failures: two examples with quadratic band touchings and algebraic ground-state correlations differ in whether their spectra admit the required analytic factorization. Conversely, exponentially decaying ground-state correlations suffice for local preparation even without translation invariance. Spatial locality and preparation speed are separate issues throughout.

**Background.** A dark jump annihilates every target ground state. For free fermions this forces a linear jump to contain only quasiparticle operators that remove positive-energy excitations. Attraction additionally requires that no excited mode escape dissipation. Locality asks whether those mode-selective operators can be assembled with exponentially decaying real-space coefficients. At a sign-changing crossing, an analytic coefficient that vanishes throughout the forbidden side cannot suddenly become nonzero on the allowed side. The higher-dimensional criterion generalizes this argument to the analytic factorization of the full multiband characteristic polynomial.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a classification of a basic locality constraint on dissipative preparation, rather than another cooling protocol for a chosen model. It explains why similar low-energy dispersions or correlation decays need not imply similar preparation possibilities. The result is directly useful for quantum simulation, provided its scope—free targets, linear jumps, and a dark ground-state subspace—is retained when interpreting the no-go theorem.

## arXiv-2026-10-30 — Item 9

### Subsystem Spacetime Code
**Esther Xiaozhen Fu, Aranya Chakraborty, Daniel Gottesman — arXiv:2610.40357**

A circuit can pass through instantaneous codes with good distance and still fail after only a few faults. Measurements may erase syndrome information before it is recorded, or faulty outcomes may corrupt a logical update. A useful distance theory must therefore describe the entire measurement history.

The authors construct a subsystem spacetime code for Clifford circuits with Pauli measurements. Its gauge group consists precisely of faults harmless to the circuit, and its stabilizer center records detector relations. The subsystem-code distance equals the circuit fault distance, turning a temporal fault-tolerance problem into a code-theoretic one.

The construction classifies stabilizers by whether their values are eventually read, still recoverable, or irreversibly lost. This identifies where a dynamical code loses protection. Under an explicit correctability condition, measurement-only circuits satisfy $d_{\rm circuit}=\min(d_{\rm unmasked},d_{\rm meas})$: protection is limited by available syndrome information or by undetectable harmful measurement faults. The paper also analyzes circuit composition, where joining two individually protected pieces can remove detectors or change which earlier syndromes remain accessible. The distance formulas require their stated conditions; instantaneous distances alone do not supply a general composition rule.

**Background.** A temporarily masked stabilizer has not been read within the chosen window, but its value remains encoded in the final state and could be recovered later. A permanently masked stabilizer has lost that information from both the record and the remaining state. Treating masked values as gauge degrees of freedom captures this distinction algebraically. Spacetime propagation relations then identify faults that can be moved through the circuit without changing its protected action. A detector is a parity relation among measurement outcomes fixed in fault-free operation; its violation supplies the actual observable syndrome.

**Why it matters?** This is a structural tool for designing and diagnosing dynamical fault tolerance. Its value lies in locating the information loss responsible for reduced distance and explaining how interfaces between circuit components create new vulnerabilities. It could guide improved Floquet codes and code-switching protocols, although distance characterization alone does not establish an efficient decoder or a favorable threshold.

## arXiv-2026-10-30 — Item 10

### Toric codes all the way down: decoupling translation-invariant topological CSS codes in higher dimensions
**Mingxin He, Yifei Wang, Hao Song, Yingfei Gu — arXiv:2610.40592**

Higher-dimensional topological codes support varied excitation structures and fault-tolerant operations. To understand which structures are fundamentally different, one can ask whether a local Clifford circuit turns a code into independent higher-form toric codes. In more than two dimensions, preserving every excitation type under translation is not by itself sufficient.

The authors identify the additional obstruction as gluing between excitation layers of different dimensions. They prove a necessary and sufficient decoupling criterion on a chosen superlattice: translations must act trivially on every layer, and the inter-layer gluing must vanish.

For prime local dimension $p$ and $m$ nonzero layers, once translations preserve the individual types, an additional blocking factor $p^{m-1}$ in every direction suffices to remove the gluing. The proof produces an explicit circuit of SUM and SWAP gates, with ancillas allowed. The same structure determines when a finite torus attains its maximal logical degeneracy. The general decoupling conclusion assumes finitely many excitation types in each layer and consequently does not encompass arbitrary fracton codes.

**Background.** Higher-form toric codes place qudits on cells of different dimensions, producing pointlike, looplike, or extended excitations. A superselection layer groups excitation data at a particular dimension. Gluing contains information absent from those separate lists: in the paper’s example, creating a small excitation loop also creates a point charge. Removing the charge consistently while preserving the original translation pattern is obstructed. Coarse-graining changes the retained translation symmetry and can cancel this attachment structure because charges have finite prime order. The resulting decoupling is a physical local circuit, not just a comparison of ground-state degeneracies.

**Why it matters?** This advances the classification of higher-dimensional CSS order with a constructive equivalence theorem. It clarifies when complicated codes inherit their basic excitation structure from toric-code building blocks. Such a circuit may help transfer decoding ideas between models, while understanding how useful logical gates transform under it remains a separate and potentially productive question.

### Complex Langevin kernels for real-time SU(N) lattice gauge theory in 3+1 dimensions
**Kirill Boguslavski, Paul Hotzy, David I. Müller — arXiv:2610.37800**

Real-time gauge-theory path integrals have oscillatory weights that defeat ordinary importance sampling. Complex Langevin dynamics avoids sampling those weights directly, but can become unstable or converge incorrectly. Extending the accessible time interval therefore requires controlling correctness as well as numerical stability.

The authors modify the stochastic dynamics with kernels designed for lattice gauge theory. In pure $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ at bare coupling $g=0.5$, the rectangular regularized contour baseline reaches $2\beta$, where $\beta$ is inverse temperature. A contour-dependent phase kernel extends this to $3\beta$, and a local field-dependent kernel built from plaquette traces reaches $3.5\beta$.

They also extend a drift-distribution correctness criterion to the kernel-modified process. The successful runs show the required exponential suppression of large drifts, alongside checks against Euclidean one-point observables and controlled excursions away from unitarity. At the longest interval, the simpler kernels fail these diagnostics. The evidence establishes a more capable simulation framework in the tested pure-gauge setting; unequal-time correlators are reserved for subsequent work.

**Background.** Complex Langevin replaces the complex-weight integral by stochastic evolution in a complexified field space. A kernel changes drift and noise together while preserving the intended stationary complex weight, provided analyticity and convergence assumptions hold. Field-dependent kernels also require a derivative contribution to the drift. Their purpose is to steer sampling away from dangerous excursions, not to alter the physical action. The Schwinger–Keldysh contour includes forward and backward real-time evolution and a thermal imaginary-time segment; its geometry helps organize which kernel phases can stabilize different parts of the process.

**Why it matters?** Direct nonperturbative real-time gauge calculations remain a major obstacle in nonequilibrium field theory. A substantial extension of the validated time window is therefore useful even before a new physical observable is extracted. The important next tests are continuum control, more demanding correlation functions, and whether the kernel strategy remains effective beyond the particular couplings and pure-gauge theory demonstrated here.

## arXiv-2026-10-30 — Item 12

### A Zoo of Fermionic Relative Rényi Negativities in the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev Models
**Linjing Jiang, Pengfei Zhang — arXiv:2610.39349**

Mixed-state entanglement is difficult to calculate in interacting fermion systems. Integer-replica quantities are more accessible than logarithmic negativity, but common Rényi proxies can be positive even for separable states. This paper constructs a family whose nonzero values genuinely witness entanglement within the fermionic separability convention used.

The authors combine the partially time-reversed density matrix and its adjoint in different orders, then compare their moments at the same replica order. The resulting relative Rényi negativities vanish for separable states. Expressing partial time reversal through an operator-size superoperator supplies a path integral at fixed integer replica number, avoiding a replica-limit calculation.

Applications to SYK and the Maldacena–Qi coupled model reveal that the family’s members behave differently. Some relative negativities exhibit first-order changes as the subsystem fraction varies, caused by competing large-system saddle points and invisible to ordinary thermodynamic observables. A minimal-change class has temperature dependence consistent with its proposed use as a logarithmic-negativity proxy. That encouraging behavior is evidence from the studied models, not a proof that every member is an entanglement monotone.

**Background.** Fermionic partial time reversal multiplies an operator string by a phase determined by how many Majorana operators lie in the chosen subsystem. Thus $\rho^{T_A}$ can be non-Hermitian even when the original density matrix is Hermitian. For mixtures of product states whose local factors preserve fermion parity, the transformed state remains Hermitian. Moments with different orderings of the transform and its adjoint then agree. Their disagreement witnesses entanglement. The size superoperator counts the relevant Majorana factors, turning those phase insertions into a tractable modification of replica boundary conditions.

**Why it matters?** The main advance is an entanglement-certifying replacement for replica diagnostics that can otherwise give false positives. The path-integral construction makes it useful in controlled interacting models, while the competing saddles expose information transitions that standard observables miss. Establishing monotonicity or quantitative comparisons with logarithmic negativity would strengthen the promising proxy interpretation.

### Inevitability of the Petz transpose map
**Jacopo Surace, Chenxu Li, Francesco Buscemi — arXiv:2610.40158**

Classical Bayes inference runs a stochastic description backward without requiring the underlying dynamics to be reversible. Quantum theory has several candidate reversals, so why does the Petz map recur in recovery, inference, and information theory? This paper identifies consistency requirements that select it uniquely.

A retrodiction assignment must reverse the reference transition from a chosen prior to its predicted output. The authors require the assignment to respect sequential and independent parallel composition, agree with inversion of unitary channels, and return the original channel when retrodiction is applied twice. For finite-dimensional full matrix algebras, these requirements uniquely determine the Petz transpose map. Singular reference states are handled by restricting to their supports.

The result also derives preservation of probabilistic mixtures rather than assuming it. Dropping the requirement of reversal twice leaves a family of consistently rotated Petz maps, identifying precisely where the remaining freedom enters. Reversing the reference transition does not mean recovering every possible input after an information-losing channel.

**Background.** For prior $\pi$ and forward channel $\Phi$, write $\sigma=\Phi(\pi)$. The selected reverse channel is

$$\mathcal R_{\pi,\Phi}(X)=\pi^{1/2}\Phi^\dagger(\sigma^{-1/2}X\sigma^{-1/2})\pi^{1/2}.$$

The inverse predicted-state factors reweight an output operator, the trace adjoint transfers it backward through the forward dynamics, and the prior factors restore the input weighting. For diagonal classical data this becomes Bayes’ rule. Sequential consistency reverses the order of composed channels; parallel consistency prevents an unrelated ancillary system from changing the prescription. The theorem applies to one assignment satisfying these rules across all reference states and channels, which is stronger than choosing a reverse separately for each example.

**Why it matters?** This is a conceptual uniqueness result, not a new approximate-recovery performance bound. It explains why a particular recovery construction is structurally natural and separates that explanation from claims about reversing physical irreversibility. The connection to QEC is substantive: it clarifies the inferential role of the reference state behind a map that frequently enters recovery guarantees.

### Defining 2-group symmetries on lattices and extracting their anomalies
**Yitao Feng, Yu-An Chen — arXiv:2610.37745**

Generalized symmetries are often specified through background fields and cohomology, while a microscopic model presents local operators and circuits. To use higher-group ideas constructively, one needs to recognize their defining structure and anomalies directly in those operators. This paper develops that connection for a class of finite central 2-groups.

The authors give local composition relations in which ordinary symmetry operations close up to higher-form defects. Associativity of the local fusion data identifies the Postnikov class controlling the mixing. They then extract anomalies in one spatial dimension by restricting transformations to an interval and comparing how the residual endpoint operations compose.

Explicit qubit constructions in one, two, and three spatial dimensions realize a 2-group with $G=\Pi_2=\mathbb Z_2$. The examples have the same nontrivial mixing class but include both anomalous and anomaly-free actions, realizing all of the stated cohomological anomaly classes in these dimensions. The shared mixing therefore does not by itself determine the anomaly.

**Background.** An ordinary symmetry acts on local charged objects, whereas a one-form symmetry is associated with extended charged operators. In a 2-group, the two structures are linked: composing ordinary transformations can leave a higher-form defect. The Postnikov class records the mismatch between two local ways of composing three transformations. Full operators remain associative; the nontrivial information lies in their local factorization. An anomaly is a further obstruction. Restricting a symmetry to a region exposes boundary remnants, and comparing their fusion orders yields phases that cannot be removed by consistent local redefinitions. This distinguishes higher-group mixing from anomalous realization.

**Why it matters?** The contribution makes higher-group symmetry operational at the lattice level. It provides ingredients for constructing microscopic models and checking their anomalies without beginning with a continuum action. The scope is a specified class of finite central 2-groups with compatible local fusion data, but within that setting the explicit examples provide a useful bridge between quantum circuits, topological phases, and field-theory anomaly matching.

### Fate of Quantum Magic
**Liuke Lyu, Erik S. Sørensen, William Witczak-Krempa — arXiv:2610.39059**

Entanglement and nonstabilizer resources need not disappear together as a many-body state heats or relaxes. Determining their relative lifetimes is difficult because familiar pure-state magic diagnostics can become misleading for mixed states. This paper studies the question using distance to the full convex set of stabilizer mixtures.

The authors develop a fidelity-based optimization method for mixed-state magic and apply it to Ising chains, generic spin chains, and the Kitaev model. Along the thermal trajectories studied, entanglement generally disappears before magic. In generic quantum quenches that lose both resources, the ordering is usually reversed. An integrable Ising quench provides an instructive exception to simple thermal expectations because its late-time state retains additional conserved information.

The numerical method reaches mixed states of up to twelve qubits, supplemented by exact membership certificates for smaller blocks and extrapolations. Thus the broad trajectory ordering is supported by controlled examples and ensembles of models, rather than a universal theorem. The paper also constructs experimentally accessible witnesses for extensive nonstabilizer preparation resources that survive optimization over local spin frames.

**Background.** The stabilizer polytope contains a finite neighborhood of the maximally mixed state. Entering it produces a finite-temperature or finite-time death of magic, not merely a gradual decrease toward zero. Its boundary contains many entangled states, making magic-first disappearance plausible for generic state-space paths. Thermal states of local Hamiltonians follow more structured paths with restricted entanglement, explaining the opposite common ordering. The Bures-distance calculation optimizes fidelity over stabilizer mixtures. Its approximate optimization supplies an upper bound on distance, so demonstrating vanishing magic benefits from explicit membership certificates rather than a small numerical distance alone.

**Why it matters?** The paper gives a clearer mixed-state account of how computational resources survive equilibration. The distinction between thermal and dynamical paths shows why energy density or entanglement alone cannot determine resource availability. For quantum simulation and nonequilibrium physics, the useful advance is both methodological and diagnostic: it makes resource decay a property of the actual trajectory through mixed-state space.
