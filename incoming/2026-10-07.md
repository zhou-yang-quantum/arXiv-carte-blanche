# arXiv-2026-10-07

The eleven papers in this historical selection connect microscopic constructions to emergent physics and practical capabilities. Chaotic circuits, driven topological models, and measurement-conditioned critical chains offer different routes to universal behavior. Two complementary lattice-to-continuum studies address Hawking radiation and conformal invariance, while algebraic renormalization links topological constraints to conformal data. The fault-tolerance papers examine both new protective constructions and their resource implications. The final selection tests the assumptions behind the usual prohibition on thermal symmetry breaking in one dimension.

## arXiv-2026-10-07 — Item 1

### Kardar-Parisi-Zhang superdiffusion in chaotic quantum circuits
**Rustem Sharipov, Urban Duh, Sun Woo P. Kim, Friedrich Hübner — arXiv:2610.07143**

Why should a conserved charge spread superdiffusively in a chaotic quantum system? An exponent resembling Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) scaling does not by itself identify the mechanism or establish the full correlation profile. A further obstacle is that equilibrium currents vanish in Hamiltonian chains with a single on-site conserved charge, blocking the usual nonlinear hydrodynamic route to KPZ behavior. The authors construct discrete-time circuits that evade this obstruction through a conserved density that varies within the spatial unit cell.

This construction permits an equilibrium current whose dependence on density supplies the required hydrodynamic nonlinearity. From microscopic equilibrium quantities, the authors calculate the velocity of a charge fluctuation, its broadening scale, and its predicted stationary correlation profile. Tensor-network simulations of a two-site qutrit circuit agree with these predictions without fitting transport parameters: the evidence concerns the shape and normalization of the profile as well as the exponent \(z=3/2\), corresponding to a width growing as \(t^{2/3}\).

A second construction uses three-site qubit gates. Here the chemical potential changes the predicted transport from diffusion to superdiffusion, while long-lived coherent quasiparticles delay the approach to the KPZ profile. These are deliberately constructed chaotic examples; their behavior does not establish KPZ transport as a generic property of chaotic circuits.

**Background.** The relevant conserved charge is staggered: different sites within a circuit cell contribute differently, although the complete charge is conserved after each period. Matching this structure to the discrete evolution allows an equilibrium current that the corresponding homogeneous-charge setting would forbid.

Nonlinear fluctuating hydrodynamics then expands the current around an equilibrium density \(\rho_0\): \(j(\rho_0+u)=j(\rho_0)+vu+\tfrac12j''(\rho_0)u^2+\cdots\). Here \(u\) is a density fluctuation and \(v=j'(\rho_0)\) is its propagation velocity. The quadratic term makes a fluctuation change its own local propagation speed. Together with fluctuations and dissipation, this produces the noisy Burgers mechanism underlying KPZ scaling. The construction matters because it supplies this term microscopically rather than inferring it from an observed exponent.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual advance is a concrete realization of the nonlinear hydrodynamic mechanism in chaotic quantum dynamics, separating it from explanations based on integrable quasiparticles. Its parameter-free correlation profile also makes it a useful benchmark for non-equilibrium simulation. The qubit example adds a practical lesson: coherent excitations can obscure the asymptotic universality class for substantial times, so identifying transport requires more than fitting a spreading exponent.

## arXiv-2026-10-07 — Item 2

### Protecting bosonic codes from ancilla-induced errors with continuous-variable flags
**Thomas Décultot, Ronan Gautier, Mazyar Mirrahimi — arXiv:2610.07139**

An oscillator can store quantum information economically, but controlling it through an ancillary qubit introduces a particularly damaging fault. If the ancilla decays during a gate, the oscillator receives an unwanted rotation or displacement whose magnitude depends on the unknown decay time. Detecting that a fault occurred is therefore insufficient: the resulting continuous family of errors can extend beyond what the bosonic code corrects.

The authors add an auxiliary oscillator whose final phase-space position records the propagated error. Measuring this flag by heterodyne detection—jointly estimating its two quadratures—provides a correction signal. Feedback then removes the inferred error up to a code stabilizer, whose action leaves the encoded information unchanged. The protective sequences use the same dispersive interactions, rotations, and displacements already needed for the underlying gates.

For conditional rotations, one flag supports protection to all orders in ancilla decay. Conditional displacements admit first-order protection with one flag and all-orders protection with two. Simulations using circuit-QED parameters predict more than three orders of magnitude fewer logical errors during four-component cat-code parity measurement, and a finite-energy GKP lifetime approaching the ideal-ancilla benchmark. These are modeled improvements; finite flag resolution and imperfections remain part of the performance calculation.

**Background.** During a dispersive gate, the ancilla state determines the oscillator's rotation rate. A decay switches that rate partway through the operation, turning the jump time into a rotation error. The flag experiences a related evolution, converting this hidden timing information into a measurable phase-space trajectory. For displacement gates, the sequences must similarly encode the accumulated translation.

The trajectory need only distinguish errors that act differently on the logical state. Two physical rotations or displacements differing by a stabilizer may share a correction outcome without harming the information. This is how the protocol turns a continuous fault into a code-compatible residual error. Coherent flag states have overlapping measurement distributions, however: increasing their separation improves discrimination, while larger flag amplitudes also increase sensitivity to oscillator imperfections. Thus all-orders fault encoding does not mean exact correction with a finite, noisy flag.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful fault-propagation construction because it preserves information that a binary flag would discard. Rather than asking the storage code to tolerate every ancilla-induced rotation or displacement, it makes those errors observable and correctable. The broader connection to quantum error correction is the joint design of gates, syndrome information, and feedback. Its eventual architectural value depends on whether the simulated suppression survives the added oscillator's control and readout costs.

## arXiv-2026-10-07 — Item 3

### Spontaneous Hawking Radiation from Dynamical Black-Hole Formation in Quantum Spin Systems
**Shunichiro Kinoshita, Keiju Murata, Daisuke Yamamoto, Ryosuke Yoshii — arXiv:2610.07794**

A microscopic simulator of Hawking radiation should connect vacuum particle creation to a specified continuum theory and a measurable lattice observable. Horizon-like propagation alone leaves open whether the emitted signal has the predicted quantum-field-theoretic origin. The authors address this by starting in a spin-chain ground state representing the early-time vacuum, then changing the couplings to encode black-hole formation without inserting initial excitations.

Their continuum target is a Majorana field in a time-dependent, two-dimensional spacetime. Spatially and temporally varying local spin couplings implement the prescribed geometry. The authors calculate the renormalized continuum energy density and identify a local spin observable that reproduces it quantitatively, including its dependence on surface gravity. Measuring energy density avoids the highly nonlocal spin representation of individual particle occupations.

The agreement holds during a finite radiation window whose duration grows logarithmically with system size. At later times, the lattice cutoff changes the dynamics. The demonstrated match is therefore an energy-weighted integral of the Hawking spectrum within a controlled time interval, rather than a mode-by-mode reconstruction of the radiation.

**Background.** The geometry is expressed through a position-dependent flow velocity: sufficiently strong flow carries both families of light rays in the same direction, creating a horizon. In the spin realization, an antisymmetric exchange interaction between neighboring spins encodes this flow. The spin-to-fermion mapping then identifies the long-wavelength field and its coupling to the geometry.

The observable dictionary is equally important. A flat observation region separates the radiative signal from local curvature effects, and subtracting its initial energy density removes the vacuum contribution that diverges in the continuum limit. The resulting local spin expectation value can be compared directly with the renormalized field-theory prediction. The finite window reflects the eventual sensitivity of horizon dynamics to short wavelengths that a lattice cannot reproduce indefinitely.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a quantitative bridge between curved-spacetime QFT and accessible many-body observables. Its strength is the combination of a prescribed formation process, vacuum initial state, observable dictionary, and explicit cutoff limitation. For quantum simulation, this supplies a concrete validation problem before attempting interacting fields or more demanding radiation correlations. Those extensions remain further steps beyond the free-field energy-density benchmark established here.

## arXiv-2026-10-07 — Item 4

### Chiral Parafermions in Floquet $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Topological Order
**Melissa Will, Harald Schmid, Jan Egger, Adam Gammon-Smith, Michael Knap, Frank Pollmann — arXiv:2610.07156**

Periodic driving can give topological order an additional dynamical structure: each cycle can change an excitation's anyon type while transporting fractionalized information along the boundary. Extending this physics beyond the Majorana-based \(\mathbb Z_2\) setting is difficult because the corresponding parafermion models generally interact. The authors construct a driven clock-spin model that makes these processes explicitly tractable at a special operating point.

The model applies three successive bond pulses on a generalized Kitaev honeycomb lattice. Its emergent variables are constrained parafermions coupled to conserved \(\mathbb Z_N\) gauge fields. At the Clifford point, the drive moves the endpoints of string operators around closed bulk orbits but translates boundary endpoints along the edge. The bulk evolution exchanges electric and magnetic anyon labels, while boundary correlations reveal directional parafermion propagation.

The authors quantify the associated fractional information flow using the radical chiral unitary index, probe exchange statistics dynamically, and diagnose bulk anyon conversion with a loop observable. Stabilizer simulations access the Clifford point, while short-time matrix-product-state calculations find a surviving chiral edge response away from it. The latter evidence supports persistence over accessible times; it does not establish generic long-time stability against heating.

**Background.** Clock spins have \(N\) local states, with operators that cyclically shift the state or attach an \(N\)th-root phase. A string transformation combines these operators into parafermions: exchanging their order produces such a phase rather than the Majorana minus sign. Here these variables also obey local constraints, so the mapping does not turn the model into a collection of independent free particles.

A physical string has parafermionic endpoints joined by a conserved gauge string. Tracking its endpoints therefore exposes the fractionalized dynamics without treating a single parafermion as a local spin observable. At the boundary, their directed motion carries a fraction of the information associated with translating an entire local qudit. The radical chiral index characterizes this quantized net transport; the authors extract it through a chiral mutual-information protocol.

**Why it matters?** This is a concrete extension of driven topological dynamics to \(\mathbb Z_N\) systems, with observables that connect an emergent gauge description to simulations on qudits. Its methodological value is the combination of an analytically trackable circuit limit and tests outside that limit. It provides a structured setting for studying how anyon permutation and edge information flow constrain one another. Establishing a robust phase over longer times requires additional control of heating and perturbations.

### A Single Conformal Coordinate Decodes Outcome-Resolved Measurement-Induced Entanglement
**Reyhaneh Khasseh, M. A. Rajabpour — arXiv:2610.07995**

After measuring part of a critical chain, how much of the resulting bit string is needed to predict the entanglement between the two unmeasured regions? An averaged entropy cannot answer this question: different microscopic records might have the same statistical distribution while retaining distinct conditional entropies. The authors seek a prediction for each individual outcome, despite the record's lattice-scale roughness.

Their proposal extracts one compact coordinate from the record using weights fixed by the subsystem geometry. It is a geometrically weighted occupation sum, reduced modulo \(2\pi\), rather than a particle count or simple imbalance. The universal entropy is then predicted by

\[
S_{\alpha,L}(m)\simeq F_{\alpha}^{(g)}\bigl(h,\delta_L(m)\bigr).
\]

Here \(m\) denotes the measurement record, \(L\) the chain size, \(\alpha\) the Rényi index, \(h\) the conformally mapped geometry, and \(g\) the compact-boson coupling. The equation asserts that the outcome dependence of this universal observable enters through \(\delta_L\) alone in the scaling regime.

Exact free-fermion identities provide microscopic support at the XX point, and numerical calculations test the relation across geometries and Rényi indices. Interacting XXZ calculations retain the same record coordinate while changing the known continuum coupling. That interacting identification is supported numerically; an analogous microscopic derivation remains open. The compression concerns universal entropy for typical outcomes in the scaling limit, while other properties of the conditioned state can retain further record dependence.

**Background.** Measuring the two intervening intervals produces boundary data in the Euclidean field-theory description. Mapping the slit geometry to a cylinder converts the record into two compact boundary profiles. Their relative angular average is the surviving coordinate.

The key step is a cancellation in the normalized replica partition function. Each nonzero boundary Fourier mode contributes the same action on the connected replica surface as in the separate normalization factors, so its dependence cancels before averaging over outcomes. Compact winding sectors behave differently: connected replicas share one winding sum, whereas their normalizations contain independent sums. This leaves sensitivity to the relative boundary average modulo the field period. The remaining microscopic question is whether a rough lattice record realizes that continuum coordinate, which the XX analysis and XXZ tests address at different levels of control.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual advance is an observable-specific reduction of measurement randomness: a large record can contain far more information than is needed to predict its universal conditional entanglement. This gives a substantive connection between measurement-conditioned states and boundary field theory, beyond an ensemble average. It also suggests a route to entropy prediction without reconstructing every conditioned wavefunction. Extending that capability requires identifying which measurements, critical theories, and observables admit the same cancellation mechanism.

### Conformal invariance of the six-vertex model's height function
**Emile Averous, Hugo Duminil-Copin, Tiancheng He, Piet Lammers, Ioan Manolescu — arXiv:2610.07454**

Knowing a lattice model's bulk solution does not automatically establish its continuum theory in a bounded domain. Boundaries can influence fluctuations throughout a critical system, and conformal predictions require control of that influence as the lattice spacing vanishes. This paper addresses that gap for the six-vertex height field by transferring established full-plane Gaussian behavior into domains with specified boundary data.

The authors prove convergence to a Gaussian free field with Dirichlet boundary conditions, including the harmonic mean profile imposed by the boundary heights. Their argument combines full-plane convergence, reflection positivity, and qualitative estimates that control correlations and boundary behavior. The result establishes the conformal scaling limit for a continuous range of interacting vertex models rather than only an isolated solvable parameter.

The theorem has explicit restrictions. It treats isotropic vertex weights, uses the available full-plane GFF input, and specifies lattice boundary approximations with piecewise constant heights and adjacent jumps of \(\pm1\). Its stated established coverage includes \((-1,-1/2]\) and a neighborhood of \(\Delta=0\), where \(\Delta\) labels the vertex-weight parameter. It does not prove the result throughout the entire expected critical regime or for arbitrary boundary conditions.

**Background.** The useful bridge is approximate harmonicity of multipoint height correlations. Away from coincident insertions, continuum Gaussian-field correlations obey a mean-value property in each coordinate. The proof establishes the corresponding large-scale property for lattice correlations, then combines it with boundary control to identify the domain-dependent limit.

Reflection positivity supplies inequalities by treating reflected observables as a positive inner product. This lets known full-plane behavior constrain correlations in less symmetric geometries. The boundary assumptions matter because the allowed heights are tied to lattice parity: the theorem uses boundary arcs compatible with alternating parity classes and their permitted height jumps. Once these boundary values and the interior harmonicity are controlled, the continuum correlations can be identified with the appropriate Dirichlet field.

**Why it matters?** This is a foundational advance in the lattice-to-CFT correspondence: it makes boundary-sensitive continuum predictions rigorous across a continuous family. It separates what bulk integrability provides from the additional reasoning needed to establish conformal behavior in general geometry. For interests in exactly solvable models and continuum emergence, that distinction is especially useful. Connections to Ashkin–Teller and related percolation models offer further directions, while their development is left to subsequent work.

### Algebraic Tensor Network Renormalization and Holographic duality
**Chenqi Meng, Tian Lan, Zhengcheng Gu — arXiv:2610.08787**

A numerical renormalization scheme can represent a critical point accurately and still drift away from it if allowed relevant perturbations enter during coarse graining. Preserving ordinary microscopic symmetry may be insufficient when the infrared theory has additional generalized symmetry constraints. The authors ask whether those constraints can be built into the tensors themselves, making criticality stable within the permitted space and retaining the information needed to distinguish conformal sectors.

Their \(\mathcal R\)-TNR framework makes local tensor spaces obey the fusion and recoupling rules of a category \(\mathcal R\), and preserves those rules under Loop-TNR coarse graining. For suitable choices, broad families of allowed initial tensors flow to the same gapless fixed point. Ising, tricritical Ising, and three-state Potts examples illustrate this behavior. The construction also adapts the transfer calculation to resolve topological sectors, extracting scaling dimensions, conformal spins, and magnitudes of sector-resolved structure constants.

The resulting “topological bootstrap” recovers infrared conformal data from finite categorical and geometric input together with an initial tensor that need not be finely tuned within the construction. The stability statement is conditional on those constraints: perturbations outside the permitted tensor space can restore relevant directions. The examples therefore support a symmetry-protected route to numerical criticality, rather than universal stability of the corresponding CFTs under arbitrary lattice perturbations.

**Background.** A fusion category specifies which charge or defect labels can combine and the consistent changes of basis between different orders of combination. Unlike group multiplication, combining generalized symmetry defects can have several possible outputs. An \(\mathcal R\)-tensor only has components compatible with these rules; contractions and truncations must remain within the same allowed spaces. Loop-TNR optimizes tensors collectively around a plaquette, reducing the accumulation of short-distance correlations while respecting that algebraic structure.

The holographic interpretation places the constrained theory on the boundary of an auxiliary topological bulk. Bulk anyon labels organize the boundary's conformal sectors. In particular, symmetry-preserving local perturbations lie in the trivial bulk sector; absence of relevant operators there explains the protected stability. This bulk is an organizing topological theory, and the construction does not require gravitational dynamics.

**Why it matters?** The work combines a useful numerical method with a conceptual explanation of why some constrained flows can reach criticality without parameter tuning. Its significance lies in making generalized symmetry operational during coarse graining and in recovering more than the unlabelled conformal spectrum. This connects nonperturbative field-theory computation to topological structure. Broader impact will depend on how readily suitable categorical input can be identified for less familiar theories and how the method performs beyond the demonstrated examples.

## arXiv-2026-10-07 — Item 8

### Cyclic triorthogonal codes in prime dimension
**Shiroman Prakash — arXiv:2610.08012**

Fault-tolerant qudit computation needs economical ways to supply non-Clifford resources, but finding codes with the required transversal gate structure is a restrictive algebraic search. This paper uses cyclic symmetry to organize that search. Its central observation is that the relevant triple-overlap constraints become simple conditions on a code's finite-field frequency support.

For prime local dimension \(p\) and code length coprime to \(p\), the author derives spectral criteria for two constructions and exhaustively enumerates maximal codes within those constructions over stated length ranges: through 121 for \(p=3\), and through 96 for \(p=5,7\). A separate qubit result characterizes odd-length cyclic triply-even codes through classical triorthogonality, with enumeration through length 763.

The qudit examples include \([[19,1,3]]_3\) and \([[7,1,3]]_7\), each encoding one logical qudit with distance three. They improve the reported distillation thresholds and overhead exponents relative to the shortest Reed–Muller and Reed–Solomon comparators. The seven-dimensional example begins the family \([[p,1,2\lfloor p/6\rfloor+1]]_p\). These are improvements within specific code and distillation comparisons; the exhaustive searches are also restricted to the constructions being enumerated.

**Background.** Triorthogonality controls sums of coordinatewise products of three generator rows. In the odd-prime formulation here, triples involving stabilizer directions vanish, while a repeated logical direction can retain a nonzero value. This cancels unwanted dependence of the transversal diagonal phase on stabilizer representatives while preserving the intended logical phase. The general definition does not impose a separate self-orthogonality condition; the qutrit gate requires its own phase arithmetic.

Cyclic symmetry makes these constraints searchable. Shifting a codeword preserves the code, so a finite-field Fourier transform describes it by allowed frequencies. Coordinatewise multiplication adds frequencies. Triple-product constraints therefore become sumset restrictions: one checks which frequencies can occur as sums of three allowed ones, rather than testing every triple of codewords. This converts a large collection of algebraic constraints into a finite combinatorial problem.

**Why it matters?** The methodological contribution is a systematic search framework that produces concrete improvements in qudit magic-state distillation. It connects a code's algebraic structure directly to the cancellation needed for logical gates and then to resource metrics. This is useful for assessing alternatives to familiar qubit constructions. Turning the reported gains into an architectural advantage would additionally require accounting for physical qudit noise, implementation costs, and the surrounding distillation protocol.

## arXiv-2026-10-07 — Item 9

### QCD_2: 't Hooft equation vs. relativistic Schroedinger equation
**Andrei Smilga — arXiv:2610.08673**

How faithfully can a relativistic two-body potential model reproduce a confining quantum field theory? Two-dimensional QCD in the large-\(N\) limit offers a sharp test because its meson spectrum follows from the field-theoretic 't Hooft equation. A much simpler alternative assigns the quark pair relativistic kinetic energy and a linear confining potential, but agreement with a classical picture need not extend to the quantum spectrum.

Smilga compares the 't Hooft equation with the Hamiltonian

\[
H=2\sqrt{p^2+m^2}+\sigma|x|,\qquad \sigma=\frac{g^2N}{4}.
\]

Here \(x\) and \(p\) are the relative coordinate and momentum, \(m\) is the quark mass, and \(\sigma\) is the string tension. A canonical relation between the classical descriptions explains their common leading semiclassical spectrum. The quantum calculations nevertheless have different subleading WKB corrections, which substantially affect the ground state.

The numerical comparison finds much closer agreement for excited states: by excitation index \(n=3\), the reported mass discrepancy is at most about \(2.5\%\), and decreases with excitation. This percentage concerns masses; the paper also plots their squared ratio, whose deviations are correspondingly larger. The result calibrates the accuracy of this particular spectral approximation without establishing equivalence of the two theories or their other observables.

**Background.** The 't Hooft wavefunction is expressed in terms of the fraction of the meson's light-front momentum carried by a constituent. Its singular integral equation incorporates the field-theoretic bound-state dynamics. The square-root, or Salpeter, Hamiltonian instead quantizes a pair with relativistic kinetic energy in an instantaneous potential.

The important distinction is between classical equivalence and quantum equivalence. A canonical transformation preserves the classical action underlying leading WKB quantization, explaining the matching asymptotic spectrum. It does not automatically identify two separately defined quantum operators and their subleading corrections. Comparing those corrections exposes information lost when replacing the integral bound-state equation by the simpler potential description.

**Why it matters?** This is a focused clarification of when a physically intuitive confinement model is quantitatively reliable. Its value is in explaining both its success for excited levels and its failure to capture important low-lying quantum corrections. For QFT and solvable-model work, it provides a useful example of how agreement in classical dynamics can coexist with inequivalent quantum spectra, and of why leading semiclassical agreement alone is an insufficient validation of a reduced description.

## arXiv-2026-10-07 — Item 10

### Fault-tolerant resource estimation for ground-state preparation via Lindblad simulation
**Marius Bothe, Patrick Schoepf, Nick S. Blunt — arXiv:2610.08667**

Engineered dissipation can prepare low-energy states by preferentially removing energy, but a favorable algorithmic scaling does not establish an affordable fault-tolerant implementation. Each simulated dissipative step must distinguish cooling transitions from heating transitions, and inaccurate discrimination can spoil the desired steady state. This paper evaluates that cost for a single-ancilla Lindblad approach applied to the fermionic Hubbard model.

The authors derive rigorous error bounds with explicit prefactors and compare them with circuit-level numerical behavior. Parameters chosen from empirical convergence studies can reduce estimated resources by orders of magnitude relative to the rigorous bounds. Their quoted benchmark is approximately \(7.7\times10^8\) T gates for one unit of Lindblad evolution of a 36-site linear Hubbard model targeting the low-energy subspace. The larger-system empirical estimate extrapolates parameters informed by smaller simulations.

Accurate filtering of energy transitions dominates the cost. Crucially, one unit of evolution is a costing benchmark, not a complete ground-state preparation estimate. Total preparation also depends on the time needed to approach the desired state and on maintaining accuracy throughout that evolution. The empirical savings therefore demonstrate a substantial gap between worst-case bounds and practical parameter choices while still leaving a demanding resource requirement.

**Background.** The algorithm filters a simple operator \(A\) by combining its time-evolved versions:

\[
K=\int ds\,f(s)e^{iHs}Ae^{-iHs}.
\]

In the energy basis, the Fourier transform of \(f\) weights each matrix element according to its energy change. Suppressing positive changes constructs a cooling jump operator. An exact filter can make the ground state dark, but convergence to it also requires suitable transitions connecting excited states.

A unitary coupling to one ancillary qubit, followed by discarding or resetting that qubit, implements an approximation to a dissipative step. Evaluating the filter requires repeated Hamiltonian evolutions over a finite time grid. Resolving a small energy difference demands a long time window; truncation can leak weight into unwanted heating transitions. This energy-resolution requirement explains why few ancillas do not imply a cheap circuit.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a resource audit that identifies a specific algorithmic bottleneck. It makes the distinction between provable error control and empirically effective cooling quantitatively consequential. For quantum algorithms and simulation, the useful direction is to improve transition filtering and convergence together: reducing qubit overhead alone leaves the dominant gate cost unresolved. Comparisons with other preparation methods must also include the full evolution time and the same target accuracy.

## arXiv-2026-10-07 — Item 11

### Spontaneous symmetry breaking at any temperature in a local one-dimensional model
**Andrew Lucas — arXiv:2610.07693**

The usual one-dimensional domain-wall argument relies on the finite cost of inserting a domain of one symmetry sector inside another. Can a strictly local model evade that conclusion by changing its local state space and the way symmetry acts? Lucas constructs such an example using two unbounded integer variables per site and a symmetry whose action changes along the chain.

For every finite positive inverse temperature \(\beta\), the paper constructs uncountably many infinite-volume Gibbs states, parameterized by \(\mathbb R^2\). The discrete flavor symmetry acts freely on this family, producing distinct symmetry-related states. More general members admit an interpretation in terms of localized domain walls between asymptotic sectors. A regulated finite periodic system has a unique Gibbs measure, but that measure concentrates into separated symmetry-related clusters as the regulator grows.

There is a significant interpretive qualification: no normalizable probability measure can remain invariant under the freely acting infinite flavor translations in the first place. The substantive result is therefore the construction and thermal structure of the Gibbs states, supported by the finite-system concentration result, rather than symmetry noninvariance alone. A continuous-state modification similarly breaks a noncompact helical symmetry; the assumptions of the usual Mermin–Wagner argument do not apply.

**Background.** The interaction is

\[
H=\sum_n\lVert x_n-Ax_{n+1}\rVert_1,
\]

where \(x_n\in\mathbb Z^2\), the norm sums absolute component values, and the integer matrix \(A\) has determinant one and eigenvalues \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\) with \(\lambda>1\). It favors configurations following \(x_n\approx A^{-n}c\). A flavor transformation adds \(A^{-n}a\) at site \(n\), for an integer vector \(a\); shifting the chain changes this transformation's label. This intertwining of translation and flavor is the helical symmetry.

Different sector trajectories separate exponentially along the chain, so inserting a competing domain need not have the bounded wall cost familiar from finite-state chains. The unbounded local variables enable this mechanism despite nearest-neighbor interactions. The Gibbs states are defined through DLR consistency: conditional on the exterior of any finite interval, the interior has the appropriate Boltzmann distribution. This avoids requiring a global infinite-volume partition function.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful conceptual example of how locality, local state-space size, and symmetry structure jointly govern thermal order. It sharpens the scope of familiar one-dimensional intuition by supplying an explicit equilibrium construction outside its assumptions. Its relation to helical repetition-code constraints also creates a substantive bridge to statistical-mechanical views of error correction. It does not establish a finite-dimensional quantum memory or a practical self-correcting device; those would require additional constructions and stability results.
