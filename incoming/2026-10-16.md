### Region bounds, error margins and certified positivity in the numerical conformal bootstrap; Or the first mathematically rigorous bounds on the 3d Ising CFT critical exponents
**Marten Reehorst — arXiv:2610.20701**

The conformal bootstrap can constrain an interacting field theory without solving its microscopic dynamics. Its numerical precision, however, raises a separate question: which reported exclusions are mathematical consequences of crossing symmetry, and which still depend on numerical approximations? Checking many parameter values does not exclude an unnoticed allowed region between them, and positivity for approximate conformal blocks need not imply positivity for the exact blocks.

Reehorst separates the numerical search for an exclusion functional from its exact certification. The construction simultaneously controls continuous parameter regions, the infinite spin tail, conformal-block truncation errors, and arithmetic rounding. Positive margins accommodate the analytic error bounds; exact polynomial sign certificates then verify the resulting inequalities. The search software proposes a candidate, while a separate calculation establishes whether it actually proves the desired exclusion.

Applied to the single-correlator Ising bootstrap, this certifies a small allowed rectangle within a larger excluded domain and gives conditional two-sided bounds on critical exponents. The assumptions include a gap above the stress tensor: the next even spin-two primary must satisfy $\Delta_{T'}\geq11/2$. Analytic ingredients for removing that assumption through mixed correlators are established, but that extended certification is not completed here.

**Background.** An exclusion functional is a linear combination of derivatives of the crossing equation. If it assigns incompatible signs to the identity contribution and every operator allowed by a proposed spectrum, no positive set of squared operator-product coefficients can solve crossing. Its positivity must therefore hold over entire continuous families of operator dimensions.

Bernstein polynomials provide a useful certificate because their basis functions are nonnegative on a bounded interval or box. Nonnegative expansion coefficients consequently establish positivity throughout that region. Subdivision makes this test more effective. The paper combines this finite certificate with analytic control of the unbounded directions and of the difference between approximate and exact blocks.

**Why it matters?** The advance is a reproducible route from numerical exclusion to a theorem under explicit physical assumptions. It strengthens the reliability of nonperturbative QFT calculations and can also improve searches by excluding regions together. The remaining spectral assumption matters when interpreting the Ising application: rigorous certification of the calculation does not independently establish every premise identifying the physical universality class.

## arXiv-2026-10-16 — Item 2

### Stability of Quantum LDPC States under Local Pauli Noise
**Tai-Hsuan Yang, Andrew D. Kim, Akash Vijay, Jong Yeon Lee — arXiv:2610.22357**

A decoding threshold establishes recoverability of encoded information, but mixed-state phase stability asks how noise changes the state's spatial information structure. Sparse stabilizer checks alone do not immediately answer that question: conditioning on overlapping syndromes can correlate initially independent errors over long distances.

The authors study the normalized code projector, $\rho_0=P_{\mathcal C}/\operatorname{Tr}P_{\mathcal C}$, which averages over logical states while retaining the code's stabilizer constraints. They prove exponential decay of noise-induced conditional mutual information across a buffer in two regimes. At weak noise, correlations require a connected error cluster crossing the buffer. At strong noise, they require a connected sequence of sites retaining enough information to transmit conditional correlations. Bounded check weight and participation limit the number of such paths.

The hypotheses differ substantially. The weak-noise theorem covers separating tripartitions of the full system. The strong-noise theorem requires an initially Markov partition and a locally generated description of all normalizer information visible in the buffer. Subsystem partitions need additional control: the paper supplies a counterexample to stability from the LDPC assumptions alone. A space-uniformity condition supports topological-entropy stability, while finite-dimensional lattice geometry permits recovery in a constant number of quasi-local layers.

**Background.** The reduction to classical probability works because Pauli errors move the code projector between orthogonal syndrome sectors without selecting a particular logical state. Entropy changes can therefore be expressed through syndrome distributions. The difficult correlations arise after conditioning on the buffer's syndrome, rather than from direct correlations between the original error variables.

The normalizer consists of Pauli operators commuting with every stabilizer. “Local-normalizer completeness” requires bounded-support generators whose restrictions reproduce every normalizer degree of freedom seen inside the buffer. This supplies local hidden variables for the strong-noise argument. It is a structural requirement beyond merely having sparse checks.

Petz recovery is a reference-state-dependent reversal of a quantum channel, analogous to reversing a classical noisy process using its prior distribution. Here spatial decay allows recovery to use enlarged local blocks; disjoint blocks operate in parallel. Constant layer count still permits block size to grow with the desired global accuracy.

**Why it matters?** This provides a general analytic connection between sparse-code structure, conditional correlations, and mixed-state stability, extending beyond individual lattice-code mappings. Equally useful is its identification of the geometric assumptions that cannot be dropped. For QEC and noisy many-body phases, it distinguishes stability of the canonical code state from a guarantee about arbitrary encoded states or the efficiency of a practical decoder.

## arXiv-2026-10-16 — Item 3

### Long-lived local coherences at high temperature from frozen domain walls
**Sarang Gopalakrishnan, Romain Vasseur — arXiv:2610.20724**

Conserved densities can retain memory through hydrodynamic tails, but a local spin-raising operator changes the conserved magnetization and has no overlap with those densities. Its coherence should therefore dephase much faster. Finding algebraic persistence in a chaotic system requires a mechanism beyond the usual projection onto hydrodynamic modes.

The mechanism here uses quantum-group symmetry in easy-axis spin chains. Represent up and down spins as upward and downward steps of a height profile. Near a sufficiently deep valley, a local spin flip can act like a symmetry operation of the surrounding window. The environment temporarily cannot distinguish the two branches of the resulting coherence. Its survival becomes tied to the motion and return of that valley.

For the transverse autocorrelation $C(t)=\langle\sigma_x^-(t)\sigma_x^+(0)\rangle$, the authors obtain a lower bound of order $t^{-2}$ on a moving time average, using symmetry, locality, and a numerically supported non-undershoot assumption. This is not an unconditional pointwise bound. In the infinite-temperature, large-anisotropy XXZ limit, an explicit calculation gives $C(t)\sim\sqrt{3}/(18\sqrt{t})$. Extensions to chaotic chains use a hydrodynamic argument for valley survival and transport; their asymptotic exponents are less firmly established than this solvable limit.

**Background.** The relevant quantum group is a deformation of spin-rotation symmetry. Its raising generator contains magnetization-dependent strings, so different configuration changes carry very different amplitudes. Directly projecting a local spin flip onto this generator gives a poor bound because rare configurations dominate its norm.

The authors instead use the amplitude-normalized part of the generator's polar decomposition. This operator remains conserved but removes those extreme weights. In the strong-deformation limit, it selects a spin associated with a deepest height minimum, exposing the connection to a local coherence. A finite-window Mazur bound then supplies persistent overlap. Locality lets that window describe the infinite system until disturbances from its boundary arrive. The non-undershoot assumption connects the relevant finite-time average to the finite-window plateau.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual contribution is a symmetry-based route to slow dephasing that survives beyond integrability. It shows how a charge-changing observable can inherit slow behavior without becoming an ordinary conserved density. This opens a useful direction for non-equilibrium theory and constrained quantum simulation. Establishing the non-undershoot property analytically and resolving longer-time chaotic scaling would strengthen the broadest conclusions.

## arXiv-2026-10-16 — Item 4

### All-temperature superfluidity in two dimensions
**Bilal Hawashin, Soumyadeep Chaudhuri, Igor F. Herbut, Michael M. Scherer — arXiv:2610.20717**

Two-dimensional superfluidity usually disappears when thermal entropy makes free vortices favorable. The prohibition on continuous symmetry breaking does not itself require this disappearance: a Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless phase has algebraic correlations rather than true long-range order. The question is whether vortex unbinding must eventually occur as temperature increases.

The authors construct a controlled field-theory setting where it need not. A complex scalar couples to a critical O(N) sector at a biconical fixed point. The negative coupling between sectors makes thermal fluctuations favor a nonzero scalar amplitude. Integrating out massive fluctuations leaves a compact phase field governing long-distance behavior.

The decisive feature is that stiffness grows with temperature. At the fixed point, the dimensionless ratio satisfies $\rho_s/T\propto N$, where rho-s is the phase stiffness, T is temperature, and N counts components of the real vector field. For sufficiently large N, vortex operators remain irrelevant. A separate estimate makes their microscopic fugacity exponentially small in N, placing the effective theory inside the basin of the algebraically ordered phase.

Thus the result concerns superfluidity at every nonzero temperature in the tuned continuum theory, controlled through a large-N expansion. It does not require spontaneous breaking of the continuous symmetry. Transferring the conclusion to a microscopic model with a finite ultraviolet scale would require establishing how far its fixed-point description remains applicable.

**Background.** A compact phase field permits configurations that wind by a full turn around a point. These vortices are absent from a treatment that keeps only small, smooth phase fluctuations. Their energy and positional entropy both grow logarithmically with system size, so their competition depends on stiffness divided by temperature.

Stiffness alone does not completely specify the starting point of the BKT flow. Vortex fugacity measures the statistical weight of creating a core before accounting for its long-distance phase texture. Large stiffness makes vortex insertions irrelevant near the smooth-phase theory; sufficiently small fugacity ensures that coarse-graining actually approaches that regime. The paper addresses both requirements. The biconical fixed point supplies a joint scale-invariant theory of the two sectors, allowing thermal fluctuations in one sector to reinforce the other's stiffness without introducing a separate fixed energy scale.

**Why it matters?** This is a conceptual extension of finite-temperature ordering in relativistic field theory. It identifies how scale invariance can keep the energy–entropy balance favorable to superfluidity as temperature changes. For QFT and critical phenomena, the important next questions concern finite-N corrections and realizations with a substantial scaling regime. The large-N mechanism is already informative without assuming that a generic two-dimensional system shares it.

### Infinite magic and the emergence of the continuum
**Leo Shaposhnik, Lorenzo Leone, Lennart Bittel, Dimitris Saraidaris, Alexander Jahn, Jens Eisert — arXiv:2610.20790**

Stabilizer states can reproduce extensive entanglement and useful features of quantum codes, but those successes do not establish that they can reproduce the local structure of relativistic QFT. Continuum field theories have distinctive algebras of regional observables. The question is whether their emergence requires a resource that entanglement alone misses.

The authors prove that the canonical state representation of an infinite stabilizer system has no type III factors in the central decomposition of its regional algebras. They then obtain a quantitative result for infinite products of entangled qubit pairs: the algebra on one side is type III if and only if a locally optimized measure of magic becomes unbounded. Optimizing local bases is essential because arbitrary basis-dependent nonstabilizerness need not reflect the entanglement spectrum controlling the algebra.

This establishes a precise obstruction within the stated constructions. The general stabilizer result keeps the lattice fixed while taking it infinite; the necessity-and-sufficiency result concerns the special entangled-pair family. Relating these statements to arbitrary scaling limits with vanishing lattice spacing remains an additional problem.

**Background.** In the Gelfand–Naimark–Segal construction, a state defines inner products between operators through its expectation values. Completing this space produces a Hilbert-space representation adapted to that state. Closing the represented regional observables gives a von Neumann algebra, whose structure can differ radically from a finite tensor factor.

A factor has no central observables beyond scalar multiples of the identity. Decomposing over the center separates sectors distinguished by classical labels. Type III factors have no nonzero finite projections and lack the ordinary intrinsic trace used to describe a finite subsystem by a density matrix. This is an algebraic distinction; divergent entropy by itself does not establish it.

For the entangled-pair construction, the optimized magic is $-\log F_{\mathrm{stab,LO}}$, where the fidelity is the greatest squared overlap with a stabilizer state after arbitrary independent unitaries on the two sides. A bipartite stabilizer state has equal nonzero Schmidt weights. Local optimization therefore asks how well the actual Schmidt spectrum can resemble a flat spectrum, removing irrelevant choices of Schmidt vectors. Infinitely many Bell pairs illustrate why infinite entanglement alone is insufficient: their spectrum remains flat.

**Why it matters?** This turns a proposed link between magic and continuum physics into concrete algebraic theorems. It identifies a structural limitation of stabilizer descriptions that conventional entanglement diagnostics can miss, making it particularly relevant to the interface of QFT, quantum codes, and holographic models. A broader physical resource criterion would require extending the framework to explicit continuum scaling limits and understanding finite-accuracy approximations, where exact algebraic obstructions need not directly determine simulation cost.

## arXiv-2026-10-16 — Item 6

### Magna: simulating magic state factories as cheaply as Clifford circuits
**Yifei Wang, Yingfei Gu — arXiv:2610.22315**

Evaluating a magic-state factory requires its acceptance probability and accepted output fidelity under faults. Following the complete many-qubit state can be unnecessarily expensive: both intermediate states and the desired outputs carry substantial non-Clifford resources, even when only these two scalar quantities are needed.

Magna constructs tensor networks for those quantities directly and simplifies each network globally. The reduction exploits algebraic structure related to transversal gates on quantum codes, concentrating the remaining non-Clifford dependence into a smaller set of coordinates. Pauli fault insertions preserve the reusable structure. The framework also samples intermediate outcomes causally when later corrections depend on nonlinear classical rules, including decoder outputs.

The demonstrations include Bravyi–Haah distillation producing up to 100 output magic states without an exponential increase in cost with output count, and distance-five cultivation protocols. The relevant complexity is the representation rank left after reduction. That rank can be one even when the output state itself has large magic; the title describes this favorable structure, rather than a general efficient simulator for arbitrary non-Clifford dynamics.

**Background.** A probability or fidelity is a closed contraction containing a circuit and its conjugate. Traced-out outputs, accepted measurement records, and overlap with the ideal target impose different boundary conditions. These boundaries can remove computational complexity that would remain necessary if one insisted on constructing the output wavefunction.

In Magna's reduction, stabilizer constraints restrict basis labels to an affine subspace. Directions along which the diagonal phase is only Clifford can be summed out efficiently, much as stabilizers identify equivalent representatives while logical coordinates retain the nontrivial action. The residual coordinates determine the remaining expansion cost. A transversal gate's algebra is useful here because its phases act compatibly with precisely such code constraints.

Nonlinear feedforward needs additional care: a decoder's classical decision cannot generally be absorbed into a simple linear parity condition. Sampling the required records before evaluating that decision preserves the protocol's causal order. Under the stochastic Pauli fault model, the expensive structural simplification can still be reused across fault samples.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful methodological advance for evaluating fault-tolerant resource preparation. Its broader lesson is that the complexity of a requested scalar can be much smaller than the complexity of the state producing it. For QEC architecture comparisons, the benefit depends on the reduced ranks of the actual protocols and compatibility with the chosen noise model. The reported examples demonstrate substantial scope without establishing that every factory has the same favorable reduction.

### Classical Capacity of Single-Mode Bosonic Gaussian Channels
**Gerardo Adesso, Ludovico Lami, Vittorio Giovannetti — arXiv:2610.22308**

A channel acting on one oscillator still poses a many-body optimization problem: entangling signals across many uses might transmit more classical information per use. For phase-sensitive Gaussian noise, limited energy creates another obstacle. Squeezing a signal to accommodate the noise consumes photons that could instead encode the message through displacement.

The authors prove that independently prepared Gaussian signals, with Gaussian-distributed displacements, attain the unrestricted classical capacity of every single-mode Gaussian channel at every input energy. The central step is a sharp additive lower bound on joint output entropy in terms of input second moments, valid even for entangled, non-Gaussian inputs. Combined with upper bounds on average-output entropy, it closes the gap between a single-use Gaussian optimization and the regularized capacity.

They also distinguish two energy constraints above capacity. A hard total photon-number cutoff gives an exponential strong converse: successful decoding becomes exponentially unlikely. With only a mean-energy constraint, the exact rate–error relation is $C_\varepsilon(N)=C_0(N/(1-\varepsilon))$. Here N is mean input photon number, epsilon is the allowed average decoding error, and the zero subscript denotes vanishing-error capacity. This concerns unassisted classical communication; arbitrary collective decoding remains allowed.

**Background.** Holevo information compares the entropy of the average received signal with the average entropy of each received signal. The first term measures how varied the received ensemble is; the second accounts for uncertainty remaining even when the transmitted label is known. Both depend on how energy is allocated between squeezing and displacement.

Proving a lower bound separately for each output mode is insufficient: correlated outputs can have joint entropy below the sum of their marginal entropies. The paper therefore needs an inequality controlling the joint output directly. This is the conceptual role of its entanglement-based entropy bound.

The distinction between a hard cutoff and a mean constraint has a simple operational origin. Under a mean constraint, a transmitter may spend more energy on a selected fraction of messages while accepting failures on the rest. A per-codeword cutoff prevents that redistribution strategy, allowing a stronger statement about failure above capacity.

**Why it matters?** This resolves a fundamental Gaussian-channel optimization, particularly the low-energy regime where noise matching and signal modulation compete. Its significance extends beyond optical engineering: it gives a setting where correlations across arbitrarily many quantum inputs can be rigorously ruled out as a communication advantage. The result concerns optimal encoding rates, while implementing the collective measurements permitted by the capacity theorem remains a separate practical challenge.

### An SYK wormhole under the microscope
**Valérie Bettaque, Martin Sasieta, Brian Swingle, Alejandro Vilar López — arXiv:2610.20699**

Gravitational wormholes can describe correlations between statistical averages of boundary observables, but a fixed microscopic Hamiltonian must still produce definite, factorizing answers. Understanding how those descriptions coexist requires a model where the relevant correlations can be derived from microscopic couplings rather than assumed through a random-matrix ansatz.

The authors study sample-to-sample fluctuations of a heavy operator's thermal one-point function in the Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev model. They derive a connected large-system saddle and propose a double-trumpet interpretation. In the double-scaling limit, correlation chords joining the two copies become states of an emergent bulk Hilbert space.

The comparison with Jackiw–Teitelboim gravity is informative because agreement is not automatic. Fixed-size Majorana strings select bulk length states, whereas the corresponding gravitational kernel is diagonal in energy. Smearing the operator over sizes permits agreement in an appropriate heavy-particle limit. For fixed microscopic couplings, coupling-dependent half-wormhole wavefunctions retain erratic information that disappears in the smooth disorder average.

**Background.** SYK couples many Majorana fermions through random many-fermion interactions. In its double-scaling limit, both the fermion number N and interaction order p grow while $p^2/N$ stays fixed. Expanding thermal traces and averaging paired couplings produces chord diagrams: each chord records a contraction between interaction insertions. Counting chords crossing a cut supplies a basis on which a transfer operator acts, turning diagram enumeration into Hilbert-space evolution.

A double trumpet is a connected Euclidean geometry with two asymptotic ends and a narrow joining region. In this calculation, cross-chords provide microscopic data for the slice joining those ends. The Schwarzian limit isolates the low-energy reparametrization dynamics associated with JT gravity, allowing a more direct geometric comparison.

The coupling-labelled states, conventionally called alpha-states, distinguish an individual disorder realization from an ensemble. Fixing that label restores the ordinary microscopic factorization. Averaging products of the corresponding half-wormhole amplitudes produces the connected, smooth wormhole contribution. This explains why a geometrically simple average need not imply a simple wavefunction for each sample.

**Why it matters?** The advance is a more microscopic account of what a wormhole amplitude captures and what information it discards. It also tests the limits of treating heavy operators as random objects: their fermionic structure leaves identifiable bulk consequences. For solvable quantum dynamics and holographic QFT, the value is the explicit bridge between coupling statistics, chord evolution, and geometry. Extending that control beyond the heavy-operator and scaling regimes is needed for a more general gravitational dictionary.

### Operator-Space Entanglement Entropy in Random Fermionic Gaussian Dynamics
**Pablo Bayona-Pena, Elena Gramellini, Lorenzo Piroli — arXiv:2610.21723**

Efficient free-fermion evolution does not guarantee that an evolving observable remains compact as a matrix-product operator. Operator entanglement measures a particular representation cost, and its behavior becomes less familiar for extended operators and dynamics without local conservation laws. An exactly tractable model can separate these issues.

The authors evolve initially unentangled Majorana strings under Brownian quadratic SYK dynamics, with random all-to-all bilinear couplings. They map operator entanglement to an auxiliary domain-wall melting problem and obtain an analytic expression for its time dependence in the thermodynamic limit. They also derive late-time Gaussian Page curves depending on both the subsystem fraction and the initial string fraction. These differ from the entanglement curves of fully Haar-random many-body states.

The solution follows the spectrum of a restricted correlation matrix under Brownian orthogonal rotations. Random-matrix methods determine its stationary distribution and time evolution, from which the operator entropy follows. The result describes a Gaussian ensemble with nonlocal couplings; its domain wall should not be interpreted as a physical diffusive front in a local chain.

**Background.** Vectorizing an operator turns its input and output indices into two copies of a Hilbert space. A spatial partition then assigns both copies of each physical site to the same side. Entanglement of this doubled state is operator-space entanglement, which constrains matrix-product-operator compression.

For the chosen Majorana strings, the doubled state is Gaussian and can be represented as occupied auxiliary modes followed by empty ones. Its correlation matrix evolves as $G(t)=R(t)P_M R(t)^{\mathsf T}$, where R is the orthogonal single-particle evolution and P-M projects onto the initially occupied modes. Restricting G to the subsystem supplies eigenvalues whose binary entropies sum to the operator entropy. The domain-wall mapping thus converts a many-body operator problem into the mixing of a one-particle projector.

The Page curve here is the stationary operator entropy as the subsystem size changes. Gaussian rotations explore a constrained family of states, so the fully random-state Page curve is not the appropriate benchmark. The retained initial string fraction supplies additional information about that family.

**Why it matters?** This provides an exact dynamical benchmark for comparing different notions of operator complexity. Large operator entanglement can make one compression scheme expensive while covariance-matrix evolution remains efficient. For non-equilibrium simulation, the useful contribution is a calculable reference against which effects of locality, interactions, and non-Gaussian resources can be isolated. The proposed connections to operator non-Gaussianity remain directions motivated by the solution rather than a general complexity theorem.

### Magic in quantum trajectory unravelings of noisy random quantum circuits
**Sara Santos, Alessio Paviglianiti, Vincenzo Savona — arXiv:2610.22318**

Noise can simplify quantum dynamics, but a trajectory simulation does not automatically exploit that simplification. The same mixed-state evolution admits different ensembles of pure trajectories, and their entanglement or magic can differ. Choosing an ensemble with less magic seems promising for stabilizer-based simulation, provided the reduction affects the trajectories that actually dominate computational cost.

The authors investigate this freedom in Haar-random and T-doped Clifford circuits with local dephasing, $\mathcal D_p(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+pZ\rho Z$. In the usual Pauli-jump representation, each noise event is a Clifford operation and cannot reduce trajectory magic. Rotating the Kraus representation instead produces informative, nonunitary updates.

Numerical optimization finds that a state-independent weak-measurement unraveling can outperform the projective-measurement representation in the high-magic regime. Projective unraveling can remain preferable when magic generation is sparse. The optimized mean magic is reduced, but the studied scaling remains extensive and the trajectory distribution becomes broad, retaining a substantial high-magic population. Exact evaluations reach twelve qubits, so the scaling evidence is finite-size numerical evidence.

**Background.** Kraus operators specify both outcome probabilities and conditional state changes. Unitarily mixing a channel's Kraus operators leaves the averaged channel unchanged while changing those conditional updates. Physically, this corresponds to changing how information in the environment is resolved. Averaged observables agree, but nonlinear functions of individual trajectories need not.

A Pauli jump changes a state without learning anything about it: its occurrence probability is state independent. A generalized measurement can instead acquire partial information and reweight amplitudes without fully projecting the qubit. That is how the alternative ensemble can lose resources that persist in the random-unitary description.

The diagnostic is the second stabilizer Rényi entropy, built from fourth moments of Pauli expectation values. It vanishes for stabilizer states and is invariant under Clifford operations. Its trajectory average is useful, but an exponentially resource-dependent simulation cost is sensitive to the full distribution. Averaging an exponential cost and exponentiating an average resource can give very different answers.

**Why it matters?** This identifies useful freedom in noisy-circuit simulation while showing why minimizing a mean resource is not yet a runtime guarantee. It connects monitored dynamics to the choice of classical simulation representation: identical density-matrix evolution can produce substantially different trajectory workloads. The next substantive step is to optimize measured computational cost, including high-resource trajectories and sampling convergence, and test whether the improvement persists at larger sizes.
