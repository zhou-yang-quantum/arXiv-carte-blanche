## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 1

### Twisted Compactification, Quotient Quantum Codes and Logical Gates
**Po-Shen Hsin — arXiv:2611.09538**

A systematic construction of codes and logical gates could replace many separate, family-specific derivations. Hsin identifies quotient codes—formed by identifying qubits and checks under a symmetry—with twisted compactification in field theory. A transfer theorem relates the quotient's logical operators and distance bounds to its parent, including new operators associated with winding around the quotient geometry.

The framework extends to entangling identifications that produce codes beyond Pauli stabilizers. A concrete gate construction uses a quarter-turn quotient of the three-dimensional fermionic toric code. Families beginning at $[[768,2,9]]$ have distance growing linearly with size and admit a finite-time, quasi-local evolution implementing logical T up to Clifford factors.

**Background.** A twist glues equivalent sheets while applying a code symmetry; the quotient retains one physical qubit per orbit. This differs from projecting onto the parent's symmetry-invariant Hilbert space. New cycles can support logical information, and a parent operation acquires phases on those winding sectors. “Quasi-local” here permits exponentially decaying interactions: an approximating constant-depth circuit needs gate ranges growing logarithmically with system size and inverse error.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a design language connecting field-theoretic topology to explicit code constructions and logical operations. Its strongest appeal is the ability to ask which geometry changes the logical action of an existing physical evolution. Turning that principle into a competitive fault-tolerant architecture will still require practical syndrome extraction, decoding, and noise analysis.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 2

### Open Quantum Time Crystals without External Thermodynamic Resources: Go and No-Go Theorems
**Yuri Ogami, Hiroyasu Tajima — arXiv:2611.09540**

Can an equilibrium environment activate persistent macroscopic oscillations without supplying work or energetic coherence? Existing open-system time crystals commonly rely on driving or reservoirs that violate equilibrium conditions. This paper separates the effects of the Hamiltonian induced by an environment from those of its dissipative action.

The authors construct a short-range chain of three-level systems whose coupling to a zero-temperature Gibbs environment activates periodic order-parameter correlations. The reduced dynamics has time-independent operators, time-translation covariance, and detailed balance. The mechanism is an induced Hamiltonian term that lifts a degeneracy and generates coherent precession within a specially correlated stationary state. Removing the dissipator preserves the oscillations; removing the induced Hamiltonian eliminates them. One microscopic implementation reproduces their periodicity exactly, beyond the Lindblad approximation.

A complementary theorem establishes the general obstruction: under the stated covariance and detailed-balance conditions, dissipation cannot activate a time crystal absent under unitary evolution with the same final Hamiltonian and stationary state. The construction therefore uses a correlated, non-Gibbs stationary mixture; it does not establish time-crystalline order in the final Hamiltonian's thermal equilibrium state.

**Background.** The diagnostic is a normalized two-time correlation function, schematically $\langle A(t)A(0)\rangle/N^2$, where the order parameter sums local operators over N sites. A stationary density matrix can support oscillatory correlations even though one-time expectation values remain stationary. The paper requires a nonconstant periodic thermodynamic limit of this correlation. Its dynamical symmetry supplies ladder operators with sharply defined oscillation frequencies, while the chosen dissipation leaves that oscillatory sector intact.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful clarification of what an environment can contribute to persistent dynamics. It identifies Hamiltonian restructuring as a distinct route to activation while proving a broad limitation on equilibrium-compatible dissipation. For nonequilibrium theory, the result sharpens the distinction between preparing correlations, preserving them, and generating their subsequent temporal motion.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 3

### 99.9...% of the SU(2) BFSS ground state
**Alexander Michel, Jaroslav Scheinpflug, Xi Yin — arXiv:2611.09837**

A tiny variational energy need not identify a ground state when scattering states extend down to zero energy. This makes the BFSS threshold bound state unusually difficult to construct and certify.

For SU(2), symmetry reduces the wavefunction from millions of components depending on 27 coordinates to 2,066 components depending on three variables. The authors combine this reduction with a variational basis adapted to the model's flat directions. Crucially, they minimize an auxiliary operator built from supercharges: it has the same zero-energy subspace as the physical Hamiltonian but a rigorously bounded positive gap in the relevant singlet sector. Their squared ground-state overlap satisfies $F\geq0.999997$. The radial profile also agrees with the asymptotic prediction extracted from a three-graviton amplitude.

**Background.** Banks–Fischler–Shenker–Susskind matrix quantum mechanics contains nine bosonic matrices and fermionic partners. Commuting matrices create valleys along which constituents can separate, producing the gapless continuum. Weighting the supercharges by a radial operator makes escape along those valleys costly without changing the desired ground-state kernel. The auxiliary gap then converts a small expectation value into an overlap certificate.

**Why it matters?** This is an unusually concrete nonperturbative wavefunction calculation with quantitative certification. The methodological lesson—constructing a better operator for certifying a state than its physical Hamiltonian—is valuable beyond matrix models. The dramatic symmetry reduction is specific to SU(2), so extending the computational achievement to larger gauge groups remains a substantial challenge.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 4

### Universality in Quantum Many-Body Spin Ratchets
**Andrea Azzali, Marko Ljubotina — arXiv:2611.08590**

When an interacting circuit transports spin preferentially in one direction, which features require integrability? An earlier exact drift velocity for integrable spin ratchets suggested a universality extending beyond the solvable models.

The authors prove that the connected spin correlation's center obeys $\bar{x}(t)=v_{\mathrm d}t$ at every time step. Charge conservation and translation invariance suffice; full spin-rotation symmetry and integrability are unnecessary. Translation invariance of the ensemble similarly fixes the disorder-averaged first moment. The velocity depends on the two spin representations and background magnetization, while explicit spatial patterns can change it.

**Background.** Ratchets alternate two different spin representations. Each gate combines an interaction with a swap, carrying the two representations in opposite directions. The stationary reference ensemble weights conserved magnetization. Charge conservation and translation invariance make the interacting gates drop out of the relevant current correlation, leaving a first-moment sum rule whose current is fixed by the swaps. Unequal spin fluctuations then give a nonzero drift.

**Why it matters?** The result identifies an exact transport statement that survives removal of integrability. It provides a clean benchmark for studying which dynamical signatures follow from conservation and circuit geometry, and which contain genuinely model-dependent information. The theorem fixes the center of the profile; its broadening and higher moments remain separate physical questions.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 5

### Analytic Bounds on Conformal Defect Spectra
**Yuma Furuta, Wataru Harada, Yuya Kusuki, Yin Tang — arXiv:2611.08582**

An impurity can be unstable even when the surrounding bulk is exactly critical, but determining its relevant perturbations usually requires difficult defect spectroscopy. This paper asks how much stability information can instead be extracted from a few accessible defect observables.

The authors bootstrap four-point functions of defect-changing operators. The two crossing channels involve spectra on different defects, and their relative vacuum normalization is fixed by the defect entropies. Positivity then yields analytic upper bounds on the lowest nonvacuum scaling dimension using those entropies and the dimension of an operator that connects the defects. Detailed bulk spectral data are unnecessary.

Applications to symmetry-related pinning-field defects in three-dimensional vector models are particularly interesting. With the stated numerical inputs and symmetry assumptions, the bounds exclude stable impurity order with three or more orientations in $O(2)$ and $O(3)$, or four or more in $O(4)$, independently of their arrangement. Separate fuzzy-sphere calculations of domain-wall dimensions support these exclusions. The remaining possibility of two-state order in the latter models is a candidate, rather than a general proof of stability.

**Background.** A defect-changing operator sits where one line defect changes into another. For several possible ordered impurity orientations, such an insertion acts as a domain wall along the defect. A direct sum of the individual defects represents all the candidate orientations together; symmetry-preserving perturbations can mix these sectors even if every individual defect is stable. On a line, an allowed operator with $h<1$ is relevant and can destabilize this collective order. Defect entropy enters because it fixes the relative weight of the identity contributions in the crossing equation.

**Why it matters?** This turns sparse defect data into constraints on physically distinct impurity phases. The advance is both methodological and conceptual: stability of a collection of ordered sectors cannot be inferred from stability of its components. It offers a tractable use of conformal methods for impurity dynamics, while leaving the eventual infrared endpoints to further analysis.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 6

### Quasi-hydrodynamics beyond the conventional paradigm
**Lorenzo Gavassino — arXiv:2611.09246**

Extended hydrodynamic equations often work surprisingly well even when their usual justification—a few almost-conserved quantities separated parametrically from everything else—is missing. Are such successes uncontrolled closures, or can they still define systematically improvable effective theories?

Gavassino supplies three kinetic examples in which the conventional assumptions fail separately. In one, omitted modes relax only three times faster than the retained current. In another, infinitely many slow modes exist, but only one additional combination couples to the energy observables being described. A third produces a slow current through a bottleneck in momentum diffusion, without invoking weakly broken translation symmetry.

The common construction projects the kinetic operator onto relevant slow and fast sectors and integrates out the latter through a convergent resolvent expansion. A finite spectral gap gives a finite low-frequency, long-wavelength domain of control; the gap need not become parametrically large. Within that domain, increasing the derivative order improves accuracy without adding new dynamical fields. These are results for the stated linearized kinetic systems, rather than a universal guarantee for arbitrary moment closures.

**Background.** The Onsager inner product measures kinetic perturbations using their quadratic free-energy cost. In these variables the collision operator is positive and self-adjoint, making its relaxation spectrum directly useful. The relevant space also depends on the observables: in the radiative-transfer example, infinitely many distortions of the photon spectrum are slow but carry no energy and decouple exactly from the two temperatures. They can be removed before identifying the effective slow sector. This is the essential reason that retaining every microscopic slow mode is unnecessary.

**Why it matters?** The paper replaces a restrictive physical story about weakly broken symmetries with a more precise spectral criterion. That broadens the justified use of nonequilibrium effective descriptions while preserving an explicit error-control mechanism. It is especially useful for distinguishing a successful phenomenological fit from an effective theory that can actually be improved at fixed field content.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 7

### Optimal asymptotic pure-state conversion under thermal operations
**Tatsuya Yamanouchi, Naoto Shiraishi — arXiv:2611.08974**

Free energy governs many thermodynamic state transformations, but coherent superpositions of different energies carry an additional constraint: an energy-conserving interaction with a thermal bath cannot supply an arbitrary phase reference. Combining these two restrictions is generally harder than satisfying either one separately.

For asymptotic conversions between pure states, the authors prove that the two familiar constraints are sufficient. In a finite-dimensional system with commensurate energy gaps, the optimal output-copy rate is

$$R_{\max}=\min\left\{\frac{\Delta F_\psi}{\Delta F_\phi},\frac{V_\psi}{V_\phi}\right\}.$$

The ratios compare the input and target excess free energies above the Gibbs state and their energy variances. This formula also requires compatibility of their exact time-translation symmetries: every time translation leaving the input invariant must leave the target invariant. Otherwise the rate vanishes.

The difficult part is achievability under actual thermal operations, with no supplementary coherence resource. The proof engineers a Gibbs bath so that the total input and output energy distributions match asymptotically, then constructs one energy-conserving unitary that works for the bath's typical components. Thus no additional obstruction survives in this pure-state, independent-copy limit.

**Background.** Thermal operations attach a Gibbs-state ancilla, apply a unitary conserving total energy, and discard an output subsystem. Their time-translation covariance makes pure-state energy variance a resource for coherent timing information. For many identical copies, energy distributions acquire a universal asymptotic form controlled by their means and variances, enabling the bath-matching construction. Exact spectral periods still matter: a broad distribution does not erase the discrete energy-spacing information responsible for the symmetry condition.

**Why it matters?** This is a complete conversion theorem in a setting where quantum coherence remains operationally relevant. It identifies two independent bottlenecks and explains why coherent thermodynamics is generally irreversible even asymptotically. The result is a substantive foundation for quantum thermodynamics, with the important scope restriction that general mixed-state conversions are not characterized here.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 8

### The instanton sum in two-dimensional self-dual models
**Mendel Nguyen — arXiv:2611.09648**

Instantons provide a compelling picture of nonperturbative physics, yet summing them reliably enough to explain a mass gap remains difficult. Restricting a theory to self-dual configurations offers a controlled test: what physics does an exact instanton sum actually retain?

Nguyen studies the self-dual $\mathrm{CP}^{N-1}$ sigma model, whose path integral is restricted to holomorphic configurations. In adapted fluctuation variables, the instanton measure is one-loop exact and equals the ordinary model's one-loop instanton measure. For two components, a classical instanton-gas calculation can consequently be repurposed as an exact partition-function sum expressed through free massive Dirac fermions.

The crucial result is that the self-dual model nevertheless remains gapless. Conserved currents separate into holomorphic and antiholomorphic pieces, and their exact two-point functions retain power-law decay after the instanton sum. The massive auxiliary fermions therefore do not furnish a full dual description of the theory. The measure argument and the gaplessness extend to general component number, although the partition-function sum is not explicitly evaluated in closed form there.

**Background.** The complex-projective sigma model describes a complex vector modulo its common phase, with topological sectors labeled by the winding of the field configuration. Holomorphic configurations minimize the action in positive-charge sectors. Here a Lagrange multiplier enforces holomorphicity throughout the path integral, rather than merely selecting saddles for an approximation. This changes the theory. A partition function can then admit a useful auxiliary representation without preserving the dictionary of local operators; the current correlators expose precisely that missing information.

**Why it matters?** The paper gives a sharp diagnostic for programs that approach nonperturbative QFT through self-dual sectors. An exact instanton sum and an emergent mass parameter do not by themselves establish a physical mass gap. Understanding how the gap appears when omitted configurations are restored is now a concrete question in a simpler model, with potential lessons for analogous Yang–Mills constructions.

### Andreev reflections and optimal truncation in the unitary Fermi droplet
**Silas R. Beane, Adele Le Borgne, Domenico Orlando, Susanne Reffert, Maximilian Schwick — arXiv:2611.08580**

Large-charge expansions turn a strongly interacting quantum system into an effective superfluid problem, but a trapped cloud has an edge where its density and pairing gap vanish. That creates a second expansion region and complicates any attempt to estimate the accuracy of bulk calculations.

The authors study a unitary Fermi gas with large flavor number $N$ and particle number $Q$ at fixed $Q/N$, followed by a large-ratio expansion. At leading large flavor number, the grand potential reduces to a spectral problem in an inhomogeneous pairing background. In three dimensions, every harmonic-trap bulk contribution beyond the first three terms integrates to zero. An effective-field-theory argument traces this cancellation to zeros of Euler beta functions, independently of the bulk low-energy constants, with related cancellations in odd dimensions and anisotropic harmonic traps.

Anharmonic traps behave differently: their bulk series grows factorially. Complex periodic trajectories determine its leading Borel singularities and hence its optimal truncation. High-order coefficients in quartic and sextic traps support the predicted singularity locations and oscillatory patterns. The termination statement concerns the bulk grand potential at fixed chemical potential; edge contributions remain, and transforming to fixed charge produces further terms.

**Background.** The Bogoliubov–de Gennes operator mixes particle-like and hole-like excitations of the paired gas. Andreev reflection converts one branch into the other. In this problem, analytically continued particle–hole cycles in complex space determine the large-order expansion even though no real zero-energy quasiparticle orbit traverses the gapped bulk. Borel singularities encode this factorial growth: their distance sets where adding further terms stops improving the approximation and estimates the exponentially small remainder.

**Why it matters?** This links large-charge QFT, semiclassical quasiparticle dynamics, and quantitative control of asymptotic expansions. The harmonic cancellation is striking, but the broader methodological gain is identifying the physical saddle structure behind truncation errors. A complete trapped-cloud prediction still needs the edge analysis, making the separation of bulk and boundary effects central to the result.

### Magic Can Superactivate Quantum Capacity
**Chunhe Xiong, Sunho Kim, Shunlong Luo, Lin Zhang, Shao-Ming Fei, Junde Wu — arXiv:2611.09594**

Two channels that individually cannot transmit quantum information can sometimes do so jointly. This superactivation is established, but identifying the physical resource responsible for it requires more than an abstract existence proof. Here the interactions are held Clifford, and the resource is placed entirely in environmental states.

The authors construct a discrete quantum convolution channel with zero quantum capacity but positive private capacity. Its Choi state has positive partial transpose, while a particular classical input ensemble produces correlations more informative to the receiver than to the full environment. A half-erasure channel then converts those private correlations into positive joint coherent information through the Smith–Yard mechanism.

They also build an activating auxiliary inside the convolution family itself. Finite-block encoding and recovery turn repeated auxiliary uses into a sufficiently reliable higher-dimensional erasure channel. Both physical channels separately retain zero quantum capacity, but their joint use transmits quantum information. Replacing either environmental state by any stabilizer mixture destroys the activation in this construction. An environmental magic measure also bounds the activated capacity from above.

**Background.** Discrete quantum convolution mixes a signal and a fixed environmental state through a Clifford unitary and discards one output. Varying the environmental state changes the noise without changing the interaction. Private capacity concerns confidential classical information; it can remain positive when quantum capacity vanishes. In the activation mechanism, the auxiliary sometimes delivers a purification of the private input ensemble, making its otherwise inaccessible coherent correlations usable. The environment's magic is therefore tied to an operational communication capability, beyond merely labeling a nonstabilizer state.

**Why it matters?** This supplies an explicit bridge between the resource theory of magic and a collective capability of noisy channels. The necessity statement is specific to the constructed convolution pairs, rather than a universal explanation of superactivation. Within that scope, it is a substantial conceptual example of how a resource in the noise can enable quantum information processing under restricted interactions.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 11

### Raman anyon-Hubbard model in a 1D Bose lattice gas
**Josep Cabedo, Claudio Iacovelli, Leticia Tarruell, Alessio Celi — arXiv:2611.09855**

Accessing many-body anyonic physics in one dimension requires engineering the phase acquired when particles pass through one another. Existing lattice schemes can impose such phases, but driving-induced heating and restrictions on site occupation complicate their extension beyond a few particles.

This proposal starts from a Raman-coupled Bose gas with unequal interactions between its internal states, loaded into a one-dimensional optical lattice. The internal composition of a lower-band particle depends on momentum. Consequently, spin-dependent interactions become current–density interactions after projection into that band. Reorganizing these terms reveals density-dependent tunneling with a direction-sensitive phase.

At weak chiral coupling, the effective model reproduces the standard anyon-Hubbard model. Beyond that limit, its tunneling amplitude and statistical phase both depend on local density, yielding a distinct model rather than the periodically identified statistical coupling of the standard construction. Numerical comparisons with the underlying flux ladder recover asymmetric momentum distributions, directional expansion, and chiral two-particle bound states. The authors identify accessible potassium-39 parameters, with the effective description requiring sufficiently strong Raman coupling and interaction energies below the band separation.

**Background.** In one dimension, exchange proceeds through a collision, so a statistical phase can be encoded in occupation-dependent hopping. A Jordan–Wigner transformation attaches a string counting particles on one side of a site: moving through an occupied region changes the string phase, translating bosonic operators into anyonic ones. The Raman construction produces this mechanism through the momentum dependence of the dressed particles' internal states. Its departure from a pure phase factor at stronger coupling explains why the resulting lattice theory has additional physics.

**Why it matters?** This is a concrete route from a controllable atomic Hamiltonian to many-body statistical interactions, without requiring periodic lattice modulation. Its appeal extends beyond realizing a familiar target: the deviations from the standard model are derived and potentially observable. Experimental validation of the effective couplings and the many-body regime would make it a useful platform for quantum simulation of chiral dynamics and generalized statistics.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 12

### Thermodynamics of Speed and Efficiency with Quantum Feedback
**Yue Liu, Ryotaro Honma, Ruicheng Bao, Tan Van Vu — arXiv:2611.08564**

Feedback changes a thermodynamic process in two ways: it uses measurement information, and it directly moves the system after a detected event. Accounting only for the information term can therefore miss how feedback modifies the usual cost of operating quickly.

For continuously monitored dynamics with instantaneous, outcome-dependent unital feedback, the authors derive a finite-time current–dissipation relation. It yields a speed limit for redistribution of density-matrix eigenvalues and a power–efficiency relation for stationary information engines. The speed limit contains both an information contribution to entropy production and a separate drift generated directly by the feedback maps.

A qubit-reset example shows why this separation matters. Feedback immediately reverses detected thermal excitations, permitting a reset to a specified nonzero error probability while attaining the generalized Landauer bound in finite time for appropriate bath spectra. A collective engine provides a second application: its information efficiency approaches unity with finite total output power in the large-system limit, enabled by diverging dynamical mobility. These statements use the paper's system-and-reservoir accounting; the detector's energetic cost is outside the reset analysis.

**Background.** Quantum jump feedback applies a channel conditioned on a resolved reservoir event. A thermal jump and its controller response need not produce the same net state displacement as the jump alone. The current bound constrains the dissipative part, while the feedback drift can supply part of the desired transformation directly. Likewise, information efficiency counts controller energy and consumed information among the engine's inputs. Approaching unit efficiency at finite power requires a growing kinetic prefactor, so the collective limit does not describe a fixed finite engine evading its trade-off.

**Why it matters?** The work provides a useful framework for distinguishing information-assisted reduction of dissipation from direct control of the trajectory. Its strongest contribution is a common inequality linking several operational bounds, illustrated by explicit protocols. For nonequilibrium quantum control, it makes the role of measurement and feedback more quantitative while keeping the resource accounting visible.

### Violent relaxation redux
**Michael L. Nastac, Yonadav Barry Ginat, Robert J. Ewart, Michael Barnes, Alexander A. Schekochihin — arXiv:2611.09531**

Collisionless gravitating systems relax rapidly, yet the classic maximum-entropy theory constrained by all collisionless invariants often predicts their final states poorly. This paper proposes that the constraints themselves evolve on a timescale much shorter than conventional two-body relaxation.

The mechanism is gravitational turbulence: irregular, large-scale gravitational fields stretch the distribution into progressively finer phase-space structures. The authors derive small-scale turbulent spectra and identify a cutoff where those fluctuations meet finite-particle noise. Exponential mixing reaches that cutoff in a time scaling as $\tau_{\mathrm{diss}}\sim\tau_{\mathrm{dyn}}\log N$, with N the particle number. One-dimensional simulations support the spectra and the logarithmic timescale.

The resulting picture is a sequence of instantaneous Lynden-Bell equilibria. Entropy maximization proceeds quickly, while the effective phase-space constraints change more slowly. At late times the evolving constraints can make the minimum energy compatible with them approach the conserved total energy. This “minimum-energy, maximum-entropy” endpoint therefore does not require the system to lose total energy. Extending the picture to actual three-dimensional galaxies and dark-matter halos remains a proposed application.

**Background.** Collisionless Vlasov dynamics preserves the phase-space volume occupied by every value of the distribution function. These volumes, sometimes called waterbag content, encode infinitely many Casimir invariants. Lynden-Bell's theory maximizes a mixing entropy while fixing that content. The new argument concerns finite-particle, coarse-grained evolution: turbulent filaments eventually reach a noise floor where their information is no longer represented by a smooth distribution. This changes the effective constraints without contradicting exact conservation in the ideal smooth Vlasov equation.

**Why it matters?** The paper offers a physically specific mechanism for relaxation through a changing family of constrained equilibria. That is a worthwhile broader direction in nonequilibrium statistical mechanics, especially when infinitely many conservation laws seem to obstruct ordinary thermalization. The scaling theory and one-dimensional tests are substantial evidence, while a predictive account of universal three-dimensional halo profiles will require further work.

### Entanglement in Collective Neutrino Oscillations on a Quantum Computer
**Katarina Bleau, Nikolina Ilic, Joachim Kopp, Ushak Rahaman, Xin Yue Yu — arXiv:2611.08595**

A quantum simulation of neutrino flavor evolution becomes scientifically necessary only if correlations omitted by conventional kinetic equations affect observable dynamics. Entanglement in a few-neutrino model alone does not establish that need in a dense astrophysical environment.

The authors compare full multiparticle evolution with mean-field kinetics in two-mode models of collective oscillations. Analytic arguments and classical simulations show convergence to mean-field predictions as particle number grows with the collective interaction scale held fixed. Their optimized treatment reaches millions of neutrinos on classical hardware; superconducting-processor demonstrations concern small systems and limited evolution times.

They then examine situations outside the persistent all-to-all setting. A stream with long-lived, spatially restricted interactions develops entanglement and flavor dynamics missed by the mean-field equations. The authors discuss whether unusually extended neutrino wave packets could realize this regime, but the relevant production physics remains uncertain. Sequences in which each pair interacts only once can generate entanglement in toy models; their estimates nevertheless suggest that realistic weak couplings strongly suppress its astrophysical importance.

**Background.** Coherent forward scattering changes flavor while preserving the participating momenta. In a dense gas, the coherently enhanced contribution of many weak encounters can yield accurate one-particle evolution even though the complete state has a much larger Hilbert space. A persistent local stream changes this reasoning: each neutrino repeatedly interacts with a small correlated neighborhood, and increasing the total stream length does not create the same mean-field averaging. Wave-packet overlap determines which interaction geometry is physically appropriate.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful assessment of where quantum simulation could add physics rather than simply reproduce a small entangled system. It combines a controlled recovery of mean-field behavior with counterexamples identifying what assumptions must fail. The astrophysical implications remain conditional on wave-packet production and propagation, making those microscopic inputs more important than the presence of entanglement by itself.

**Matti Järvinen, Elias Kiritsis, Francesco Nitti, Andrea Olzi, Edwan Préau — arXiv:2611.08628**

At low temperature and finite density, modes usually treated as nonhydrodynamic can become as soft as diffusion. A local hydrodynamic expansion may then miss the response even when frequencies and momenta are small compared with the density scale.

The authors calculate the longitudinal charged-current correlator in a holographic plasma with quark and isospin chemical potentials. A matched asymptotic expansion connects the near-horizon throat to the asymptotic bulk region. The calculation reaches next-to-leading order while retaining the full frequency-to-temperature dependence, allowing it to resolve the interaction between a diffusion-like pole and a tower of infrared modes.

At zero isospin chemical potential, the analytic formulas reproduce collisions of diffusion with those infrared poles. Finite isospin adds flavor precession and separates the colliding modes for real momenta. A local two-pole treatment remains finite where perturbation theory about isolated poles fails. Comparisons with the full numerical correlator show improved spectral functions and pole locations beyond ordinary thermal hydrodynamics, within the low-energy regime $\omega,k,T,|\mu_3|\ll\mu_q$.

**Background.** Here the symbols denote frequency, momentum, temperature, isospin chemical potential, and quark chemical potential. The near-extremal geometry contains an effectively two-dimensional anti-de Sitter throat whose modes have temperature-scale spacing. As temperature vanishes, their tower becomes a branch cut. These modes cannot simply be integrated out near a collision with diffusion. Quasinormal modes are damped bulk fluctuations corresponding to retarded-correlator poles; matching the throat response to the outer geometry preserves their frequency dependence while expanding against the much larger density scale.

**Why it matters?** This supplies an explicit, strongly coupled example of how hydrodynamic response must be reorganized when additional soft modes approach it. The main value is the controlled analytic resolution of a crossover, rather than a quantitative prediction for QCD matter. It complements general effective-theory criteria with a detailed calculation of the poles and spectral weight that those criteria must accommodate.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 16

### Arrival-time problem in the quantum information era - observation of a Zeno crossover in monitored arrival-time distributions
**Yousef Mafi, K. G. S. H. Gunawardana, Kim Pöyhönen, Teemu Ojanen — arXiv:2611.09814**

Measuring when a quantum particle arrives also changes its motion. Increasing detector resolution can eventually suppress detection through the quantum Zeno effect, so an arrival-time distribution must specify the monitoring protocol that produces it.

This paper adopts periodic projective monitoring at the end of a spin chain or charge-conserving circuit. It calculates the first-detection distribution and finds a crossover between rapid-monitoring suppression and a low-monitoring regime with resonant, nonmonotonic behavior. In the chain limit, the characteristic optimal period is near $\Delta t\sim2\pi/W$, where W is the single-particle bandwidth. This is a bandwidth estimate for a broad optimum, not an exact universal monitoring time.

Mid-circuit measurements on superconducting hardware resolve the two regimes and agree with quantum-trajectory predictions after noise suppression and mitigation. The crossover also survives in the studied interacting multiparticle circuits and two-dimensional geometry, while interference changes the detailed distributions. Late-time accuracy is limited by residual noise and the increasing cost of symmetry-based postselection.

**Background.** The first-detection distribution is built from trajectories with no earlier clicks. Every null measurement projects the state away from the detector site, so it changes the subsequent propagation even though no particle was observed. At short periods, the probability of reaching the detector during one interval is quadratic in that interval, leading to vanishing detection per unit time as monitoring becomes arbitrarily frequent. At longer periods, spectral phase differences can become commensurate with the sampling interval, producing dark or nearly dark combinations that are repeatedly missed.

**Why it matters?** The experiment demonstrates a theoretical principle worth retaining despite the batch's many hardware-focused submissions: time-of-arrival statistics are properties of monitored dynamics. The combination of analytic bounds, trajectory calculations, and interacting circuit measurements makes that dependence operationally clear. It provides a useful quantum-simulation setting for studying measurement backaction in transport without claiming a detector-independent resolution of the general arrival-time problem.

## arXiv-2026-11-09 — Item 17

### Motion-Induced Polaron Resonance beyond the Orthogonality Catastrophe
**Jia Wang, Xia-Ji Liu, Hui Hu — arXiv:2611.09390**

A static, infinitely heavy impurity can excite arbitrarily many low-energy particle–hole pairs, destroying its quasiparticle residue through the Anderson orthogonality catastrophe. Can externally prescribed motion restore a coherent resonance even when recoil remains absent?

The authors study a repulsive contact impurity moving at constant velocity through a one-dimensional ideal Fermi gas.
