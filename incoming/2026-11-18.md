### Robust Passive Quantum Memory in Three Dimensions, Part I: Thermal and Zero-Temperature Perturbative Stability
**Patrick Hayden — arXiv:2611.21749**

A passive quantum memory must withstand two different threats: thermally activated logical errors and coherent changes to its Hamiltonian. An energy barrier against thermal errors does not automatically protect a code against weak stray interactions. This distinction is especially consequential in three dimensions, where recent multiscale constructions have improved the prospects for thermal self-correction but their perturbative stability remained unresolved.

Hayden studies a three-layer variant of the multiscale welded codes introduced by Balasubramanian, Davydova, and Lin. The construction encodes one qubit using geometrically local stabilizer checks of bounded weight and degree. Alternating replacement steps amplify the syndromes of both error types across successively larger spatial scales, while maintaining bounded physical qubit density.

The paper establishes two separate results for this common code family. Without Hamiltonian perturbations, below a positive critical temperature, the memory lifetime grows stretched-exponentially with system size under single-site Davies thermal baths satisfying detailed balance and bounded-rate assumptions. At zero temperature, sufficiently weak static perturbations with superpolynomial spatial decay preserve a uniform bulk gap and produce ground-space splitting smaller than every inverse power of system size. Appropriately dressed logical observables consequently have superpolynomially long closed-system lifetimes. The regime combining positive temperature with nonzero perturbations belongs to the companion paper, rather than following automatically from these two statements.

**Background.** Welding replaces coarse checks and qubits with connected local gadgets, forcing a logical error to accumulate increasingly extensive syndromes as it crosses the hierarchy. The third layer supplies a local majority rule that simplifies syndrome cleaning. The perturbative argument needs more than this barrier: local constraints must reproduce the relevant ground-space relations inside a surrounding buffer. Here that buffer grows proportionally to the region being examined, requiring an extension of standard local topological quantum order criteria. Quasi-adiabatic continuation then supplies a quasi-local change of basis from the unperturbed ground space to the perturbed one; the logical observables must be transformed with that basis.

**Why it matters?** This closes an important structural gap between thermal protection and Hamiltonian stability in a three-dimensional memory construction. The contribution is an existence theorem and stability framework, with practical overhead and implementation still separate questions. It is particularly valuable for understanding how error confinement, local ground-space consistency, and coherent dressing must cooperate before passive protection becomes physically convincing.

### Exact Dynamic Scaling of Quantum Jump Statistics in Monitored Many-Body Systems
**Kazuki Yamamoto, Taiki Ishiyama, Toranosuke Matsubara, Kazuya Fujimoto, Tomohiro Sasamoto, Ryusuke Hamazaki — arXiv:2611.22029**

Measurement-induced phenomena are often characterized using quantities that require reconstructing individual trajectories or reproducing rare measurement records. The raw detection record offers a more accessible possibility: can fluctuations in where and when measurements occur themselves reveal universal many-body dynamics?

For a half-filled fermion chain with continuous local density monitoring, the authors calculate fluctuations in the number of jumps observed within a subsystem. They separate ordinary Poisson counting noise from an additional contribution generated by time-integrated density correlations. An exact finite-size solution of the Liouvillian autocorrelation function determines the latter's dynamic scaling:

$D_{M}^{\mathrm{anom}}(L,t)=\gamma^{3}L^{\alpha}f_{M}(t/(\gamma L^{2}))$.

Here $D_{M}^{\mathrm{anom}}$ is the anomalous contribution to the time derivative of the counting variance, $L$ the chain length, $M$ the monitored subsystem size, and $\gamma$ the monitoring strength; the hopping amplitude is set to one. The size exponent is one for a single observed site and three for a half-chain. The associated variance follows by integrating this noise in time. The same exponents and scaling function survive interactions in the strong-monitoring limit, while quantum-trajectory simulations test the counting predictions directly.

**Background.** Density monitoring suppresses coherence but preserves particle number, leaving slow transport modes. A density fluctuation changes the future local jump rate, correlating detection events over the time needed for particles to redistribute. Integrating these correlations produces fluctuations larger than independent counting would predict. Observing only part of the chain is essential: for the entire chain, the fixed total particle number removes this anomalous contribution. Strong monitoring allows rapidly decaying coherences to be eliminated, exposing the slow dynamics responsible for the interacting result.

**Why it matters?** This supplies an analytically controlled, postselection-free probe of monitored many-body dynamics, including an exact benchmark for finite-size extrapolation. It connects measurement records to hydrodynamic universality without assuming that counting statistics diagnose an entanglement transition. Establishing which interacting systems retain this scaling at finite monitoring strength is the substantive next test of its broader reach.

## arXiv-2026-11-18 — Item 3

### Non-Invertible Quantum Cellular Automata I: Microscopic Definition of Non-Invertible Symmetries
**Sahand Seifnashri, Shu-Heng Shao — arXiv:2611.22928**

Commuting with a Hamiltonian is insufficient to make an operator a useful physical symmetry: some locality requirement is also needed. For unitary symmetries, conjugation maps local observables to nearby observables. Extending this prescription to a non-invertible operator fails even for the identity observable, whose image can become a global symmetry operator.

Seifnashri and Shao formulate the symmetry directly through its action on local observables. Their non-invertible quantum cellular automata are quantum channels satisfying locality preservation, dualizability, and purity. The definition is made on the observable algebra of an infinite lattice, where genuinely non-invertible examples can satisfy these conditions. The paper constructs extensive examples, including anomalous on-site symmetries, and proposes a general correspondence with topological defects. That general correspondence remains conjectural here.

**Background.** Locality preservation means that an observable's support grows by only a fixed distance. Dualizability requires the channel defined by the infinite-temperature trace pairing to exist and preserve locality too; this extends the two-sided locality enjoyed by an invertible transformation and its inverse, without demanding an inverse. Purity means that the map cannot be split into distinct completely positive pieces. It excludes treating an arbitrary combination of operations as an elementary symmetry. This is a property of the channel, rather than of an input state, and its non-invertible realization relies on the infinite-system algebra. A topological defect is a localized modification of the Hamiltonian that can be moved through local equivalences.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual gain is a microscopic criterion that distinguishes a generalized symmetry from a merely conserved nonlocal operator or an arbitrary noisy channel. This gives lattice models a common language with continuum symmetry defects. For the intersection of QFT, quantum channels, and many-body phases, the especially useful question becomes which consequences of symmetry follow from these operational axioms alone.

### Modular Hamiltonian and modular flow of a massive scalar in an interval
**Raúl E. Arias, Daniel Fondevila — arXiv:2611.21739**

The modular Hamiltonian contains substantially more information than entanglement entropy: its eigenvectors determine how observables evolve under the reduced state's modular flow. For a massive scalar on an interval, knowing the entropy and spectral data had not supplied this explicit flow at arbitrary $mL$, with mass $m$ and interval length $L$.

The authors construct the missing eigenvectors as superpositions of massive waves associated with the two endpoints. Their amplitudes obey a linear integral equation whose successive iterations describe repeated propagation and reflection between the endpoints. A Painlevé V equation fixes the normalization, completing the spectral construction of the modular Hamiltonian.

This permits localized wave packets to be followed through the interval's causal diamond. Their commutator profiles spread and can peak ahead of the corresponding conformal trajectory: the massive flow cannot be represented simply as motion along a spacetime orbit.

**Background.** Modular flow is generated by minus the logarithm of the reduced density matrix, rather than by the physical Hamiltonian. Each endpoint initially contributes the familiar half-space, or Rindler modular modes. The reflection expansion couples these two endpoint problems across the finite interval. This supplies the spatial eigenfunctions needed to reconstruct the nonlocal quadratic kernels, whereas entropy only requires their spectral information. The shifted packet peaks therefore describe non-geometric modular evolution, rather than faster physical signal propagation.

**Why it matters?** This creates an unusually explicit nonconformal benchmark for modular dynamics. Its value extends beyond another entropy calculation: one can inspect how locality is lost when a mass couples endpoint structures that conformal symmetry otherwise organizes geometrically. That makes it useful for testing approximations to entanglement Hamiltonians and for separating universal short-distance behavior from genuinely nonlocal QFT information.

### Strong eigenstate thermalization in Singer-cycle Clifford circuits
**Naoto Tsuji — arXiv:2611.22391**

Showing that almost every eigenstate looks thermal leaves an important loophole: an initial state can overlap strongly with the exceptional eigenstates. A rigorous account of thermalization for arbitrary initial states therefore benefits from an explicit model in which those exceptions can be excluded.

Tsuji constructs local Clifford Floquet circuits whose action on Pauli operators is governed by a Singer cycle, a special finite-field symplectic transformation. Every nonidentity Pauli operator follows an orbit of length $2^{L}+1$, where $L$ is the number of qubits. Endpoint Hadamard and phase gates mix the two Pauli sectors, changing the structure relative to earlier shift-register circuits with exceptional nonthermal states.

Using finite-field character sums, the paper bounds both diagonal deviations from infinite-temperature values and off-diagonal matrix elements for every Floquet eigenstate and every observable of fixed support. The bounds scale as the inverse square root of Hilbert-space dimension, with constants independent of eigenstate labels. A separate temporal-fluctuation analysis establishes that local observables remain near their thermal values for almost all times, for arbitrary initial states in the thermodynamic limit.

**Background.** The Singer-cycle condition is stronger than merely requiring a long circuit period. It organizes the entire Pauli action into equally long nontrivial orbits with useful irreducibility properties. Following these operator orbits avoids constructing all eigenvectors explicitly. Character sums then control the phase cancellations entering matrix elements uniformly, which is the step needed to remove exceptional eigenstates. Long recurrence times remain compatible with equilibration for almost all times.

**Why it matters?** This is a sharp solvable example in which strong eigenstate thermalization and dynamical equilibration can both be proved. Its Clifford structure also separates thermal appearance of local observables from generic computational hardness. For non-equilibrium theory, that distinction helps identify which ingredients of thermalization require complex many-body amplitudes and which can arise from highly organized operator dynamics.

### Non-equilibrium topological order from Floquet anyon proliferation in the toric code
**Francesco Petiziol — arXiv:2611.23132**

Periodic driving usually threatens topological order by creating excitations and heating the system. A more selective drive raises a different possibility: proliferating only a particular anyon species might erase some ground-state structure while preserving a distinct form of long-range entanglement.

Petiziol studies toric-code quenches driven to create and propagate fermionic $f$ anyons. The central technical result decomposes entropies and logarithmic negativity into a toric-code ground-state contribution and an additional quasiparticle contribution. This is nontrivial because the mapping from spins to fermions is nonlocal.

For the analyzed drive, the fermionic dynamics is noninteracting, allowing large-system calculations and an interpretation through a periodic Gibbs ensemble. In the studied high-frequency regime, the long-time negativity retains its ground-state topological contribution even though entropy-based diagnostics depart from pure-state topological order. This realizes a coherent-driving counterpart of intrinsic mixed-state topological order at the level of the locally equilibrated state. The proposed extension to interacting drives that thermalize within the fermionic sector is a scenario motivated by these results, rather than an equally complete demonstration.

**Background.** The $f$ excitation is the fermionic composite of the toric code's electric and magnetic anyons. Restricting proliferation to this species constrains which information heating can erase. Although the evolving global state remains pure, its local late-time description can be mixed. Entropy then includes classical correlations, making negativity especially useful for identifying surviving quantum entanglement. The derived negativity contribution is also sensitive to fermion-parity structure; it cannot simply be replaced by a standard Gaussian-fermion negativity calculation.

**Why it matters?** The paper supplies both a diagnostic tool and a concrete mechanism for order surviving selective heating. It links coherent Floquet dynamics to mixed-state topological phenomena without assuming an external decohering channel. The broader phase claim will depend on the robustness of the constrained anyon dynamics when interactions and additional excitation processes are introduced.

## arXiv-2026-11-18 — Item 7

### Simple Models of Thermal Order in $2+1$ Dimensions
**Michael Smolkin, Lev Yung — arXiv:2611.21842**

Can order survive arbitrarily strong heating in a simple, ultraviolet-complete field theory? Existing examples often require many fields or elaborate tuning, making it difficult to see which ingredients are essential.

Smolkin and Yung exhibit persistent thermal order with as few as two real scalar fields. A sufficiently negative mixed quartic interaction, compatible with a bounded potential, lets fluctuations in one sector drive condensation in the other. Resumming thermal masses yields a controlled high-temperature expansion in $1/\ln(T/\lambda)$, where $T$ is temperature and $\lambda$ a characteristic quartic-coupling scale. Coupling the condensing scalar to massless Dirac fermions also generates a fermion mass and breaks parity and time reversal.

The continuous-symmetry extensions distinguish several kinds of order. For two components, growing stiffness sustains a Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless phase at arbitrarily high temperature, with algebraic correlations becoming progressively slower to decay. For three or more components, continuous symmetry remains unbroken, but the characteristic ordered-domain size grows with temperature. Selected conclusions are checked using functional renormalization group calculations.

**Background.** High-temperature dimensional reduction leaves the zero-frequency scalar modes as an effective two-dimensional statistical theory. The mixed coupling changes their self-consistent masses, so heating can favor one condensate instead of restoring both symmetries. In the continuous sector, the important quantity is stiffness relative to thermal fluctuations: it grows logarithmically with temperature. For two components this suppresses vortex proliferation, producing quasi-long-range order without contradicting the Mermin–Wagner theorem. The continuum fields have unbounded amplitudes, so their high-temperature limit differs from that of a fixed finite-dimensional spin at each site.

**Why it matters?** The advance is the simplicity and control of the mechanism, rather than the general observation that heating can favor order. These models make symmetry non-restoration and high-temperature quasi-order accessible to standard field-theory tools. They offer useful targets for quantum simulation, while a physical realization would need to retain the effective field theory over a sufficiently broad temperature range.

### Entanglement cost and classical capacity of multimode Gaussian states and channels
**Yuan Gao, Gerardo Adesso — arXiv:2611.22915**

Gaussian states admit compact covariance descriptions, but their operational resource costs need not obviously be Gaussian problems. Preparing many copies jointly, or using non-Gaussian decompositions, could in principle outperform the most natural Gaussian protocol.

The authors prove that these possibilities do not lower the formation cost of a finite-energy bipartite Gaussian state with finitely many modes:

$$E_{\mathrm{C}}(\rho_{V})=E_{\mathrm{F}}(\rho_{V})=E_{\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{G}}(\rho_{V})=G(V).$$

The three quantities are the asymptotic entanglement cost, unrestricted entanglement of formation, and Gaussian entanglement of formation. Their common value is a covariance optimization, denoted by $G(V)$. It is additive on Gaussian product states, strongly superadditive across mode groups, and lower-bounds the corresponding formation quantities for arbitrary states with the same covariance.

The state theorem also yields the entanglement cost of multimode Gaussian channels and their unassisted classical capacity under a mean quadratic-energy constraint. Pure Gaussian signals with Gaussian displacement modulation achieve the capacity independently across channel uses. Correlations among modes within one use remain part of the optimization.

**Background.** A Gaussian preparation starts from a pure Gaussian covariance lying below the target covariance and adds classical random displacements to supply the remaining fluctuations. Those displacements do not change the entanglement of each pure preparation. The difficult step is proving that arbitrary non-Gaussian ensembles cannot do better. The argument first exploits geodesic convexity along covariance paths preserving purity and the covariance constraint, then extends a supporting quadratic entropy bound to arbitrary pure states using a relative-entropy inequality. For channel capacity, minimizing average output entropy becomes a formation problem for the output and environment in a channel dilation.

**Why it matters?** This is a broad resource-theory result with computational consequences: optimizations over unrestricted states and many channel uses reduce to covariance problems for one device use. It strengthens the role of second moments from a convenient description to an operationally complete input for these tasks. The remaining covariance optimization can still be substantial, but it replaces a much less tractable quantum optimization.

### Local interference algorithms for observables in large random circuits
**Philippe Suchsland, Brayden Ware, Vladislav D. Kurilovich, Vadim Smelyanskiy, Kostyantyn Kechedzhi — arXiv:2611.23207**

A highly entangled wavefunction can be expensive to represent while selected observables remain accessible. The obstacle for stochastic approaches is then more specific: can one restore the interference discarded by trajectory sampling without reconstructing the entire quantum state?

The authors organize interference corrections by their extent in circuit spacetime. They study charge correlations in particle-number-conserving random circuits and out-of-time-ordered correlators in unconstrained random circuits. Starting from positive-weight stochastic descriptions, they add corrections involving clusters of gates. Tensor-network contraction inside blocks, Monte Carlo sampling between blocks, and a complementary matrix-product-state implementation make the expansion concrete.

Numerical performance supports polynomial scaling in circuit size and inverse target precision. Replica statistical models explain why corrections decay rapidly with their spacetime extent. This provides evidence for efficient simulation of these observables in the studied ensembles, rather than a worst-case classical simulation theorem for arbitrary circuits.

**Background.** A correlator can be expressed as a sum over pairs of forward and backward histories. Keeping only histories that coincide gives a classical stochastic process but loses coherent excursions in which the histories separate and rejoin. The expansion restores progressively larger excursions. Randomness suppresses sufficiently extended interference contributions, making their organization by locality useful. Importantly, the calculation targets a given circuit's observable; ensemble averaging is used to analyze approximation errors.

**Why it matters?** This gives a constructive reason that operator spreading need not imply an intractable observable calculation. It is useful both as a simulation method and as a sharper classical benchmark for quantum dynamics experiments. The substantive broader question is whether the same localization of relevant interference survives physically structured Hamiltonian dynamics beyond random circuit ensembles.

### Structure Constants of Planar N=4 SYM at Any Coupling
**Simon Ekhammar, Alessandro Georgoudis, Nikolay Gromov — arXiv:2611.21843**

Nonperturbative spectral data do not by themselves solve an interacting conformal theory: one also needs the coefficients determining how its operators interact. In planar maximally supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory, integrability has made the spectrum accessible much more broadly than these structure constants.

This paper converts a recent conjecture for a class of three-point functions into a numerically usable finite-coupling construction. The example couples two half-BPS operators to the spin-two member of the Konishi multiplet. All state-dependent ingredients are obtained from the quantum spectral curve. Determinant representations, a relation between previously independent building blocks, and a normalization algorithm make the conjectured expression evaluable between weak and strong coupling.

The resulting curve reproduces known perturbative and string-theory limits and satisfies available conformal-bootstrap bounds. The calculation also produces new six- and seven-loop coefficients and strong-coupling information from numerical fits. These checks support the conjectural framework underlying the construction; the paper does not derive a general formula for arbitrary interacting three-point functions.

**Background.** At weak coupling, Baxter functions encode Bethe rapidities as their zeros. The quantum spectral curve replaces this polynomial description with functions obeying analytic continuation and gluing conditions that encode finite-coupling dimensions and charges. Separation of variables suggests interpreting these functions as wavefunction building blocks whose overlaps become determinants of integrals. The paper develops this viewpoint sufficiently to compute the selected structure constants, including finite-size effects that invalidate a simple asymptotic magnon treatment.

**Why it matters?** This moves integrability beyond predicting energies toward calculating interaction data across the coupling range. The combination of explicit numerics and independent bootstrap constraints is especially persuasive as a method of testing nonperturbative proposals. Its broader importance depends on extending the construction to less restricted external states, where a fuller solution of the planar theory would become substantially closer.

### An infrared-safe $S$-matrix for $\mathcal N=4$ super Yang-Mills
**Maria Nocchi, Leonardo Rastelli — arXiv:2611.21713**

Massless gauge-theory amplitudes are simple enough to organize striking structures, yet infrared divergences obstruct treating them as exclusive nonperturbative observables. A useful replacement should remain well-defined at finite coupling and finite gauge-group rank while retaining a controlled relation to scattering.

Nocchi and Rastelli place the four-dimensional gauge theory on rigid AdS spacetime with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Boundary correlation functions then provide finite observables, including a flavor-current-multiplet four-point function corresponding to elementary two-to-two supergluon scattering. They compute this correlator through one loop: tree level follows from the microscopic theory, while AdS unitarity reconstructs the loop answer using lower-order conformal data.

In the large-Mellin-variable regime that yields flat-space scattering, the usual one-loop amplitude is recovered and its infrared divergences reappear as logarithms. The distinction between a nonperturbatively defined observable and the perturbative calculation performed here is essential: the definition works beyond the loop expansion, while its present evaluation is at weak coupling.

**Background.** Here AdS is the spacetime on which the gauge theory lives. Dirichlet boundary conditions turn the bulk gauge group into a global boundary symmetry with an associated conserved current. Its multiplet supplies boundary operators creating the relevant colored excitations. Mellin space organizes their correlators using variables analogous to scattering invariants. For this conformal bulk theory, the AdS radius enters when boundary data are converted to flat-space momenta; the finite correlator itself needs no infrared subtraction to exist.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual advance is a concrete exclusive observable connecting amplitude simplicity with nonperturbative conformal methods at finite rank. The one-loop calculation checks that connection rather than merely proposing a regulator. It opens a route toward bootstrap constraints on scattering-related dynamics, with boundary-condition dependence forming part of the physical definition.

### Localization identities for spectral determinants: two-dimensional QCD and Ising field theory
**Bercel Boldis, Nikolay Gromov, Gregory P. Korchemsky, Alessandro Testa — arXiv:2611.21724**

A spectral determinant packages an entire bound-state spectrum into one function, but this is only useful analytically if the determinant can be evaluated without solving every eigenfunction. In planar two-dimensional QCD and the meson problem of Ising field theory, powerful relations to Baxter functions had supplied that shortcut while remaining conjectural.

The authors prove identities reducing logarithmic derivatives of spectral determinants, and ratios between spectral sectors, to Baxter functions and their derivatives at special complex points. The mechanism combines finite-rank commutators with virial identities for the integral operators describing the bound states. They also identify a sufficient rational closure condition that extends the reduction and determines the required evaluation points.

Applications include a quartic gauge-field potential and operators interpolating between the QCD and Ising problems. In the Ising application, the meson equation uses the two-particle Bethe–Salpeter approximation; the exact identities solve the associated spectral problem within that scope.

**Background.** The determinant's zeros locate meson masses, and its expansion generates sums of inverse spectral powers. Its logarithmic derivative ordinarily requires a trace of the full resolvent, hence information along an integration contour. Baxter functions encode that resolvent through an auxiliary finite-difference equation. Finite-rank commutators constrain the infinite-dimensional operator problem through finitely many matrix elements; virial identities turn these constraints into spectral relations. The additional rationality conditions ensure this reduction closes. “Localization” here is the replacement of a contour calculation by special-point data, rather than spatial localization or supersymmetric localization.

**Why it matters?** This converts a successful conjectural tool into a proven mechanism and explains when it should generalize. The contribution is methodological: it exposes hidden integrable structure in confining spectral problems and makes systematic mass asymptotics and spectral-sum calculations more secure. Its transferability to other integral kernels is at least as interesting as the individual model applications.

### From nonlocal correlations to local symmetry breaking: Asymmetry dynamics of crosscap states
**Konstantinos Chalas, Pasquale Calabrese, Colin Rylands — arXiv:2611.23224**

Local symmetry restoration after a quench is familiar when the initial state stores symmetry breaking locally. What happens when that coherence is initially hidden in long-range entanglement, so that small subsystems appear symmetric?

The authors construct charge-deformed crosscap states from entangled fermion pairs at opposite points of a ring. Their initial entanglement has a Page-like profile, while subsystems smaller than half the system have vanishing entanglement asymmetry. Following a number-conserving free-fermion quench, this pattern can reverse: subsystem entropy decreases while asymmetry increases. A quasiparticle account of antipodal correlations quantitatively reproduces the behavior found in exact calculations.

**Background.** Each antipodal pair coherently combines an empty and a doubly occupied configuration. Tracing out one partner removes that coherence, leaving a charge-diagonal local state. A region containing a complete pair can access it. Entanglement asymmetry measures the entropy change caused by removing coherence between subsystem-charge sectors. During the quench, the balance between split and complete pairs changes: correlations initially crossing the subsystem boundary can become internal, reducing entanglement with the complement while exposing charge coherence. The globally prepared state already breaks the symmetry; symmetric evolution redistributes that existing resource.

**Why it matters?** This gives a clear solvable counterexample to treating local symmetry restoration as the default outcome of symmetric dynamics. The useful conceptual distinction is between possessing global coherence and making it locally accessible. That distinction should matter more broadly when interpreting quench diagnostics, nonlocal initial states, and protocols that convert shared correlations into local symmetry resources.

### CFTs with Large Virasoro Primary Gaps
**Ziming Ji, Jiahua Tian — arXiv:2611.23231**

Which gaps allowed by consistency constraints can actually occur in explicit two-dimensional CFTs? A particularly sharp question asks for a compact, unitary bosonic full theory with a unique vacuum and no nonvacuum Virasoro primary of dimension two or below. Such examples would also avoid relevant and marginal scalar deformations.

Ji and Tian construct infinite families with exact all-spin primary gaps three and four. They start from lattice CFTs whose lattice geometry excludes light charged states, then gauge carefully chosen finite symmetries to remove light oscillator primaries. Independent sign reversals of left- and right-moving currents eliminate a dimension-two current bilinear that survives a common action on both chiral sectors.

The authors verify anomaly cancellation and analyze both invariant untwisted states and every twisted sector. Explicit examples include gap three with equal left and right central charges 48, and gap four at central charges 128, alongside families extending to arbitrarily large central charge.

**Background.** An asymmetric orbifold gauges a symmetry acting differently on the two chiral halves. Projection onto invariant states removes unwanted operators, but gauging also introduces twisted sectors whose boundary conditions can generate new light states. A convincing gap construction must control both effects and show that the gauging has no anomaly. “Virasoro-primary gap” excludes descendants in the vacuum module: the stress tensor can remain at dimension two without violating the claimed gap.

**Why it matters?** These constructions turn an existence question into explicit conformal theories and provide useful benchmarks for modular-bootstrap bounds. The physical interest is the realization of isolated fixed points with no relevant or marginal scalar directions. The gap remains fixed as central charge grows, so these examples do not by themselves establish the much stronger spectral sparseness associated with a semiclassical gravitational description.

### Expressivity of two-dimensional Gaussian fermion tensor network states
**Pok Man Tam, William Musk, Zhehao Dai — arXiv:2611.23227**

An area law does not generally guarantee an efficient exact tensor-network representation in two dimensions. Establishing when entanglement genuinely controls representation cost therefore requires more structure than the area law alone.

For two classes of translation-invariant Gaussian fermion states—single-band paired states and half-filled two-band insulators, including semimetals—the authors prove an exact equivalence between a Rényi-0 area law and a finite-bond-dimension Gaussian fermion tensor network. The same condition is characterized by compactly supported Wannier-type orbitals and by momentum-space wavefunctions expressed through ratios of trigonometric polynomials. **Background.** The important bridge is exact compact support. An area-law bound on the reduced-state rank forces certain Gaussian modes to be fully occupied or empty within a finite region. These supply localized orbital relations. Finite real-space support becomes finite polynomial degree after Fourier transformation, and that algebraic structure permits an explicit tensor-network construction. Wannier-type orbitals need not have mutually orthogonal lattice translates; for paired states they can mix particles and holes. The condition is substantially stronger than the familiar von Neumann area law and concerns exact representation.

**Why it matters?** This identifies a nontrivial two-dimensional setting where entanglement, localized orbitals, and tensor-network expressivity can be connected quantitatively in both directions. It is a useful theoretical benchmark for quantum simulation methods and for understanding which structural features enable compression. Extending the result toward controlled approximation, additional bands, or interacting states would address the more general numerical questions.

### Simplifying models of noisy stabilizer QEC on leakage qubits by Pauli twirling
**Alex Kwiatkowski, Aaron J. Friedman — arXiv:2611.21928**

Leakage can persist between correction cycles and alter how later gates act. Consequently, a logical error rate inferred from one cycle need not determine performance after many cycles, even when the underlying component noise is Markovian.

The authors develop leakage-dephased Pauli twirling for physical systems with two computational levels and one leakage level. Twirled processes become Pauli maps conditioned on transitions between leakage patterns. Applying this structure to a stabilizer correction cycle produces a classical Markov process on the joint syndrome and leakage pattern, with logical Pauli maps attached to its transitions. Transfer matrices then give an exact expression for memory-experiment success probabilities within this model.

**Background.** Pauli randomization acts within the computational levels while leaving the leakage level unchanged. Separately dephasing between leakage patterns removes coherences that would obstruct a classical pattern description. Both operations are needed for the structural simplification. Once the enlarged syndrome–leakage state is retained, it acts as a hidden memory: discarding it can make the logical dynamics non-Markovian. Exactness applies to channels with the resulting twirled structure; applying the twirl only as a noise-model approximation does not establish equivalence to arbitrary coherent leakage dynamics.

**Why it matters?** This provides a principled connection between component-level leakage and repeated logical performance, rather than replacing persistent leakage by independent Pauli faults. The conceptual payoff is identifying which additional classical state restores a Markov description. It should be useful for logical characterization and statistical-mechanics-style transfer-matrix analyses, while the size of that state space still determines computational feasibility.

### QEDist: Practical Exact Distance Certification for Stabilizer Codes
**Jong Yeon Lee — arXiv:2611.21709**

Finding a logical operator establishes only an upper bound on code distance. Selecting a finite quantum code with confidence also requires excluding every lighter logical operator, a task that becomes difficult even when the parity checks are sparse.

QEDist performs this exclusion through a structured tree search. Candidate supports grow one qubit at a time, with rigorous bounds discarding branches that cannot be completed within the weight budget. A useful improvement incorporates already satisfied checks: repairing a violated check can disturb another, forcing additions that a bound based only on currently violated checks misses. Stabilizer equivalence, verified permutation symmetries, and lifted-product structure further reduce the search.

### Double-Bracket Quantum Algorithms for Preparing Multiple Excited States
**Salahuddin Abdul Rahman, Özkan Karabacak, Rafal Wisniewski — arXiv:2611.22774**

Preparing excited states requires controlling a whole low-energy subspace while preventing candidate states from collapsing onto the same ground state. Penalty methods and separately optimized circuits can make this orthogonality constraint expensive and sensitive to earlier preparation errors.

The proposed algorithm evolves an orthonormal ensemble with one shared unitary circuit. Its design follows a double-bracket flow of an ensemble operator, a weighted sum of projectors onto those states:

$$\dot{\Omega}=[[\Omega,\hat{H}],\Omega].$$

Here the dot denotes the designed flow time and the Hamiltonian determines the target energy ordering. The number of ensemble states sets the highest targeted excitation; the weights determine whether the flow resolves individual eigenstates, one selected excited state, or their joint low-energy subspace. **Background.** The nested commutator generates an energy-lowering flow while preserving the ensemble operator's eigenvalues. A group-commutator construction compiles the flow from Hamiltonian evolution and weighted phase operations. The recursive compilation repeatedly embeds the preceding circuit, making depth grow exponentially with the number of iterations.

**Why it matters?** The useful advance is a unified, ansatz-free treatment of several excited states with explicit convergence structure. It offers a clean connection between continuous matrix flows and quantum state preparation. ### Quantum Reference Frames, Locality, and Microcausality in Quantum Field Theory
**Christopher J. Fewster, Leon Loveridge — arXiv:2611.21768**

Local QFT observables are usually assigned to sharply specified spacetime regions. A physical quantum reference frame cannot provide that ideal localization for free, so it is necessary to ask which locality and causality statements survive when observables are defined relative to it.

Fewster and Loveridge impose the relativistic spectrum condition on translation reference frames. Their localization observables cannot be projection-valued, and broad classes cannot approach arbitrarily sharp localization even in a weaker sense. For a frame state with a Schwartz localization density, choosing that state turns a local observable into a rapidly decaying average over translations. The resulting almost local observables have spacelike commutators that decrease faster than every inverse power of separation.

Operations with finitely many almost local Kraus operators obey corresponding approximate no-signalling bounds. The paper leaves open the strongest operational question, involving all physically realizable operations, and also identifies the high-energy cost of increasingly accurate recovery of local observables.

**Background.** A quantum reference frame supplies a translation-covariant positive-operator-valued measure: localization probabilities are associated with positive effects rather than sharp spacetime projectors. Relative observables combine translations of the system observable with the frame's localization effects. After fixing the frame state, its probability density supplies the smearing kernel. A Schwartz localization density is smooth and rapidly decreasing, allowing approximation by observables in progressively larger bounded regions with errors smaller than any inverse power of their size. That approximation gives the commutator and disturbance bounds.

**Why it matters?** This makes the cost of ideal spacetime localization explicit within QFT rather than treating the frame as an external classical label. It also carefully separates nonzero commutator tails from unrestricted operational signalling claims. The broader contribution is a concrete framework for replacing exact locality by controlled relational bounds, connecting field-theory axioms to the physical resources used to define observables.
