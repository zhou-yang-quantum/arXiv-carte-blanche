## arXiv-2026-11-26 — Item 1

### Bound magic in multi-qubit systems
**Jonas Kitzinger, Jens Eisert, Ravi Kunjwal — arXiv:2611.34872**

A noisy state can lie outside the stabilizer polytope without it being obvious how to turn that departure into a clean computational resource. The decisive question is whether arbitrarily many copies, processed adaptively with stabilizer operations, eventually yield pure magic. In odd local dimensions, positivity of a discrete Wigner function supplies an obstruction. The corresponding argument has been missing for qubits.

The authors construct a family of sets from mixtures of outputs of PPT Pauli channels, meaning Pauli channels whose Choi states have positive partial transpose. They prove closure under tensor products and all stabilizer operations, including conditioning on measurement outcomes. On one qubit the set is exactly the stabilizer octahedron; on three qubits it already contains nonstabilizer states. Consequently, collective processing cannot extract a single-qubit magic state. Together with the reduction of any pure multiqubit magic state to single-qubit magic, this rules out distillation of any pure magic.

An explicit example applies a specially chosen Pauli channel to a superposition of the all-zero and all-plus product states. A magic witness establishes that its output is outside the stabilizer polytope, while the channel construction puts it inside the closed family. Appending stabilizer qubits extends the example to every larger system.

**Background.** The PPT condition here concerns the channel's input–output Choi state, obtained by applying the channel to half of a maximally entangled state. It is not a partial-transpose test on a chosen spatial division of the output. A PPT channel need not destroy all entanglement in higher dimensions, which leaves room for its output to retain nonstabilizerness. The crucial property is closure even under individual postselected branches: neither accumulating more copies nor retaining favorable measurement outcomes can escape the obstruction.

**Why it matters?** This supplies an asymptotic separation between possessing qubit magic and distilling it under the standard free operations. It sharpens the resource accounting behind fault-tolerant computation: a nonzero magic diagnostic alone does not certify a usable distillation supply. The construction also opens a substantive connection between noisy-channel geometry, bound entanglement, and the operational meaning of many-body magic.

## arXiv-2026-11-26 — Item 2

### Bound Entanglement with Negative Partial Transpose Exists
**Islam Faisal — arXiv:2611.34921**

Entanglement that cannot be distilled is well established for states with positive partial transpose. Whether a negative partial transpose always leaves some route to distillation has resisted attempts to control arbitrary numbers of copies. Testing a few tensor powers cannot settle the issue, because collective protocols may unlock a resource invisible at small copy number.

This paper presents an explicit proposed resolution using a Werner state of two eight-level systems,
$$\rho=\frac{23I-3\,\mathrm{SWAP}}{1448}.$$
Here SWAP exchanges the two systems. The state is positive and normalized, while its partial transpose has a negative eigenvalue. The author claims that every separable operation on any number of copies produces a two-qubit output whose Bell-state fidelity is at most approximately 0.604. A bound uniformly below one excludes asymptotically faithful distillation.

The argument rewrites the state using two local completely positive maps. An auxiliary matrix certificate establishes positivity relations that survive tensor powers; these are then used to bound output coherence by output populations. This is the essential step beyond finite-copy numerics. The paper provides rational certificate coefficients and a symmetry reduction to small matrix blocks. The finite certificate is intended to control every tensor power, rather than approximate an increasing sequence of distillation problems.

**Background.** A separable operation has product Kraus operators on the two parties and includes every protocol using local operations and classical communication. Thus an obstruction for separable operations also obstructs ordinary distillation. The Choi representation converts complete positivity of a map into positivity of a matrix. Complete copositivity instead means that composing the map with transposition is completely positive. The proof combines these properties to obtain an inequality valid at every copy number; positivity of the target state alone would not provide that control.

**Why it matters?** If the full argument withstands independent checking, it settles a central question about irreversible entanglement manipulation and implies failures of additivity and convexity of distillable entanglement. Its relevance extends beyond this particular state: the proposed mechanism is a tensor-stable obstruction to collective resource extraction. The decisive new ingredient is the all-copy coherence bound, rather than the easily checked negative eigenvalue of the partial transpose.

## arXiv-2026-11-26 — Item 3

### Theory of Anomalous Diffusion and Superdiffusion in Integrable Spin Chains
**Jitendra Kethepalli, Andrew Urilyon, Jacopo De Nardis — arXiv:2611.33274**

A spin packet can spread with the familiar diffusive or Kardar–Parisi–Zhang scaling while its transferred-charge distribution disagrees with the statistics normally associated with that universality class. Explaining this requires more than extracting a dynamical exponent: a two-point function and the full distribution of integrated current probe different aspects of fluctuating quasiparticle motion.

The authors study a multispecies soliton gas that retains the scattering shifts of the XXZ chain. In the easy-axis regime, $\Delta>1$, large, nearly immobile particles are displaced by a mobile bath. Conditional on those displacements, the current distribution is Gaussian, but its variance fluctuates from realization to realization. Averaging therefore produces a non-Gaussian mixture, with finite-time rounding from incompletely screened mobile species.

At the isotropic point, $\Delta=1$, there is no immobile terminal species. Large particles remain mobile, and their discrete charge-transfer events invalidate the Gaussian approximation. A self-consistent Poisson description instead gives a nearly Gaussian center with nonuniversal tails. Meanwhile, the spin two-point function is controlled by the position distribution of a large tracer. Its coupling to arbitrarily slow species generates long-range velocity fluctuations and the superdiffusive scale $x\sim t^{2/3}$. Fluctuating-hydrodynamic calculations support the full KPZ scaling function; the paper does not establish that identification rigorously.

**Background.** Soliton-gas collisions preserve particle identities and velocities but shift trajectories, encoding the displacement accumulated in integrable quasiparticle scattering. “Dressing” accounts for the bath's collective response to a particle, so its effective charge and velocity differ from their bare values. Large spin bound states screen the mobile species' charge contribution. Current counting follows charge crossing a fixed cut, whereas a tracer distribution follows the wandering of a particular collective carrier; those need not have the same limiting statistics.

**Why it matters?** The contribution explains how universality of spreading can coexist with nonuniversality of current fluctuations. It offers a physical framework for going beyond average hydrodynamics in exactly solvable systems, while identifying where discreteness and screening enter. The distinction is useful whenever a transport exponent is being used to infer a much stronger stochastic description.

## arXiv-2026-11-26 — Item 4

### The OPE of lines beyond CFT
**Jan Albert, Matthew Forslund, Alexandre Homrich — arXiv:2611.33272**

The potential between two static probes samples a quantum field theory at the scale of their separation. At short distances a UV fixed point should constrain it, but a massive theory still retains long-distance information in the state surrounding the probes. The problem is to separate the calculable short-distance dependence from that nonperturbative information.

The authors propose a fusion expansion for parallel line defects in relevant deformations of a CFT. Bringing the lines together produces a fused defect equipped with a local effective action. Its Wilson coefficients can be calculated in conformal perturbation theory around the UV theory, while correlation functions and condensates on the fused defect retain the infrared dependence. This organizes the short-separation expansion without requiring the full massive problem to be perturbatively soluble.

The proposal is checked in solvable scalar and gauge-theory examples and, more substantially, for magnetic impurities in the two-dimensional Ising model. Density-matrix renormalization group calculations of the impurity potential agree with the expansion; integrability supplies independent defect-condensate information. The resulting separation of coefficients and condensates applies to correlators with additional distant insertions as well as to the static potential. Its regime is asymptotically small separation compared with the scales generated by the deformations.

**Background.** A line defect is an extended modification of the theory, such as a probe worldline or an impurity acting throughout time. Its fusion cannot generally be represented by copying the ordinary local-operator OPE as a scalar-weighted sum of lines. Instead, corrections accumulate locally along the fused line and exponentiate into an action. This preserves path-integral factorization when the line is cut into segments. The condensates are expectation values of operators living on that fused defect; their values are dynamical data, rather than additional UV matching coefficients.

**Why it matters?** This is a reusable QFT framework connecting UV conformal data to observables in massive theories. The combination of perturbative matching, exact integrable information, and numerical tests makes the proposal more concrete than a formal fusion rule. It suggests ways to extract defect condensates from short-distance measurements, or to predict probe interactions once those condensates are known.

### Using a static spacetime subsystem code to calculate Floquet code distance
**Ethan Egger, Milad Marvian — arXiv:2611.33337**

The robustness of a Floquet code depends on the order of its measurements. A Pauli fault can be harmless at one time and logically damaging at another, so distances inferred from instantaneous code spaces need not capture the schedule's actual protection. Existing mappings to static spacetime codes do not automatically preserve the relevant distance.

The authors construct a modified subsystem code on copies of the data qubits at successive times. Gauge generators represent elementary harmless changes to the fault history: a measured Pauli inserted at its measurement, or paired Pauli faults on opposite sides of a measurement with which they commute. Additional generators join the time boundaries. This removes artificial boundary constraints and makes the correspondence between circuit detectors and static stabilizers work in both directions.

They prove equality between the Floquet code's spacetime distance and the subsystem code's distance. The construction also preserves the number of logical qubits and geometric locality, with time supplying one extra dimension. A useful corollary is that reversing the measurement sequence preserves distance. The result concerns the stated spacetime Pauli-fault distance; it does not by itself compute a stochastic threshold or determine the performance of a decoder.

**Background.** In this mapping, multiplying by a gauge operator changes the representation of a fault history without changing its logical effect. Stabilizers test whether a history violates any detector, while nontrivial dressed logical operators represent undetectable histories modulo those harmless changes. A finite circuit cut normally leaves some checks “open”: the static code can impose constraints corresponding to measurements beyond the cut. Joining the temporal boundaries supplies the missing closure algebraically, rather than adding new operations to the physical schedule.

**Why it matters?** This turns a genuinely dynamical error-correction question into an exact static subsystem-code problem. The gain is a reliable algebraic language for comparing measurement schedules, not a general efficient algorithm for finding distance. It should help distinguish improvements due to time ordering from protection already visible in a static stabilizer description.

## arXiv-2026-11-26 — Item 6

### Chiral Domain-Wall Modes in Topological Adaptive Quantum Dynamics
**Asadullah Bhuiyan, Haining Pan, Chao-Ming Jian — arXiv:2611.33328**

Repeated measurements tend to remove long-range quantum correlations. Can a topological mismatch nevertheless force a critical channel to survive along an interface in monitored dynamics? The interesting case is one where the protocol prepares the neighboring bulk phases but never specifies an interface state.

The authors build charge-conserving free-fermion adaptive circuits targeting a Chern insulator in a central slab and a trivial insulator outside. They repeatedly measure occupations of localized modes and correct unwanted outcomes using ancillas. Although the two target prescriptions become incompatible near the walls, individual trajectories develop chiral interface modes. Their logarithmic entanglement and charge fluctuations, together with algebraically decaying correlations, are consistent with a chiral free-fermion CFT ground state.

Starting from a maximally mixed state, entropy becomes concentrated near the walls and decays algebraically over the simulated window. The bulk purifies rapidly, whereas the wall's Lyapunov gap closes with its length. This behavior is conditional on the measurement record: the density matrix averaged over outcomes relaxes at a finite rate and has no corresponding dissipation-free interface mode. The CFT identification is supported by several numerical diagnostics, rather than derived as an exact continuum limit.

**Background.** Feedforward makes the measurements corrective: an undesired occupation is followed by a unitary exchange with a fresh ancilla, removing an excitation relative to the local target. The authors interpret the bulk as a cooling environment for the wall. Excitations near the interface can propagate into either neighboring region and be removed, indirectly cooling a state that was never explicitly targeted. A Lyapunov gap measures the separation between asymptotic growth or decay rates of the trajectory evolution; its closing diagnoses slow purification. Averaging trajectories discards the record and can conceal this conditional structure.

**Why it matters?** This provides a concrete mechanism by which topology organizes critical quantum information in adaptive dynamics despite Born-rule randomness. It extends the connection between topological boundaries and monitored phases beyond localized zero-dimensional modes. For quantum simulation and error correction, the central lesson is that protection visible in conditioned trajectories must be assessed separately from protection in the unconditioned channel.

## arXiv-2026-11-26 — Item 7

### Optimal Controlled Quantum Instrument for Monitored Many-Body Dynamics
**Jimin Li — arXiv:2611.33472**

Rare measurement histories can reveal dynamical phases that ordinary experiments almost never sample. Postselection pays an exponential repetition cost, but replacing it by physical control becomes subtle when the desired bias depends nonlinearly on the evolving quantum state. A state-dependent rescaling of jump probabilities need not define a linear quantum instrument.

The paper resolves this for biases admitting a suitable finite partition of the reachable states. Within each cell the bias is constant, no-jump evolution stays in the cell, and each measurement outcome sends it to a definite next cell. An additional register tracks that label. The enlarged system supports a linear tensor-network description of the biased record, whose normalization yields a physical controlled instrument.

When the lifted transfer map has the required positive leading eigenoperator, the construction generates the desired long-time biased record statistics, with the standard endpoint reweighting, at optimal rates. In the general construction the physical conditional states are transformed by a positive operator, so reproducing the record ensemble does not mean leaving the original states unchanged. Examples include a precessing qubit and fermions with gain and loss biased by particle number. An entropy-biased measured Majorana chain is also discussed, but the proposed entanglement-transition analysis requires an additional spectral assumption.

**Background.** A quantum instrument is an outcome-resolved collection of completely positive maps whose sum preserves trace. The extra register makes the bias computable from a finite memory of the record, avoiding the need to apply an arbitrary nonlinear function to an unknown state. In tensor-network language, putting the record state into left canonical form enforces the normalization of the instrument. Operationally this is a generalized Doob construction: it changes the dynamics so that histories selected by a statistical bias become typical. Optimality refers to a variational cost involving the relative entropy of record probabilities.

**Why it matters?** This gives a constructive bridge from biased-trajectory statistical mechanics to realizable monitored dynamics. Its useful advance is a precise class of nonlinear state observables for which an enlarged instrument exists. Extending the method into a scalable experimental recipe would require manageable registers and control operators; the existence construction alone does not guarantee those resource costs.

### Vacuum correlation measures as CFT partition functions
**Hewei Frederic Jia — arXiv:2611.34922**

Vacuum mutual information between separated regions is difficult to calculate even when the underlying CFT is well understood. Replacing its entropies by Rényi entropies gives accessible replica quantities, but the resulting entropy difference loses important information-theoretic properties. Sandwiched Rényi relative entropy preserves data processing, at the price of negative fractional powers of the marginal density matrices that obstruct the usual replica geometry.

For two disjoint balls, the author reorganizes the joint density matrix by radial sewing. Half-powers of the single-ball density matrices absorb those inverse powers, leaving positive fractional powers with a geometric modular-flow interpretation. Contracting the intermediate-state sums glues punctured spheres through cylinders, turning integer-order sandwiched Rényi mutual information into a vacuum-normalized CFT partition function.

At $n=2$, the sewn geometry is a thermal circle times a spatial sphere. The corresponding thermal partition function gives an upper bound on ordinary mutual information. For $n>2$, a thermal cycle shrinks at a finite separation of the original balls, producing a divergence before the regions touch. Explicit CFT evaluations are checked against density-matrix calculations. This divergence concerns the higher-order measure, not an assertion that ordinary mutual information becomes infinite at that separation.

**Background.** Vacuum modular flow for a ball is unusually tractable: the evolution generated by the logarithm of its reduced density matrix is a conformal transformation, and becomes a rotation after Euclidean continuation. Fractional density-matrix powers therefore specify geometric rotation angles. Sewing means summing a complete set of intermediate states to join path integrals along matching cuts. Here it converts an awkward operator product into a smooth manifold with handles. At higher replica order, the “hot spot” is a local infinite-temperature limit of that manifold, explaining why a separated-region measure can diverge.

**Why it matters?** The paper makes a proper information-theoretic correlation measure accessible through standard CFT partition functions and exposes a sharp difference between Rényi orders. It offers a useful bridge between continuum entanglement, spectral data, and lattice calculations. The finite-separation singularity also identifies a concrete limitation of using high-order correlation measures as interchangeable substitutes for mutual information.

### Gaussian Is All You Need: Renyi Optimizers and Optical Channel Simulation
**Yuan Gao, Gerardo Adesso — arXiv:2611.33955**

Knowing the asymptotic entanglement cost of a Gaussian state does not say how accurately it can be prepared with fewer resources. That question requires optimizing Rényi versions of entanglement of formation over all pure-state decompositions. A Gaussian target need not, merely by symmetry or convenience, have a Gaussian optimum.

The authors prove Gaussian optimality and additivity for finite-energy multimode Gaussian states at Rényi orders above one. The optimal ensemble consists of correlated local displacements of a pure Gaussian seed, whose choice can depend on the Rényi order. Their argument supplies a positive Gaussian operator that bounds a Schmidt-spectrum norm for arbitrary pure states; averaging the bound over any decomposition shows that non-Gaussian alternatives cannot improve it.

Consequences include a Gaussian closest separable state in fidelity and an exact strong-converse exponent for entanglement dilution below cost. A related norm theorem establishes Gaussian optimizers for the unconstrained minimum output Rényi entropy of arbitrary finite-mode Gaussian channels at orders at least one. For pure-loss and quantum-limited amplifier channels, the paper also determines finite-resource simulation fidelity for any number of uses, including adaptive tests.

**Background.** A seed covariance sits below the target covariance: the missing covariance can be supplied by classically correlated random displacements performed locally by the two parties. Every member of this ensemble has the seed's entanglement, since displacements are local unitaries. The nontrivial issue is proving optimality against every other decomposition and against decompositions correlating multiple copies. A strong-converse exponent quantifies how rapidly preparation fidelity vanishes when the entanglement rate is below cost. Gaussian optimality is task-specific: the optimal finite-Schmidt-rank optical simulation resource constructed here is generally non-Gaussian.

**Why it matters?** This converts several difficult bosonic resource optimizations into covariance-based problems and gives an operational account of limited-resource performance. The reach beyond independent, phase-insensitive optical modes makes the theorem scientifically substantial. It is relevant to quantum simulation and information processing with bosonic degrees of freedom, while the non-Gaussian simulation resource illustrates why the title should not be read as a universal prescription for optimal control.

### Controlling truncation errors in loop tensor network renormalization
**Rikuto Fushio, Kokin Cho, Yuta Yamamoto, Akira Otsuki, Masahito Yamazaki — arXiv:2611.33807**

A finite-bond-dimension tensor calculation can leave a critical fixed point and develop a gap. Increasing the bond dimension may postpone the failure, but it does not identify which physical perturbation the truncation has introduced. This paper asks whether that perturbation can instead be measured, predicted, and canceled.

For symmetry-preserving LoopTNR on the six-vertex critical line, the authors identify a small injected dimerization as the relevant error. Its early-flow amplitude predicts the eventual correlation length through the sine-Gordon mass–coupling relation, including the normalization rather than just a scaling exponent. The tested correlation lengths span thirteen orders of magnitude. Their irregular dependence on bond dimension follows the fluctuating seed amplitude, explaining why a larger bond dimension need not give a longer critical window.

An alternating microscopic counterterm cancels the seed. A twisted-boundary readout determines its sign, enabling short calibration runs to predict useful compensation. Repeated corrections to coarse-grained tensors keep the tested flows within the paper's spectral criticality criterion through the last computed step, 160, while revealing residual marginal drift. These are controlled results for the specified model, symmetry constraints, and algorithm; the paper does not establish a universal truncation law.

**Background.** The continuum dimerization operator has dimension $K$, the Luttinger parameter, and is relevant for $K<2$. It alternates neighboring bonds. Although forbidden by the original one-site translation, it is allowed by the enforced internal symmetry and by the algorithm's two-tensor unit cell. A tiny seed therefore grows under coarse graining and eventually creates a mass. Ordinary spectral shifts are quadratic in the seed and hide its sign. Twisted boundary conditions produce a level splitting linear in it, turning the continuum identification into a practical counterterm prescription.

**Why it matters?** The methodological advance is treating numerical truncation as a specific renormalization-group perturbation that can be diagnosed and compensated. This is valuable for extracting CFT and integrable-field-theory data over long scale ranges. Applying the strategy elsewhere would require identifying the algorithm's allowed relevant operators and finding observables that resolve their couplings.

### Monotonic probes of asymmetry and robustness of the quantum Mpemba effect
**Riccardo Travaglino, Michele Mazzoni, Pasquale Calabrese — arXiv:2611.34853**

A state initially farther from symmetry can sometimes restore it faster than a less asymmetric state: the quantum Mpemba effect. But this comparison depends on what measures “farther.” Conventional Rényi entanglement asymmetries are convenient to calculate yet can increase under symmetry-preserving operations, raising the possibility that some apparent relaxation behavior reflects the diagnostic.

The authors replace entropy differences by sandwiched Rényi divergences between a state and its symmetrized version. These are genuine asymmetry monotones. A two-parameter replica construction relates their calculation to charged moments and establishes an index inversion, $\alpha\leftrightarrow1/\alpha$, connecting them to conventional Rényi asymmetries. The relation is exact for pure states and holds at leading order in the relevant scaling limits for homogeneous quenches, including interacting integrable systems, and for Haar-random states.

For quenches from tilted ferromagnetic states, the Mpemba crossing survives throughout the family of monotones. Spatially inhomogeneous preparations provide a more discriminating test: the entropy differences and the monotones can evolve qualitatively differently. A solvable model also shows that crossing patterns can depend on Rényi order. Robustness in the homogeneous protocols therefore does not imply independence from every choice of measure or initial state.

**Background.** Symmetrization removes coherences between different charge sectors by averaging symmetry rotations. Charged moments are replicated density-matrix traces with such rotations inserted; their angle dependence resolves the charge content of the replicas. The new construction keeps separate replica parameters for the state's power and the surrounding symmetrized-state powers before analytic continuation. For pure states, the index inversion expresses different weightings of the same charge probabilities: one order emphasizes the distribution's bulk while its reciprocal probes the tail more strongly.

**Why it matters?** This strengthens the interpretation of a widely studied nonequilibrium effect by checking it against quantities with an operational monotonicity property. It also supplies counterexamples to treating all asymmetry diagnostics as equivalent. The useful connection to integrable dynamics is methodological: charged-moment calculations can address physically justified resource measures, rather than only convenient entropy differences.

### Planckian bounds on thermalization times of strongly coupled open quantum systems
**Jesús Jiménez-Rodríguez, John Calsamiglia, Martí Perarnau-Llobet — arXiv:2611.34401**

The scale $\hbar/(k_B T)$ is often invoked as a minimum thermalization time, but strong coupling changes both the dynamics and the equilibrium state being approached. A useful bound must explain when that scale survives, rather than assume that the reduced system still thermalizes to its bare Gibbs state.

The authors formulate thermalization as an information-acquisition task. The bath, interaction, and initial joint state are held fixed while the system Hamiltonian varies in a nearby family. The same mechanism must prepare the appropriate reduced equilibrium state for every member. If those targets are distinguishable, the dynamics needs time to acquire the Hamiltonian information distinguishing them. Bounds on parameter discrimination then give a lower bound on thermalization time from equilibrium properties alone.

In a local limit the relevant sensitivity is quantum Fisher information, which can be obtained through static susceptibilities and equilibrium variances of system observables. Exactly soluble fermionic examples, the spin-boson model, and an antiferromagnetic environment illustrate two outcomes. Strong coupling can erase equilibrium sensitivity to the system Hamiltonian and remove a Planckian constraint, or leave that information in sufficiently low-frequency fluctuations and preserve it. The result is conditional on robust thermalization across the family, not a bound for preparing one preselected state.

**Background.** The mean-force Gibbs state is the system marginal of the interacting system–bath Gibbs state. It includes the effect of correlations and interaction energy, so it can differ strongly from the Gibbs state of the isolated system Hamiltonian. A response measurement asks how this marginal changes when that Hamiltonian is perturbed. Dividing an observable's squared susceptibility by its variance gives accessible information about the perturbation. The equilibrium fluctuation spectrum determines how much of that information lies within the thermal frequency window.

**Why it matters?** This gives a precise criterion for when Planckian reasoning constrains strongly coupled relaxation. Its advantage is that equilibrium calculations can bound dynamics that are otherwise difficult to simulate. The distinction between acquiring Hamiltonian information and reaching one fixed target offers a productive direction for open-system thermalization and quantum speed-limit theory.

### Identifying Renormalization Group Interfaces of Massless WZW Flows $\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(2)_k \to \widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(2)_1$
**Yuma Furuta, Yuya Kusuki, Toshiki Onagi, Yin Tang — arXiv:2611.33354**

Different half-integer-spin chains can flow to the same infrared CFT, so identifying that endpoint loses much of the information about how the flow occurred. An interface between ultraviolet and infrared theories can retain this information in its operator spectrum. Constructing the interface is usually harder than identifying either bulk fixed point.

For the conjectured massless Wess–Zumino–Witten flows in the title, the authors combine global spin symmetry and the center symmetry to identify the interface up to topological dressing. After folding, the two sides contribute a diagonal current algebra at level $k+1$. Center invariance fixes its representation label to $\rho_*=(k+1)/2$ for odd $k\geq3$. This predicts different low-lying interface spectra for different ultraviolet levels despite their common infrared endpoint.

Spin-chain calculations test the predictions at levels three and five. The spectra support the proposed interface families and distinguish them from several alternatives. The all-level statement remains an identification for the conjectured flows under the stated symmetry structure; the lattice checks do not establish every flow or uniquely determine every possible dressing.

**Background.** Folding reflects one side of an interface, replacing a junction between two CFTs by a boundary condition for their product. Preserving a diagonal current means matching the sum of the two current algebras at that boundary; their levels add. Boundary representation labels then organize interface excitations. The center is the order-two transformation that flips the WZW matrix field's sign; invariance selects the middle label exchanged by this action. A topological dressing is obtained by fusing a deformable symmetry line onto the interface. It changes interface data without changing the two bulk theories, explaining why symmetry can fix a family while leaving a residual choice requiring dynamical evidence.

**Why it matters?** The paper turns a qualitative statement about common infrared behavior into quantitative, ultraviolet-sensitive spectral tests. It connects nonabelian CFT structure to spin-chain numerics without requiring a complete microscopic solution of the flow. The broader attraction is a method for recovering information about renormalization trajectories that ordinary endpoint diagnostics discard.

### Transition Path Sampling for Rare Events in Non-Markovian Open Quantum Systems
**Ritesh Pant, Kai Müller, Tarun Gera, Doran I. G. B. Raccah — arXiv:2611.34883**

The lifetime of a metastable quantum state may be governed by transitions so rare that almost every simulated trajectory is uninformative. Bath memory makes the sampling problem harder: changing one noise value while ignoring its correlations changes the physical ensemble. Rare-event methods developed for Markovian dynamics therefore need a different construction.

The authors formulate transition path sampling for non-Markovian quantum state diffusion with Gaussian baths. They express the correlated bath noise as a linear transformation of independent Gaussian variables. Monte Carlo proposals replace subsets of those independent variables, regenerate the full correlated noise, and evolve the associated trajectory. Conditioning acceptance on the desired transition samples reactive paths with the correct measure. Overlapping endpoint windows then recover absolute probabilities, rather than only a collection of successful trajectories.

For normal-to-superradiant transitions in the unbalanced open Dicke model, the method agrees with brute-force sampling where the latter has statistics and reaches probabilities of order $10^{-8}$. At 500 atoms, a million brute-force trajectories produce no reactive event, while the path calculation resolves the transition probability and supplies the information needed to extract a rate. The underlying Gaussian-bath representation is formally exact; practical calculations still require convergence of the numerical hierarchy.

**Background.** The Monte Carlo chain runs through entire noise histories, not through the physical system's instantaneous states. It can therefore be Markovian as a sampling algorithm while faithfully representing non-Markovian physical evolution. Quantum state diffusion represents the reduced open-system dynamics by stochastic pure-state trajectories. A hierarchy of auxiliary states carries the bath memory during each propagation. Sampling the independent noise variables preserves that memory automatically, because the correlated physical noise is reconstructed after every proposal.

**Why it matters?** This extends a useful rare-event tool into strongly coupled quantum dynamics with memory. It makes metastable lifetimes accessible without extrapolating exclusively from smaller systems where switching is common. The demonstrated advantage concerns sampling rare trajectories; its application to a new problem must retain the corresponding bath model, trajectory interpretation, and basin definition.

### Neural Network Decoders For Qubit Loss
**J. Wilson Staples, Winston Fu, Yihe Dong, Jeff D. Thompson — arXiv:2611.34891**

A lost qubit changes the subsequent circuit by missing gates until it is replaced. A later measurement may flag that loss without revealing its exact time. This creates both a decoding complication and useful side information. Extending the benefit beyond surface codes has been limited partly by the cost of generating realistic loss-affected training and benchmark data.

The authors introduce the *slim* sampler, extending fast Pauli-frame sampling to persistent loss for a specified class of circuits. Its output trains convolutional neural decoders that use both measurement results and loss heralds. Surface-code tests approach the accuracy of expensive most-likely-error benchmarks. For the $[[144,12,12]]$ bivariate bicycle code, convolutions are adapted to the code's check–qubit graph and neighboring rounds.

The code uses a walking extraction circuit: reversing selected final CNOTs exchanges data and ancilla roles, so every physical qubit is measured and reset every other cycle. This limits the lifetime of persistent loss without extra qubits. For physical rates around 0.7–1% in the tested pure-loss limit, logical error rates are over a thousand times lower than for pure Pauli noise at the same physical rate. The comparison is model- and circuit-specific: the walking circuit has Pauli fault distance ten despite the underlying code's nominal distance twelve, and the reported memory tests measure logical Z observables.

**Background.** The sampler relies on a zero-reference condition: an all-zero measurement record must remain an allowed noiseless reference even when arbitrary entangling gates are skipped. This lets random frame bits and loss indicators propagate through Boolean update rules without recomputing an expensive stabilizer reference for each loss configuration. The herald is delayed evidence that a qubit was lost during its lifetime, rather than an exact gate-resolved erasure location. Feeding that information directly to the network allows it to infer correlations that a fixed decoding graph would miss.

**Why it matters?** The contribution combines a simulation tool with evidence that heralded loss can remain advantageous in a high-rate code under persistent, delayed-detection noise. It enables more realistic comparisons between extraction circuits and decoders. The next substantive question is how well that advantage survives the full mixture of unheralded faults, imperfect loss detection, and logical operations required by an architecture.
