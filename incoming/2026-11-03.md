### Establishing N\'eel order in the square-lattice antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model: a bootstrap proof
**Nisarg Chadha, Taige Wang, Pavel A. Nosov, Michael G. Scheer, Eslam Khalaf — arXiv:2611.03241**

The square-lattice spin-one-half Heisenberg antiferromagnet is a striking case where overwhelming evidence for an ordered ground state has outpaced proof. Finite-size scaling and accurate variational states do not themselves certify spontaneous symmetry breaking in the infinite system.

The authors combine infrared bounds with a Hamiltonian bootstrap to close this gap at zero temperature. Weighted momentum-space sum rules relate staggered order to finite-distance correlations; an infrared susceptibility bound limits how much spectral weight can remain outside the ordering wavevector. Positivity, stationarity, and ground-state consistency constrain the remaining local correlations through semidefinite optimization. Certified tensor-network energy ceilings tighten these constraints. Converting numerical output into exact rational certificates yields a strictly positive order-parameter bound at the isotropic point and several anisotropies.

**Background.** The bootstrap searches over locally consistent correlation data rather than wavefunctions. Its feasible set contains the actual ground state, so minimizing an order-parameter lower bound over this larger set remains rigorous. The infrared input supplies the missing control over arbitrarily long wavelengths; local positivity alone would not certify the ordered phase.

**Why it matters?** This settles a longstanding foundational question while providing a useful model for computer-assisted many-body proofs. The broader opportunity is to turn accurate numerical knowledge into certified statements about phases. Extending the method requires an appropriate infrared bound, so its immediate reach is clearest for models with reflection positivity.

## arXiv-2026-11-03 — Item 2

### Mutual information to all orders from modular flow: a replica-free approach
**Hewei Frederic Jia — arXiv:2611.03630**

Mutual information between separated regions is finite and physically informative, yet calculating it in an interacting CFT usually confronts an awkward analytic continuation in replica number. This paper develops a direct long-distance expansion for two vacuum balls without that continuation.

The construction cuts the Euclidean preparation of the joint density matrix into two caps and an intervening annulus. Inserting dilatation eigenstates across the annulus gives a radial sewing expansion: the identity produces the product of the individual reduced states, while other states supply their correlations. A modular response expansion of relative entropy then turns these corrections into integrals of ordinary vacuum multipoint correlators. The entire two-point sector resums into a modular integral of the sphere thermal partition function. Ten radial orders in Ising CFT, involving up to twenty spin insertions, agree well with lattice data; free theories provide analytic checks.

**Background.** Modular flow evolves operators using the logarithm of a reduced state rather than the physical Hamiltonian. For a vacuum ball it is a known geometric conformal transformation. This makes the abstract response formula calculable after sewing, replacing replica combinatorics with correlators in the original theory. General convergence of the resulting all-orders series remains open.

**Why it matters?** The main gain is a systematic bridge from CFT data to a nonlinear information quantity beyond its leading exchange contribution. For QFT and entanglement, this is a substantial methodological advance. Controlled resummation would be needed to turn the long-distance framework into a general finite-separation calculator.

### Enforced Gaplessness by Combining Non-Anomalous Symmetries
**Takamasa Ando, Kantaro Ohmori — arXiv:2611.03164**

Can two symmetries that separately permit trivial gapped phases nevertheless force a system to remain gapless when imposed together? The obstacle is to constrain their combined action without first identifying the potentially complicated symmetry algebra they generate.

The authors use a symmetry span: two larger symmetry groups share a subgroup, but embed its symmetry operators differently. A relative symmetry-protected topological twist makes the gapped phases permitted by the two embeddings incompatible. Their one-dimensional example combines a finite group with two continuous charge symmetries. They construct continuum realizations and a local lattice Hamiltonian with finite-dimensional sites, and exclude gapped phases with finitely many ground states, including symmetry-breaking possibilities. The general argument concerns phases admitting a topological-field-theory description; that qualification becomes important in higher dimensions.

**Background.** An embedding specifies more than which group elements coincide: it also identifies the phases at junctions of symmetry defects. An SPT twist changes this junction data, effectively attaching a subgroup SPT response when a larger symmetry is restricted. The two restrictions can therefore demand different topological data for the same subgroup. Continuous-symmetry breaking would supply gapless modes, while the remaining finite-symmetry breaking patterns also fail the compatibility test.

**Why it matters?** This provides a practical route to robust criticality from manageable pieces of ultraviolet symmetry data. The conceptual advance is that anomaly-free components can hide an obstruction in their relative embedding. It broadens symmetry-based reasoning about many-body phases without requiring a direct solution of their strongly coupled dynamics.

### Exhaustive Construction of Generalized Parent Hamiltonians for Matrix Product States
**He-Ran Wang, Lorenzo Gotta, Sanjay Moudgalya — arXiv:2611.00203**

Designing a Hamiltonian around a desired many-body state is useful for exact models and nonthermal eigenstates. Conventional parent Hamiltonians impose a stronger condition than necessary: each local Hermitian term preserves the state individually, whereas unwanted local actions might cancel in their sum.

This paper exhaustively characterizes that additional freedom for translation-invariant injective and block-injective matrix product states with periodic boundaries. Neighboring differences of local, generally non-Hermitian operators telescope away, allowing a globally Hermitian Hamiltonian to preserve the target state through cancellations. A linear tensor equation constructs the allowed terms. After removing conventional parent contributions and other redundancies, inequivalent constructions are governed by the commutant of the MPS transfer matrix. Examples connect this freedom with non-Abelian symmetry and vanishing correlation length.

**Background.** Injectivity means that products of sufficiently many MPS tensors span the full algebra on the virtual bonds. It lets a global eigenstate condition become a complete local tensor constraint. The transfer-matrix commutant consists of maps that commute with the transfer matrix; its structure counts surviving cancellation mechanisms. Block-injective states retain several such sectors, allowing symmetry-breaking examples.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a classification tool rather than another isolated scar construction. It makes the space of compatible interactions accessible from the target state's local tensors. For exactly solvable models and non-equilibrium physics, this offers a systematic way to ask which nonthermal states can be embedded in otherwise unconstrained spectra.

### Lieb-Robinson Light Cones in Long-Range Bosonic Systems
**Carla Rubiliani, Cheng Shang, Tomotaka Kuwahara — arXiv:2611.00776**

Long-range couplings and unbounded bosonic occupation each weaken familiar locality arguments. Their combination raises a sharper question: how fast can information propagate if the initial density is controlled but the dynamics can concentrate particles?

The authors obtain state-dependent propagation bounds and construct protocols that attain the leading light-cone power in a substantial parameter regime. Bosons are gathered into blocks, creating highly occupied mediating sites whose nonlinear interactions are amplified. This effective sublattice supports rapid information transfer. For spatial dimension $d$, coupling-decay exponent $\alpha$, and integer density-interaction degree $p$, the matching regime is $p\geq4$ and $2d<\alpha\leq2d+1$. There the leading cone $R\sim t^{\beta_{\rm opt}}$ has
$$\beta_{\rm opt}=1+\frac{pd-\alpha+1}{\alpha-2d}.$$
The endpoint upper bound allows an arbitrarily small exponent loss.

**Background.** The degree $p$ specifies how interaction energy grows with occupation, rather than how many sites a term touches. Initial local particle-number moment bounds limit the available amplification without imposing a permanent occupation cutoff. Information propagation also differs from particle transport: a relay can transmit a local distinction without moving the original particles all the way to the receiver.

**Why it matters?** The matching construction identifies a physical mechanism behind a sharp worst-case speed limit. This is useful for understanding locality and simulation errors in bosonic dynamics. It also cautions against importing spin-system light cones using only the spatial decay exponent: allowed nonlinear amplification is an equally important input.

Local rotational invariance within narrow energy windows fixes their index structures and entropy scaling, while additional energy-dependent functions carry independent dynamical information. Higher-order free cumulants separate this irreducible multi-trace data from contributions assembled out of simpler trace statistics.

**Why it matters?** This expands the statistical description of quantum chaos to observables sensitive to correlated late-time fluctuations. The connection between many-body operator statistics and gravitational amplitudes is substantive rather than merely analogical. The proposed framework still requires dynamical input: rotational invariance constrains the form of its data, not their values in every chaotic Hamiltonian.

For translation-invariant nearest-neighbor spin-one-half chains, the authors classify twelve regular integrable families and numerically minimize a normalized Frobenius distance to their parameterizations and local similarity transforms. **Why it matters?** The useful shift is from following one deformation to surveying the surrounding integrable landscape. It could help organize comparisons of weakly nonintegrable models and identify unexpected solvable neighbors. ### Exponential suppression of bit-flip errors in dissipative cat qubits
**Ruikang Liang, Rémi Robin — arXiv:2611.01296**

Dissipative cat qubits promise strongly biased noise, but a low-order perturbative calculation cannot exclude higher-order terms that spoil exponential bit-flip suppression at large cat size. This paper addresses that gap for the full oscillator generator.

The model is
$$\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}=D[a^2-\alpha^2]+\epsilon D[a],$$
where $D$ denotes a Lindblad dissipator, $a$ is the annihilation operator, $\alpha$ is the coherent-state amplitude, and $\epsilon$ is the single-photon-loss rate relative to engineered two-photon dissipation. For sufficiently large amplitude and uniformly small positive loss ratio, the authors isolate a two-dimensional slow spectral subspace. Localization estimates around the two coherent components bound the slow decay rate by a polynomial prefactor times an exponentially decreasing function of $\alpha^2$. The result controls the infinite-dimensional problem beyond a finite perturbative truncation.

**Background.** The protected logical alternatives are the two separated coherent components. A single lost photon predominantly changes their relative phase; switching the components is the suppressed logical bit flip. Even and odd superpositions instead have definite photon parity, so a parity change should not be confused with that bit flip. The slow imbalance mode describes rare transfer between components.

**Why it matters?** This supplies a rigorous foundation for a central noise-bias assumption used in bosonic QEC. The advance is uniform control of the remainder, rather than a new optimal decay exponent. Extending comparable control to additional noise channels and driven gates would make the result more directly applicable to complete error-correction architectures.

**Claudio Arezzo, Antonello Scardicchio — arXiv:2611.02025**

When energy condenses onto one site, relaxation may require that concentration to migrate through very atypical configurations. Numerically distinguishing exponential, stretched-exponential, and large-power equilibration times is difficult; identifying the underlying bottleneck analytically is more informative.

The authors study reflecting Brownian motion on a surface of fixed mass and onsite quartic energy, replacing the kinetic dynamics of the discrete nonlinear Schrödinger model. Above the condensation threshold, configurations separate into large regions labeled by the condensate site, connected through narrow passages where two sites share the concentration. Trial functions and complementary geometric estimates bound the spectral gap from both sides. For fixed energy density above threshold, they establish $\tau_{\rm eq}=e^{\Theta(\sqrt N)}$; below threshold the relaxation time stays bounded with size. Transferring these conclusions to Hamiltonian dynamics is explicitly unproved.

**Background.** Brownian motion here explores the constrained occupation surface with its intrinsic metric and reflecting boundary. The physical hopping graph disappears from this surrogate. Its slowest relaxation rate is half the first nonzero Laplacian eigenvalue. The barrier is entropic: the connecting passage has very little available measure despite lying on the same energy surface. A one-site occupation can detect condensation, but estimating migration requires the two-site bottleneck.

**Why it matters?** This gives a sharp example of slow equilibration governed by state-space geometry rather than a simple energetic saddle. The distinction between equilibrium geometry and the chosen dynamics is useful for non-equilibrium theory. A comparison with physically local or Hamiltonian transport is the crucial next step before interpreting the bound as a universal relaxation law.

### Construction of the 2D non-Abelian Yang-Mills-Higgs measure via stochastic quantisation
**Bjoern Bringmann, Sky Cao, Martin Hairer, Wenhao Zhao — arXiv:2611.03640**

A formal gauge-field path integral does not automatically define a continuum probability measure. Stochastic quantisation proposes to construct that measure as the stationary law of an auxiliary noisy gradient flow, but non-Abelian gauge redundancy and singular fluctuations obstruct global control.

For a two-dimensional torus and a trivial bundle with compact connected gauge group, the authors construct renormalized Yang-Mills-Higgs dynamics on gauge orbits. A gauge-covariant regularization and local estimates are combined with energy decay of deterministic Yang-Mills flow to prevent blowup and obtain uniform-in-time bounds. Noise also drives the process away from unstable high-curvature stationary configurations where deterministic decay is ineffective. The limiting Markov process has a unique invariant measure, providing a non-perturbative construction at fixed couplings.

**Background.** The regularized dynamics uses smoothing adapted to a background connection, so estimates track curvature rather than a gauge-dependent potential. Its paracontrolled decomposition separates explicitly understood rough stochastic pieces from a more regular remainder, allowing otherwise undefined nonlinear products to be renormalized. The approximations need not be Markovian; the renormalized process on gauge-equivalence classes is. Uniform bounds provide existence of a stationary law, while noise-driven accessibility and regularization properties establish uniqueness.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantial constructive-QFT result for interacting gauge and matter fields. It demonstrates that stochastic quantisation can supply a definition where controlling a formal measure directly is difficult. Its broader importance lies in the gauge-covariant analytical machinery; extrapolation to higher-dimensional gauge theories would require major additional work.
