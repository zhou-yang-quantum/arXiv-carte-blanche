## arXiv-2026-10-26 — Item 1

### Asymptotic scars arising from the remnants of quantum phase transitions
**Leonard Logarić, Shane Dooley, Tsuyoshi Okubo, Nobuyuki Yoshioka — arXiv:2610.32037**

Can the slow excitations of a quantum critical point survive when its ground state is moved into the middle of a many-body spectrum? This would connect critical slowing down to unusually persistent states far from equilibrium, without requiring those states to remain exact eigenstates at finite size.

The authors establish such a connection for local frustration-free Hamiltonians. They deform each projector term by placing a bounded Hermitian operator between two copies of that projector. Low-energy excitations of the original Hamiltonian then have an energy variance bounded by their original excitation energy. As the reference gap closes, these states become increasingly stationary under the deformed dynamics. Numerical studies of Fredkin and Motzkin chains and a two-dimensional model illustrate the mechanism. The deformed Motzkin construction also produces extremely stable states with numerical evidence of volume-law entanglement.

**Background.** Asymptotic quantum many-body scars need not be eigenstates in a finite system: their energy uncertainty vanishes as the system grows. The Mandelstam–Tamm bound converts this shrinking uncertainty into a growing lower bound on the time needed to lose fidelity. Frustration freedom supplies the bridge to criticality. The original ground state violates none of the local projector constraints, and a low-energy excitation violates them only weakly. Sandwiching bounded operators between the same projectors limits how strongly the new Hamiltonian can act on that excitation. The variance theorem controls stability; identifying a scar additionally requires nonthermal structure and an appropriate position in the spectrum.

**Why it matters?** This offers a constructive connection between equilibrium critical structure and exceptional non-equilibrium dynamics. Its value is greater than another isolated scar example: reference models with different gap scalings become a systematic source of different stability scales. The entangled Motzkin examples also challenge the intuition that persistent exceptional states must be weakly entangled. Robustness to perturbations outside the projector construction remains an important test of the mechanism’s physical reach.

## arXiv-2026-10-26 — Item 2

### Multicriticality in the Generalized Schwinger Model
**Federico Ambrosino, Bernardo Zan — arXiv:2610.31748**

The massive Schwinger model provides a particularly accessible gauge-theory realization of Ising criticality. Can modifying its electric-field energy produce higher multicritical behavior, and does that behavior survive beyond large-color approximations that suggest it?

Ambrosino and Zan introduce an auxiliary field coupled to the field strength and study the quartic potential

$$V(B)=\frac{1}{2}\left(B^{4}+\tau B^{2}\right).$$

Semiclassical analysis and density-matrix renormalization group calculations reveal first-order and continuous boundaries between phases with preserved and spontaneously broken charge conjugation. Their meeting point has a finite-size spectrum consistent with the tricritical Ising conformal field theory. The broader phase diagram also contains successive first-order lines with Ising endpoints. The numerical investigation emphasizes the thermodynamic limit at fixed lattice spacing; it does not perform a complete continuum extrapolation of the phase diagram.

**Background.** The auxiliary field $B$ does not introduce an independent propagating scalar. Its coupling to the gauge field gives a first-order description of the electric sector; a quadratic potential recovers ordinary Maxwell dynamics after elimination of the auxiliary variable. The quartic term instead changes the energetic competition between flux configurations, while $\tau$ and the fermion mass provide two tuning parameters. This matters because tricritical Ising has two relevant symmetry-preserving perturbations: reaching it generically requires tuning both. Gauss’s law allows the gauge field to be eliminated on an open chain, making the resulting interacting matter problem accessible to tensor networks.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a concrete gauge-theory route to multicritical infrared physics, supported by spectral evidence rather than an order-parameter plot alone. It provides a useful bridge between continuum field theory, conformal criticality, and lattice simulation. For quantum simulation, the model supplies a richer benchmark than ordinary Schwinger criticality, although realizing the modified electric energy and controlling continuum effects would be separate challenges.

## arXiv-2026-10-26 — Item 3

### Temporal Degeneracy Limits Message Passing Decoding of QLDPC Codes Under Repeated Noisy Syndrome Measurements
**Dimitris Chytas, Nithin Raveendran, Bane Vasić — arXiv:2610.31877**

Increasing code distance cannot guarantee better practical protection if a decoder repeatedly gets stuck on small ambiguous fault histories. This paper isolates an obstruction introduced by noisy syndrome repetition itself, independently of the usual degeneracy associated with spatial stabilizers.

The authors characterize syndrome-invisible histories whose accumulated data error vanishes. Their elementary generators have weight fixed by the local check degree. Symmetric combinations support constant-weight competing errors, including weight-four patterns for the bivariate bicycle codes studied. Under an isolation assumption, deterministic message-passing decoders with identical local rules cannot choose a consistent correction on these configurations. The authors then introduce spatial and temporal differences in min-sum updates, supplemented by syndrome-guided realizations, and demonstrate improvements under phenomenological noise. These simulations probe the full graph beyond the theorem’s isolation assumption.

**Background.** An elementary temporal ambiguity can be built from two faults on the same data qubit in successive rounds, together with faults on its adjacent intervening readouts. The data faults cancel in accumulated parity, while the readout faults hide their syndrome signature. Complementary portions of such a history can therefore be equally valid corrections. The problem for an isotropic decoder is that a graph symmetry exchanges these alternatives while preserving all local syndrome information. Symmetric messages remain symmetric at every iteration, although selecting either alternative requires breaking that symmetry. The isolation assumption ensures that messages arriving from outside the configuration do not already distinguish its two sides.

**Why it matters?** This identifies a decoder-design issue that code-distance scaling alone cannot resolve. The connection between degeneracy and symmetry of the inference dynamics is especially useful when comparing qLDPC codes with realistic measurement histories. The proposed remedy is computationally economical at fixed iteration and ensemble budgets, but its practical standing still depends on circuit-level noise, latency, and resource-matched comparisons with competing decoders.

## arXiv-2026-10-26 — Item 4

### Low-frustration block encodings by patch routing
**Minzhao Liu, Abeynaya Gnanasekaran, Ananth Kaushik, Siddharth Hariprakash, Alireza Marefat Khah, Martin Roetteler, Grant Salton — arXiv:2610.32946**

Fault-tolerant simulation can waste substantial effort when a lattice Hamiltonian is implemented as a long list of separately controlled Pauli terms. Such decompositions can also discard useful information about cancellations and competing interactions within a local region.

The authors instead encode a small interaction patch jointly. A coherent routing circuit brings the selected patch to a fixed location, applies the local encoding once, and reverses the routing. More elaborate local encodings can therefore improve spectral bounds without being replicated at every lattice position. Reported estimates give a 15–17-fold reduction in leading T-gate cost for phase estimation on a 120-by-120 frustrated Heisenberg lattice relative to the compared Pauli constructions. These estimates exclude initial-state preparation and overlap costs.

**Background.** A block encoding places a normalized Hamiltonian inside a larger unitary, allowing algorithms to manipulate its spectrum through unitary operations. Its normalization and energy shift influence how accurately an eigenphase must be resolved to determine an energy. Treating a patch jointly can retain its actual local spectrum instead of bounding every interaction separately. Patch routing makes that potentially expensive local operation reusable: the patch address remains in superposition while controlled swaps move the appropriate degrees of freedom to the same workspace. Larger patches improve spectral information at the price of more difficult local circuits. “Low-frustration” concerns the resulting algorithmic representation and bounds; the target Hamiltonian remains physically frustrated.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful algorithmic advance because it ties resource reduction to the local physics of a Hamiltonian. It suggests a systematic trade-off between classical analysis of small clusters, local circuit synthesis, and global simulation cost. That makes it relevant to quantum simulation beyond a single model. Whether the quoted savings translate into a compelling complete calculation will depend especially on preparing states with sufficient overlap and on implementing the routing efficiently.

## arXiv-2026-10-26 — Item 5

### Intertwining Defects and Fermion-Boundary Scattering from TQFT
**Kantaro Ohmori, Masataka Watanabe, Pengcheng Wei, Yunqin Zheng — arXiv:2610.31737**

A symmetry-preserving boundary must return the quantum numbers carried by an incident fermion. Yet when incoming and outgoing fermions transform differently, ordinary outgoing multiparticle states may offer no apparent way to do so—even when the symmetry is anomaly-free. This is a concrete puzzle in the radial reduction of fermion scattering from monopoles.

The authors construct conformal boundaries using an auxiliary three-dimensional topological quantum field theory. Scattering can produce excitations attached to non-invertible topological lines, and sometimes a superposition of distinct sectors whose branching probabilities follow from topological data. Explicit examples include higher-charge QED monopoles and the charge-three Standard Model monopole. These are solutions of the reduced two-dimensional boundary problem; incorporating dynamical gauge fields and higher partial waves into the four-dimensional interpretation is left open.

**Background.** Unfolding turns reflection from a boundary into transmission through a defect. Incoming and outgoing symmetry currents then appear as two different embeddings of the same current algebra. An intertwining defect converts one embedding into the other while preserving the physical symmetry. The three-dimensional construction represents these embeddings by different topological interfaces sharing a common chiral boundary; a junction between the interfaces supplies the required defect. An outgoing line need not have an inverse under fusion, so its endpoint is more general than an ordinary fermion operator transformed by a group symmetry. If the relevant line decomposes into several elementary types, the outgoing state has several superselection sectors. This enlarged state space resolves the apparent mismatch of quantum numbers.

**Why it matters?** This turns generalized-symmetry structure into a calculational tool for a physical scattering question. The significance is both conceptual—ordinary Fock-space intuition can miss allowed outgoing sectors—and constructive, through explicit boundary conditions and probabilities. It is a worthwhile extension of familiar conformal-field-theory methods toward nonabelian boundary problems. A complete monopole-scattering application requires additional dynamics; the reduced solution is an essential ingredient rather than that complete calculation.

## arXiv-2026-10-26 — Item 6

### Shear viscosity from complex light-cone expansion
**Inês Aniceto, Pavel Kovtun, Alex Ratcliffe, Michał Spaliński — arXiv:2610.32892**

Hydrodynamic transport is an infrared property, but it must ultimately follow from microscopic dynamics. Can information about a strongly coupled theory near the high-momentum light cone recover its long-wavelength shear diffusion, without passing through a kinetic description?

The authors investigate holographic theories described by Einstein gravity. Their key observation is that the shear mode approaches the light cone at large imaginary momentum. An all-orders semiclassical expansion in this regime, followed by resummation and analytic continuation, reaches the small-momentum dispersion relation

$\omega(k)\sim-i{\cal D}k^2$.

Here the imaginary frequency describes diffusive relaxation, with a diffusion coefficient proportional to shear viscosity. For the five-dimensional anti-de Sitter black brane, ten large-momentum terms recover that coefficient to about 0.45 percent. The reconstruction also gives strong numerical evidence that the continued mode becomes analytic and gapless at the origin.

**Background.** Quasinormal modes are damped gravitational perturbations satisfying an ingoing horizon condition and the appropriate asymptotic boundary condition; their frequencies give poles of the field theory’s thermal response. Continuing momentum away from the real axis lets one follow the shear pole into a regime accessible to a high-momentum expansion. The resulting series diverges factorially. Borel resummation compensates that growth and reconstructs a function at finite momentum; Padé approximants, ratios of polynomials matching local series data, then continue it toward the origin. Imaginary momentum is an analytic probe, rather than a physical unstable perturbation.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual achievement is a quantitative connection between microscopic spectral behavior and macroscopic transport. Direct small-momentum holographic calculations remain easier for obtaining the viscosity itself; the attraction here is understanding how that information is already encoded elsewhere in the response function. Extending this strategy to less special field theories would be particularly valuable, but the demonstrated reconstruction is not yet a general ultraviolet-to-infrared theorem.

## arXiv-2026-10-26 — Item 7

### Fundamental bound on Wigner negativity generation from environmental collisions
**Isaac Layton, Takahiro Sagawa, Ken Funo — arXiv:2610.32315**

Decoherence is often said to wash quantum interference into approximately classical motion. This paper asks a sharper question: when does an open quantum evolution preserve every initially nonnegative Wigner function exactly, even in a nonquadratic potential?

For the studied Markovian collision model, the answer is a momentum-resolved condition,

$$\gamma(k)\geq\frac{2|\widetilde{V}_{\circ}(2k)|}{\pi\hbar}$$

for every nonzero wave number. Here $\gamma(k)$ is the rate density of momentum kicks $\hbar k$, and $\widetilde{V}_{\circ}$ is the Fourier transform of the potential after removing its quadratic part. Satisfying the bound gives exact positivity-preserving dynamics. Violating it allows a sufficiently momentum-squeezed Gaussian state to develop negativity. A related heating bound supplies a simpler necessary condition for this form of classicality. The principal setting neglects friction and assumes appropriate regularity of the potential.

**Background.** The useful representation is a jump process in momentum. Quantum evolution in a nonquadratic potential contributes signed jump rates, which cannot generally be interpreted as classical probabilities. Environmental collisions add positive jump rates. When they dominate every negative contribution, the combined generator becomes a legitimate classical stochastic generator. This explains why replacing collisions by Gaussian diffusion can lose the decisive information: the distribution of individual kick sizes matters, beyond its second moment. Positivity preservation concerns the entire set of positive Wigner states; failure of the condition does not imply that every experimentally accessible initial state becomes negative.

**Why it matters?** This replaces a qualitative competition between nonlinearity and decoherence with a sharp criterion inside a specified open-system framework. It is especially useful for non-equilibrium physics because it identifies when a classical stochastic representation is exact, rather than merely accurate for coarse observables. The heating criterion also offers an operational diagnostic, although showing an ability to generate negativity is distinct from preparing a state with readily measurable negativity.

## arXiv-2026-10-26 — Item 8

### The solution of the Hopfield model with Gaussian patterns
**Francesco Camilli, Pierluigi Contucci, Emanuele Mingione — arXiv:2610.32618**

The Hopfield model is a standard bridge between disordered statistical mechanics and associative memory, yet its Gaussian-pattern version has resisted a full thermodynamic treatment. The obstacle is structural: after introducing Gaussian hidden variables, the disorder covariance is a product of two overlaps, so familiar convexity arguments do not apply.

The authors present a variational solution at every temperature and positive pattern load. They separate the hidden layer into a direction on a sphere and a fluctuating radius, solving the fixed-radius Ising–spherical problem before optimizing the radius against its entropic cost. A Hamilton–Jacobi formulation uses convexity of the entropic functional in place of convexity of the covariance. The resulting transition temperature is $T_g=1+\sqrt{\alpha}$, where $\alpha$ is the number of patterns per visible spin, and replica symmetry is broken throughout the low-temperature phase. This letter explains the construction and consequences while deferring detailed proofs to a forthcoming work.

**Background.** An overlap measures similarity between two equilibrium configurations in the same disorder realization. A replica-symmetric description replaces the organization of these similarities by a much simpler order parameter; a glass can require an overlap distribution describing many competing states. Here there are overlaps for both the Ising and spherical species. The hidden radius is a separate thermodynamic variable: increasing it strengthens the interaction but makes the Gaussian configuration exponentially less typical. Optimizing this competition prevents an uncontrolled accumulation of hidden-layer fluctuations. Gaussian patterns also have continuous rotational symmetry in pattern space, so their thermodynamics should not be identified with the retrieval behavior of the binary-pattern model.

**Why it matters?** If the full proof supports the announced result, this is a substantial advance in an important solvable disorder problem. The broader attraction is the combination of radial decomposition and a variational treatment adapted to nonconvex interactions. It offers a worthwhile direction beyond familiar integrable quantum chains. The detailed low-temperature overlap structure, including whether replica symmetry breaking is full, remains unresolved even after obtaining the thermodynamic variational principle.

## arXiv-2026-10-26 — Item 9

### Fermion Scattering on Quantum Computers
**Steven Abel, Michael Spannowsky, Simon Williams — arXiv:2610.32882**

Real-time scattering is a compelling quantum-simulation target, but a useful benchmark must distinguish genuine resonance dynamics from packet preparation, finite observation times, and finite-volume effects. Coupled fermions and bosons add the practical question of how to represent both efficiently.

The authors formulate a hybrid qubit–oscillator encoding of lattice Yukawa theory in one spatial dimension and study collisions using matrix-product-state simulations. Near resonance, fermion–antifermion packets excite a localized scalar response, accompanied by enhanced entanglement and partial energy transfer back into the fermionic sector. An independently fitted scalar correlator gives a decay width close to the leading-order lattice prediction. Energy scans are compatible with packet-averaged resonance profiles, but widths inferred from short-time responses depend appreciably on the measurement window. These are classical reference calculations for a proposed quantum implementation.

**Background.** A qumode is an oscillator degree of freedom whose continuous quadratures can represent the scalar field and its conjugate momentum; fermionic occupations are encoded in qubits. Their Yukawa coupling becomes a conditional oscillator displacement controlled by local fermion occupation. This combines the natural local representations of the two particle species. For interpreting the dynamics, a resonance line shape is broadened by the incoming packets’ energy spread, while finite observation time changes what a fitted response measures. A vacuum two-point correlator provides a separate lifetime reference. Local scalar occupation after a collision is therefore not automatically an asymptotic scalar-particle yield.

**Why it matters?** The useful contribution is a physically interpretable benchmark linking field-theory dynamics to a specified hybrid architecture. It also illustrates the care needed when extracting particle properties from local real-time signals. This connects quantum simulation with familiar scattering theory without reducing the task to gate compilation. Progress toward quantitative scattering parameters will require better control of dressed incoming states, finite-volume effects, and the operations needed on actual hardware.

## arXiv-2026-10-26 — Item 10

### Two-Layer Verification for Fault-Tolerant Magic-State Preparation in High-Rate Quantum Codes
**Naoyuki Kanomata, Hayato Goto — arXiv:2610.32267**

Encoding several logical qubits together can reduce storage overhead while making state verification less informative. A convenient transversal measurement may test only a product of logical verification operators, allowing two incorrect outputs to conceal each other.

Kanomata and Goto address this with independent verification of small constituent blocks followed by collective verification after embedding them into the target code. Their explicit construction combines three verified Steane-code states into a $[[21,3,5]]$ block. Conditional logical-error probabilities show approximately cubic suppression in their circuit-level simulations. The non-Clifford T-state tests use ideal final error detection; noisy error-detecting teleportation is tested with an S-state proxy. The noise model also neglects single-qubit gate and memory errors, so the evidence is a scoped numerical demonstration.

**Background.** The blind spot is an even-parity ambiguity in verification eigenvalues. A product test detects an odd number of sign flips, but two sign flips restore its accepted sign. Local checks first establish each constituent state’s eigenvalue separately, suppressing faults that would be invisible to the later product test. An embedding circuit then rearranges and entangles those blocks into the higher-rate code. Because this circuit can introduce new faults, the second layer combines the product check with syndrome-based error detection. Acceptance requires passing both layers. Error-detecting teleportation transfers the encoded state through an ancillary resource while exposing detectable errors; it supplies a noisy implementation of the final detection stage in the proxy study.

**Why it matters?** This identifies a simple information deficit that can undermine otherwise attractive collective verification schemes. The proposed hierarchy is a useful design principle for codes carrying several logical qubits, where a compact transversal operation may reveal too little about individual outputs. Its broader impact depends on finding comparable constituent-block decompositions in other codes and establishing complete preparation costs, including acceptance probability and fully noisy non-Clifford verification.
