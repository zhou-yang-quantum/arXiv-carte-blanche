# arXiv-2026-10-09

This historical batch brings together twelve papers on quantum simulation, fault tolerance, integrable field theory, and non-equilibrium matter. Several ask how an apparently inaccessible structure becomes calculable or observable: scattering through circuit dynamics, environmental memory through controlled interventions, and conserved-charge spectra through differential equations. A complementary theme is the role of constraints in organizing protected states and phases, with particular attention to the distinction between microscopic information-theoretic properties and continuum universality.

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 1

### Analog quantum simulation of $φ^4$ field theory with a superconducting transmission line
**Ilan T. Rosen, Neill C. Warrington, Max Hays, Christopher McNally, Joshua Lin, Stephen Sorokanich, Julian Bender — arXiv:2610.11071**

Real-time scattering in an interacting quantum field theory remains difficult precisely where perturbation theory becomes unreliable. Imaginary-time Monte Carlo gives access to equilibrium information but does not directly follow a collision, while digital simulation must represent an unbounded field using finite qubit registers. The authors propose a superconducting transmission line whose dynamical variables themselves represent a scalar field in one spatial dimension.

The construction uses coupled fluxonium-like circuit elements. Their node fluxes supply the field amplitudes, inductive couplings supply the spatial-gradient energy, and Josephson nonlinearities supply the interaction. Circuit parameters tune the local potential between a single well, suitable for particle wave packets, and a double well, supporting distinct vacua and kink–antikink configurations. The proposal includes protocols for preparing collisions and measuring their spatially resolved evolution.

The quantitative validation compares low-energy spectra of the circuit and lattice $φ^4$ theory in the single-well regime. This tests whether unwanted higher powers in the circuit potential remain small for the states considered. It does not amount to an experimental demonstration of scattering, and the double-well collision proposal has a different evidentiary status from the spectral comparison.

**Background.** The useful circuit variable is a node flux, proportional to the superconducting phase difference. Capacitance supplies its kinetic term, while an inductive connection between neighboring nodes penalizes their difference, reproducing a lattice field gradient. A linear shunt inductor allows the phase coordinate to range beyond one Josephson period. A practical junction-array superinductor approximates this behavior over the relevant range, so effective unboundedness must be checked against the states being populated. Flux bias makes the Josephson potential even and gives a positive quartic term; balancing its quadratic contribution against the inductive one selects the single- or double-well regime. The field-theory correspondence therefore depends on both the accessible amplitude range and suppression of higher-order terms.

**Why it matters?** This is a concrete platform proposal for observing the interior of an interacting scattering event, including the passage from particle excitations to nonlinear soliton dynamics. Its significance for quantum simulation lies in matching the hardware’s native continuous coordinate to the field, potentially reducing encoding overhead. The next decisive step is dynamical validation: demonstrating that preparation, readout, circuit imperfections, and higher-order interactions permit quantitatively controlled collisions in the intended field-theory regime.

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 2

### Single-shot magic state factories for quantum LDPC codes
**Varun Menon, Rohan Mehta, Andi Gu, Andrei C. Diaconu, Daniel Bochen Tan, Xiao Xiao, Michael J. Gullans, Qian Xu, Hengyun Zhou, Mikhail D. Lukin, J. Pablo Bonilla Ataides — arXiv:2610.10731**

The storage advantage of a high-rate quantum code can disappear if producing its non-Clifford resources requires expensive auxiliary blocks and many measurement rounds. This paper addresses that architectural mismatch by assigning resource generation and preservation to different, algebraically related codes.

Tricycle codes generate several logical magic states in parallel through transversal gates, but their asymmetric protection makes them less suitable for retaining the resulting fidelity. Quadcycle codes provide stronger, more balanced protection and single-shot correction. The central construction is an efficient transfer between them: one layer of physical CNOT gates, followed by measurements of the source blocks, teleports the logical resource into the receiving blocks. Those measurements also provide information for rejecting faulty preparations. The intermediate resource is an entangled logical state that can subsequently yield two CCZ states.

Under the specified circuit-level noise model at physical error probability $10^{-3}$, the authors report output error rates approaching $10^{-9}$ and space-time savings of 10–40 relative to their comparison protocols. They also compile the factory into a reconfigurable neutral-atom layout. These are modeled resource results: the comparison converts competing T-state costs into CCZ-equivalent yields, omits the associated conversion-circuit contributions, and excludes decoder latency for all schemes.

**Background.** The names tricycle and quadcycle refer to constructions with three and four algebraic cyclic directions; they do not require hardware occupying three or four spatial dimensions. The fourth direction is added in a way that retains an embedding of the tricycle structure. This embedding enables a *homomorphic CNOT*: the physical coupling respects the relations among code checks and implements the desired logical transfer without decoding the state into bare qubits. Measuring the source then completes teleportation. Single-shot correction supplies the other essential ingredient: relations among syndrome outcomes provide enough redundancy to handle measurement faults using a number of rounds that stays constant as distance grows. Together, these properties keep the transfer shallow while moving the resource into a code capable of preserving it.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is an architectural method for carrying the rate advantage of qLDPC memories into universal computation. Its strongest idea is the compatible interface between codes with complementary capabilities. That interface matters for resource-intensive quantum algorithms, including field-theory simulation, where non-Clifford operations can dominate cost. Realizing the projected advantage requires the assumed connectivity, transport, noise performance, and decoding throughput to coexist in a complete implementation.

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 3

### Markov length can diverge in systems whose universal physics is spatially Markovian
**Yu-Hsueh Chen, Tarun Grover — arXiv:2610.11235**

If two states flow to the same renormalization-group fixed point, which information-theoretic distinctions between them should survive as universal physics? This question becomes sharp for mixed-state phase definitions based on local reversibility, because algebraic conditional correlations can obstruct equivalence to a state with finite Markov length even when their amplitude is extremely small.

The authors start from critical classical Gibbs states of finite-range Hamiltonians. Their ordinary correlations can be long-ranged while their conditional correlations vanish once the buffer exceeds the interaction range. An RG-irrelevant perturbation that breaks detailed balance can produce algebraic conditional correlations without changing the limiting fixed point. The resolution is that their amplitude vanishes with a positive power of the lattice cutoff. At every fixed cutoff the Markov length is infinite, yet the conditional correlations disappear in the continuum limit.

Solvable models establish this mechanism; its occurrence at the Ising-symmetric critical point of Toom’s cellular automaton is a prediction. The paper also derives a one-dimensional criterion linking decay faster than the inverse square of buffer length to a spatially Markovian scaling limit, subject to its assumptions about ultraviolet finiteness, translation invariance, and the entropy scaling expansion.

**Background.** The conceptual bridge is the order of limits. Let $a$ be the lattice spacing, $l$ the size of each outer interval, and $l_B$ the intervening buffer. In the one-dimensional scaling argument,
\[
I(A:C|B)\simeq (l/l_B)^2 g(l_B/a).
\]
If $g(x)\sim x^{-\delta}$ with $\delta>0$, the expression has an algebraic tail as $l_B$ grows at fixed $a$, but vanishes as $a\to0$ at fixed physical lengths. An asymptotic decay classification detects the tail regardless of its small amplitude; the continuum fixed point discards that amplitude. Breaking detailed balance supplies the microscopic departure from a local Gibbs distribution that allows this otherwise absent conditional dependence to appear.

**Why it matters?** This is a conceptual qualification of how information-theoretic phase distinctions relate to RG universality. It explains how a valid obstruction to microscopic local reversibility can coexist with unchanged continuum physics. For work on decohering and non-equilibrium phases, it suggests tracking the cutoff dependence of conditional correlations alongside their decay exponent before interpreting a divergent information length as a new universal phenomenon.

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 4

### Constructing Large and Structured Decoherence-Free Subspaces in Hybrid Quantum Systems
**Catalin-Mihai Halati, Tony Jin, Roderich Moessner — arXiv:2610.12348**

Engineered dissipation can prepare a chosen state, but designing an entire protected manifold with controllable many-body structure is harder. A large stationary space alone says little about whether it contains interesting correlations or how its states are reached. The authors develop a construction that makes both its size and its internal organization accessible through algebra.

Quantum matter is coupled to a lossy bosonic mode through a non-Hermitian matter operator $\mathcal O$. Matter states satisfying $\mathcal O|\psi\rangle=0$ cannot activate the relevant coupling and form the dark manifold. The design problem becomes finding physically realizable operators with many structured null vectors. Representation theory supplies such operators by organizing them as algebraic lowering operators.

The worked example couples a cavity to directed hopping of bosons or fermions on a chain. An $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb C)$ structure yields an exponentially large steady-state manifold. Analytical constructions and numerical calculations reveal long-range correlations, volume-law entanglement, and boundary accumulation constrained by the manifold’s structure. Time-dependent matrix-product-state simulations also follow relaxation from generic initial states. These results concern protection under the engineered dynamics; they do not establish a memory stable against arbitrary additional noise.

**Background.** A lowering operator annihilates the lowest-weight state of each irreducible representation. When a many-body space decomposes into many such representations, there can be many independent lowest-weight states, even if the operator itself is simple. Here a particle’s position along the chain plays the role of a spin projection, making directed hopping the analogue of lowering. Particle statistics determine how these effective representations combine. This turns counting and constructing dark states into an algebraic problem.

A second, commuting pairing structure organizes delocalized particle pairs at positions symmetric about the chain center. Its pair number is conserved by the full dissipative evolution, whereas some structure of the dark manifold emerges only after relaxation. This distinction explains why the final manifold can have more symmetry than the dynamics that prepares it. It also constrains the skin effect: directional hopping favors boundary accumulation, but interference and pair constraints prevent an unrestricted pileup of particles at one edge.

**Why it matters?** The advance is a constructive connection between dissipative constraints, representation theory, and correlated stationary manifolds. It broadens dissipative state engineering from selecting isolated targets to designing families of states whose degeneracies and correlations can be understood analytically. The connection to passive quantum-information protection is substantive, but turning this manifold into a useful memory would require controlled logical access and an analysis of perturbations outside the engineered loss channel.

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 5

### Quantum annealing of the random transverse-field Ising model in one, two, and three dimensions
**István A. Kovács, Neil Shah, Róbert Juhász — arXiv:2610.11411**

Slowing an anneal should reduce excitations, but strong disorder creates a broad distribution of gaps and relaxation scales. A single characteristic critical timescale can therefore obscure the actual events at which defects form. This paper asks how those events organize as a random transverse-field Ising model is driven toward its ordered regime.

The authors use strong-disorder renormalization to describe annealing as the progressive assembly of strongly coupled spin clusters. Applying adiabaticity conditions to elementary events in this assembly identifies where excitations can arise. They develop a scaling theory for the statistics of these events and test it through extensive renormalization calculations in one, two, and three dimensions. In one dimension, free-fermion calculations provide an independent check.

The resulting defect density decreases only logarithmically with annealing time, with exponents tied to the dimension-dependent infinite-disorder critical point. The authors also map potential excitation events in energy versus control-parameter space, offering information that could guide schedule design. The higher-dimensional evidence comes from the strong-disorder treatment, and the event maps do not themselves demonstrate an optimal schedule.

**Background.** Strong-disorder renormalization repeatedly eliminates the largest local energy scale. A dominant bond locks spins into an effective cluster, while a dominant transverse field removes a locally polarized degree of freedom; weaker couplings are updated perturbatively. At an infinite-disorder fixed point, the distribution of effective energy scales broadens under this procedure, making the hierarchy of decimations increasingly important. Length and characteristic time are then related through activated scaling, schematically $\log\tau\sim\xi^\psi$, rather than a finite dynamical power law. Consequently, obtaining appreciably larger correlated clusters can require exponentially longer times, explaining why defect suppression becomes logarithmically slow.

**Why it matters?** This is a controlled scaling account of a physically important limitation on annealing-based preparation. It connects the universal structure of a disordered critical point to the practical return from increasing runtime. For non-equilibrium physics and quantum algorithms, the useful methodological step is resolving individual excitation bottlenecks rather than relying only on a global minimum gap. Whether tailored schedules improve the asymptotic scaling remains a further question.

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 6

### Generalised Gibbs Ensembles from the ODE/IM Correspondence: the Lee--Yang Model
**Sujay K. Ashok, Max Downing, Faisal Karimi, Adarsh Sudhakar, Roberto Tateo — arXiv:2610.10716**

Generalized Gibbs ensembles introduce independent chemical potentials for higher conserved charges. Although integrability constrains these ensembles, it does not immediately tell us how to represent their charge deformations within the correspondence between differential equations and quantum integrable models. Merely relabeling the energy of the undeformed differential equation cannot supply all the independent sources that the ensemble requires.

The authors construct a genuine deformation of the cubic oscillator associated with the chiral Lee–Yang conformal field theory. They fix the deformation order by order by requiring its leading large-energy semiclassical period to reproduce exactly the source term of the thermodynamic Bethe ansatz. Once that requirement is imposed, subleading periods predict the deformed conserved-charge eigenvalues rather than serving as additional fitting conditions.

They work out the ensemble involving the spin-5 charge for the ground state and the identity primary. The latter needs an additional pole whose position is constrained so that it introduces no nontrivial monodromy. Charge eigenvalues agree with independent CFT calculations at the orders checked, and Borel-resummed ground-state energies agree with numerical Bethe-ansatz solutions over many orders of magnitude in chemical potential. The explicit tests concern these states in a nonunitary model; a full ensemble partition function would require a broader treatment of the spectrum.

**Background.** In the ODE/IM correspondence, solutions of a Schrödinger-type equation are continued between sectors of the complex coordinate plane where different asymptotic solutions decay. The coefficients relating these solutions, called Stokes multipliers, obey the same functional relations as quantities in the integrable quantum theory. Semiclassical contour integrals, or WKB periods, then translate the differential equation’s asymptotic data into quantum conserved charges.

The paper uses this dictionary in reverse: prescribe the generalized ensemble’s Bethe-ansatz source, then engineer the potential that produces it. An *apparent singularity* is a pole in that potential around which the solutions nevertheless remain single-valued. Requiring this trivial monodromy restricts the pole’s location and allows the identity-primary spectral problem to retain the appropriate functional relations. Borel resummation subsequently gives usable energies from the divergent perturbative series.

**Why it matters?** This is a predictive calculational extension of an established integrability correspondence. Matching the leading source while successfully predicting subleading charges gives it more force than a formal analogy between two descriptions. It offers a route for studying higher-charge deformations of solvable field theories, with a clear next test: extending the construction to descendants, additional chemical potentials, and other models while preserving the independent spectral checks.

### Measure Now, Mitigate Later: Virtual Error Cancellation for Logical Quantum Circuits
**Oles Shtanko, Saswata Roy, Shashwat Kumar, Zlatko Minev — arXiv:2610.12400**

Finite-distance error correction leaves a residual bias in computational estimates, while increasing code distance demands more hardware. Error mitigation offers another tradeoff, but commonly requires separate noise characterization or modified circuit executions. This paper asks how much can be recovered after an ordinary logical computation using the syndrome records it already produced.

Virtual error cancellation combines the decoder used during execution with a more expensive offline decoder. Their agreement or disagreement partitions the recorded shots into sectors with different logical-error statistics. Signed weights assigned to these sectors cancel the average residual error contribution. The quantum circuit has already finished; the correction acts on estimates assembled from its outcomes.

The authors develop protocols for universal logical circuits and demonstrate more than three orders of magnitude of suppression relative to the decoded logical error rate in distance-7 rotated-surface-code simulations. The reported benchmark uses code-capacity noise. Their procedure learns parameters of an assumed detector error model from stored syndromes, so bypassing separate calibration does not mean dispensing with noise modeling. Exact cancellation requires correct parameters, and its sampling cost grows because negative weights increase variance.

**Background.** The extra resource is the information in the *difference between decoder decisions*. An offline decoder need not meet the latency budget for real-time feedforward and can identify suspicious records that the online decoder treated differently. Postselection would discard such records; signed reweighting instead uses them to subtract the estimated error contribution. Schematically, the weights obey $\mathbb E[w]=1$ and $\mathbb E[wp_\alpha]=0$, where $p_\alpha$ is the syndrome-conditioned probability of logical error type $\alpha$. The first condition preserves normalization, while the second cancels each error channel on average. Since error probabilities are nonnegative, cancellation generally requires some negative weights. Their second moment sets the additional shot cost.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a way to trade offline classical computation and extra samples for more accurate outputs from finite-size logical processors. It gives stored syndromes value beyond their original role in immediate recovery, potentially benefiting algorithms whose useful output is an expectation value. Establishing the practical gain requires testing realistic circuit-level and mismatched noise models, together with the accumulated sampling cost of long computations.

### Hamiltonian framework for Chiral Gauge Theories on a Disk Boundary
**Srimoyee Sen — arXiv:2610.11035**

A nonperturbative formulation of a chiral gauge theory must reproduce its chiral spectrum and topological content. For quantum simulation, it must also admit a Hamiltonian description. A disk-boundary construction addresses the first problem by realizing chiral fermions on the boundary of a higher-dimensional system, but its original prescription for extending gauge fields into the interior depends on boundary configurations at different times. That dependence obstructs ordinary Hamiltonian evolution.

Sen proposes extending the gauge fields separately on each time slice by solving equations that exclude the time direction. The interior fields then become instantaneous functionals of boundary fields. The paper obtains explicit expressions for Abelian gauge fields and explains why the disk geometry accommodates this prescription.

The other important question is whether changing the extension preserves the construction’s nonperturbative physics. Explicit lower-dimensional Abelian calculations check the behavior of interior singularities and boundary topological charge. For the four-dimensional non-Abelian setting, the argument also uses topological considerations and perturbative support. The proposed retention of the construction’s strong-CP properties should therefore be read with that distinction in evidence; this is a framework toward Hamiltonian treatment, rather than a completed quantum simulation of the Standard Model.

**Background.** The additional dimension supplies a spatially varying fermion mass whose interface binds a chiral boundary mode. Gauge fields are dynamical on the boundary, but the parent fermion system also needs a prescription for the gauge field in the interior. That extension is auxiliary structure with physical constraints: it must respect boundary gauge invariance and handle topologically nontrivial configurations consistently. An extension obtained from a spacetime boundary-value problem can depend on future as well as past boundary fields. Solving a spatial problem at each instant removes this particular temporal nonlocality. Smoothness and possible interior singularities remain essential because localized fermion zero modes can affect anomaly and topological physics.

**Why it matters?** This is a targeted advance on an identifiable obstruction between Euclidean chiral-gauge constructions and real-time quantum simulation. Its value is the explicit instantaneous Abelian extension and the accompanying examination of topology. For the broader QFT program, the decisive continuation is a controlled non-Abelian Hamiltonian implementation with the desired continuum spectrum and nonperturbative observables.

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 9

### Quantum non-Markovian response spectra
**Aaron Sander, John F. Kam, Gauthameshwar S., Robert Wille, Kavan Modi — arXiv:2610.10684**

Resetting accessible qubits need not erase the environmental trace of an earlier experiment. Hidden degrees of freedom can retain that trace and alter later outcomes, producing correlations that a sequence of isolated channel descriptions misses. Full reconstruction of the multi-time process is expensive, so the practical question is how much of this memory can be resolved through selected system-only experiments.

The authors organize controlled histories and future probes into a response matrix. Its entries record how future observations depend on earlier interventions after a specified system reset. A singular-value decomposition resolves independent patterns of observable history dependence. Repeating the construction with different cuts and intervening resets probes where memory resides operationally and how it persists.

The framework is tested in an interacting spin process and a two-qubit storage-and-retrieval benchmark. Suitable linear combinations of response data can also witness quantum memory by excluding a specified classical feed-forward model. The spectrum is generally dependent on the chosen probes; equality with an underlying process-tensor spectrum requires the paper’s recovery conditions. A failed quantum-memory witness is inconclusive, and detectable memory need not remain quantum.

**Background.** A *causal break* discards the accessible system and prepares it anew. If later observations still distinguish earlier histories, the distinguishing information must have survived outside that reset system. A process tensor describes the probabilities generated by entire sequences of interventions, including their disturbance of subsequent dynamics. The response matrix samples selected parts of this larger object. Its singular vectors identify combinations of histories and future measurements that communicate through the hidden memory, while its singular values quantify their relative visibility. Restricted controls may miss some such combinations. Quantum certification requires an additional witness that is nonnegative for every process in the adopted classical class; a negative measured value then excludes that class.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful intermediate description between detecting a single memory effect and reconstructing the whole process. It connects open-system dynamics to experimentally accessible diagnostics of correlated noise, a concrete concern for error correction and quantum simulation. Its practical impact depends on whether modest probe sets resolve the memory relevant to prediction or control, and on whether reliable witnesses remain accessible with realistic measurement uncertainty.

### Large Charge Jets in $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM Energy Correlators
**Alba Grassi, Cristoforo Iossa, Luigi Tizzano, Alexander Zhiboedov — arXiv:2610.10707**

Energy correlators translate quantum field dynamics into angular patterns of energy flow. Preparing a state with a large conserved charge changes the environment in which that energy propagates, raising the question of how narrow angular structures emerge and eventually give way to a broad distribution. The authors study this crossover in a controlled sector of $\mathcal N=4$ supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory at fixed gauge-group rank.

A heavy canonical operator of dimension $\Delta_H$ creates the state, and the relevant large-charge coupling scales as $\lambda\sim\Delta_H g_{\rm YM}^2$. The state induces a Higgs scale, with squared W-boson masses proportional to $\lambda$. Starting from a known finite-coupling four-point function, the authors perform the detector and Fourier transforms and analyze an infinite operator-product-expansion sum.

Writing $z=(1-\cos\theta)/2$, where $\theta$ is the detector separation, they find several angular regimes: vacuum-dominated behavior at $z\ll\lambda^{-1}$, a non-monotonic crossover near $\lambda z\sim1$, and a large-charge jet regime with correlator scaling $1/(\lambda z^3)$ over $\lambda^{-1}\lesssim z\lesssim\lambda^{-1/3}$, followed by an approximately angle-independent plateau. A parametrically broad separation of these regimes requires large $\lambda$. This concerns the specified heavy-state sector, rather than arbitrary states of the theory.

**Background.** An energy detector is represented by a light-ray operator measuring energy arriving at infinity in a chosen direction. Two detectors therefore turn a local four-point function into an inclusive angular observable. The detector transform extracts this asymptotic energy flow, while the Fourier transform selects the source momentum. Expanding in the source–detector channel organizes the same observable through operators exchanged between the state preparation and a detector. The heavy state supplies an additional physical scale, so the angular distribution can cross between vacuum-like and state-induced behavior instead of following one scaling form throughout.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is an analytically tractable example of how a charged field-theory state reorganizes energy flow across multiple angular scales at finite coupling. It connects exact correlation-function methods to real-time observables with a scattering interpretation. The broader opportunity is to determine which crossover mechanisms follow from the induced mass scale and which rely on the special supersymmetric sector. ## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 11

### Short-Range Vector Spin Glasses: Parisi Hierarchies and Real-Space Ultrametricity
**Akash Vijay, Jaewon Kim, Jong Yeon Lee — arXiv:2610.12441**

Mean-field spin-glass theory organizes equilibrium states through a hierarchy of overlaps, but spatial locality can destabilize structures that are consistent at mean field. This paper asks whether a continuous Parisi hierarchy can arise in a short-range model while still allowing its spatial fluctuations to be analyzed systematically.

The authors introduce $N$-component vector spins with random matrix couplings on nearest-neighbor links and random tensor couplings on plaquettes of a $D$-dimensional lattice. At large $N$, they obtain stable phases with one-step, continuous, and mixed forms of replica symmetry breaking. They then identify two distinct infrared limitations: random anisotropy destabilizes uniform ferromagnetism for $D\leq4$, while gapless modes associated with continuous replica symmetry breaking produce divergent leading-$1/N$ fluctuations for $D\leq2$.

The hierarchy also acquires a spatial interpretation. For two pure states of overlap $q$, a length $\xi(q)$ measures the range over which differences in their frozen magnetization patterns remain correlated. Within a continuous hierarchy, the authors find that this length is temperature-independent and decreases with overlap. Less similar states differ on longer spatial scales. These conclusions concern the constructed large-$N$ model and its fluctuation expansion; they do not settle the finite-component short-range spin-glass problem in general.

**Background.** The Parisi overlap parameter describes how pure states are grouped: nearby branches correspond to similar frozen patterns, while branches that split earlier are less similar. Ultrametricity means that this organization has a tree structure, with the relation among any three states constrained by their shared branching ancestry. One-step breaking introduces a single nontrivial level; full breaking gives a continuous hierarchy; a mixed form combines discrete and continuous sectors. The paper’s replica Goldstone modes describe soft spatial variations of the continuous hierarchy. A Ward identity protects their gaplessness, allowing long-wavelength fluctuations to test the stability of the large-$N$ solution. The new length $\xi(q)$ connects that abstract state-space organization to correlations of actual spatial patterns.

**Why it matters?** This offers a controlled setting in which a Parisi hierarchy and spatial locality can be studied together, including their infrared tensions. The particularly useful conceptual result is the proposed correspondence between branching in state space and scales in real space. It could guide broader field-theoretic approaches to disordered phases, but conclusions for small $N$ require additional control. The recorded page was inaccessible during review; the evidence summarized here is limited to the supplied abstract.

### Spin Chain with Continuous Exponential Symmetry: Topological Phase and Beyond
**Biao Lian, Zhen Bi — arXiv:2610.10682**

The familiar relation between a one-dimensional symmetry-protected phase and its two boundary modes assumes a particular spatial action of symmetry. What changes when neighboring sites carry exponentially different units of conserved charge? This paper studies spin chains in which that modulation makes the two ends inequivalent even though the protecting group remains closely related to an ordinary spin-rotation symmetry.

For an open chain of length $L$, the conserved charge is
\[
Q=\sum_{j=1}^{L}q^{L-j}S_j^z,
\]
with positive integer base $q$, together with a spin-flip symmetry that reverses $Q$. The authors analyze symmetric gapped states using symmetry constraints, an explicit valence-bond construction, and numerical spin-chain examples. The possibilities depend on the parity of $q$ and whether the physical spin $S$ is integer or half-odd integer.

For integer $S$, odd $q$ permits a Haldane-type protected phase, whereas even $q$ permits no nontrivial SPT phase of this type. For half-odd integer $S$, odd $q$ obstructs a unique symmetric gapped ground state. Even $q$ instead gives the authors’ “half-SPT”: a symmetric gapped phase with a protected mode at only one boundary and a reduced set of long-range string orders. Boundary conditions matter here: for $q\geq2$, closing the chain restricts the continuous open-chain symmetry to a size-dependent discrete subgroup.

**Background.** Charge conservation now permits a local process that raises the spin on site $j$ once while lowering it on site $j+1$ $q$ times: their changes in weighted charge cancel. This is the mechanism behind the quantum breakdown Hamiltonian, with terms $S_j^+(S_{j+1}^-)^q$ and their conjugates. Such terms need sufficiently large on-site spin to act nontrivially.

The solvable valence-bond construction makes the asymmetric edges intuitive. For $S=(q+1)/2$, each site is built from auxiliary spins $q/2$ and $1/2$, with bonds pairing components on neighboring sites into singlets of the modulated symmetry. An open chain leaves different auxiliary spins at its two ends. For even $q$, one is integer and the other half-integer, so only one carries the projective symmetry action that protects a boundary degeneracy. This explains how a one-sided protected mode can arise without contradicting the usual result for uniformly acting symmetries.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual advance is to expose how the spatial representation of symmetry enters the relation between bulk phases and boundary protection. The half-SPT construction supplies an explicit, analyzable departure from the usual paired-edge pattern. It connects symmetry classification to concrete spin-chain mechanisms; broader applications would require realizing the weighted-charge conservation and understanding how accurately it must be maintained for the one-sided protection to remain observable.
