## arXiv-2026-11-13 — Item 1

### Local enhancement of finite-strength code preparation: recoverability and Wilson coherence
**Tong Xu, Yuanchen Zhao, Dong E. Liu — arXiv:2611.14967**

A state prepared by imperfect stabilizer measurements can retain nonlocal order while failing to protect an unknown logical input. The distinction matters because finite measurement strength changes the encoding coherently: it does more than add a classical probability of reporting the wrong syndrome. The paper asks whether concentrating measurement accuracy in a small part of a code can repair this operational failure.

The obstruction is local. An error on a chosen logical support has a residual overlap with the imperfect codeword, with a sign that depends on the logical input. Independent errors can therefore leak logical information into environmental coherences. Accumulating these weak signals along a logical operator makes bounded measurement strength insufficient as the code grows.

For a fixed toric logical register and sufficiently weak local-stochastic noise, the authors prove that strengthening neighborhoods of selected logical supports restores asymptotically faithful recovery. For distance $d$, strength $O(\log d)$ suffices; the construction enhances $O(d\log d)$ checks, a vanishing fraction of the lattice. A second requirement remains: distant physical representatives of the same logical operator must act alike. Enhanced tubes restore this equivalence in the strict three-dimensional deconfined phase, whereas two-dimensional encodings can both be recoverable and still disagree on that action. These statements concern the specified conditioned, all-plus preparation and its subsequent error model.

**Background.** At finite strength, a check measurement acts as a filter: it changes the amplitudes of different check sectors without completely projecting away the unwanted sectors. Consequently, multiplying a logical string by stabilizers need not leave its action unchanged. The Wilson overlap compares two such representatives on one fixed encoding. Recovery asks whether noise can be reversed; this overlap asks whether substituting one physical implementation for another preserves a coherent logical operation. The intervening bulk can matter for the latter even after local leakage has been suppressed.

**Why it matters?** This establishes a useful separation between many-body order, recoverability, and coherent logical control. Its constructive result also shows that preparation resources need not be improved uniformly. For QEC and statistical-mechanical descriptions of noisy states, the central lesson is that a bulk phase diagnostic must be connected to the particular information-processing task it is supposed to certify.

## arXiv-2026-11-13 — Item 2

### Geometric Theory of Quasiperiodically Driven Systems
**Chengzhi Ye, Marin Bukov — arXiv:2611.16683**

Periodic driving admits an effective Hamiltonian because one can organize evolution around a repeated cycle. With several incommensurate frequencies, no such cycle exists. Simply replacing irrational frequency ratios by rational approximations can produce misleading limiting eigenstates, while the usual quasienergy shifts become dense. A framework for quasiperiodic dynamics must therefore identify what remains stationary without relying on a finite Floquet zone.

The authors organize evolution through dynamically invariant subspaces, described by projectors over the torus of drive phases. Their defining relation is $i\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla P=[H,P]$, where $P$ is the transported projector, $H$ the Hamiltonian, and $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ the frequency vector. The Hamiltonian moves these subspaces exactly as the drive phases advance. An average-energy operator labels them, while a gauge potential generates their transport. A variational construction in operator space on the frequency lattice provides a way to approximate these objects.

Two independent distinctions organize the resulting dynamics. A subspace can be one-dimensional or possess irreducible internal rotations; its bundle over the phase torus can also admit or obstruct a globally smooth basis. These possibilities give four classes. Conventional Floquet structure extends to the one-dimensional, topologically untwisted class. The other classes require geometric or nonabelian generalizations. Solvable examples and numerical studies illustrate the classification and the proposed onset of ergodicity through merging invariant spaces.

**Background.** An invariant subspace here moves with time; it need not be an eigenspace of the instantaneous Hamiltonian. Counterdiabatic transport is the part of the generator that moves its basis without allowing escape from it. Returning around drive-phase loops can then rotate states within a higher-dimensional subspace, producing nonabelian holonomy. Topological twisting is a separate obstruction: local basis choices exist, but they cannot be joined into one smooth basis over the whole torus. Projectors avoid that basis-choice problem. The frequency lattice represents Fourier harmonics of the drive, so its operator-space formulation can impose invariance throughout phase space rather than along a chosen sequence of approximate periods.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a structural framework for dynamics and control beyond periodic driving, with a clear explanation of why familiar Floquet constructions sometimes fail. Its usefulness for large interacting systems will depend on tractable approximations to the invariant operators. Nevertheless, it provides concrete objects with which to formulate Hamiltonian engineering, geometric transport, and ergodicity questions in quasiperiodically driven quantum simulators.

## arXiv-2026-11-13 — Item 3

### Going beyond the standard quasiparticle picture: Entanglement spreading after quenching from highly entangled states
**Elisa Sabadini, Vincenzo Alba — arXiv:2611.15409**

The quasiparticle picture connects entanglement growth after a quench to the separation of initially entangled excitations. In interacting integrable systems, its familiar quantitative form assumes a pair structure associated with special initial states. What survives when the initial state already contains a more extended pattern of entanglement?

The authors study $n$-rainbow states: repeated cells containing $n$ nested Bell pairs across $2n$ sites. As the cells grow, the local stationary state approaches infinite temperature, with corrections of order $1/n$. This local simplification conceals a substantial difference in entanglement dynamics. In the free XX chain, each cell produces correlated multiplets involving $2n$ quasiparticle species. Their individual correlations weaken, but collectively they suppress entanglement production; the asymptotic growth rate vanishes as $n\to\infty$.

In the interacting XXZ chain, the accessible time-dependent density matrix renormalization group simulations instead agree with a finite growth rate given by the exchange of thermodynamic entropy across the cut. The free-chain suppression and its characteristic oscillations are absent over the tested times. The paper also studies a junction between a rainbow state and an infinite-temperature state, proposing a formula that separates transported thermal entropy from entanglement generated on the initially pure side. The interacting conclusions remain numerical evidence over finite times, and the proposed loss of multiplet coherence through scattering is a mechanism to investigate rather than a derived result.

**Background.** For an entangled pair, its contribution across a cut is determined by whether its two members lie on opposite sides. A multiplet requires the entropy of the whole subset on either side; counting independently crossing pairs can overestimate that entropy. This is why small correlations between individual species can have a large collective effect. Yang–Yang entropy is the thermodynamic entropy of the stationary Bethe quasiparticle distribution. Matching its flux does not establish an actual microscopic pair decomposition of the evolving state.

**Why it matters?** The work separates local equilibration from the organization of entanglement and gives a controlled free-model benchmark against which an interacting surprise becomes visible. It broadens quench theory beyond the special initial states that make pair formulas exact. Understanding when scattering erases multiplet information would sharpen the connection between integrability, entropy transport, and entanglement hydrodynamics.

## arXiv-2026-11-13 — Item 4

### Learning Quantum Hamiltonians from the Dynamical Structure Factor
**Nisarga Paul, Thomas Schuster, Jason Alicea — arXiv:2611.15859**

A measured excitation spectrum often constrains a Hamiltonian only after repeated comparisons with expensive many-body simulations. This is a serious obstacle to identifying interactions in quantum simulators, where validating the implemented model can be as difficult as predicting its dynamics. The paper asks how much can instead be extracted directly from ordinary two-point spectroscopic data.

The central observation is that low-order spectral moments of the dynamical structure factor are short-time derivatives of correlation functions. These derivatives become nested commutators with the Hamiltonian, exposing local couplings without requiring a simulation of the entire spectrum. The authors develop complementary protocols using high-temperature expansions, strong-field polarization, and identities following from the fluctuation–dissipation theorem.

The last route is particularly direct for one- and two-body spin Hamiltonians. Both sides of a linear system, $A\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol b$, can be constructed from measured zeroth and first spectral moments, with the unknown couplings collected in the vector. Appropriate rank conditions then give identifiability, while conditioning controls robustness. The paper also establishes a useful limitation: at low temperature, interactions that affect only excited states inaccessible to the measured operator can become exponentially hard to learn. Neither a detailed spectrum nor dynamical data alone guarantees sensitivity to every coupling.

**Background.** A spectral moment integrates the response with a power of frequency as weight. Although the full response contains complicated many-body propagation, a fixed low-order moment probes only a bounded number of commutators and hence a finite neighborhood for a finite-range Hamiltonian. At high temperature, thermal averages become controlled polynomials in the couplings. A strong field supplies a different simple reference state. The fluctuation–dissipation route instead uses measured static correlations to evaluate coefficients that would otherwise require knowing the interacting state. Access to enough spin channels and a nonsingular response matrix are substantive requirements.

**Why it matters?** Hamiltonian learning becomes a spectroscopic inverse problem with explicit algebraic structure and failure conditions. This is a broadly useful method for quantum simulation: it can turn calibration and model validation into tasks that do not require solving the target many-body system first. The practical next step is to establish robustness under incomplete frequency coverage, instrumental resolution, and restricted polarization access.

## arXiv-2026-11-13 — Item 5

### Subsystem Flow: Quantum Error Correction, Petz Recovery, and Ergodic Inclusions
**Yidong Chen, Nima Lashkari, Kwing Lam Leung — arXiv:2611.15887**

A spatial subsystem in continuum QFT is naturally specified by its observable algebra, without an intrinsic tensor factorization or reduced density matrix. How can one formulate information loss and recoverability in that setting, starting from a region and a reference state rather than an engineered encoding?

The authors compare two intrinsic evolutions: modular flow in the larger algebra and modular flow inferred from the subsystem alone. Their mismatch defines subsystem flow. Exact reference-preserving recoverability corresponds to modular autonomy, meaning that the larger modular evolution preserves the smaller algebra. This recasts an established operator-algebraic criterion as an error-correction condition.

Away from autonomy, the infinitesimal mismatch measures relative-entropy loss. Following its orbit identifies both the algebra generated by the subsystem's history and a persistent core shared by all transported copies. Analytic continuation connects the same construction to Petz recovery and to new positive inclusion parent Hamiltonians. Their ground spaces encode the persistent core, while repeated recovery gives dissipative evolution toward it. The paper establishes strong generation results for standard QFT inclusions, but distinguishes this algebraic ergodicity from dynamical mixing. Its proposal to diagnose local chaos from inclusion spectra remains conjectural.

**Background.** For a full-rank finite-dimensional reference state, modular flow acts as $O\mapsto\rho^{it}O\rho^{-it}$. It is determined by the state and is generally different from physical Hamiltonian evolution. A conditional expectation projects the larger observable algebra onto the retained one while respecting its algebraic structure. When it preserves the reference state, its dual supplies a consistent extension of subsystem states that is exactly recoverable after restriction. Petz recovery provides a state-dependent reconstruction associated with information-preserving restriction. In QFT, modular operators replace density-matrix formulas, allowing these ideas to survive the absence of subsystem tensor factors. The persistent core consists of observables common to all modularly transported subsystem algebras, rather than simply observables fixed pointwise by the flow.

**Why it matters?** This is a conceptual bridge between algebraic QFT, quantum recovery, and emergent dynamics. The advance lies in organizing several information-theoretic and spectral constructions around one flow, including genuinely new parent-Hamiltonian results. It does not yet supply a practical decoder or a universal chaos diagnostic, but it offers a precise language for asking which subsystem information remains reconstructible in continuum theories.

## arXiv-2026-11-13 — Item 6

### Ensemble phase transitions in measurement-unitary hybrid quantum circuits
**Saptarshi Mandal, Sthitadhi Roy — arXiv:2611.17322**

A density matrix can remain featureless while the ensemble of states realizing it changes completely. Monitored circuits make this distinction physical: different measurement records prepare different conditional states, even when their average is unchanged. The paper asks whether the purification transition can be understood as a transition in this entire ensemble.

A reference qubit is initially entangled with a scrambled many-body system. Conditioning on measurements during the circuit produces a trajectory ensemble on the reference. Measuring the remaining system at the final boundary further resolves each trajectory into a projected ensemble. In Haar-random circuits, increasing the measurement rate exchanges the roles of these two sources of randomness. Below the purification transition, each reference trajectory remains nearly maximally mixed, while final measurements generate a Haar-distributed pure-state ensemble. Above it, bulk records themselves prepare a Haar ensemble of pure reference states, and final measurements have essentially nothing left to steer.

Higher-moment diagnostics and finite-size scaling locate these changes at the purification transition. Clifford circuits reveal an additional restriction: final measurements produce only a first-design ensemble, whereas the trajectory ensemble, when circuit randomness is also included, can reproduce the first three Haar moments. The distinction between averaging measurement records and averaging circuits is essential to these statements.

**Background.** The projected ensemble is defined separately within a fixed bulk trajectory. Its first moment is that trajectory's reduced reference state. Thus, a maximally mixed reference permits many very different pure-state ensembles; it does not imply Haar randomness. The mechanism here is a transfer of steering capacity between temporal boundaries and bulk measurements. Entanglement with the unmeasured system enables final-boundary steering, while purification consumes that resource. Non-stabilizer structure is additionally needed to distribute the resulting states beyond the restricted ensembles accessible to Clifford dynamics.

**Why it matters?** This adds a useful operational interpretation to measurement-induced transitions: they reorganize which measurement records can prepare a subsystem's state. It connects monitored dynamics to deep thermalization without mistaking a mixed first moment for a random ensemble. The numerical evidence supports this picture in the studied circuits; whether the same organization extends to less random Hamiltonian settings is an interesting broader question.

### Emergent Anyons in a Weakly Interacting Gauged Chern Insulator
**Sven Bachmann, Leonardo Goller, Marcello Porta — arXiv:2611.14658**

Gauging the charge parity of a Chern insulator is expected to turn its topological response into anyonic flux statistics. Effective field theory makes this mechanism plausible, but a microscopic derivation must establish that flux defects cost energy, the physical system stays gapped, and the proposed braiding operations actually act within its ground-state sector.

The authors carry out this program for a weakly interacting Qi–Wu–Zhang model coupled to a binary gauge field. Reflection positivity and chessboard estimates select a flat magnetic vacuum and bound the energy of flux defects in a controlled parameter neighborhood. Together with the interacting fermionic gap, these estimates isolate four locally indistinguishable physical ground states whose splitting vanishes rapidly with size.

The many-body Chern number $C$ then determines the algebra of dressed noncontractible flux loops. In the thermodynamic limit, $W_2W_1=e^{i\pi C/2}W_1W_2$. For $C=1$, the relative phase is $i$. The construction therefore connects a microscopic interacting Hamiltonian to a nontrivial flux-loop algebra rather than assuming that algebra from a topological effective action. The gauge fluxes are conserved in the studied Hamiltonian; the result does not cover arbitrary electric-field dynamics or strong matter interactions.

**Background.** A bare gauge-string operation changes a torus holonomy, but it also changes the boundary conditions experienced by the fermions. Quasi-adiabatic continuation dresses that operation with a spatially localized response of the gapped matter, transporting its ground state consistently. The commutator of two dressed loops becomes Berry transport around a quarter of the boundary-twist torus. Locality makes the curvature asymptotically uniform, so that quarter captures the stated fraction of the Chern flux. The separate flux-energy bound is what makes this ground-sector geometry describe a stable physical phase.

**Why it matters?** The significance is the controlled microscopic connection between interacting band topology, a many-body gap, and anyonic statistics. It extends the role of exactly solvable models by proving the mechanism in an interacting setting with restricted but explicit assumptions. For topological QFT and QEC, it supplies a concrete example in which both local indistinguishability and logical loop algebra follow from the Hamiltonian.

## arXiv-2026-11-13 — Item 8

### Persistent-mediator architecture for universal fault-tolerant quantum computation with color codes
**Zhi-Cheng He, Jingbo Wang, Dong E. Liu — arXiv:2611.16691**

Two-dimensional color codes offer protected Clifford operations, while suitable three-dimensional color codes supply a transversal T gate. Accessing both capabilities usually requires changing the data encoding or consuming an auxiliary encoded state. Could the higher-dimensional block instead remain protected and serve repeated operations on a separate two-dimensional register?

The paper constructs a closed logical cycle with a persistent mediator. A matched cross-dimensional controlled-Z couples the blocks; two complementary joint logical measurements, a transversal T layer on the mediator, and outcome-dependent Clifford corrections transfer the T action to the data. A final disentangling operation returns the mediator to its initial logical plus state. All outcomes are accepted, and neither data block is destructively read out.

The main physical construction is membrane-like lattice surgery for the joint logical X parity. Its check updates preserve the merged logical information and spatial distance. Complementary string-like surgery supplies the other parity measurement. Simulations at several distances show error suppression and sequential reuse for a Clifford surrogate of the cycle. These tests preserve the interface geometry and syndrome extraction but replace the non-Clifford layer, so they do not establish the fault distance or threshold of the actual noisy T gadget.

**Background.** In a three-dimensional color code, a logical X operator can occupy a membrane. A surgery procedure designed to join string operators therefore cannot simply measure the required cross-dimensional parity. The proposed interface adds face and edge ancillas, extending Z checks so that new X checks commute with them through paired overlaps. Multiplying the new X checks gives the desired joint logical operator. Splitting removes the temporary interface degrees of freedom and restores separate encoded modules. The smallest matched pair is the seven-qubit Steane code and fifteen-qubit Reed–Muller code. Returning the mediator's logical state does not remove its physical errors; continued correction remains necessary.

**Why it matters?** This is a concrete architectural alternative for accessing complementary gate capabilities while retaining distinct encodings. The contribution is the protected interface that closes the reuse cycle, rather than reuse at the abstract circuit level alone. Whether it reduces overhead requires a full non-Clifford fault analysis and a comparison that includes maintaining the three-dimensional block between calls, extraction ancillas, connectivity, and possible parallel preparation in consumptive schemes.

### Higher-Group Statistics on Lattices
**Chen Yang, Yu-An Chen — arXiv:2611.16962**

Braiding statistics becomes richer when moving one defect changes another: a domain wall may conjugate an anyon, and deforming a string may transfer a particle. Studying each defect species separately then misses physical information. The challenge is to define closed statistical processes whose phases remain meaningful despite arbitrary local choices in the hopping operators.

The authors construct higher-group statistics directly from lattice moves. A valid process restores the complete configuration of every coupled defect species, while its phase is unchanged by allowed local redefinitions of the moves. They give algorithms that generate such processes from locality relations and a complementary construction that starts from homology classes, avoiding enumeration of an exponentially large configuration space.

Two examples make the additional information tangible. Charge-wall processes distinguish two order-four toric codes with identical intrinsic anyon statistics and the same charge-conjugation symmetry; gauging that symmetry yields the dihedral and quaternion groups, respectively. In three dimensions, a closed Kitaev chain has a loop-flip process with phase $+i$. Tracking intermediate exchange of fermion parity with physical fermions refines the usual sign-valued loop-flip information. The general classification theorem concerns the specified spherical lattice framework and complete set of moves.

**Background.** A higher group describes coupled conservation and composition laws for defects of different dimensions. Its role here is operational: a membrane move that changes a loop may also create or move point defects, so closing the loop geometry alone is insufficient. To isolate a statistical phase, one composes moves until both geometry and all lower-dimensional labels return, then checks that local phase conventions cancel. The homology construction packages the spacetime history of these moves into a closed cycle; pairing it with anomaly data computes the same measurable phase.

**Why it matters?** This gives explicit microscopic diagnostics for information that ordinary particle braiding leaves unresolved. It connects higher-symmetry and anomaly classifications to operator sequences in lattice models, including structures relevant to topological codes. The contribution is both conceptual and methodological: it explains what must be tracked and supplies ways to construct the processes, rather than only assigning an abstract invariant afterward.

### Restoring Heisenberg Scaling in Hamiltonian Estimation despite Noise Uncertainty
**Yan Luo, Lingna Wang, Hongzhen Chen — arXiv:2611.15487**

Correcting decoherence does not necessarily restore precise sensing. If an unknown noise parameter produces the same coherent response as the Hamiltonian parameter being estimated, a perfectly coherent output can still leave the signal statistically ambiguous. This paper determines when that ambiguity destroys Heisenberg scaling and how an additional noisy system can remove it.

A spin example exposes the mechanism. Error correction detects transverse spin flips, but correcting a flip about an unknown axis also adds an unknown logical phase. The average accumulated correction phase looks like a frequency shift. Only fluctuations in the recorded error count distinguish the two contributions, leaving frequency estimation with standard-quantum-limit scaling despite preserved coherence.

For general Markovian dynamics with arbitrary parameter-independent control and noiseless ancillas, the authors derive a necessary and sufficient condition for an individual Hamiltonian parameter to retain quadratic information growth. Its generator must remain distinguishable from noise and from coherent contributions of all nuisance parameters. They also determine the optimal asymptotic coefficient. A signal-insensitive reference exposed to an independent copy of the same parameterized noise can restore the known-noise optimum through a joint encoding. The reference must share the relevant unknown noise parameters; an arbitrary extra noisy qubit is insufficient.

**Background.** The Lindblad span contains operator directions whose signal effects cannot be separated from correctable noise. With known noise, a useful sensing generator must have a component outside this span. Unknown noise directions enlarge the indistinguishable space because changing a jump operator can induce a coherent logical response. Joint encoding with a suitable reference makes nuisance responses cancel between opposite logical codewords while retaining the sensor's signal response. This is cancellation of parameter sensitivity, even though the physical noise events on the two systems are independent. The optimality statements are local estimation results for each target parameter, not a guarantee that every multiparameter optimum is simultaneously attainable.

**Why it matters?** The paper identifies an estimation obstruction that coherence-based performance tests can miss. It extends QEC-assisted metrology from correcting physical disturbances to preserving identifiable signal directions. The reference construction is a substantive design principle, while its practical value will depend on matching the unknown noise dependence and implementing the rapid correction and control assumed by the bounds.

### Quantization Breaks Classical Mpemba Constraints
**Yue Liu, Tan Van Vu, Frédéric van Wijland, Hisao Hayakawa — arXiv:2611.15349**

A hotter state can sometimes relax faster because it excites the slowest decay mode less strongly. Establishing a specifically quantum mechanism for this Mpemba effect requires comparing quantum and classical dynamics derived from the same microscopic model. Comparing unrelated master equations can otherwise attribute differences to quantization that actually come from different approximations.

Starting from the Caldeira–Leggett model, the authors compare weak-coupling quantum population dynamics with its classical energy-diffusion limit. For thermal initial states, they prove that classical energy diffusion forbids the asymptotic Mpemba reversal in single wells and symmetric double wells. Local diffusion forces population to pass through intermediate energies, imposing monotonicity on the relevant slow-mode overlap.

Quantum transitions can bypass intermediate levels. In an anharmonic well, position matrix elements permit nonadjacent jumps, and a suitable bath spectrum can make high-energy populations relax through these direct pathways. Numerical examples exhibit the resulting reversal, while decreasing the effective Planck constant suppresses it and restores the classical constraint as $\hbar\to0$. Discrete levels alone are insufficient: the harmonic oscillator, with only adjacent transitions, does not display the mechanism.

**Background.** The Pauli master equation evolves energy populations after the Born–Markov and secular approximations; the states in this study remain diagonal in energy. Its classical counterpart is an equation for diffusion in continuous energy after weak-damping and orbital averaging. This is a particular classical limit, distinct from overdamped position diffusion. The no-go result therefore does not prohibit every classical single-well Mpemba effect. The slowest nonstationary mode has a temperature-dependent initial amplitude. Nonadjacent quantum transitions can reshape that mode so that raising the preparation temperature reduces its amplitude, even though the hotter state starts farther from equilibrium.

**Why it matters?** This is a clear example in which quantization changes the connectivity of relaxation pathways rather than merely changing rates or adding coherence. Its controlled quantum-classical comparison makes the conclusion more informative than another demonstration of anomalous relaxation. The mechanism also suggests how structured environments could be used to engineer relaxation, provided the weak-coupling description remains valid.

### Information Thermodynamics in Closed Quantum Many-Body Dynamics
**Yusuke O. Nakai, Shoki Sugimoto, Ryusuke Hamazaki — arXiv:2611.16329**

A pure many-body state can look thermal to every local probe while retaining enough global structure to yield extensive work under unrestricted operations. Which description governs a realistic measurement-and-feedback protocol: its local thermality or its global purity?

The authors prove an operational equivalence between microscopic thermal equilibrium and the corresponding canonical ensemble. With local measurements, finite-time evolution under local Hamiltonians, and feedback terms depending only on nearby outcomes, applying the same protocol extracts the same work density in the thermodynamic limit. The proof controls the support of outcome-averaged energy observables with a Lieb–Robinson bound, despite the nonlocal conditional state update caused by individual measurements.

For the canonical state, an exact identity expresses extracted work through measurement-acquired information, the Holevo information remaining in the final branches, and the final state's relative entropy from equilibrium. The paper then shows precisely how the equivalence can fail: allowing each local controller to use distant outcomes exposes hidden long-range correlations. A state built from spatially separated GHZ triplets yields an extensive advantage under noisy measurement and collective processing of the classical records. Work here means the system's energy reduction; apparatus operation and memory-reset costs are outside that accounting.

**Background.** Microscopic thermal equilibrium requires local reduced states to agree with thermal ones; it does not require a globally mixed state. In the triplet example, any sufficiently small contiguous region contains at most one member of each GHZ triplet and is exactly maximally mixed. Combining three distant noisy outcomes estimates their shared spin more reliably than a controller restricted to one local record. This explains why locality of the quantum Hamiltonian alone is insufficient: nonlocal classical communication can make an outcome-dependent energy observable sensitive to correlations outside a local light cone. Holevo information measures residual distinguishability between final branches, accounting for information that has not been converted into system-energy reduction.

**Why it matters?** This gives a precise many-body statement of when local thermal physics controls feedback-assisted work extraction. It also identifies classical communication as an operational resource that can unlock correlations invisible to local equilibrium tests. The result connects measurement dynamics, locality, and thermodynamics in a form relevant to quantum simulators preparing thermal-looking pure states, without treating their unrestricted global ergotropy as freely accessible.

### Observation of Genuine Multi-Time Quantum Memory
**Konstantin Beyer, Valentin Link, Surihan Sean Borjigin, Qian Cao, Orion Lee, Kater Murch, Igor Pikovski, Philip Taranto — arXiv:2611.17268**

Memory in an open system need not be quantum: a classical record stored by the environment can influence subsequent evolution. Even establishing that every pair of observed dynamical maps admits such an explanation leaves a further question. Can those pairwise explanations be assembled into one consistent history?

The authors prove that they need not be. They derive a three-time witness for qubit channels and construct an exact example in which every pair has a classical-memory realization, while the triple does not. The first channel is amplitude damping, followed by channels that preserve distinguishability along different Bloch-sphere directions. Their combined requirements cannot be satisfied by one evolving classical record.

A superconducting-qubit experiment realizes continuous dynamics with an exchange-coupled memory qubit and a mixture of opposite local drives. Channel tomography at different evolution times supplies the witness without interventions during the evolution. The measured three-time violation is accompanied by numerical classical decompositions for all measured pairs in the relevant observation window. That time-window qualification matters: extending the observations can reveal pairwise quantum memory that was previously hidden.

**Background.** A classical-memory model may branch into different quantum operations, store the branch label, and condition later channels on the accumulated labels. Each pair can admit a different decomposition; genuine multi-time quantum memory arises when no single sequence of such updates fits all the channels simultaneously. The witness combines the initial channel's displacement of the maximally mixed state with later preservation of distinguishability along incompatible directions. These are maps from a common initial time, obtained by ordinary channel tomography. The method tests their joint compatibility rather than reconstructing the full process through a sequence of intermediate interventions.

**Why it matters?** The experiment demonstrates a theoretical principle with a minimal accessible system: quantum memory can be a consistency obstruction visible only across several times. This is stronger than observing information backflow or failing to find a pairwise witness. It suggests that noise characterization for quantum devices should sometimes test temporal compatibility across multiple snapshots, while keeping the distinction between the observed channel family and a fully reconstructed multi-time process explicit.

## arXiv-2026-11-13 — Item 14

### CSS Circuits: useful quantum circuits that can be simulated with $O(1)$ cost per gate
**Craig Gidney, Cody Jones — arXiv:2611.14944**

Large QEC simulations are affordable partly because repeated samples can often be generated by tracking Pauli errors relative to one noiseless reference sample. Atom loss complicates this strategy: losing a qubit removes subsequent gates, changing the underlying circuit itself. Recomputing reference behavior for each changed circuit can become expensive.

The authors identify a restricted but useful gate set for which that obstacle disappears. CSS circuits use X and Z gates, X- and Z-basis measurements, and CNOTs, including adaptive and probabilistic choices. After intended Pauli gates are absorbed into the tracked frame, every resulting circuit has a valid all-zero reference sample. Sampling therefore requires only two classical bits per qubit and constant work for each gate. The family still supports teleportation, error correction, and entanglement purification.

The simulator is a local hidden-variable model. Bell-test constructions show that adding Hadamard gates, phase gates, or controlled-Z gates cannot be handled by simply extending this same local representation. This is a structural limitation of the construction, rather than a general lower bound against every constant-cost simulation method for larger gate sets.

**Background.** The two bits encode bit-flip and phase-flip components of a Pauli frame. CNOT propagates the former forward and the latter backward. A Z measurement reports the bit-flip flag while randomizing the conjugate phase flag; an X measurement does the reverse. The key simplification is that a valid reference outcome remains known even when earlier random choices or loss change which allowed gates occur. Ordinary flip simulation lacks that guarantee for arbitrary Clifford circuits.

**Why it matters?** This is a small, transparent algorithmic result with a potentially substantial effect on loss-aware QEC benchmarking. It also isolates a useful boundary within stabilizer computation: several entanglement-assisted tasks survive in a family whose measurement statistics admit a particularly simple classical description. Applicability depends on expressing the actual circuit and loss model within that restricted family.

### U(1)-Symmetric Spin SYK Models: Ordering, Chaos, and Transport
**Shiyong Guo, Enrico Rinaldi, Jad C. Halimeh, Brian Swingle — arXiv:2611.15896**

The fermionic Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev model offers controlled access to strongly interacting dynamics, but encoding its interactions on qubits introduces long parity strings. A spin analogue avoids those strings. The important question is whether it preserves the desired physics rather than merely making a different chaotic model easier to implement.

The authors construct random charge-conserving interactions among $N$ spins, with $q$ spins participating in each term. **Zachary Bogorad, Roni Harnik — arXiv:2611.14641**

A quantum search algorithm needs a coherent, repeatable oracle, whereas wave-like dark matter produces a signal with finite coherence time. This appears to obstruct applying amplitude amplification to a broad frequency search, especially when detecting the weak signal requires integration over many coherence times. The paper shows how a dispersive response can evade that obstacle in principle.

A cavity is coupled to a qubit register so that its resonance frequency depends on the register state. In the rotating-frame oscillator, the dispersive energy shift is $\Delta E=-|\mathcal E|^2/\delta$. It depends on the squared drive amplitude and detuning, rather than the drive's fluctuating phase. Adiabatically following the displaced oscillator and switching the effective interaction on and off returns the cavity to vacuum while leaving a frequency-dependent phase on the register. Long integration can therefore generate a repeatable quantum phase even though the underlying field loses phase coherence.

Quantum signal processing converts this smooth response into an approximate Grover oracle. Amplitude amplification then gives a search-time improvement proportional to $\sqrt{N_{\rm bins}}$ over a sequential scan at fixed sensitivity, up to the stated overheads. Finite signal coherence still affects the sensitivity scaling; the proposal does not recover every advantage available for a perfectly coherent field. Its toy implementation also requires register coherence throughout the quantum search at error rates far beyond present capabilities.

**Background.** The frequency register coherently controls cavity detuning rather than representing independently measured frequency channels. A useful oracle must mark the signal bin while leaving other branches almost unchanged. Quantum signal processing synthesizes a threshold-like transformation of the accumulated phase through repeated controlled operations and rotations. The proposed implementation can require photon blockade or another nonlinearity to restrict the cavity's relevant levels. Adiabatic tracking also requires detunings large compared with the signal linewidth; the binning construction avoids relying on the uncontrolled near-resonant branches.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual result is that a stochastic physical signal can participate coherently in a quantum computation through a phase-insensitive response. This connects quantum algorithms to sensing at the level of the physical oracle, where ideal query advantages often become difficult to realize. The paper is a proof of principle; practical impact requires a detector and error-correction architecture compatible with the required coherent interactions.
