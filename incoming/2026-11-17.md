## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 1

### Dressed higher-form symmetries in mixed state quantum matter
**Tibor Rakovszky, Sarang Gopalakrishnan, Curt von Keyserlingk — arXiv:2611.19442**

Topological order can survive noise even when its original loop operators cease to be exact symmetries. Defining their replacements is subtle: pulling an operator back through a recovery channel generally preserves neither unitarity nor the algebra that encodes logical operations and particle statistics.

The authors identify a sufficient condition that repairs this problem. A decoder assigns one definite Pauli correction to each syndrome. Pulling a bare symmetry through this deterministic recovery multiplies it by a syndrome-dependent phase operator, preserving unitarity on the original Hilbert space. Suitable quasi-local decoders then preserve the logical algebra and excitation statistics at scales larger than their recovery range. The paper constructs such recovery schemes for noisy toric-code states and finite-temperature examples, with approximation errors controlled in the thermodynamic limit.

**Background.** The dressed operator is $\widetilde W=\mathcal R^\dagger(W)$: measuring it before recovery reproduces the expectation of the bare operator afterward. Different corrections for the same syndrome can contribute opposite phases; averaging them can shrink a unitary into a contraction. A unique correction retains a definite phase instead. Strong symmetry requires $W\rho=\rho$, whereas weak symmetry requires only $W\rho W^\dagger=\rho$. Preserving the stronger operator structure is what makes the inherited braiding algebra meaningful.

**Why it matters?** This is a conceptual advance in connecting error correction to mixed-state phases. Recoverability becomes a way to construct physical symmetry operators, rather than merely certify that information can be restored. The particularly useful feature is the simultaneous control of locality, unitarity, and logical algebra. Extending the construction beyond its stabilizer-based recovery setting would test how broadly this organizing principle applies.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 2

### Calculating correlation functions in integrable spin chains from local conserved charges and currents
**Kohei Fukai — arXiv:2611.20181**

Knowing an integrable model's spectrum does not make its local correlations easy to calculate. Even simple-looking factorized answers can require elaborate derivations, leaving unclear why they simplify or which excited states they describe.

Fukai gives an operator-level explanation using current minors: carefully ordered, antisymmetrized products of conserved densities and currents. Their expectation values in a nondegenerate joint eigenstate are determinants of one-point density and current averages. Continuity equations eliminate the intervening off-diagonal contributions. Decomposing an observable into these building blocks therefore converts a correlation calculation into algebra and known current averages.

For the XXZ chain, the method proves previously conjectured short-distance formulas, including finite-volume excited states. Because the decompositions are identities in the Temperley–Lieb algebra, they also apply in other representations. The claim that every local operator in that algebra admits the required decomposition remains a conjecture.

**Background.** Generalized currents describe the transport of one conserved charge under evolution generated by another charge. They form a matrix whose additional density column permits determinant-like combinations. A two-row minor subtracts two exchanged density-current products, with a necessary one-site shift. These shifts make the lattice continuity equations compatible with the antisymmetrization. The Temperley–Lieb algebra supplies representation-independent relations among the local interaction operators.

**Why it matters?** The main value is explanatory as well as computational: factorization follows from local conservation laws rather than appearing as a fortunate outcome of the Bethe ansatz. It creates a direct bridge between quantities familiar from integrable transport and microscopic correlations. A constructive proof of the proposed spanning property would substantially enlarge the method's reach.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 3

### Static Bounds on Thermal Fast Scrambling
**Yixuan Ma, Weikang Li, Dong-Ling Deng — arXiv:2611.20958**

The thermal chaos bound constrains an exponential growth rate under a factorization assumption. Establishing a corresponding scrambling-time bound directly in a disordered Hamiltonian's own Gibbs state is harder: the state and dynamics depend on the same random couplings.

The authors replace the real-time assumption with static information about the operator-size distribution of the square root of the Gibbs state. This controls thermal correlators on the boundaries of a complex-time analytic strip; analyticity then limits their subsequent growth. For the Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev model and specified random local-interaction spin ensembles, they obtain, at every fixed inverse temperature $\beta$, the high-probability bound $t_c\ge(1-\epsilon)\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\log N$ for any fixed $0<\epsilon<1$ and sufficiently large $N$. The result concerns fixed Majorana pairs in the fermionic model and a fixed source averaged over targets in the general spin result.

**Background.** Expand $\sqrt{\rho}$ in products of Majorana operators, or analogous operator strings. The number of elementary factors is the operator's size; squared expansion coefficients define its distribution. Local probes test this distribution through conjugation. The paper turns the resulting static deviations into bounds on out-of-time-order correlators. The scrambling time here is a first crossing of a fixed correlator threshold, so the theorem does not require fitting an exponential-growth window.

**Why it matters?** This strengthens the foundations of thermal fast scrambling by establishing the expected logarithmic timescale without assuming the real-time behavior one seeks to constrain. The static-to-dynamic strategy is especially interesting for non-equilibrium theory. Its further promise depends on finding comparably strong equilibrium estimates in other interacting systems; the theorem itself supplies a lower bound, not a demonstration of saturation.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 4

### Quantum Cooling by Classical Shadows
**Xuepeng Wang, Debanjan Chowdhury — arXiv:2611.18834**

Imaginary-time evolution suppresses excited states, but implementing this nonunitary cooling on quantum hardware usually requires postselection, repeated state estimation, or recursively constructed operations. This paper asks whether the measurement randomness of the device can itself generate the cooling process.

Its shadow chain uses one randomized measurement snapshot to specify the next unitary update. Measurement destroys the current state, so the device restarts and replays the previously recorded updates before proceeding. The saved classical history replaces a recursive preparation tree with a reproducible chain. In a controlled small-step limit, the conditional drift follows imaginary-time cooling. The authors derive bias and trajectory-variance bounds and test thermal observables in small spin chains.

**Background.** A snapshot in classical shadows specifies a rank-one projector associated with a randomly rotated measurement outcome. Its Born-weighted average contains the current density matrix plus an identity contribution. Taking a commutator with the Hamiltonian removes the identity and produces the cooling direction. However, this average is diluted by the register's Hilbert-space dimension. The effective cooling step is consequently exponentially small in the number of qubits. The trajectory concentration bounds also have a specified small-step regime.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual contribution is an operational connection between measurement records, auxiliary-field histories, and imaginary-time dynamics. It separates the cost of lowering temperature from an exponential system-size cost that remains substantial. This is an interesting foundation for quantum simulation methods, particularly if structured measurement ensembles can improve the dimension dependence. The present construction should be assessed as a new mechanism rather than an established scalable cooling advantage.

[arXiv 2611.18834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18834)

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 5

### Emptiness formation in quantum fluids: a hydrodynamic solution
**Alexander G. Abanov, Boris A. Khanikati — arXiv:2611.21581**

An interval containing no particles is an extreme fluctuation of a quantum fluid. Small-amplitude hydrodynamics cannot describe the evacuation of a macroscopic region; one needs a nonlinear optimal fluctuation that connects the empty interval to the surrounding equilibrium fluid.

The authors construct that fluctuation through an auxiliary-kernel method. A kernel representing the equation of state generates the modes needed to reduce the imaginary-time boundary problem to one integral equation. Its solution determines the density, current, vacuum boundary, and leading probability exponent. For the Lieb–Liniger gas, the method derives a previously conjectured rate equation and connects it to an existing elliptic solution. Hydrodynamic numerics support the resulting vacuum geometry.

For an interval of half-length $R$ at background density $\rho_0$, the result concerns $-\log P(R)=(\rho_0R)^2f+o(R^2)$, where $f$ is the dimensionless leading rate. Its identification with the microscopic probability assumes that gradient and endpoint corrections remain subleading.

**Background.** A hydrodynamic instanton is the optimal density-current history in imaginary time, constrained to be empty at the observation time. A hodograph transformation trades spatial coordinates for fluid variables and simplifies the nonlinear equations. The auxiliary kernel then encodes the equation of state in a form resembling Bethe-ansatz dressing. It need not represent actual microscopic scattering: the method requires this thermodynamic representation, rather than microscopic integrability itself.

**Why it matters?** This provides a concrete route from equilibrium thermodynamics to a strongly non-Gaussian fluctuation and its spatial structure. It is particularly appealing at the intersection of integrability and non-equilibrium physics. The broader opportunity is to identify nonintegrable fluids admitting useful kernel representations, while checking the saddle and boundary-layer assumptions needed to recover microscopic probabilities.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 6

### On reflection positivity of lattice chiral fermions
**Zhiyao Lu, Shu-Heng Shao — arXiv:2611.20084**

Euclidean overlap fermions retain an exact lattice version of chirality, apparently offering something unavailable in a conventional local Hamiltonian regulator of a single anomalous Weyl fermion. Understanding this difference requires checking whether the Euclidean theory reconstructs a positive Hilbert space.

Lu and Shao show that the full overlap Weyl fermion fails the required reflection-positivity structure in two and four dimensions at finite lattice spacing. The obstruction is topological: a local, translation-compatible reflection respecting the charge would require a globally nonvanishing section of a nontrivial Weyl bundle. In two dimensions, opening a boundary also makes the action nonlocal, consistent with the continuum gravitational anomaly.

The distinction from the overlap Dirac fermion is instructive. That theory is reflection positive, but its compact axial symmetry is not reflection invariant; a reflection-invariant axial symmetry is instead noncompact.

**Background.** Reflection positivity makes Euclidean time reflection into a positive inner product, enabling Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction. The overlap chiral projector depends on the lattice Dirac operator and relates fields at different times. Reflection must preserve this constrained field space. A restricted subalgebra of projected observables can satisfy positivity without providing a local reconstruction of the complete Weyl field algebra; the paper carefully separates these statements.

**Why it matters?** This clarifies an important conceptual boundary between Euclidean regularization, exact chiral symmetry, and Hamiltonian realizability. It is relevant both to nonperturbative QFT and to attempts to simulate chiral theories. The finite-spacing obstruction identifies which structural requirement needs scrutiny; assessing continuum reconstruction or anomaly-canceling collections requires the corresponding additional analysis.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 7

### Mixed-state topological transitions with hidden long-range correlations
**Ze-Min Huang, Frank Pollmann, Sebastian Diehl — arXiv:2611.20159**

A decohering symmetry-protected topological state can lose its topological response while ordinary local correlations remain short-ranged. The usual diagnostic of criticality then seems absent. This paper identifies where the divergent correlations have gone.

The authors study a symmetry-twisted ensemble, in which each contribution is weighted by its symmetry charge. Its transfer matrix can become gapless independently of the ordinary transfer matrix. A change in the topological response is accompanied by nondecaying twisted correlations and a singular twisted partition function. The mechanism is demonstrated in a decohered cluster state and a spin-1 chain, with simulated measurement snapshots showing access at mesoscopic sizes.

**Background.** The twisted expectation is $\langle O\rangle_U=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho UO)/\operatorname{Tr}(\rho U)$, with $U$ a global symmetry operator. These weights can have signs or phases, so this is not generally a positive statistical ensemble. Charge-resolved measurements allow the weighting to be performed afterward. With only average symmetry, the twisted transfer matrix is not constrained to share the ordinary matrix's spectrum; this permits a critical mode invisible to conventional correlations. The normalization can be small, making accurate estimation expensive.

**Why it matters?** The paper offers a useful physical account of an otherwise puzzling mixed-state transition: criticality survives in a symmetry-conditioned diagnostic. It also connects the theoretical response invariant to measurements rather than leaving it solely as an abstract classification tool. The proposed mesoscopic tests are meaningful, while the potentially exponential sampling cost limits straightforward extrapolation to large systems.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 8

### Relaxation Critical Dynamics of Strong-to-Weak Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking
**Jiaqiang Li, Xianquan Yan, Han-Ze Li, Shuai Yin, Shuo Liu — arXiv:2611.20581**

Critical relaxation can remember how a state was prepared, even after equal-time observables have acquired their critical scaling. Mixed states offer initial conditions unavailable to pure states, raising the possibility of distinct temporal boundary universality.

The paper makes this precise in a monitored circuit with measurements and recorded dephasing locations. Two symmetry diagnostics map to nested bond-percolation problems. An initially ordered diagnostic connects the initial row into one cluster; a disordered one leaves that row free. A state with strong-to-weak symmetry breaking imposes different boundaries on the two graphs, a combination impossible for a pure state.

Autocorrelation exponents separate into initial and observation contributions. At the respective critical points, the mixed initial state gives initial dimensions of one third for the weak-symmetry diagnostic and zero for the strong-symmetry diagnostic. Stabilizer simulations support the predictions. The same geometry determines how an initially stored bit becomes inaccessible from the system while remaining recoverable with environmental information.

**Background.** A wired boundary joins all initial sites in a percolation graph; a free boundary adds no such connections. An autocorrelation asks whether an initial point connects to a later point and therefore probes both temporal endpoints. An equal-time correlation probes the observation slice instead. The two graphs are nested because dephasing connects one replicated field while projective measurements can connect both or neither.

**Why it matters?** This is an exactly controlled example of non-equilibrium boundary criticality that depends essentially on mixedness. It goes beyond assigning static phases by explaining relaxation and information loss from specific initial conditions. The model's exact mapping makes it a useful benchmark for testing whether analogous preparation-dependent exponents persist beyond percolation-solvable monitored dynamics.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 9

### Decoding SO(5) Deconfined Multicriticality: Conformal Bootstrap and Fuzzy-Sphere Spectroscopy
**Zhijin Li, Tinghong Shen — arXiv:2611.20214**

The apparent transition between Néel and valence-bond-solid order exhibits extensive scaling without an agreed asymptotic explanation. A real multicritical CFT and slow flow near complex fixed points can produce deceptively similar finite-size behavior. Comparing a few exponents is therefore insufficient.

The authors compare a candidate SO(5) solution of mixed-correlator bootstrap constraints with independent fuzzy-sphere spectra and matrix elements, alongside existing quantum Monte Carlo data. They identify 52 conformal primaries at ten Landau orbitals and compare both dimensions and three-point coefficients across the approaches. This gives a substantially richer consistency test than matching energy levels alone.

A relevant symmetry-singlet scalar supports a multicritical interpretation. Its fuzzy-sphere dimension is $\Delta_s=2.816$ at $N=12$, but remains appreciably above the bootstrap estimate. The authors favor a real unitary CFT while explicitly leaving quantitative continuum tests and more general complex-fixed-point explanations open.

**Background.** SO(5) combines the three Néel components and two valence-bond-solid components into one vector. A relevant singlet preserves that symmetry while driving the theory away from criticality, so reaching the fixed point can require an additional tuning. Fuzzy-sphere regularization replaces fields on a sphere with finitely many Landau orbitals. Radial quantization relates excitation energies to operator dimensions; special conformal generators distinguish primaries from descendants without identifying them by proximity to bootstrap predictions.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a stronger, interaction-sensitive test of a proposed strongly coupled QFT. Agreement of operator couplings is more discriminating than approximate conformal towers alone. This is compelling progress toward resolving deconfined criticality, although larger-system control of the singlet and the numerical bootstrap assumptions remain important for turning the proposed identification into a settled continuum picture.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 10

### Readout errors limit how far adaptive quantum circuits can entangle
**Chenfeng Cao, Ruge Lin — arXiv:2611.20069**

Measurement and feedforward can join short entangled pairs across an arbitrarily long chain in constant quantum depth. This shortcut depends on knowing the measurement parities: errors in the classical reports eventually erase the information needed to correct the endpoints.

Assuming ideal gates and independent readout-bit flips at a fixed nonzero rate below one half, the authors prove a finite entangling reach. On square-lattice layouts with a unique route and bounded qubits per site, a depth-$D$ circuit leaves sufficiently distant endpoints exactly separable beyond $\exp[O(D^3)]$, for every reported record, including rare postselected records.

A matching nondestructive parity measurement reaches $\exp[\Theta(D^3)]$ by distributing spare qubits near each join and repeatedly reading copied outcomes. A chain permits only $\exp[\Theta(D^2)]$. More generally, constant-depth entanglement across graph distance $L$ requires at least order $L\log L$ qubits.

**Background.** A depth-limited neighborhood around a join provides a bounded number of independent reports. In two dimensions, its area supplies roughly depth squared spatial copies, each supporting roughly depth readouts. Instantaneously broadcasting one report does not add independent evidence. The proof tracks an unavoidable uncertainty contribution across many cuts; a unique route forces the protocol to cross every cut.

**Why it matters?** This establishes a physical resource limit for adaptive circuits, beyond gate depth alone. It also explains why the placement of ancillary qubits matters: a large reservoir far from the joins cannot supply the same local readout redundancy. The result is directly relevant to measurement-assisted simulation and logical operations, with the geometry and noise assumptions defining the theorem's scope.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 11

### Algebraic instantons and non-Gaussian vacuum in quantum mechanics
**Rahim Aziz, Hao Geng, Thomas Schaefer, Mithat Ünsal — arXiv:2611.19761**

Textbook instanton calculus assumes harmonic fluctuations around each vacuum. When a minimum is quartic, the harmonic frequency vanishes, the instanton develops algebraic tails, and the usual determinant formula becomes infrared divergent. The tunnelling problem itself nevertheless has a finite answer.

The authors resolve this in the periodic potential $V(x)=16\lambda\sin^4(x/2)$, with angular coordinate $x$ and positive coupling $\lambda$. They treat the barrier-crossing core semiclassically but evaluate the long vacuum segments using the non-Gaussian quartic-oscillator vacuum. Matching the segments produces a finite one-instanton amplitude, with a prefactor scaling as $\hbar^{4/3}$ at fixed mechanical parameters, in close agreement with numerical spectra.

The quantum-generated vacuum gap also changes the distant interaction between instantons: the classical algebraic interaction crosses over to exponential decay controlled by that gap.

**Background.** The translation zero mode of an ordinary instanton is separated from the continuum of fluctuations by a harmonic gap. Here it lies at a continuum threshold, making the naive Gaussian treatment unreliable in the long tails. Cutting the path integral inside an overlap region lets the core and exact vacuum descriptions be joined by momentum matching. The vacuum fluctuations must be treated as part of the background physics, not as a small Gaussian correction everywhere.

**Why it matters?** This is a clear methodological lesson for nonperturbative QFT: a well-defined classical saddle can still demand nonperturbative treatment of its surrounding vacuum. The quantum-mechanical example provides a controlled laboratory for that issue. Its analogy with dynamically gapped field theories is suggestive, while transferring the matching construction to those theories remains a separate challenge.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 12

### Optimal Frobenius light cones for power-law systems in any dimension
**Laura Shou, An Le, Twesh Upadhyaya, T. C. Mooney, Alexey V. Gorshkov — arXiv:2611.18829**

Long-range interactions permit faster information spreading than short-range Hamiltonians, but the answer depends on what kind of spreading is measured. A worst-case state can respond differently from the infinite-temperature average encoded by the normalized Frobenius norm of a commutator.

For two-site interactions decaying as $1/r^\alpha$ in spatial dimension $d$, the authors establish the optimal propagation exponent in the summable regime $\alpha>d$, up to subpower corrections. The transition to a linear light cone occurs at $\alpha=d+1$. They also locate the transition between logarithmic-type and algebraic propagation at $\alpha=d$, and the onset of propagation times decreasing with system volume at $\alpha=d/2$. In the nonsummable regimes, these statements use interactions without a size-dependent Kac normalization.

**Background.** The normalized Frobenius norm averages squared singular values, whereas the operator norm selects the largest one. The proof exploits this difference through spectral clipping: large block eigenvalues are reduced before applying a multiscale operator-norm argument. A concentration estimate shows that clipping changes little in Frobenius norm, allowing the sharper bound to be transferred back to the original Hamiltonian. This supplies the step that earlier one-dimensional arguments could not readily generalize.

**Why it matters?** The work sharpens the distinction between possible signaling and typical operator spreading, both relevant to quantum simulation and non-equilibrium dynamics. Its main contribution is a rigorous propagation boundary rather than a prediction that generic models saturate it. The clipping strategy may also be useful whenever rare large eigenvalues obstruct bounds on average dynamical behavior.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 13

### Localization transition in a spin chain with an exact Fock-space tree
**Bernhard Jobst, Simone Warzel, Alexander Altland, Frank Pollmann — arXiv:2611.21445**

Viewing many-body localization as Anderson localization on a configuration graph is appealing, but generic graphs contain both dense loops and strongly correlated disorder. Replacing them by trees simplifies calculations while leaving uncertain which microscopic features survive.

This paper constructs a local constrained spin-1 chain whose domain wall explores an exact binary Fock-space tree. Advancing the wall converts the next zero spin into either of two nonzero states; retreating removes the final choice. Random physical fields generate correlated configuration energies, so the model retains an important difficulty absent from an independently disordered tree.

The authors rigorously bound domain-wall spreading at sufficiently strong disorder. A cavity recursion permits numerical exploration of exponentially many configurations and indicates a transition near disorder strength 7.13 in the paper's hopping units; the sufficient rigorous localization bound is much larger, about 67. The analysis focuses on a special large dynamical sector rather than typical states of the full spin Hilbert space.

**Background.** Each tree vertex records the entire sequence of choices made by the advancing wall. Its energy is a sum of the same physical random fields with different signs, creating correlations across branches. A cavity recursion evaluates a vertex's Green function from its descendant branches after removing the parent edge. The absence of loops makes this recursion exact for each disorder realization.

**Why it matters?** This supplies a microscopic benchmark for separating the effects of graph loops from correlated disorder. The clean construction, rigorous strong-disorder control, and numerical transition make it useful for understanding localization mechanisms. Its fragmented sector structure is also the reason to avoid treating it as a resolution of localization in generic interacting chains.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 14

### Resolving and Suppressing Temporally Correlated Noise in Quantum Error Correction
**Alec Eickbusch, Nick Noll, Michael Newman, Matt McEwen, et al. — arXiv:2611.21578**

A quantum memory can suppress spatial error chains while remaining vulnerable to persistent faults along time. Logical measurements and lattice surgery expose this second direction, so memory scaling alone does not establish that the relevant errors are controlled.

Using Willow, the authors scale the surface code stability experiment from $3\times3$ to $7\times7$ patches and obtain a timelike block suppression factor $\mu_T=1.47\pm0.25$. Longer runs reveal a slower suppression regime explained by data-qubit heating into $|3\rangle$ and subsequent leakage transport.

A walking stability circuit periodically exchanges data and measurement roles, allowing every qubit to be reset. On a small patch it removes the leakage-induced knee and reaches an error-probability upper bound of $3.20\times10^{-8}$ at 60 cycles. A separate offline analysis finds gains from stopping according to decoder confidence.

**Background.** The stability patch has an overcomplete set of same-basis stabilizers whose product is fixed. Repeated measurements must recover that constraint; a fault chain spanning the temporal boundaries can corrupt it. Thus the experiment probes a different spacetime observable from a stored logical qubit. Walking brings leaked population onto qubits that undergo multilevel reset, interrupting the persistent temporal mechanism. It carries an overhead in the ordinary Pauli-error regime.

**Why it matters?** This experiment merits attention for demonstrating a theoretical principle: fault tolerance needs independent control of spatial and temporal failure mechanisms. The diagnosis and circuit-level remedy make that distinction concrete. The quoted small-patch upper bound is not a general logical-gate error rate, but the benchmark offers a sharper way to assess architectures intended to perform logical operations.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 15

### Quantum Dynamics of Dynamics
**Takano Taira, Hiroyasu Tajima, Philip Taranto — arXiv:2611.19265**

A reduced-state evolution does not generally determine what happens after an intervention at an intermediate time. The intervention can disturb correlations with the environment, changing later response functions even when the unperturbed density-matrix trajectory is known.

The authors formulate continuous evolution for a quantum comb: a process that accepts operations as inputs and predicts their outputs. Under divisibility assumptions at this higher level, they derive a quantum comb master equation with encoder–decoder correlation terms coupling temporal legs. A generalized Born–Markov coarse-graining supplies a microscopic realization. Examples address correlated evolution of input and output sides, two-time response, and environmental effects on chaos diagnostics.

**Background.** A comb retains the open slots where an experimenter can insert a preparation, operation, or measurement. Contracting those slots with chosen operations produces observable probabilities or a final state. Its encoding and decoding generators extend the input and output sides of the process. Correlation terms link those sides rather than evolving them as independent channels. Complete-positive divisibility of this enlarged object is a specific closure assumption; the resulting equation is not an unrestricted solution of arbitrary environmental memory.

**Why it matters?** The useful advance is a time-local language for evolving experimentally relevant processes, beyond tracking density matrices alone. This could help organize response theory and monitored dynamics when interventions matter. Its practical value will depend on obtaining manageable microscopic generators and determining when the higher-order divisibility and coarse-graining assumptions hold in concrete many-body problems.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 16

### Rational conformal interfaces between the Ising and tricritical Ising models from a tetrality web
**António Antunes, Jin Chen, Junchen Rong — arXiv:2611.18831**

An interface between different critical theories can transmit energy and map operators across an RG flow. Constructing such an interface is difficult even when both bulk theories are rational: the junction need not preserve enough chiral symmetry to remain exactly tractable.

The authors construct six transmitting Ising–tricritical-Ising interfaces by relating four auxiliary descriptions. Extending the folded product theory exposes permutation symmetries that generate twisted boundary states; reducing to their common invariant algebra gives an alternative construction using ordinary Cardy states. Both routes yield the same six interfaces, alongside eighteen factorized ones.

The transmitting interfaces include Gaiotto's RG wall and its dressing by an Ising duality line. All have energy transmission coefficient $35/48$, while boundary entropies, correlations, and operator spectra distinguish them. The paper computes the annulus amplitudes needed to resolve these differences.

**Background.** Folding turns the two sides of an interface into a boundary of their product theory. Cardy states satisfy the requirement that the corresponding open-channel spectrum has nonnegative integer multiplicities. Choosing a rational chiral algebra organizes this spectrum into finitely many sectors. The tetrality web changes that choice to make otherwise hidden gluing operations accessible. Attaching a topological line can alter other sectors while preserving normalized stress-tensor transmission.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantial exact laboratory for transport and operator matching between distinct CFTs. It also illustrates why one transport coefficient cannot identify an interface. The construction supplies concrete data for studying interface perturbations and RG flows, while its rational symmetry restrictions leave the broader space of conformal junctions open.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 17

### From Quasinormal Mode Poles to Branch Cuts at Finite $N$
**Navid Abbasi — arXiv:2611.20912**

Classical holographic relaxation is usually organized by isolated quasinormal modes, giving exponentially damped response. The question is whether this remains the correct late-time description after the first bulk quantum correction.

In a scalar model on a planar five-dimensional anti-de Sitter black brane, a one-loop diagram combines pairs of quasinormal frequencies. Integrating their continuous relative momentum produces branch cuts. For pairs with opposite real-frequency signs, the leading large-momentum terms cancel, leaving damping that decreases as $q^{-1/3}$. The cuts consequently reach arbitrarily small frequencies despite the gapped tree-level spectrum.

Balancing this weak damping against thermal suppression selects progressively larger momenta at later times. The resulting one-loop contribution has the form $G(t)\sim N^{-2}t^\alpha e^{-Ct^{3/4}}$, with $C>0$, and eventually exceeds the classical exponential. The conclusion concerns this perturbative, planar infinite-volume setting.

**Background.** Quasinormal modes are damped bulk fluctuations with ingoing horizon conditions. Their complex frequencies become poles of boundary response functions. A continuum of summed pair frequencies replaces an isolated pole with a cut, much as multiparticle continua do in ordinary QFT. Here the unusual feature is the accumulation toward zero frequency within a fixed one-loop channel, producing stretched-exponential decay without invoking a conserved diffusive mode.

**Why it matters?** The paper identifies a concrete mechanism by which a parametrically small quantum correction changes the late-time hierarchy of relaxation. It is an interesting connection between thermal field-theory loops and holographic dynamics. Establishing the ultimate finite-$N$ response would require control of higher orders; the demonstrated contribution already shows that the classical approximation is nonuniform in time.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 18

### Optimal Quantum Sensing Algorithms for Discrete-Frequency Spectroscopy
**Yu-Jie Liu, Richard R. Allen, Ruohan Shen, Bingtian Ye, Timothy T. Chang, Joonhee Choi, Soonwon Choi — arXiv:2611.21560**

Spectroscopy often scans candidate frequencies individually, even when the task requires only detecting a signal or locating one component. That strategy can waste sensing time by acquiring more information than the question demands.

For signals supported on a known set of $N$ frequencies, the authors construct algorithms for detection, search, and learning the spectral coefficients. Their optimal sensing time scales as $T=\widetilde O(\sqrt N/\epsilon)$, where $\epsilon$ specifies sensitivity or component accuracy and the tilde suppresses logarithmic factors. Detection and search require only one controlled sensor qubit. General signal learning uses coherent ancillary processing, with a lower bound separating it from sensor-only strategies.

The central operation is a randomized projection of the spectrum into the sensor's accumulated phase. Restricting successive projections to subsets locates frequencies. Coherently controlling many projections and applying quantum Fourier transforms recovers the coefficients for learning.

**Background.** A sequence of $\pi$ pulses reverses the sensor's coupling to the signal, creating a chosen temporal filter. Rather than tuning this filter to one frequency, the algorithm spreads its response across the candidate set. A random projection has a typical overlap reduced by the square root of the number of candidates, explaining the required interrogation time. Learning must retain many such components coherently instead of extracting one scalar projection per experiment.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful example of quantum algorithms built around physical sensing dynamics and explicit task structure. The optimality statements concern sensing time, while useful implementations also require adequate coherence, pulse bandwidth, and processing resources. The combination of attainable single-qubit protocols and a resource separation for learning makes the work broader than a proposal for a particular sensor platform.

### Closing the loop on memory: accurate, shallow and transferable decoding of quantum LDPC codes
**Zhou You, Ying Li, Xiao Yuan, Yuan Yao — arXiv:2611.21086**

Belief propagation can circulate increasingly confident but mutually inconsistent messages on a quantum-code decoding graph. Adding memory can accelerate convergence on some syndromes while destabilizing others, making a single predetermined memory strength an awkward compromise.

TEMPO-BP adjusts each variable's memory strength during decoding. The next belief depends affinely on that strength once the incoming check messages are known. This permits a bounded predictive update, gated by local inconsistency and confidence, while retaining parallel message passing.

On the $[[144,12,12]]$ gross code at 0.4% physical error, a single run gives 0.73% per-shot logical failures versus 2.51% for the tested BP-plus-ordered-statistics baseline. Parallel ensembles reduce serial iteration requirements, and parameters transfer unchanged to four other code families. These are circuit-level numerical results; hardware latency gains remain implementation-dependent.

**Background.** Algorithmic memory feeds part of a variable's previous log-likelihood back into its next update. It therefore acts as feedback gain on loops of the decoding graph. The controller predicts how changing that gain will move the next belief, then selectively brakes or retains reinforcement. The paper's mode analysis explains particular amplification mechanisms, including temporal loops, but does not prove convergence of the full adaptive decoder. Finding a syndrome-consistent correction also remains distinct from choosing the correct logical class.

**Why it matters?** This is a practical decoding advance with a useful dynamical interpretation. Its appeal is that adaptation occurs within local message passing, accompanied by transfer tests rather than only code-specific tuning. The next decisive steps are performance under more varied noise and schedules, and implementations that include controller arithmetic and parallel hardware costs in their latency measurements.

## arXiv-2026-11-17 — Item 20

### Flexible ground-state search algorithm on hybrid Clifford-tensor network manifolds
**Hari Timsina, Antonio Francesco Mello, Meghadeepa Adhikary, Marcello Dalmonte, Mario Collura — arXiv:2611.18848**

Tensor-network ground-state searches become expensive when geometry or periodic boundaries produce entanglement poorly represented by a modest bond dimension. Some of that difficulty can be shifted into a Clifford circuit, but choosing a circuit solely to reduce entanglement need not optimize the desired energy.

The authors instead select Clifford transformations by their effect on the variational energy. They transform the Hamiltonian, rerun density-matrix renormalization group, and iterate over candidate two-qubit updates, including physically local pairs that may be distant in the tensor-network ordering. Clifford conjugation preserves the number of Pauli terms, making the transformed Hamiltonian straightforward to track.

Benchmarks on Ising rings, long-range chains, and tori improve energies at fixed bond dimension. The reported reductions in required bond dimension reach a factor of forty in a two-dimensional comparison; this is a benchmark-dependent gain rather than a general complexity guarantee.

**Background.** The variational state is $C|\psi\rangle$, with a Clifford circuit acting on a matrix product state. Equivalently, optimize the simpler state against $H_C=C^\dagger HC$, represented as a matrix product operator. Changing the Clifford frame reallocates correlations between the circuit and the tensor network. Energy-based selection searches this enlarged variational family directly, without requiring each accepted update to be an entanglement-reducing move.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful numerical method rather than a new solvable many-body structure. Its strength is a concrete optimization principle that exploits the inexpensive handling of Clifford correlations. Broader tests on frustrated or less favorable Hamiltonians will determine how generally the savings survive, including the cost and operator complexity of repeated frame changes.
