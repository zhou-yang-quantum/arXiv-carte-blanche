## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 1

### An efficient classical algorithm for OTOCs in chaotic quantum dynamics
**Yunchao Liu, Yihui Quek, Shreya Vardhan, Tianci Zhou — arXiv:2611.12773**

Can a classical computer predict scrambling in an individual chaotic circuit, including fluctuations hidden by circuit averaging? Ordinary wavefunction methods encounter rapidly growing entanglement, while direct sampling struggles with cancellations. This makes individual-circuit out-of-time-ordered correlators attractive tests of quantum advantage.

The authors reorganize four-point and eight-point correlators around a much smaller space of permutation configurations. Chaos suppresses long excursions outside this space through fluctuating phases. Their membrane cluster expansion retains short excursions and sums the remaining histories efficiently in one dimension. Numerical calculations support accurate predictions even where circuit-to-circuit fluctuations are appreciable. Polynomial cost at inverse-polynomial accuracy follows under quantitative suppression and amplitude-composition assumptions; the paper supplies analytical arguments and numerical evidence for them.

**Background.** Replicating forward and backward evolution introduces different ways to pair the copies. Spatial boundaries between these pairings behave like domain walls, or membranes. A “bubble” is a temporary departure from this simple pairing structure. Truncating bubble duration preserves the dominant interference without representing the full evolving state.

**Why it matters?** This is a consequential simulation strategy: chaotic dephasing itself supplies a simplification. It strengthens the classical benchmark for scrambling experiments and offers a useful effective description of non-equilibrium dynamics. Efficient extension to two dimensions remains unresolved.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 2

### Commuting-check gauging for fault-tolerant logical measurements
**Zongyuan Wang — arXiv:2611.12236**

Measuring a logical symmetry through local checks is attractive for lattice surgery, but checks that commute only inside the code space create a problem: a fault can move the state outside that space, after which successive measurements can randomize one another.

The paper introduces completion qubits that cancel these unwanted commutators throughout the Hilbert space. For bounded-block Clifford involutions of qubit stabilizer codes, completion preserves the gauged code space and distance, with constant-factor overhead when the check structure stays sparse. Gauging and ungauging a distance-$d$ parent code then implements a logical measurement with circuit fault distance proportional to $d$ in $O(d)$ rounds. An explicit two-toric-code construction attains the parent distance exactly.

**Background.** Gauging distributes a global symmetry among local Gauss-law checks whose product recovers the logical operator. For a conflicting check pair, one completion qubit carries an X operation in one check and a controlled central-check operation in the other. Their commutation factor cancels the original one; additional pinning checks preserve the intended encoded subspace.

**Why it matters?** This removes a structural obstacle to repeated syndrome extraction for non-Pauli logical measurements. It provides a concrete bridge between gauging, non-Abelian code constructions, and fault-tolerant operations, while leaving practical decoding and implementation costs to be assessed.

[arXiv 2611.12236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12236)

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 3

### Bridging Gaps with Tensor Networks: From the Haldane Gap and Beyond
**Sirui Lu — arXiv:2611.11049**

The spin-one Heisenberg chain is a basic example of a gapped quantum magnet, yet certifying a gap uniformly in system size is much harder than estimating it numerically. Its frustrated local energetics also obstruct several familiar finite-size criteria.

This work recasts a recent computer-assisted proof in tensor-network language and identifies reflection positivity as its organizing principle. Finite-ring partition-function certificates propagate through alternating doublings of length and inverse temperature. From the same finite inputs, the author obtains $\Delta_L>0.0729$ for all even $L\geq36$, with unit exchange coupling, and certifies gaps in seven additional chains and ladders. The general theorem is formalized in Lean 4.

**Background.** The imaginary-time partition-function network can be contracted along either space or time. Reflection positivity makes the spatial transfer operator positive, allowing both contractions to define density operators. Squaring them improves their purity while doubling the corresponding extent. Coupled purity inequalities turn a finite certificate into a uniform spectral estimate.

**Why it matters?** The scientific gain is an understandable, reusable route from finite calculations to thermodynamic statements. The bound remains below the numerical gap, but the mechanism and certified scope are valuable for rigorous many-body theory and tensor-network methods.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 4

### Lieb-Robinson bound for one-dimensional continuum fermions
**Marius Lemm, Hailun Lu, Harman Preet Singh — arXiv:2611.11655**

Locality is indispensable for controlling many-body dynamics, but the continuum kinetic energy allows arbitrarily fast particles. Consequently, lattice proofs based on bounded propagation speeds do not directly apply to ordinary interacting continuum Hamiltonians.

The authors establish a state-dependent locality bound for one-dimensional fermions with smooth, nonnegative, finite-range interactions. Spatially separated observables have small evolved graded commutators in a norm weighted by the initial state, provided suitable local particle-number and energy moments are controlled. The estimate is uniform in total system size. Its effective cone expands exponentially with time, so it does not establish a constant maximal velocity.

**Background.** The proof divides space into intervals treated as “pseudosites,” each retaining infinitely many internal modes. Momentum-adapted localization observables keep track of both where particles are and how energetic they are. Local energy bounds control high-momentum occupation, while a specifically one-dimensional exclusion estimate prevents excessive accumulation. These ingredients permit a controlled reduction to locality estimates in the pseudolattice description.

**Why it matters?** This opens a route toward applying locality-based simulation and stability arguments to standard continuum systems. The principal advance is the framework and thermodynamic uniformity; sharper time dependence, higher dimensions, and downstream applications remain substantial tasks.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 5

### Efficient classical simulation of Fermi-Hubbard models with Kramers symmetry
**Chaithanya Rayudu, Jun Takahashi, Ojas Parekh, Andrew Zhao — arXiv:2611.10039**

Removing the fermion sign problem makes Monte Carlo weights usable, but does not guarantee that sampling equilibrates efficiently. Establishing that second step is essential for distinguishing practical success from provable classical tractability.

The authors construct a determinantal worm Markov chain and prove polynomial-time partition-function estimation for an attractive Kramers-symmetric class. Examples include attractive Hubbard models with real hopping on arbitrary graphs and, after a particle-hole transformation, half-filled repulsive models on bipartite graphs. The guarantee controls relative error and has polynomial dependence on system size, inverse temperature, and inverse accuracy.

**Background.** Kramers symmetry pairs one-particle modes through an antiunitary operation squaring to minus the identity. Here it protects nonnegative determinant weights even in an enlarged configuration space containing auxiliary worm configurations. Those extra configurations connect otherwise difficult sampling moves. The associated determinant structure supplies the log-concavity ingredients used to prove rapid mixing, closing the gap between positivity and algorithmic efficiency.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantive boundary result for classical simulation of interacting fermions. Its relevance to quantum simulation lies in identifying a broad class requiring stronger classical baselines. Polynomial complexity alone does not establish competitive practical runtimes or extend the result to generic doped repulsive Hubbard systems.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 6

### Proof of the QNEC, without assumptions and under a weight
**Thomas Faulkner — arXiv:2611.11920**

Quantum energy conditions connect local energy to entanglement, but an inequality established on a dense set of states need not extend automatically when relative entropy is unbounded. Earlier algebraic proofs retained a finite-null-energy purification assumption.

Faulkner removes that assumption in the half-sided translation framework. Relative entropy becomes a convex function of the cut through a nested family of observable algebras. In the stationary-black-hole interpretation, this compares exterior fields with the Hartle-Hawking state. An additional construction replaces the reference state with a weight; its general convexity statement applies on intervals where the relative entropy is finite.

**Background.** A half-sided modular inclusion is a nesting of algebras for which modular flow preserves the nesting in one time direction, generating a positive translation operator. This packages null deformation into algebraic structure. A weight generalizes a positive state functional by permitting infinite normalization. It can describe reference structures unavailable as normalizable equilibrium states in the chosen representation.

**Why it matters?** This closes a genuine logical gap in an important QFT inequality and expands its algebraic setting. The possible connection to evaporating black holes is motivation for the weight construction, rather than a completed result including gravitational backreaction.

[arXiv 2611.11920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11920)

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 7

### Markov-Shielded Typicality for Thermal Quantum States
**Atsushi Iwaki, Chisa Hotta — arXiv:2611.10593**

Can thermal equilibrium be represented variationally by a deterministic pure state, without sampling random thermal representatives? The immediate obstacle is that a pure state's global entropy vanishes, making ordinary free-energy minimization unsuitable.

The authors instead minimize energy minus temperature times a sum of local conditional entropies. Their Markov-shielded typical states aim to reproduce Gibbs-state marginals while connecting continuously to the ground state. One-dimensional matrix-product-state calculations support this picture. Analytical bounds bracket the optimized free-energy density under explicit assumptions: existence of a sufficiently locally thermal pure state and approximate Markov behavior of the relevant mixed-state minimizer.

**Background.** Each site's entropy is conditioned on a finite neighboring shield rather than the entire preceding chain. The omitted contribution is a sum of conditional mutual informations. Thus shield length controls a specific locality approximation to thermodynamic entropy. Crucially, optimizing an actual wavefunction also avoids the problem that separately consistent local density matrices need not come from any global physical state.

**Why it matters?** This offers a constructive connection between approximate quantum Markov structure and thermal wavefunction algorithms. The conceptual proposal is broader than the present numerical evidence; efficient optimization and reliable behavior beyond the demonstrated one-dimensional setting remain questions.

[arXiv 2611.10593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10593)

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 8

### Discretising 2D Fermionic CFTs
**Dazhuo Cheng, Hexuan Li, Gong Cheng, Ling-Yan Hung — arXiv:2611.11807**

A tensor representation built directly from continuum conformal data could avoid repeatedly rediscovering a critical fixed point numerically. For fermionic theories, however, local amplitudes must also reproduce global spin structures and the signs generated by fermion exchange.

The authors construct tensors from three-point functions of boundary-changing operators on triangular patches. Fermionic contraction rules, boundary weights, and spin-structure data allow these patches to recover both Neveu-Schwarz and Ramond sectors. Cylinder and strip constructions extract bulk and boundary spectra. Truncated tensors reproduce low-lying free-Majorana scaling dimensions; the broader rational-theory prescription is not numerically tested throughout its proposed scope.

**Background.** A boundary-changing operator sits where two conformal boundary conditions meet, so its three-point function naturally supplies a triangle's three tensor indices. Gluing creates small holes. A weighted boundary-condition sum makes these holes shrink away without changing the continuum amplitude. Fermionic signs around noncontractible cycles then distinguish antiperiodic Neveu-Schwarz from periodic Ramond propagation.

**Why it matters?** This is a constructive bridge between boundary CFT and tensor-network simulation. Its value is an analytically informed starting point with the correct fermionic sectors. Finite descendant truncation remains an approximation, even when the underlying untruncated construction satisfies exact sewing identities.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 9

### One Measurement, Many Fronts in Kitaev and Cluster Chains
**Nicholas Musat, Stephan Haas, Rishabh Jha — arXiv:2611.10912**

What propagates after one local measurement, and why can different observables reveal different disturbances? Conditioning on the outcome changes distant expectations through pre-existing correlations, while measurement backaction also destroys correlations with the measured site.

Exact solutions for Kitaev and cluster chains separate front kinematics from visibility. Dispersion fixes position and broadening; a factor associated with creating quasiparticles and another associated with detecting them determine amplitude. If either vanishes at the fastest modes, the leading front is suppressed. For a density measurement, density and bond-energy responses exchange their $t^{-4/3}$ and $t^{-2/3}$ laws between topological and trivial phases. The usual short-range width scales as $t^{1/3}$; long-range couplings can change it.

**Background.** The front is a caustic: nearby momentum components arrive together where group velocity is maximal. Expanding the dispersion there produces Airy-type scaling. A zero in the measurement or observable weight removes the leading stationary-phase contribution, exposing a faster-decaying term. Outcome-conditioned changes do not imply controllable instantaneous signaling.

**Why it matters?** This gives precise benchmarks for measurement-driven dynamics and shows why a propagation cone alone cannot characterize information visibility. The mechanism is especially useful for interpreting observable-dependent signatures in exactly solvable monitored systems.

[arXiv 2611.10912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10912)

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 10

### One Bit, Many Records: Information Replication in Quantum Many-Body Dynamics
**Dan-Bo Zhang, Yan-Ming Chen — arXiv:2611.11446**

A disturbance spreading across a system need not leave readable evidence behind: scrambling can make its initial cause locally inaccessible. When can one classical preparation bit instead become visible to many separated observers?

The proposed mechanism compares two branches evolving under the same Hamiltonian. An unperturbed nonthermal reference remains recognizable, while a local perturbation activates spreading and volume-law entanglement. An entropy-capacity argument then guarantees an extensive number of disjoint readable regions at a fixed nonzero information threshold. Embedded-dimer dynamics demonstrates the mechanism, and stabilized PXP dynamics extends it to a recurrent reference.

**Background.** A low-entanglement reference occupies a restricted effective support in each sufficiently large block. A perturbed branch with extensive interval entropy cannot remain almost entirely inside those reference supports on nearly every block. Local tests for departure therefore distinguish the branches in many places. The records can be correlated and incomplete; replication concerns a classical bit and does not clone an unknown quantum state.

**Why it matters?** This identifies a meaningful alternative to scrambling as an outcome of information spreading. It connects nonthermal states and constrained dynamics to the emergence of redundant records, with an explicit criterion explaining when entanglement growth makes an event locally visible.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 11

### Matrix-vector models for the 3D Ising CFT
**Kaichi Mizohata, Tatsuya Seko, Asato Tsuchiya — arXiv:2611.12071**

Fermions in a lowest Landau level on a sphere reproduce three-dimensional Ising conformal data unusually well at small sizes. Understanding their relation to conventional matrix formulations could clarify why this regulator works and how to generalize it.

The authors introduce a family of angular-momentum-dependent interaction deformations and rewrite each member as matrix–vector quantum mechanics. Integrating out the fermionic vectors gives a matrix model. A Gaussian deformation yields a heat-kernel propagator for the auxiliary matrices. Exact diagonalization with up to sixteen fermions finds Ising-like critical spectra over a range of deformation widths, with no observed low-spectrum signature of UV/IR mixing.

**Background.** Lowest-Landau-level projection leaves finitely many orbitals while preserving rotations. Under radial quantization, excitation energies on the sphere encode scaling dimensions. Here the Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation introduces matrix-valued collective fields; angular-momentum damping becomes their propagator. UV/IR mixing would mean that ultraviolet regulator effects generate anomalous long-distance behavior rather than decoupling normally.

**Why it matters?** This makes a successful numerical regulator accessible to matrix-model methods and provides a tunable family retaining the same apparent infrared theory. The finite-size spectra support universality, but do not constitute a general proof that UV/IR mixing is absent in the continuum limit.

[arXiv 2611.12071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12071)

### Symanzik Effective Hamiltonian for Lattice Quantum Field Theories
**Fernanda Steffens, Carsten Urbach — arXiv:2611.12520**

Hamiltonian field-theory simulations need controlled continuum extrapolations just as Euclidean lattice calculations do. However, discretizing space while leaving time continuous changes the symmetry constraints, so an improvement prescription cannot simply assume a Lorentz-invariant operator basis.

The authors construct the effective Hamiltonian directly from dimensional analysis and lattice symmetries. They classify corrections through second order in spatial lattice spacing for scalar quartic theory and pure gauge theory, reduce redundant operators, and compare tree-level matching with established results. They also relate the construction to an anisotropic effective action through a perturbative Legendre transformation.

**Background.** Symanzik improvement represents lattice artifacts by higher-dimension continuum operators, then tunes lattice couplings to cancel their leading effects. In a Hamiltonian formulation, terms ordinarily tied together by boosts can have independent coefficients. Commutators with the leading Hamiltonian generate changes of variables, providing an additional way to identify operators that do not independently affect on-shell physics.

**Why it matters?** This is methodological groundwork for precision tensor-network and quantum simulations of QFT. It organizes which artifacts require tuning and why. The present achievement is the framework and explicit classical matching; determining nonperturbative improvement coefficients is a future program, rather than an accomplished reduction in quantum-computing resources.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 13

### Liberation in the Heavy Limit
**Sridip Pal, Gordon Rogelberg — arXiv:2611.11594**

When do very heavy CFT operators behave, at leading exponential order, like generalized free fields? Large external dimension alone is insufficient: light exchanged operators can collectively overwhelm the identity contribution.

The paper characterizes “liberation” through sparseness of the exchanged spectrum weighted by squared OPE coefficients. At the crossing-symmetric point this condition is necessary and sufficient. Positivity and crossing extend the result to two kinematic wedges; additional low-twist sparseness controls much of the remaining domain. Examples with spinning constituents demonstrate genuine failures elsewhere. In two dimensions, Virasoro symmetry obstructs liberation unless central charge grows faster than the external dimension.

**Background.** Liberation fixes the logarithm of the correlator divided by external dimension. It allows subexponential multiplicative corrections, so it does not assert that the correlator's ratio to its generalized-free counterpart approaches one. Sparseness must constrain OPE-weighted contributions rather than merely count operators. Twist controls a different part of the exchange spectrum, explaining why stronger assumptions are needed near mixed lightcone kinematics.

**Why it matters?** This sharpens when identity dominance supports a free-particle interpretation of heavy probes. It gives a useful criterion and explicit boundaries to universality, connecting conformal bootstrap reasoning with semiclassical propagation without requiring detailed string-theory machinery.

[arXiv 2611.11594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11594)

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 14

### Real-Time Path Integral Resurgence: Which Classical Path Contributes?
**Job Feldbrugge — arXiv:2611.12510**

Complex classical trajectories can encode tunneling in real time, but finding saddle points does not determine which ones belong in the physical propagator. Computing their integration-cycle intersection numbers directly is especially difficult for a path integral.

This work extracts relations between saddles from the divergent high-order expansions around them. Borel-Padé analysis reconstructs an adjacency graph, and a directional rule in the Borel plane determines which contributions become active. For the exactly solvable Rosen-Morse barrier, the resulting resummation reproduces the propagator with exponential accuracy and tracks changes in contributing saddles, including the tunneling trajectory. Extension to relativistic constrained propagation is outlined.

**Background.** Resurgence uses the factorial growth of perturbative coefficients to detect other saddles: their action differences appear as singularities after Borel transformation. Adjacency records which saddles are connected by these singularities. Stokes phenomena change the contributing combination as parameters move; higher-order events can also change the adjacency graph itself.

**Why it matters?** This is a promising practical method for organizing real-time semiclassics from local expansion data. Its exact benchmark is persuasive, but the saddle-selection rule remains unproved for general path integrals. Applications to field theory require further control of that step.

[arXiv 2611.12510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12510)

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 15

### The hidden symmetry operators of the asymmetric quantum Rabi model and its two-photon and two-mode variants in closed form
**Marcus Kollar, Arka Dutta — arXiv:2611.12859**

Bias breaks the familiar parity symmetry of the quantum Rabi model, yet level crossings reappear when the bias is an integer multiple of the oscillator frequency. The existence of a hidden symmetry was known; a general explicit operator was missing.

The authors obtain that operator at every integer bias. It combines the symmetric model's parity with an ordered tridiagonal-determinant construction built from intermediate-bias operators. Two commutation identities prove the result inductively. The same algebraic relations yield all-order expressions for the two-photon and two-mode variants, extending previously available low-order constructions.

**Background.** An intertwiner maps a Hamiltonian at one bias into the Hamiltonian at the opposite bias. Composing it with the operation that reverses bias produces a commuting symmetry of the original model. The determinant-like object, called a continuant, obeys a three-term recurrence with a fixed operator ordering. At integer bias the sequence closes, explaining why those special values support the symmetry.

**Why it matters?** This converts an existence result into a transparent, reusable construction. It is a focused advance in exact solvability, illuminating the relation between spectral crossings and hidden symmetry in experimentally relevant light–matter models. It does not amount to a new solution of generic interacting many-body dynamics.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 16

### Quantum Open Effective Field Theory: anomalous decoherence
**Gabriela Barenboim, Daniel Boyanovsky — arXiv:2611.10287**

A bath with slow critical fluctuations need not erase coherence at a constant rate. Thresholds and long memory can invalidate the infinite-time assumptions behind scattering-rate estimates, even when system–environment coupling is weak.

The authors compare influence-functional and quantum-master-equation descriptions of a scalar probe, working to second order in its coupling. They examine a conformal environment, threshold and infrared enhancement, and a bath at a thermal critical point. Time-dependent rates recover non-Markovian behavior missed by a constant golden-rule rate. In the critical case, matching the probe frequency to critically slow bath modes enhances decoherence and connects its scaling to the bath's dynamical exponent.

**Background.** A finite-time rate samples a frequency window rather than only the spectral density exactly on shell. Near a threshold, the shape of that window matters as it narrows. At criticality, relaxation time also grows with wavelength, so a probe can remain coupled to slowly evolving fluctuations. The influence functional encodes the corresponding noise and memory kernels, while the master equation follows their consequences for density-matrix coherence.

**Why it matters?** This provides a useful QFT treatment of structured environments and a conceptual bridge between critical dynamics and decoherence. Its quantitative conclusions belong to the stated weak-coupling approximation, rather than arbitrary strong system–bath coupling.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 17

### Quantum entanglement as a resource for self-organization and stabilization
**Kazuki Ikeda — arXiv:2611.11175**

Can entanglement help create spatial order that later survives without it? Establishing this requires more than observing entanglement alongside a pattern: one must bound what separable dynamics could achieve under the same constraints.

The paper constructs a driven-dissipative lattice of bosonic dimers whose intermode coherence controls transport. It derives finite-time pattern-intensity bounds for admissible separable evolutions, allowing classical correlations, and exhibits quantum dynamics exceeding them. An extended environment responds to developing order by suppressing coherence and activating classical transport. Within one time-independent Lindblad model, the established pattern then persists as local entanglement disappears.

**Background.** A Turing instability occurs when transport destabilizes a spatially uniform state that local dynamics alone would preserve. Here coherence inside each dimer controls the destabilizing transport. Partial-transpose inequalities limit that transport for separable local states. Comparing the resulting bound with pattern intensity identifies an operational role for entanglement, rather than merely a correlation between two diagnostics.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual contribution is a separation between resources needed to establish order and those sufficient to maintain it. This is a worthwhile direction for autonomous non-equilibrium physics. The necessity claim is conditional on the engineered channels, local spaces, and admissible dynamical constraints, not a universal statement about pattern formation.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 18

### One-Particle Resonances and Eigenstate Thermalization in Periodic Free-Fermion Lattices
**Tristan Benoist, Vojkan Jakšić, Claude-Alain Pillet, Anna Szczepanek, Cornelia Vogel — arXiv:2611.12701**

Integrability preserves extensive microscopic information, yet macroscopic density can still be sharply concentrated. Can that statement hold for every energy eigenstate, including arbitrary superpositions inside degenerate eigenspaces?

For nearest-neighbor free fermions on periodic cubic lattices, the authors bound squared particle-number deviations from uniform-density equilibrium by order $NL^{d-1}$. Here $N$ is particle number, $L$ the linear size, and $d$ spatial dimension. At fixed positive density, the probability of a fixed relative deviation therefore vanishes at least inversely with linear size. Matching examples establish optimality of the uniform scaling.

**Background.** The number operator is decomposed by one-particle energy transfer. Acting on an eigenstate, different transfers produce orthogonal contributions. The main difficulty is controlling how many momentum pairs share one nonzero energy gap. Arithmetic relations among roots of unity give a sharp resonance count; zero-transfer contributions require a separate estimate. This handles degeneracies without choosing a privileged eigenbasis.

**Why it matters?** This precisely separates macroscopic equilibration from full local thermalization. The theorem strengthens an exactly solvable benchmark by removing logarithmic losses and geometric restrictions. Its scope is particle-number concentration, so it does not imply that all observables in this integrable model obey conventional eigenstate thermalization.

[arXiv 2611.12701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12701)

### Self-Energy Construction for Chain Representations of Open Quantum Systems
**Pengju Chen, Da-Wei Luo, Ting Yu — arXiv:2611.12782**

Mapping a structured environment to a chain makes non-Markovian simulation manageable, but replacing its infinite tail introduces an error. Comparing tail approximations is awkward when moving the system–bath boundary changes the apparent environmental response.

The authors formulate that response as a self-energy propagated by a continued-fraction recurrence. An asymptotically uniform chain has an analytic fixed-point boundary self-energy associated with a semicircular residual spectrum. Incorporating the retained chain's approach to uniformity improves the closure. A pseudo-hyperbolic distance compares responses consistently under shifts of the cut, and numerical examples use it to fit finite damped-mode replacements more accurately than time-correlation fitting.

**Background.** Eliminating one oscillator changes the next site's response through a fractional-linear transformation. Repeating this operation produces the continued fraction. A uniform infinite tail must reproduce its own response after one elimination, giving a quadratic fixed-point equation. The comparison geometry is chosen to remain unchanged when the same lossless chain segment is added to both environmental descriptions.

**Why it matters?** This supplies a principled diagnostic for bath approximation in open-system simulation. Its useful advance is separating closure quality from arbitrary boundary placement and retained depth. Broader dynamical error guarantees would require translating response-distance control into bounds for the observables of interest.

## arXiv-2026-11-10 — Item 20

### Dephasing-Induced Melting of Crystalline Spectral Form Factors
**Preethi Gopalakrishnan, Adolfo del Campo — arXiv:2611.12888**

Nearly equally spaced energy levels produce repeated coherent revivals, resembling Bragg peaks in diffraction. How quickly does environmental dephasing erase this spectral order, and is the initial decoherence rate a reliable guide?

For energy dephasing, the authors resolve the spectral form factor into contributions from different level separations. They find an extended intermediate regime with peak scaling $K_{\rm Bragg}(p)\propto(\gamma p)^{-1/2}$, where $p$ is revival order and $\gamma$ dephasing strength. Revivals persist until neighboring-level coherences decay, even when the initial purity-loss rate is large. Static spectral fluctuations and temporally correlated noise supply distinct attenuation mechanisms.

**Background.** Energy dephasing damps a coherence at a rate proportional to the square of its energy gap. Widely separated levels disappear first, while a shrinking band of nearby pairs still realigns at revival times. Counting surviving separations produces the algebraic envelope. Eventually even adjacent-level coherences vanish, terminating that regime; spectral order need not disappear on the initial global decoherence timescale.

**Why it matters?** This clarifies which coherence scales an open-system spectral diagnostic actually probes. It gives a useful analytical connection between environmental noise and spectral rigidity, with the algebraic law restricted to the identified regime and nearly crystalline spectral setting.

[arXiv 2611.12888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12888)
