### Measurement-induced criticality for non-Abelian anyons: The monitored golden chain
**Zhaohui Zhi, Bo Han, Romain Vasseur, Andreas W. W. Ludwig, Simon Trebst, Guo-Yi Zhu — arXiv:2610.18965**

Can local measurements sustain criticality in a non-Abelian system while revealing its globally conserved topological charge? These questions are connected: neighboring fusion measurements do not commute, so learning the charge also reorganizes the many-body state.

The authors repeatedly measure neighboring Fibonacci anyons and represent the resulting Born-weighted trajectories by a random tensor network. Inverse-size Lyapunov gaps, logarithmic entanglement, and finite-size corrections to measurement-record entropy support a non-unitary conformal description. Charge sharpening is consistent with dynamical exponent $z=1$, and a decoder learns the global charge in a time proportional to chain length. The evidence is numerical; it does not identify the complete continuum theory analytically.

**Background.** Fibonacci fusion obeys $\tau\times\tau=\mathbf{1}+\tau$: two anyons can have either vacuum or Fibonacci total charge. Overlapping pair measurements ask incompatible questions about these fusion channels. The topological symmetry threads an auxiliary anyon around the chain; composing such operations follows fusion rules rather than a group law, making the symmetry non-invertible. Lyapunov gaps measure differences between long-time growth rates of trajectory transfer matrices, providing the monitored counterpart of finite-size energy gaps.

**Why it matters?** This supplies a concrete bridge between non-Abelian topological information and measurement-generated criticality. Its significance lies in connecting an observable learning timescale to a candidate continuum theory, extending the familiar Abelian setting. Establishing the theory's operator content and the extent of symmetry protection would turn the numerical identification into a more complete classification.

## arXiv-2026-10-15 — Item 2

### Instability of Markov chains under infinitesimal coherent dissipation
**Amir-Reza Negari, Yijian Zou, Timothy H. Hsieh — arXiv:2610.18983**

A useful diagnostic of mixed-state phases should distinguish robust structure from correlations that appear under negligible local noise. Markov length is attractive because it controls conditional correlations and local recovery, but its stability is not guaranteed by ordinary light-cone bounds.

The authors construct local dissipation that takes a product state to infinite Markov length at every positive time. In the solvable fermionic example, jumps $F_j=c_j^\dagger+c_{j+1}^\dagger$ create a coherent superposition on neighboring sites. A zero in their momentum-dependent injection rate, combined with entropy's nonanalytic behavior near zero occupation, produces algebraic conditional correlations. Related spin constructions and rapidly relaxing steady states show that the effect is not confined to fermionic notation.

**Background.** For regions separated by a buffer, $I(A:C|B)=S(AB)+S(BC)-S(B)-S(ABC)$ measures correlations remaining after conditioning on that buffer. Exponential decay with buffer width defines finite Markov length; an algebraic tail defeats every finite exponential length, even when its amplitude is tiny. Here the daggered operators create fermions at the indicated sites. The mechanism concerns conditional information, so ordinary propagation bounds remain intact.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantive counterexample to treating infinite Markov length as evidence of slow relaxation or intrinsic mixed-state complexity. Local reversibility survives under the authors' specified accuracy and locality allowances. For mixed-state phases and recovery-based reasoning, the lesson is that decay laws must be considered together with operational error tolerances.

## arXiv-2026-10-15 — Item 3

### Invertible subalgebras for chiral semion topological order
**Yu-An Chen, Meng Sun, Nathanan Tantivasadakarn, Zongyuan Wang, Bowen Yang — arXiv:2610.18953**

Chiral topological order resists strictly two-dimensional commuting-projector realizations with ordinary local tensor factors. An alternative is to describe the chiral observables through a local operator subalgebra, but making that description explicit requires more than identifying the anyon statistics.

Starting from a four-state toric code and condensing a bosonic charge-flux composite, the authors construct complementary semion and anti-semion algebras on a square lattice with one qubit per edge. They give non-Pauli generators and prove local completeness. Transporting the semion factor between layers produces an explicit three-dimensional non-Clifford quantum cellular automaton. Eight repetitions reduce to translation and a finite-depth circuit after adding ancillas.

**Background.** The commutant of an algebra consists of operators commuting with everything in it. An invertible subalgebra and its commutant jointly reconstruct every microscopic local operator, with uniformly bounded enlargement of support. This resembles factorization into two subsystems, but need not factor site by site—the distinction that accommodates chirality. A quantum cellular automaton is an invertible operator transformation with bounded spreading in both directions; such locality does not guarantee realization by a circuit of bounded depth.

**Why it matters?** The advance is a constructive microscopic account of chiral observables beyond Pauli algebra, including a precise connection to previously constructed semion automata. This makes abstract topological structure available for explicit circuit reasoning. It is particularly valuable for understanding how locality, topological field theory, and quantum information flow fit together; practical state-preparation advantages would require additional implementation work.

## arXiv-2026-10-15 — Item 4

### Kinetic Equations and Turbulence in Large $N$ Vector and SYK-like Models
**Vladimir Rosenhaus, Daniel Schubring — arXiv:2610.19130**

Kinetic theory usually relies on well-defined particles or waves whose collisions can be treated perturbatively. Far from equilibrium at strong coupling, spectral broadening can undermine both that picture and the assumption that collisions depend only on the present distribution.

The authors use large-component expansions to separate these possibilities. Vector models retain the standard kinetic structure, with momentum-dependent scattering mediated by the dressed auxiliary field. Random-coupling models of Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev type instead require earlier-time propagators determined self-consistently. Scaling arguments suggest an infrared inverse cascade with occupation $n_k\propto k^{-d}$, where momentum magnitude is indexed by k and d is spatial dimension. Convergence of the collision integral remains unestablished, so this is a candidate turbulent solution.

**Background.** A Hubbard–Stratonovich field replaces a quartic interaction by exchange of an auxiliary collective field; its retarded propagator incorporates repeated scattering. In random-coupling models, the leading large-component diagrams instead dress the individual propagators strongly. Their finite frequency width represents excitations without sharply defined energies. A memory-dependent collision term retains this temporal structure rather than replacing each encounter by an instantaneous transition rate. An inverse cascade transports occupation toward smaller momenta.

**Why it matters?** The main contribution is methodological: it identifies when strong coupling can be absorbed into a scattering amplitude and when the kinetic description itself must retain memory. That distinction connects controlled QFT expansions to non-equilibrium transport. Demonstrating convergence, stability, and dynamical formation of the proposed cascade would establish the more ambitious turbulence claim.

## arXiv-2026-10-15 — Item 5

### Logical CCZ state cultivation
**Yuta Hirasaki, Jong Yeon Lee — arXiv:2610.20591**

A native logical non-Clifford gate does not automatically provide a usable resource-state factory: its logical couplings may entangle unwanted qubits, and the output must reach a sufficiently protected memory. This paper addresses both obstacles for direct CCZ-state preparation.

Bivariate tricycle codes provide the gate; carefully chosen lifted bicycle codes select its inputs and receive its outputs. Selective initialization removes unwanted logical couplings, leaving two independent CCZ states. Decoder-based rejection suppresses errors before storage in higher-distance memories. Circuit-level simulations report joint infidelity $4.10\times10^{-7}$ with $8.89\%$ acceptance at single-qubit, CZ, and CCZ error rates of $0.01\%$, $0.1\%$, and $0.5\%$, respectively. The resource comparison favors direct preparation at the smallest factory's specified target.

**Background.** The cup-product construction supplies a trilinear logical phase through a constant-depth physical CCZ circuit. Its several logical couplings must be isolated to obtain separate resource states. A projection-preserving lift enlarges the bicycle code into multiple sheets while retaining a map to its original checks. A two-sheet lift couples to only the desired logical observable, enabling selective measurement; a three-sheet lift retains both needed logical degrees of freedom for transfer. Cultivation here means preparation with decoder-based postselection followed by escape into stronger memory.

**Why it matters?** This turns an algebraically available gate into a substantially more complete fault-tolerant primitive. The broader contribution is deliberate engineering of inter-code logical coupling. Its computational advantage depends on acceptance, connectivity, and the assumed native three-qubit noise model; the reported savings should be assessed within those conditions.

## arXiv-2026-10-15 — Item 6

### Viscous hydrodynamics from correlated losses
**Aleksandr N. Mikheev, Orjan Ameye, Oded Zilberberg, Jeff Maki — arXiv:2610.19083**

Can particle loss create the collisions needed for fluid behavior in an otherwise ideal gas? Independent removal leaves surviving particles ballistic, but loss requiring two particles to meet also changes their scattering.

The authors derive kinetic theory for a dilute three-dimensional gas with two-body loss. The optical theorem requires an elastic contribution alongside absorption. At strong loss, quantum Zeno suppression reduces actual pair removal while elastic scattering approaches the unitary limit. Currents then relax before the gas appreciably depletes. Calculated viscosity and thermal conductivity approach dilute unitary-gas values; loss-induced heating gives $\eta,\kappa\propto t^{3/8}$, with eta denoting viscosity, kappa thermal conductivity, and t time. The result applies in the nondegenerate regime.

**Background.** Strong local absorption continuously tests whether particles overlap. The resulting suppression of overlap is a dissipative quantum Zeno effect: increasing the nominal loss strength can reduce successful loss events. Elastic scattering remains because the outgoing wave is modified even when particles survive. The hydrodynamic requirement is a separation of timescales—momentum redistribution must occur much faster than depletion. The unitary limit means saturation of the quantum scattering bound, rather than closed-system evolution.

**Why it matters?** This identifies dissipation as a source of transport-producing collisions, extending its usual roles in preparation and decoherence. It offers a concrete non-equilibrium mechanism with calculable transport coefficients and a time-dependent signature. Observing the predicted fluid would require the strong-loss hierarchy and sufficient collisionality; the calculation does not establish the same behavior in a quantum-degenerate gas.

### A modular quantum computer based on Cornucopia codes
**Zhide Lu, Weikang Li, Yinghao Ma, Hanyu Yan, Bingrun Wang, Yixuan Ma, Zheng-Zhi Sun, Dong-Ling Deng — arXiv:2610.19073**

High encoding rate is useful only if logical operations preserve its resource advantage. Individually addressing many logical qubits in one block can otherwise require enough ancillary hardware to erase the memory savings.

The authors construct a modular processor around the $[[2844,1426,18]]$ Cornucopia code. Translation symmetry organizes 1,422 logical qubits into registers served by reusable measurement gadgets; four additional logical modes are invariant. Bridges connect gadgets and modules. The basic instruction set requires an average of 4.1 physical qubits per logical qubit including workspace, compared with 3.0 for memory. Magic-state factories are excluded. Explicit merged-code constructions certify the targeted measurements and preservation of code distance.

**Background.** A code automorphism permutes physical qubits while preserving the stabilizer group. It therefore induces an operation within the logical space. Here a compatible logical basis makes translation cycle through register positions, allowing one seed gadget to address many logical qubits. The gadgets perform logical Pauli measurements through code surgery; short bridges extend these measurements between registers or modules. Choosing the logical coordinates carefully is what converts physical symmetry into reusable control.

**Why it matters?** This addresses the architectural gap between dense quantum storage and programmable logical computation. The useful comparison includes operation workspace, making it more informative than encoding rate alone. It advances a concrete qLDPC route to computation, while leaving full application cost dependent on factory provisioning, execution time, routing, and achievable logical reliability.

## arXiv-2026-10-15 — Item 8

### Quantum Folding in Chebyshev-Based Quantum Subspace Diagonalization
**Seung Park, Dongni Chen, Stefano Chesi, Kyunghyun Baek, Mahn-Soo Choi — arXiv:2610.19922**

Ground-state algorithms can estimate an energy accurately yet struggle to produce the corresponding state when the initial overlap is exponentially small. Locality suggests improving the starting state by solving smaller pieces first.

Quantum folding joins subsystem ground states, then applies Chebyshev-based subspace diagonalization to restore the missing interface correlations. The authors derive a bound linear in measurement noise and test Heisenberg chains, finding substantial improvements over Néel initialization. They also analyze physical state preparation and recursive cost. Favorable gapped examples have size-independent joining fidelity; the gapless example has a slow power-law decay, yielding a mild quasi-polynomial overlap overhead in the paper's recursion. Thus the broad polynomial-cost language requires qualification.

**Background.** Chebyshev polynomials of a rescaled Hamiltonian generate a quantum Krylov subspace. Measured overlap and Hamiltonian matrices then define a small classical eigenproblem. Its optimized eigenvector supplies coefficients, but physically applying the associated polynomial filter has an additional success cost. Folding improves the overlap entering that cost: each step corrects correlations across newly joined boundaries. A certified filter must also remain bounded throughout the rescaled spectrum; a good energy estimate alone does not ensure efficient preparation.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is an analysis connecting initialization, noise sensitivity, and actual state delivery. That is valuable for quantum simulation, where the desired output is often a reusable many-body state. The method is promising when interfaces retain favorable overlaps and gaps; extending those conditions beyond the studied chains is necessary before claiming a general scalable ground-state solver.

## arXiv-2026-10-15 — Item 9

### Petz Recovery through a Single Polar Transformation for Approximate Quantum Error Correction
**Hanzhang Zhao, Shenglong Xu — arXiv:2610.20515**

The Petz map provides a principled approximate decoder, but its inverse-square-root normalization is awkward to implement coherently. Separately compiling its nonunitary ingredients can obscure both circuit cost and error control.

The authors combine them into one polar transformation of an operator assembled from the channel and reference state. A single quantum singular value transformation implements the resulting recovery isometry, extended to avoid postselection. They bound channel error using a spectrum-weighted loss and illustrate the depth–accuracy tradeoff for random encodings and Hayden–Preskill recovery. Exponential convergence with transformation degree depends on the smallest relevant nonzero scaled singular value; poor conditioning remains costly.

**Background.** In the polar decomposition $A=V\sqrt{A^\dagger A}$, the positive factor describes singular-value magnitudes, while the partial isometry carries the corresponding input-to-output directions. Petz normalization extracts precisely this directional map on the supported subspace. Block encoding places a scaled operator inside a larger unitary circuit. Quantum singular value transformation uses that circuit and phase rotations to approximate a chosen polynomial of its singular values, here flattening nonzero magnitudes while retaining their directions.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful implementation-level unification of approximate recovery and scrambling decoders. Spectrum-weighted optimization may avoid spending equal effort on directions contributing unequally to channel error. Its practical impact depends on efficient coherent access to the channel model and encoding primitives; it supplies a decoder construction rather than a universal inexpensive reversal of noise.

## arXiv-2026-10-15 — Item 10

### Droplets obstruct Onsager-type solutions of the Ising model in a magnetic field
**Ran Luo, Jiahua Tian — arXiv:2610.20569**

The Ising model in a field has resisted the structures that solve its zero-field counterpart. A sharper question than asking for “an exact solution” is whether its free energy can belong to the differential-function classes common in integrable models.

The authors prove differential transcendence in the coupling at every fixed dimensionless field outside a countable exceptional set, in at least two dimensions. Their obstruction comes from low-temperature droplets: enclosed volume grows faster than boundary size, producing coefficient arithmetic incompatible with algebraic differential equations. An integer structure prevents the crucial contributions from cancelling. The result excludes the specified Onsager-type formulas and elliptic, hypergeometric, or Painlevé expressions, without excluding every conceivable exact representation.

**Background.** Differential algebraicity means satisfying a polynomial relation involving the independent variable, the function, and finitely many derivatives; nonlinear equations are included. A reversed-spin droplet costs boundary energy and carries a field weight determined by its volume. This geometry becomes a constraint on series coefficients. The parameters are $K=J/(k_BT)$ and $H=h/(k_BT)$, with J the exchange energy and h the field energy. The theorem varies K while holding H fixed, so its parameter path is specific.

**Why it matters?** This provides a precise obstruction to a broad family of integrability-inspired answers, rather than another unsuccessful search for one. It connects physical droplet geometry to the analytic complexity of thermodynamics. The exceptional fields and the theorem's definition of admissible formulas are essential boundaries; interpreting it as proof that the model cannot be solved in any sense would exceed the result.

## arXiv-2026-10-15 — Item 11

### Mutual Information of Measurement in Conformal Field Theories
**Israel Klich, Ravisankar Rajagopal, Diana Vaman — arXiv:2610.19286**

Entanglement measures characterize a quantum state, but experiments often access distributions of outcomes for a chosen observable. How much universal critical information survives this choice of measurement?

For scalar primaries and descendants in a large-component conformal limit, the authors calculate measurement mutual information for adjacent and widely separated intervals. Gaussian functional determinants reduce the calculation to two-point data. The adjacent-interval logarithm has a coefficient proportional to $(\Delta-\tfrac12)^2$, where $\Delta$ is the measured operator's scaling dimension. The increase for derivatives seems to challenge data processing, but a derivative measured before partitioning includes information across the boundary. The calculations do not establish the same formulas for arbitrary interacting CFTs at finite component number.

**Background.** Rényi–Shannon entropy applies Rényi weighting to classical outcome probabilities; it differs from tracing powers of a reduced density matrix. Combining regional entropies extracts shared outcome information. In the Gaussian limit, the covariance restricted to an interval determines the entropy through its determinant. Data processing requires separate operations within each region. A lattice derivative crossing their boundary uses both sets of original outcomes, so it does not satisfy that locality assumption.

**Why it matters?** The paper gives continuum benchmarks for extracting critical structure from measured data, and explains how observable choice changes the information retained. The boundary issue is especially useful when comparing derivatives or other processed observables. Extending the determinant results beyond the Gaussian limit and testing regulator-sensitive boundary terms would clarify their reach in quantum simulation and monitored systems.

[arXiv 2610.19286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19286)

## arXiv-2026-10-15 — Item 12

### Optimal Disorder Strength for Approximate Unitary $k$-Designs in Spin Chains
**Tian-Gang Zhou, Yi-Neng Zhou, Thierry Giamarchi, Julian Sonner — arXiv:2610.18954**

Analog dynamics can supply random transformations for measurement protocols without compiling long random circuits. Disorder helps generate variation, but excessive disorder pins the dynamics near the computational basis. The useful operating regime lies between these effects.

For an interacting XXZ chain, the authors derive weak- and strong-disorder estimates of the deviation from Haar moment statistics. Their crossover places the optimum near the transverse exchange scale. Independent successive quenches further suppress the moment error exponentially in quench number. Numerical calculations support these trends and demonstrate classical-shadow reconstruction. The target randomness is within a fixed-magnetization sector, a consequential restriction imposed by the Hamiltonian's conservation law.

**Background.** A unitary $k$-design reproduces Haar averages involving k copies of a unitary and its conjugate; an approximate design reproduces them within an error criterion. Such moments determine the behavior of many randomized measurement estimators. The paper's moment gap is the largest-singular-value distance from the Haar moment operator, rather than an energy gap. Classical shadows rotate a state with known random transformations, measure it, and combine the records into estimators for many observables. Their quality therefore depends on the transformation ensemble.

**Why it matters?** This offers a physical explanation and practical tuning principle for analog randomness generation. It connects localization to an operational loss of measurement capability, rather than diagnosing randomness solely through spectral statistics. The advance is a useful controlled method within the studied model; scalability to larger systems and higher moments requires evidence beyond the perturbative regimes and finite-size checks.
