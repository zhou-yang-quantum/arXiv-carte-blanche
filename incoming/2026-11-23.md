## arXiv-2026-11-23 — Item 1

### String breaking in a (1+1)-dimensional $e^+e^-$ collider
**Ross Dempsey, Anna-Maria E. Glück, Silviu S. Pufu, Benjamin T. Søgaard — arXiv:2611.27134**

String breaking is often pictured as a classical sequence: charges separate, an electric string forms, and a new pair appears. A real scattering state contains coherent alternatives, however, and calculating their probabilities requires both physically prepared incoming particles and access to the outgoing quantum state. This paper develops that calculation for the massive Schwinger model.

The authors work at $\theta=\pi$, above the critical fermion mass, where two degenerate vacua support charged domain walls. These solitons serve as the incoming electron and positron. They construct their wavepackets using infinite tensor networks, then evolve a finite window containing the collision while retaining the surrounding infinite vacuum. Heavy solitons reproduce the expected relativistic rebound under a constant string force. At smaller masses and higher collision energies, the outgoing state acquires appreciable weight in channels containing additional particles.

An especially useful observation is that energy-density tracks alone can conceal string breaking. The electric field left between the outgoing excitations distinguishes the alternatives: elastic scattering and pair production leave different vacua in the central region. Its distribution becomes bimodal, and the authors use both these distributions and projections onto two-soliton states to estimate scattering probabilities. Conditioning on the central field resolves the corresponding string-broken and string-unbroken outcomes. Agreement between the two probability estimates supports this interpretation, with higher production channels accounting for a growing discrepancy at larger energies. The simulations include checks of lattice spacing, wavepacket width, and bond dimension.

**Background.** At this special vacuum angle, a charged soliton changes one degenerate electric-field vacuum into the other. Its energy stays finite because the two asymptotic regions have equal vacuum energy. After two solitons cross, their reversed ordering can instead leave an energetic field configuration between them, producing the restoring string force. The numerical construction uses a link-enhanced matrix product operator: charge labels on the virtual bonds carry the electric flux fixed by Gauss's law, and additional link tensors apply its electric energy. This retains the gauge constraint while allowing the two different asymptotic vacua needed for a soliton.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantial computational advance in resolving nonperturbative scattering as a quantum process, including the correlations that distinguish its possible outcomes. It connects real-time QFT, tensor networks, and quantum-simulation benchmarks particularly well. The demonstrated capability remains in one spatial dimension, but its treatment of incoming solitons and outgoing channel information is more broadly useful than a simulation of a preassembled string.

## arXiv-2026-11-23 — Item 2

### Quantum Many-Body Scars from Operator Size
**Debarghya Chakraborty, Dario Rosa — arXiv:2611.27982**

Persistent oscillations in an interacting many-body system usually require special structure. A useful scar construction should explain that structure while giving a concrete interaction that could implement it. This paper supplies such an interaction for two matched copies of a system.

The authors combine the difference of the two copy Hamiltonians with an interaction measuring operator size. The Hamiltonian difference annihilates a special family of states; the size interaction approximately arranges them into equally spaced levels. Superpositions then exhibit coherent energy exchange between the copies. The construction applies to uniform-size local spin interactions, with a related analysis for the Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev model.

The approximation improves with system size at fixed position in the constructed tower. Higher tower states eventually lose sharp size concentration, and the controlled persistent energy exchange is subextensive.

**Background.** Acting with an operator on one half of a maximally entangled state turns that operator into a state of the doubled system. Orthogonalizing successive Hamiltonian powers gives the relevant Krylov basis. Operator size counts nonidentity factors in a Pauli string. Low-order basis vectors concentrate at a definite size, which a coupling between corresponding sites can measure.

**Why it matters?** The appealing advance is the translation of an algebraic scar mechanism into a microscopic coupling. It suggests a route to engineering coherent dynamics without requiring an integrable single-copy Hamiltonian. For understanding failures of thermalization, the restriction to a controlled family of initial states is also informative: it identifies precisely where the protection comes from and where it deteriorates.

## arXiv-2026-11-23 — Item 3

### Improved Digitization Bounds for U(1) Lattice Gauge Theory via Semiclassical Analysis
**Di Fang, Yu Tong — arXiv:2611.26720**

Discretizing space does not make a continuous gauge field finite dimensional. A simulation must also truncate its electric-field states, and an overly pessimistic error bound can make the required resources look prohibitive. The important question is how much of that local Hilbert space physically relevant states actually explore.

For pure U(1) gauge theory, the authors bound truncation errors for states in a finite excitation window above the interacting vacuum. They obtain sufficient electric cutoffs whose dependence on inverse lattice spacing and evolution time enters through logarithms, with improved weak-coupling dependence proportional to $1/g$ up to logarithmic factors. These are bounds on both the discarded state weight and the resulting evolution error. The setting has at least two spatial dimensions and excludes matter and background charges.

**Background.** Rescaling the Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian makes $h=g^2$ an effective semiclassical parameter. Agmon's method controls exponentially weighted wavefunctions, revealing the small tails at large electric quantum number. A local vacuum-energy comparison lets the estimate depend on excitation energy rather than extensive vacuum energy.

**Why it matters?** Resource estimates become more useful when their assumptions describe the states a simulation aims to prepare. This work illustrates how physical information about low-energy states can sharpen a general algorithmic guarantee. It is especially relevant to the connection between continuum field theory and finite quantum registers; extending comparable control to dynamical matter would increase its practical reach.

## arXiv-2026-11-23 — Item 4

### Energy and Charge Transmission at Conformal Interfaces: Relations and Bounds
**Yuma Furuta, Yuya Kusuki, Toshiki Onagi, Yin Tang — arXiv:2611.27002**

An interface can impede charge and energy differently. Conformal symmetry alone leaves their transmission coefficients independent, so a relation between them requires additional physical structure. Here that structure is a preserved current algebra.

For interfaces between Wess–Zumino–Witten models preserving the diagonal affine symmetry, the authors derive

$$c_{LR}=\frac{2\,\dim G}{k_L+k_R+2h^\vee}\,k_{LR}.$$

The two transmission coefficients are defined through stress-tensor and current correlators across the interface. The bulk levels normalize the current algebras, and the dual Coxeter number supplies the group-dependent normalization. Positivity of outgoing energy then excludes complete energy transmission at unequal levels. In linear response at zero chemical potential, the relation gives an interface-independent ratio of thermal to charge conductance. Additional neutral sectors replace the equality with bounds.

**Background.** The Sugawara construction expresses the WZW stress tensor as a quadratic current operator. Folding the interface into a boundary lets current gluing constrain stress-tensor overlaps. A coset sector contributes energy beyond this current-built stress tensor, explaining how the exact relation can loosen.

**Why it matters?** This is a clean example of symmetry controlling transport more strongly than scale invariance alone. The result offers a useful organizing principle for junctions between interacting critical theories: it distinguishes restrictions imposed by the bulk current algebra from choices specific to an interface. Its generalized Wiedemann–Franz law is particularly interesting as an exact relation outside a quasiparticle description.

## arXiv-2026-11-23 — Item 5

### Emergent Causality of Macroscopic Transport and Equilibration in Long-Range Quantum Systems
**Hongchao Li, Marius Lemm — arXiv:2611.27650**

Long-range hopping permits algebraic propagation tails, but a small tail in a single-particle amplitude does not immediately imply rapid transport of an appreciable fraction of a many-body system. This paper asks for a speed limit on that collective redistribution.

For number-conserving bosonic or fermionic systems with power-law hopping, the authors bound the probability of moving a fixed fraction of all particles between distant regions. Outside an effective cone, the suppression is exponential in the number of particles transported. Consequently, the cone becomes strict for macroscopic transport in the thermodynamic limit even though microscopic tails remain.

In spatial dimension $D$, hopping exponents $\alpha\in(D,D+1)$ give a super-ballistic cone with radius $r\sim t^{1/(\alpha-D)}$, where $t$ is elapsed time. Faster-decaying hopping gives a ballistic cone, with a logarithmic qualification at the boundary between regimes. Explicit transfer protocols establish optimality of the power-law scaling away from that boundary. The bound accommodates arbitrary density-dependent interactions under the stated locality assumptions.

The authors also derive equilibration-time lower bounds when equilibration requires macroscopic redistribution. Separately, they construct a free-fermion chain with $\alpha\in(1,3/2)$ whose macroscopic density equilibrates on the sublinear scale $t_{\mathrm{eq}}\sim L^{\alpha-1/2}$, with $L$ the chain length. This example establishes accelerated equilibration, although its exponent differs from the optimal transport-cone exponent.

**Background.** The proof exponentially weights occupation numbers according to position, favoring particles in the destination region and penalizing those in the source. Transporting many particles produces a correspondingly large weight change. Conjugating the evolution by this weight modifies hopping amplitudes in a controllable way. Density-dependent interactions commute with the weight, so even strong interactions of that form cannot bypass the resulting transport constraint. This is why a collective probability bound can be much stronger than a bound on the influence of one local operator.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual distinction between microscopic propagation and macroscopic transport is valuable for non-equilibrium physics. It produces a robust collective notion of causality and connects it directly to equilibration. The accelerated example also clarifies why long-range hopping and long-range interactions that raise energetic barriers can have very different consequences for relaxation.

## arXiv-2026-11-23 — Item 6

### No roughening transition for the Ising model on $\mathbb{Z}^d$ with $d \geq 4$
**Jianping Jiang, Sike Lang — arXiv:2611.27805**

An ordered magnet can support a boundary between its two phases, but thermal fluctuations may wash out the interface's position even while bulk order survives. Establishing interface rigidity only at very low temperature leaves open whether a separate roughening transition occurs closer to the bulk critical point. This paper closes that gap for the nearest-neighbor Ising model in four and higher dimensions.

The authors impose plus spins above a horizontal plane and minus spins below it. For every ordered temperature, written as $\beta>\beta_c(d)$ in inverse-temperature notation, they prove that the spin immediately above the plane has a positive magnetization bounded away from zero uniformly in box size:

$$\langle\sigma_0\rangle\ge c(d,\beta)>0.$$

The resulting infinite-volume interface states retain their spatial asymmetry. Thus the imposed interface remains detectable throughout the ordered phase, excluding a roughening transition in these dimensions. The theorem concerns this precise persistence of a non-translation-invariant Gibbs state; it does not provide a complete distribution of interface heights.

The proof compares the interface state to an auxiliary state obtained from vertically antiperiodic slabs. An infrared estimate controls fluctuations of vertical spin columns, and reflection-based correlation inequalities transfer a nonzero magnetization difference back to the original interface. The dimensional restriction enters through the availability of at least three horizontal directions.

**Background.** Dobrushin boundary conditions force an interface by fixing opposite phases on opposite halves of the boundary. If the interface wanders so widely that a fixed observation region forgets its height, the limiting state can recover translation invariance. Antiperiodic boundary conditions instead insert a sign reversal on closing the vertical direction, forcing a domain wall in an auxiliary geometry. Grouping each vertical column into a vector-valued spin makes long-wavelength fluctuation bounds available in the remaining directions.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantial structural result about a canonical statistical-mechanics model: it extends interface persistence from a low-temperature regime to the entire ordered phase. It also isolates three dimensions as the remaining setting where the expected separate roughening transition can occur. The interest here extends beyond the Ising model itself to how dimensionality controls the survival of extended defects.

## arXiv-2026-11-23 — Item 7

### Quantum Dobrushin comparison and locality of fermionic equilibrium states
**Lennart Becker, Harman Preet Singh, Stefan Teufel — arXiv:2611.27873**

How much can an equilibrium measurement depend on a distant change to the Hamiltonian? For classical systems, conditional probabilities turn this into a comparison of local influences. Quantum noncommutativity obstructs that direct construction. This paper develops a fermionic replacement that yields uniqueness, correlation decay, and local stability from one framework.

The main application concerns weak many-body interactions added to a quadratic fermionic Hamiltonian, allowing pairing, inhomogeneity, and sufficiently decaying long-range terms. If the quadratic system is gapped, a single temperature-independent weak-coupling interval guarantees a unique thermal equilibrium state at every positive temperature. Correlations decay uniformly in temperature, and local observables approach their ground-state expectations exponentially fast in inverse temperature. The spatial decay can be polynomial when the interactions are long ranged.

The strongest locality result allows a distant defect to be arbitrarily strong or extended. Every equilibrium state of the modified system remains locally close to the original equilibrium state, provided the interaction terms touching a buffer around the observation region are unchanged. Corresponding finite-volume statements allow arbitrary even boundary operators. Without a free gap, the weak-coupling threshold becomes temperature dependent; a separate high-temperature result treats general interactions.

**Background.** The replacement for classical conditional probabilities is a family of annihilation conditions on a doubled fermionic algebra. A thermal state has an extension annihilated by operators obtained by filtering local Majorana operators in time. For a free system these are quasiparticle annihilation operators; weak interactions perturb them into spatially localized, nonlinear operators.

The comparison theorem reconstructs any local observable as a scalar plus terms whose expectations vanish in a state satisfying those annihilation conditions. A competing state's local error is therefore bounded by how strongly it violates the conditions nearby. An auxiliary dissipative evolution controls how such errors propagate through a spatial influence kernel. A spectral gap permits temperature-uniform time filters, which explains the uniform low-temperature conclusions rather than merely assuming them.

**Why it matters?** The main advance is a reusable comparison principle, with particularly strong consequences for distant defects and boundary sensitivity. It provides quantitative control over when local equilibrium physics is insensitive to remote structure, including beyond the usual short-range setting. That is valuable background for thermal-state approximation, simulation, and the stability of many-body descriptions; the theorem's weak-coupling assumptions specify the regime where this control is established.

### Onset of quantum chaos in a family of two-dimensional conformal field theories
**Felix M. Haehl, Moshe Rozali — arXiv:2611.26694**

Random-matrix behavior is widely expected in sufficiently complicated conformal theories, yet explicit spectral demonstrations are scarce because the theories whose spectra can be calculated are often exceptionally solvable. This paper exploits a nearby solvable point: enough data remain calculable to examine how its degeneracies split under an interaction that preserves conformal invariance.

The authors study the K3 sigma model near a reflection orbifold of a four-torus at the free-fermion radius. Four symmetry-preserving exactly marginal deformations provide different directions away from the solvable point. Within degenerate levels, the leading anomalous dimensions follow from finite matrices built from free-field matrix elements. The calculation targets spin-zero, non-BPS superconformal primaries, whose dimensions are not protected by supersymmetry.

For generic deformation directions, the nonzero splittings show Gaussian orthogonal ensemble statistics after all exact symmetries are resolved and the known pairing of positive and negative shifts is accounted for. The evidence includes adjacent-gap ratios, unfolded spacing distributions, and longer-range number variance. A hidden discrete symmetry, identified numerically at even levels, is essential to obtaining the appropriate statistics. A commonly studied singlet deformation retains additional structure: its neighboring levels can approximately resemble random-matrix behavior while its longer-range spectral rigidity does not.

These are first-order results in individual degenerate blocks. They establish a controlled weak-coupling spectral phenomenon, while correlations between different undeformed levels and dynamical thermalization require further analysis.

**Background.** An orbifold includes both ordinary states invariant under the quotient and twisted states associated with its fixed points. The blow-up operators smooth those fixed points and mix the two types of states. Their first-order mixing matrix has an off-diagonal structure, producing paired shifts and many exact zero shifts. These structural correlations must be removed before testing randomness. Likewise, mixing independent symmetry sectors can hide level repulsion, so resolving the full symmetry content is part of the physical diagnosis.

**Why it matters?** This supplies unusually explicit evidence for random-matrix universality emerging inside a continuously deformable CFT. The comparison with the special singlet direction also makes a useful methodological point: a plausible nearest-neighbor statistic can coexist with substantial nonrandom structure. The work connects conformal perturbation theory and quantum chaos without relying on an ensemble average over theories.

### Single-realization Pauli twirling for suppressing coherent error accumulation in structured quantum circuits
**Dihang Sun, Tianqi Chen, Jin Ming Koh, Nannan Ma, Jiangbin Gong — arXiv:2611.27703**

Averaging randomized compilations can turn coherent errors into an effective stochastic channel. That familiar statement leaves a practical question unresolved: can one randomly compiled circuit already suppress coherent accumulation, and which circuits benefit? This paper answers through the interference between errors generated at different gates.

For weak coherent errors at selected two-qubit Clifford gates, the leading state infidelity is controlled by the variance of an accumulated error generator. Each gate's generator is transported through the ideal circuit into a common frame. If these transported errors reinforce one another, their cross-layer covariances can make the total variance grow quadratically with the number of gates $L$.

Independent Pauli dressing randomizes signs of the local error components while matched corrections preserve the ideal computation. The cross-layer covariance vanishes on averaging over these choices, leaving an $O(L)$ variance bound. Markov's inequality then gives a bound with the same linear scaling for a single sampled realization at any fixed confidence level, with a prefactor depending on the failure probability. This is a probabilistic guarantee about the leading weak-error contribution, rather than a guarantee that every draw improves a circuit.

The resulting covariance diagnostic predicts substantial benefit when the bare circuit has a large positive cross-layer contribution, and little benefit when its covariance is already nearly diagonal. Numerical studies and IBM hardware tests on GHZ preparation and Trotterized Ising dynamics support the mechanism. The perturbative analysis assumes $L\epsilon\ll1$, where $\epsilon$ measures the coherent error strength; additional control and stochastic errors affect the observed advantage.

**Background.** A transported error generator is the original gate error conjugated by the ideal circuit preceding it, so generators from different gates can be compared on the same input state. Their connected covariance measures whether they move that state in reinforcing directions. Pauli dressing changes the signs of these contributions without requiring the transported operators themselves to remain Pauli operators. A single realization means fixing one random compilation and taking its measurement shots; it does not mean estimating fidelity from one shot.

**Why it matters?** The useful contribution is a circuit- and state-dependent explanation of when randomized compiling helps, beyond the averaged-channel picture. It connects coherent-error structure to a practical suppression mechanism and should inform quantum-simulation benchmarks. The confidence bound is relatively general and correspondingly loose, leaving room for stronger predictions using more information about a circuit's covariance structure.

### Moment bounds and UV reconstruction from curved positivity cones
**Fu-Ming Chang, Shi-Lin Wan, Shuang-Yong Zhou — arXiv:2611.26692**

Scattering consistency constrains low-energy couplings, but writing those constraints explicitly becomes difficult when the allowed region has continuously varying boundary directions. Numerical sampling can approximate such a region while obscuring both its exact boundary and the high-energy physics responsible for saturating it. This paper turns that geometric problem into a moment problem.

The starting point is a dispersion relation expressing forward-scattering coefficients as positive sums of contributions from intermediate states. When several two-particle channels carry the same symmetry representation, their relative couplings vary continuously, generating curved families of extremal rays. The authors encode positive sums over these families in positive semidefinite moment matrices. For the homogeneous quadratic families studied here, these conditions are sufficient as well as necessary. Eliminating auxiliary moments then produces exact inequalities directly on effective-theory coefficients.

Applications include a two-scalar theory and sectors of dimension-eight quartic gauge interactions in the Standard Model effective field theory. The inverse problem is particularly interesting. On a saturated curved boundary, a moment matrix develops a null vector. Positivity forces the contributing high-energy coupling vectors to satisfy the corresponding linear relation, thereby constraining how a heavy state couples to different low-energy channels. The authors construct examples of heavy-field interactions realizing these boundary conditions.

**Background.** An extremal ray is an irreducible direction of a convex cone: it cannot be formed by positively combining distinct allowed directions. A continuous ray family can be parameterized by channel-coupling ratios. Its moment matrix averages outer products of those coupling vectors, so positivity follows from averaging squares. In a simple two-variable example this gives $C_1,C_3\ge0$ and $C_2^2\le4C_1C_3$. Saturating the latter makes the matrix singular. The expectation of a nonnegative square can vanish only if that square vanishes on every contributing component, which explains why a boundary condition constrains the underlying couplings.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful analytical bridge between S-matrix principles, convex geometry, and the reconstruction of possible high-energy interactions. Its value goes beyond producing stronger exclusion bounds: saturation contains structural information about relative couplings. Reconstruction remains conditional and generally nonunique, and several explicit tree-level examples are partial ultraviolet completions involving higher-dimensional interactions. Those qualifications leave a clear methodological advance rather than a general solution of the EFT inverse problem.

### Comparing weakly interacting and free quantum dynamics locally
**Jorge Acuña Flores, Joscha Henheik, Marius Lemm, Daniele Toniolo — arXiv:2611.27707**

Weak interactions need not be a small perturbation in the global norm of a macroscopic Hamiltonian: the perturbation grows with volume. Local measurements offer a better possibility, because only a finite expanding region can influence them over a finite time. This paper makes that observation into quantitative comparisons with free dynamics.

The basic procedure first restricts an evolving local observable to a region larger than its propagation cone, removes the interaction within that region, and then compares with the unrestricted free evolution. For interaction strength $\varepsilon$ in spatial dimension $D$, the resulting local error scales schematically as $\varepsilon t^{D+1}$, apart from small locality tails. The approximation therefore remains useful on time intervals growing as an inverse power of the interaction strength, uniformly in total system size.

One application transfers known one-particle diffusion results to weakly interacting, weakly disordered fermions. The quantity controlled is a spatially weighted anticommutator diagnostic measuring operator spreading. Its additional spatial weight gives an error of order $\varepsilon t^{D+3}$. The resulting diffusion statement concerns that diagnostic on finite perturbative times, with disorder averaging as in the reference result; a full kinetic or hydrodynamic description is a stronger problem.

A second application puts the small parameter in the initial state. In a Fermi–Hubbard system with low spin-down density, local multi-time spin-up correlations remain close to their free counterparts even when the on-site interaction is strong. State-dependent propagation estimates capture the scarcity of particles capable of mediating interactions. An application to out-of-time-ordered correlators recovers a geometrical pattern associated with short lattice paths.

**Background.** A Lieb–Robinson bound replaces the entire system volume with the volume of the observable's effective causal neighborhood. A first-order time integral then contributes one time factor, while the neighborhood contributes its growing spatial volume. This explains the error scaling and the finite comparison window. In the low-density application, an operator-norm bound alone cannot see the small parameter: the argument must retain information about which interaction terms are actually occupied in the chosen state.

**Why it matters?** The main strength is a transparent route from solvable one-body dynamics to controlled many-body statements without a mean-field limit. The diffusion application is technically significant, while the low-density result exposes another way free behavior can persist despite strong microscopic couplings. Extending the method to longer times will require effective interacting dynamics or higher-order control, as the authors themselves propose.

### Entropy production from crossing a quantum separatrix
**Peter Stabel, James R. Anglin — arXiv:2611.26737**

Slow driving is usually associated with reversible evolution, but a separatrix can undermine adiabatic reasoning. Quantum mechanically the issue is especially subtle: slowing the drive at fixed level spacing eventually restores adiabaticity, whereas taking a dense-spectrum limit first can retain probabilistic redistribution. This paper identifies a universal description of that redistribution under a specified averaging procedure.

The authors consider two ladders of levels whose parameter dependence produces a dense lattice of narrowly avoided crossings. Small variations in the sweep randomize the large phases accumulated between crossings while leaving the individual Landau–Zener transition probabilities approximately unchanged. The averaged dynamics can therefore be expressed as repeated mixing of level populations.

In an extreme semiclassical limit, this crossing network becomes a continuum transport problem characterized by one parameter describing its geometry. Solving its asymptotic matching determines outgoing probability profiles as well as total branching probabilities. This distinction matters for entropy: splitting a populated sector into two can preserve the leading probability per occupied state, whereas feeding one occupied branch into a larger outgoing sector dilutes it.

For a uniform ensemble initially occupying only one incoming sector in an entropy-producing merging regime, the result takes the geometrical form

$$\Delta S_d=\log\left|\frac{\dot N_{\mathrm{out}}}{\dot N_{\mathrm{in}}}\right|.$$

Here the level counts describe the connected dynamical sectors, and dots use the same monotone sweep coordinate. When both incoming sectors are occupied, entropy production depends on their profiles and overlap. Coherently evolved quantum-pendulum ensembles with slightly varying sweeps support the continuum prediction beyond its strict asymptotic limit.

**Background.** The quantum separatrix is the narrow, sweep-rate-dependent region where crossing dynamics changes between predominantly adiabatic and diabatic behavior. An adiabatic passage follows an instantaneous eigenstate; a diabatic passage approximately retains the state character across an avoided crossing. Repeated intermediate-probability passages mix populations. The relevant diagonal entropy belongs to the phase-averaged energy populations. The underlying evolution in each realization remains unitary, so the averaging assumption is essential to interpreting the entropy increase.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is an explicit connection between avoided-crossing geometry and microscopic irreversibility under imperfectly resolved driving. It explains why visibly different final probability distributions need not imply the same amount of entropy production. The controlled continuum limit and the finite-system examples make this a useful direction for understanding slow non-equilibrium processes, with the phase-randomization and initial-sector assumptions determining its scope.

### Majorana quasiparticles, mass hierarchies, and the phase diagram of the Gross-Neveu model in D=2+1
**Igor F. Herbut, Mathew Schneider — arXiv:2611.26872**

Dynamical mass generation in the Gross-Neveu model is familiar, but allowing the full internal order-parameter space can change which massive particles emerge. This paper asks whether an explicit inversion-breaking mass simply suppresses the flavor-breaking order, or instead reorganizes it into additional phases.

The authors study the sign of the interaction favoring internal symmetry breaking in the model with $N$ two-component Dirac fermions. In a real-fermion representation its internal symmetry is $O(2N)$. Decoupling the interaction in a symmetric traceless tensor channel and integrating out the fermions gives an effective potential for a uniform matrix order parameter. The analysis minimizes this potential over its eigenvalues rather than restricting the condensate to one predetermined direction.

The resulting mean-field phase diagram contains a sequence of residual symmetries $O(2N-k)\times O(k)$, with $k=1,\ldots,N$. Increasing the explicit mass takes the system through discontinuous changes in the two eigenvalue multiplicities before full internal symmetry is restored. Configurations with three distinct mass eigenvalues can solve the stationarity equations, but the authors find them unstable.

The two stable mass multiplets have different magnitudes. For odd multiplicities, their real fermions cannot all be paired into ordinary equal-mass Dirac quasiparticles. Particularly close to the transition out of the symmetric phase, the simplest ordered state contains one light Majorana branch and the remaining heavier Majorana branches, together with Goldstone modes. These conclusions follow from the uniform-field mean-field treatment; stability of the phase sequence against fluctuations remains an important further question.

**Background.** The useful distinction is between rewriting a Dirac field in real components and obtaining physically distinct Majorana excitations. A Dirac quasiparticle requires compatible real modes with equal mass. Once the tensor condensate separates the real modes into unequal-mass groups with odd degeneracies, each group has an unmatched mode. The tensor order parameter can rotate through a much larger space than a single scalar mass, allowing the system to change the sizes of these groups instead of making one direct transition.

**Why it matters?** This gives a concrete mechanism by which a standard interacting field theory can generate both a hierarchy of masses and unpaired real quasiparticle branches through ordinary symmetry breaking. The interesting step is the unrestricted tensor minimization and its sequence of competing phases. It suggests a focused target for fluctuation analyses or lattice calculations, rather than establishing an exact finite-flavor phase diagram.

### Solving fermionic sign problems with physics-informed kernels
**Kenji Fukushima, Friederike Ihssen, Renzo Kapust, Jan M. Pawlowski — arXiv:2611.27768**

A change of integration variables can sometimes remove a sign problem, but zeros of a fermionic distribution can obstruct a smooth transformation from a simple positive Gaussian. This paper addresses that obstruction by giving the starting distribution enough structure to support a regular transformation.

The underlying physics-informed kernel is a velocity field transporting both a distribution and its sampling manifold according to a continuity equation. When the flow is regular and the final manifold can be validly deformed to the desired integration contour, samples drawn with positive real weights remain usable without phase reweighting. A single such flow need not exist for the target distribution.

The proposed extension, called a PIKfan, begins with a positive-weighted sum of simpler complex distributions, each separately accessible from real Gaussian sampling. Their combination can already possess the zeros needed by the target. A common second flow transports this structured reference distribution to the physical theory. Observables are evaluated by pushing Gaussian samples along the branches and combining their results with the positive branch weights.

The demonstrations are the one-site Hubbard model across chemical potentials and a $\phi^4$-deformed extension. The paper shows how an unsuitable reference flow becomes singular and how a suitable fan avoids that obstruction. Every demonstrated integral has one variable. Constructing an efficient general algorithm for choosing branches and flows is deferred to future work, and the cost of computing the kernels remains part of the computational problem.

**Background.** The word kernel here denotes the generator of a transport map. Its continuity equation preserves the integration weight when both the density and the manifold evolve together. A regular map cannot create or destroy zeros in a region carrying weight, which explains why a zero-free reference can fail. A fan uses interference among complex branch distributions to provide the required zero structure while keeping their external combination weights positive. Two checks remain distinct: regularity on the sampled manifolds, and validity of the contour deformation connecting those manifolds to the original integral. The latter can reveal obstructions missed by monitoring sampled trajectories alone.

**Why it matters?** The conceptual advance is a more flexible transport architecture for distributions whose zeros defeat a single regular flow. It is worth following because it directly targets an important obstruction in fermionic field-theory sampling. The present evidence establishes a mechanism in analytically tractable examples. Its many-body impact will depend on controlling the number of branches, kernel-construction cost, contour checks, and observable variance as volume grows; the work does not yet establish that scaling.

### Disk 2-point functions for conformal loop ensemble: a derivation from the SLE loop CFT
**Shengzhi Jin, Xin Sun, Baojun Wu — arXiv:2611.27767**

Conformal bootstrap calculations of critical loop correlations often begin by supplying a spectrum inferred from other methods. A more geometric derivation could explain why that spectrum appears and how the correlation function survives at parameters where individual terms become singular. This paper develops such a derivation for two bulk points in a disk.

The observables distinguish loops surrounding the first point, the second point, or both, assigning separate weights to those possibilities. The authors cut a configuration along a loop separating the marked points from the boundary. Conditional independence of the inner and outer loop configurations turns the observable into a pairing of two functionals of the separating loop.

They then use the spectral resolution of the conformal field theory associated with Schramm–Loewner evolution loop measures. Known three-point data determine the primary contributions, and newly derived local Ward identities organize the descendants into Virasoro conformal blocks. Summing over the number of common surrounding loops produces a contour integral whose poles encode the discrete closed-channel spectrum. At irrational parameters, formal residue evaluation reproduces earlier bootstrap expansions. The integral formulation is designed to handle rational parameters more uniformly than those termwise expansions, where poles can collide.

There are important distinctions within the evidence. The established single-loop framework concerns simple loops. Applying its spectral resolution to the particular boundary functionals used here still involves formal steps, and the continuation to dense loops and random-cluster models is motivated by matching analytic structure and known predictions. The authors explicitly identify these extensions as requiring further justification.

**Background.** A conformal loop ensemble describes an entire scale-invariant collection of random loops; Schramm–Loewner evolution supplies the corresponding single-loop framework. Its domain Markov property says that, after revealing suitable enclosing loops, the remaining loops inside follow the same law in the remaining domain. That property enables the geometric cut. The spectral decomposition then plays the role of inserting intermediate CFT states. The closed channel describes propagation between the inner marked-point configuration and the outer boundary; in this construction, its discrete spectrum emerges from the loop weights and the poles of the resulting integral.

**Why it matters?** The attraction is a constructive bridge between random geometry and calculable CFT observables, including an explanation of spectral data that bootstrap treatments take as input. It is relevant to exactly solvable critical models and to understanding the geometric content of conformal expansions. The formal steps limit the present claim to a well-motivated derivational framework rather than a completed rigorous solution across all loop regimes.
