## arXiv-2026-10-20 — Item 1

### Quantum control as renormalization group flow
**Zuo Wang, Paul M. Schindler, Modesto Orozco-Ruiz, Nguyen H. Le, Florian Mintert, Marin Bukov — arXiv:2610.26154**

An ideal Hamiltonian can specify exactly the evolution one wants while requiring interactions that an experiment cannot produce. Counterdiabatic driving is a familiar example: eliminating unwanted excitations generally calls for long-range, multibody terms. The practical question is whether the desired final evolution can instead be generated by reshaping the few available controls.

The authors construct a flow through time-dependent Hamiltonians that preserves the target unitary while reducing the weight of inaccessible operators. Its fixed points are Hamiltonians built entirely from a prescribed accessible control subspace. The construction uses time-dependent unitary frame transformations; requiring the frame to return to the identity at both temporal endpoints preserves the complete final propagator. Suitable boundary conditions on the flow generator also make the integrated inaccessible weight decrease monotonically.

Two applications illustrate the scope. For Floquet engineering, the flow constructs periodic nearest-neighbor hopping protocols implementing a Haldane model beyond the useful regime of high-frequency expansions. For the transverse-field Ising chain, it converts an exact counterdiabatic protocol into schedules involving only the Ising interaction and transverse field, with demonstrations up to 50 spins. The resulting protocol preserves the target endpoint evolution within numerical accuracy, while allowing different intermediate motion.

Monotonic decrease is not a general convergence theorem: the flow can stall, and its numerical cost depends on the operator algebra generated during the deformation. Those qualifications matter when moving beyond the structured examples.

**Background.** A time-dependent change of frame alters both the rotated Hamiltonian and the term generated by the frame’s own motion. This freedom allows two quite different Hamiltonian schedules to produce the same endpoint unitary when $V(0)=V(T)=\mathbb{1}$. The flow parameter labels successive schedules and is separate from physical time.

The adiabatic gauge potential is the auxiliary operator that cancels transitions between instantaneous energy eigenstates during a parameter ramp. Here it supplies an exact seed. The flow redistributes its effect into accessible controls rather than simply deleting its difficult terms.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantive methodological proposal: it turns an exact but inaccessible solution into the starting point of a constructive control procedure. Its appeal for quantum simulation and non-equilibrium physics is the preservation of the target evolution throughout the deformation. Broader usefulness will depend on convergence, manageable operator growth, and experimentally tolerable schedules; the paper provides meaningful demonstrations rather than a universal efficiency guarantee.

## arXiv-2026-10-20 — Item 2

### Correlated decoding loses the surface code's effective distance below threshold
**César Hernando de la Fuente, Joonas Majaniemi, Evert van Nieuwenburg, Felix Frohnert — arXiv:2610.26785**

A decoder that performs better near threshold need not retain that advantage as physical noise decreases. Deep below threshold, the dominant question becomes whether a decoder correctly handles the smallest fault configurations. A small collection of systematically mishandled configurations can change the leading suppression exponent, overwhelming improvements on more numerous but higher-order errors.

This paper identifies such a failure in standard correlated decoding. After decoding one syndrome subgraph, the inferred edges make associated edges in the other subgraph more probable. Under circuit-level noise, however, several distinct fault mechanisms can share the same first-pass edge. Discounting all their partner edges independently allows a second-pass correction to reuse the evidence for one fault several times, without paying for the additional independent faults its proposed explanation requires.

For odd code distance $d$ and physical error probability $p$, the nominal logical error probability scales as $P_{\mathrm{L}}\propto p^{(d+1)/2}$. The tested correlated matching and clustering decoders instead develop failures on configurations of size $(d-1)/2$, producing a trend toward $P_{\mathrm{L}}\propto p^{(d-1)/2}$. The authors trace this behavior to explicit fault mechanisms and support it with simulations under two circuit-level noise models.

They repair the update by damping the conditional probabilities used for reweighting. The damping depends on the physical error rate and makes repeatedly discounted edges sufficiently costly. In the benchmarks, this restores nominal distance scaling while retaining an advantage over uncorrelated decoding. The core matching or clustering routine is unchanged.

**Background.** The subtlety is the difference between a valid marginal conditional probability and a valid joint model. Knowing that one source edge occurred can make either of two partner edges plausible. It does not make their simultaneous occurrence equally cheap. A decoder that multiplies the two conditional probabilities as though they were independent loses this distinction. The regularization compensates for that missing joint constraint; it is an empirically calibrated correction to the reweighting policy.

**Why it matters?** This changes how decoder improvements should be assessed. Thresholds and performance at presently accessible noise rates do not determine the asymptotic logical-error exponent. For QEC and its statistical-mechanics descriptions, the useful lesson is precise: introducing correlations through approximate conditional updates can damage the rare-event structure. The paper also offers a practical repair, making it more consequential than a counterexample alone.

## arXiv-2026-10-20 — Item 3

### Quantum error correction well below the color code threshold
**Ali Lavasani, Adam P. Reed, Joan M. Dreiling, Annie J. Park, Michael Mills, Matteo Puviani, Gabriel Matos, M. S. Allman, Noah Berthusen, Natalie C. Brown, Victor E. Colussi, Brian Estey, Cameron Foltz, Daniel Gresh, Reza Haghshenas, Akhil Isanaka, Nikhil Kotibhaskar, Anthony Ransford, Ciarán Ryan-Anderson, Brian Neyenhuis, David Hayes, Tzvetan S. Metodi, Andrew C. Potter — arXiv:2610.24077**

Color codes offer an attractive route to logical Clifford operations, but their practical value depends on suppressing errors strongly enough to compensate for the cost of syndrome extraction. Demonstrating this requires more than a favorable threshold estimate: larger experimental code blocks must actually become more reliable, including during logical operations.

On Quantinuum’s Helios trapped-ion processor, the authors demonstrate this behavior for triangular color codes of distances 3, 5, and 7. Combining hardware and circuit methods for leakage reduction, they obtain a distance-7 memory error rate of $5^{+4}_{-2}\times10^{-6}$ per syndrome-extraction cycle and an approximately twelvefold suppression for each increase of distance by two. The memory experiments use twenty extraction cycles; the smallest reported rate is inferred from three observed failures, accounting for its substantial uncertainty.

Below-threshold suppression also appears in two-qubit Clifford randomized benchmarking at distances 3 and 5. The distance-5 error rate is $7^{+3}_{-3}\times10^{-5}$ per logical two-qubit Clifford. Streaming neural decoders trained on emulator data also process the experimental syndrome records with promising accuracy and latency. The decoding in this study is performed offline, so the latency measurements establish compatibility with future real-time operation rather than its integration into these experiments.

**Background.** Leakage is especially damaging because a qubit outside the computational subspace can remain there and contaminate successive interactions. Optical repumping returns leaked population to the qubit subspace. The SWAP-superdense syndrome extraction circuit supplies a complementary mechanism: data and syndrome roles are exchanged so that physical qubits carrying data eventually undergo measurement and reset. Its schedule covers every data qubit over three cycles.

The color-code geometry permits transversal single-qubit Clifford gates, and trapped-ion connectivity permits transversal CNOTs between blocks. This makes logical Clifford circuits a particularly relevant test of the code’s architectural advantages.

**Why it matters?** This experiment demonstrates an important principle strongly enough to merit inclusion: color-code redundancy substantially improves both memory and logical Clifford performance on the same hardware platform. It provides concrete evidence for evaluating code families through their complete operation costs. Extending the result to universal computation still requires high-fidelity non-Clifford resources and integrated real-time decoding; the present achievement establishes two major components of that route.

## arXiv-2026-10-20 — Item 4

### The Anyon Atlas
**Jonah Berean-Dutcher, Clay Cordova, Julio Parra-Martinez, Richard Tuddenham — arXiv:2610.25439**

How much of the space of topological quantum field theories is generated by familiar gauge-theory constructions? Answering this requires a systematic inventory of consistent anyon theories and their relationships, rather than a growing collection of individually constructed examples.

The authors develop a numerical search for modular and supermodular data, reaching 22 anyon types in the bosonic case and 26 in the fermionic case. They organize the resulting entries through fusion structure, anyon condensation, and possible Chern–Simons realizations. The accompanying atlas therefore records connections between theories as well as their individual fingerprints.

The most interesting discoveries include exotic examples with 11 and 12 anyon types. For these, the authors solve the pentagon and hexagon equations and establish full unitary topological quantum field theories. This goes beyond finding matrices that pass necessary consistency tests. The larger claim—that certain examples cannot be related to any Chern–Simons theory even through generalized gauging and condensation—remains conjectural. Extremely large parent theories have not been excluded. Likewise, the numerical classification does not provide an exact certificate that every admissible datum within its rank range has been found.

**Background.** The modular $S$ and $T$ matrices encode mutual braiding and topological twists. They strongly constrain fusion but do not supply all information about combining and exchanging several anyons. F-moves change fusion bases; the pentagon and hexagon equations require different sequences of reassociation and braiding to agree. Solving them establishes the missing local consistency.

Fermionic, or supermodular, data retain a transparent fermion that braids trivially with everything. In anyon condensation, selected bosonic excitations become part of the vacuum, identifying some sectors and confining others. Witt equivalence organizes the resulting relationships up to appropriate invertible layers.

**Why it matters?** This is both a substantial classification resource and evidence that the landscape of topological field theories may extend beyond its familiar gauge-theory organization. The distinction between certified unitary examples and conjectural absence of every possible parent is essential to that assessment. For broader interests in QFT and topological quantum information, the atlas supplies concrete theories against which proposed organizing principles can be tested. Their existence alone does not establish a practical computational realization.

## arXiv-2026-10-20 — Item 5

### Bosonization for the Schwinger effect at small fermion mass: analytic formula for the 1+1D current in an arbitrary time-dependent, inhomogeneous electric field
**Samuel E. Gralla, Morifumi Mizuno — arXiv:2610.24272**

Pair production in a general electric-field profile is difficult to treat analytically because the quantum state remembers the field’s history and spatial variation. Even in one spatial dimension, directly calculating the current usually requires solving the background-dependent Dirac equation and regularizing a coincident fermion bilinear. The authors ask whether bosonization can bypass these obstacles without restricting the background to a special solvable shape.

They derive the current through second order in the fermion mass $m$ for an externally prescribed electric field with arbitrary subsequent time and space dependence. Their hybrid boson–fermion method uses the fermionic description to handle the initial massive vacuum and the bosonic description to calculate the field-dependent response. Since the bosonized current is linear in the boson field, the latter calculation avoids the composite-operator regularization needed in the direct fermion approach.

The answer contains the massless response, a nonanalytic $m^2\log m$ correction, and an analytic quadratic correction, with no linear mass term. For spatially homogeneous backgrounds, an independent fermionic calculation using adiabatic regularization reproduces the result. The expansion does not require a weak electric field, but it does require a sufficiently short observation interval: in natural units, $m\mathcal{T}\ll1$, where the calligraphic symbol denotes the elapsed timescale. Electromagnetic backreaction is outside the prescribed-background problem.

**Background.** The delicate step is the vacuum, rather than the standard bosonization dictionary. Expanding the bosonic interaction naively from the infinite past produces a logarithmic divergence. The authors instead retain the true initial vacuum while expanding the evolution after the field is switched on. Vacuum-sensitive terms are evaluated on the free-fermion side, revealing the missing logarithmic mass dependence. In the independent homogeneous calculation, low-momentum modes expose the same infrared origin. A smooth Taylor expansion in mass would therefore miss part of the physical answer.

**Why it matters?** This supplies an analytical benchmark for genuinely space- and time-dependent non-equilibrium QFT, together with a useful way to divide a calculation between dual descriptions. Its value is especially clear for checking numerical real-time methods and their treatment of vacuum subtraction. The restricted mass-timescale regime makes the benchmark controlled; extending it to long-time behavior or self-consistent electric-field dynamics would require further work.

## arXiv-2026-10-20 — Item 6

### Anomalously fast heating of ground states in dephased $SU(N)$ spin systems
**Tairan Lou, Junxiao Chen, Xiang-Ping Jiang, Mingdi Xu, Lei Pan — arXiv:2610.25310**

Can a ground state heat asymptotically faster than every positive-temperature Gibbs preparation of the same Hamiltonian under the same bath? Showing a few crossings is insufficient. One must explain why the ground state avoids slow relaxation modes and why no finite positive temperature accidentally avoids them too.

The authors study ferromagnetic exchange systems with local color dephasing, comparing preparations within a fixed sector of color occupations. Their ground state is a color-Dicke state: an equal-amplitude superposition of all allowed configurations. Its diagonal probabilities already match the final maximally mixed state. The departure from stationarity resides entirely in coherence.

Those coherences can be grouped into uniform coherence shells according to how many sites differ between the two configurations labeling a matrix element. Each shell commutes with every exchange bond and decays at a bath-determined rate. This prevents coherence from generating a population imbalance and yields the exact energy law $E_D(t)-E_\infty=(E_0-E_\infty)e^{-2\gamma t}$, where $E_0$ is the initial ground-state energy, $E_\infty$ the stationary energy, and $\gamma$ the local dephasing rate.

To establish the strong quantum Mpemba effect, the authors additionally derive an energy-resolved sign criterion ensuring a slower thermal contribution at every finite positive temperature. They prove it in an exact finite-chain example and verify it numerically in larger chains, including nonintegrable exchange. The ground-state solution extends to arbitrary connected ferromagnetic exchange graphs, but the thermal advantage needs this additional criterion; an arbitrary-size theorem for that criterion remains open.

**Background.** The slowest Liouvillian modes matter only if the initial state has a nonzero amplitude in them. Thermal states can approach the ground state continuously while their asymptotic decay rates remain different: an arbitrarily small slow amplitude eventually dominates, whereas the ground-state amplitude vanishes exactly. This also explains why asymptotic acceleration need not imply a uniformly shorter time to every chosen accuracy.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is an unusually transparent many-body mechanism for initial-state selection of relaxation channels. It separates an exact solvable trajectory from the extra conditions needed to compare an entire thermal family. That separation is useful for non-equilibrium physics beyond Mpemba terminology: algebraic restrictions on accessible operator space can control relaxation even when the surrounding Hamiltonian dynamics is nonintegrable.

## arXiv-2026-10-20 — Item 7

### The long-range Ising CFT: perturbative and nonperturbative approaches
**Zechuan Zheng — arXiv:2610.25940**

Power-law interactions turn the Ising critical point into a continuously tunable family of nonlocal conformal field theories. With couplings $J(r)\propto r^{-(d+s)}$, spatial dimension $d$ and interaction-range parameter $s$ determine the critical theory. Perturbation theory is available near the two ends of the interacting window, but neither endpoint description is weakly coupled throughout the interior.

This paper attacks that interior using several descriptions with different sources of error. It extends the expansion around the generalized free field from three to eight loops in one, two, and three dimensions, develops expansions near the opposite endpoint, and combines them through resummation. It also constructs Hamiltonian truncation for the nonlocal theory and compares with numerical renormalization group and lattice Monte Carlo results.

The principal target is the scaling dimension of the leading spin-flip-even scalar, which governs the thermal departure from criticality. The cleanest quantitative comparison is in one dimension: independently obtained results agree at the sub-percent level where the relevant extrapolations converge. The evidence is less complete in two and three dimensions, where several nonperturbative calculations remain at finite cutoff or finite lattice size. The paper’s detailed results therefore support a more differentiated picture than uniform precision across all dimensions and couplings.

**Background.** The generalized free field has Wick-factorized correlators but a continuously adjustable scaling dimension, here fixed to $\Delta_\sigma=(d-s)/2$ by the nonlocal kinetic term. Its oscillator states on the conformal cylinder provide a basis for Hamiltonian truncation: add interactions, retain finitely many states, and infer scaling dimensions from energy gaps.

A crucial correction concerns vacuum fluctuations. An excited state consumes some of the truncation budget, leaving fewer intermediate vacuum fluctuations than the vacuum itself. In this problem, their unequal contributions can survive increasing the cutoff. The authors compensate using lower-cutoff vacuum energies before extrapolating.

**Why it matters?** The strongest advance is a quantitatively cross-checked bridge between perturbative endpoints and an interacting nonlocal CFT. It also extends a useful nonperturbative field-theory method beyond local examples. For interests in critical phenomena and solvable structures, this provides a demanding benchmark where continuously varying universal data can test both analytical organization and numerical approximations. It is a precision study of selected CFT data rather than a solution of the complete theory.

## arXiv-2026-10-20 — Item 8

### Strong-to-Weak Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking Transitions on a Quantum Processor
**Zhi-Yuan Wei, Christopher Fechisin, Yu-Xin Wang, Jeet Shah, Tsung-Cheng Lu, Cheng-Ju Lin, Alexey V. Gorshkov — arXiv:2610.26229**

Mixed-state order can remain invisible to ordinary expectation values because its natural diagnostics depend nonlinearly on the density matrix. This creates an experimental obstacle: preparing a noisy state and measuring conventional correlations may entirely miss its transition. The authors develop a measurement construction for resolving strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking in a controlled family of dephased states.

Their setting starts from tunable matrix-product states subjected to power-law-correlated dephasing. On a superconducting processor, systems of 6, 9, and 12 qubits exhibit finite-size crossings and scaling collapses of nonlinear correlators while the corresponding linear correlations decay. The study maps transition estimates across initial-state and channel parameters, with additional string measurements probing mixed-state topological order.

The reconstruction uses prior knowledge of the channel in an essential way. Computational-basis samples of the prepared pure state are combined with a classically computed dephasing kernel. The same quantum data can then be reused at different noise strengths and correlation exponents. One measurement setting supplies fidelity, Rényi-2, and linear correlators; a second setting using a Hadamard test supplies string observables. Polynomial classical processing does not itself guarantee polynomial sample requirements at fixed precision.

**Background.** For the sector used here, strong symmetry means $U\rho=\rho$, whereas weak symmetry requires $U\rho U^\dagger=\rho$; $U$ flips every spin. The distinction concerns one-sided action on the density operator versus simultaneous conjugation.

The fidelity correlator compares the state with the state transformed by separated charged insertions. With $O=Z_iZ_j$, the Rényi-2 correlator is $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho O\rho O^\dagger)/\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^2)$. Its two density-matrix factors expose order unavailable to a linear expectation value. Long-distance nonlinear order diagnoses the transition even though finite preparations retain their exact sector constraint. Fidelity and Rényi-2 diagnostics need not have identical transition points or critical behavior.

**Why it matters?** The useful advance is a concrete way to make nonlinear mixed-state order experimentally accessible when the noise model is known. It connects theoretical dephasing-induced transitions to measurements without requiring full state tomography. The present evidence establishes finite-size critical signatures; larger systems and controlled sampling costs are needed for sharper thermodynamic conclusions. This is particularly relevant to the broader problem of identifying phases of noisy quantum states through operational diagnostics.

## arXiv-2026-10-20 — Item 9

### On Higher-Form Fracton Hydrodynamics with Momentum Conservation
**Ben Bobell, Rahul Nandkishore, Andrew Lucas — arXiv:2610.26862**

Unusual conservation laws can slow transport dramatically, but they cannot always be added independently to an ordinary fluid. Dipole conservation illustrates the difficulty: translating a charged configuration changes its dipole moment. The question is which generalized conservation laws can coexist with momentum conservation without requiring additional spontaneous symmetry breaking and its associated Goldstone modes.

Using the Martin–Siggia–Rose effective field theory of fluctuating dynamics, the authors derive a generalized Gauss’s law and two representation-theoretic consistency conditions. One condition controls the differential construction of conserved charges. The other tests whether the current and stress tensor can retain their symmetry-allowed tensor structure after coupling to fluid motion. Higher-form symmetries satisfy these conditions, and the resulting ideal hydrodynamics includes non-relativistic magnetohydrodynamics as the three-dimensional one-form example.

Within the stated framework, the three-dimensional analysis leaves higher-form symmetries as the compatible possibilities. The general exclusion theorem applies in sufficiently large odd dimensions relative to tensor rank and assumes an irreducible current representation. Its premises also include local constitutive relations, a time-reversal-invariant stationary description, and an exactness assumption: allowed charge-weight functions must be generated by the specified local differential potentials. These assumptions delimit the exclusion result.

**Background.** The one-form example has a divergence-free flux density, $\partial_i\rho_i=0$. Flux cannot begin or end in the bulk, so integrated charges can be expressed through boundary fluxes. This supplies the geometric mechanism that allows translation and charge conservation to coexist.

The Martin–Siggia–Rose construction introduces auxiliary response fields whose variations generate stochastic equations of motion. Here symmetry restrictions on those fields translate conservation laws into restrictions on currents. Enforcing the density constraint modifies the constitutive relations; simply appending a momentum equation would miss this necessary step.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful structural constraint on non-equilibrium effective field theory: transport symmetries and momentum conservation impose joint requirements, not a menu of independent choices. It explains the special compatibility of flux conservation with fluid motion and narrows the search for more exotic hydrodynamics. A proposed exception must identify which assumption it evades or include the additional symmetry-breaking degrees of freedom, making the result scientifically actionable despite its conditional scope.

## arXiv-2026-10-20 — Item 10

### Trapped-ion quantum simulation of (1+1)D SU(2) lattice gauge theory via loop-string-hadron formulation
**Toshiaki Kaji, Jesse R. Stryker, Yutaro Iiyama, Lento Nagano, Enrico Rinaldi, Christian W. Bauer, Koji Terashi — arXiv:2610.24091**

Quantum simulation of a non-Abelian gauge theory must represent both matter and an electric field with multiple allowed representations. In one spatial dimension, eliminating gauge variables can greatly simplify demonstrations, but retaining them tests ingredients that become essential in higher dimensions. This experiment deliberately takes that more demanding route.

The authors implement real-time dynamics of SU(2) lattice gauge theory with one fermion flavor on Quantinuum’s trapped-ion H2 processor. Their four-site construction retains local bosonic gauge registers and reaches the $j=1$ electric-flux sector, beyond the commonly used $j_{\max}=1/2$ truncation. A two-qubit register represents the bosonic occupation $n_l\in\{0,1,2\}$. They track the electric-energy profile through up to six steps of Trotterized evolution and compare the hardware measurements with the corresponding noiseless circuit.

Both noise rescaling and postselection on gauge constraints improve agreement. Averaged deviations decrease by approximately 60% and 75%, respectively, relative to raw hardware data. Postselection retains about a quarter of the shots in the deepest circuits. Its increasing sampling cost is therefore a material issue for scaling, even though it works well in this small demonstration. The comparison establishes reproduction of the chosen Trotterized dynamics; continuum and truncation convergence remain separate questions.

**Background.** The loop-string-hadron formulation builds non-Abelian Gauss’s law into a local basis described by two binary fermionic occupations and one nonnegative bosonic occupation. The remaining intersite constraints require equal flux counts at the two ends of each link. They can consequently be checked using occupation numbers rather than imposing a matrix-valued gauge constraint directly.

Matter hopping conditionally raises or lowers the flux register, keeping matter transport tied to the electric field. Retaining this register is the central representational step: the gauge field participates explicitly in the simulated dynamics.

**Why it matters?** The advance is a meaningful hardware test of a gauge-theory encoding with richer electric representations and explicit local gauge degrees of freedom. It connects a theoretically useful formulation to executable circuits while exposing the cost of enforcing its constraints under noise. For quantum simulation and QFT, this is a concrete building block toward more demanding gauge dynamics; its significance lies in demonstrating that building block rather than in the physical size of the simulated lattice.
