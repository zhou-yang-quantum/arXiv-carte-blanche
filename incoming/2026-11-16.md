## arXiv-2026-11-16 — Item 1

### Fast mixing and thermal phases from strong decay of correlations
**Chao Yin, Vedika Khemani — arXiv:2611.17433**

When does the structure of a thermal state guarantee rapid relaxation under local dynamics? Ordinary decay of connected correlations is insufficient to settle this question, while familiar sufficient conditions can demand more control over boundaries than the physical dynamics requires.

The authors identify a stronger correlation-decay condition that supports a dynamical description of thermal phases. Along smooth paths of local commuting Hamiltonians, they construct exact, two-way quasi-local dissipative connections between Gibbs states, and unitary connections between their canonical purifications, in system-size-independent time. If the path includes a product state, single-site Glauber dynamics has a constant spectral gap throughout it. Applications include the entire high-temperature Potts phase in finite dimensions and frustrated perturbations of field-polarized Ising systems. A constant relaxation gap should be distinguished from constant-time global mixing.

**Background.** The condition controls how a local state responds to conditioning on an arbitrarily large distant region, including atypical configurations. It is stronger than connected-correlation decay but permits boundary behavior excluded by strong spatial mixing. Quasi-local generators have rapidly decaying long-range tails; these tails allow exact connections despite correlations at arbitrarily large separation. Requiring connections in both directions prevents trivial erasure from identifying every thermal state with a product state.

**Why it matters?** This makes thermal phase structure constrain a concrete sampling dynamics. The connection to information storage is particularly valuable: it helps distinguish robust slow relaxation from a limitation of existing mixing proofs, using quantum locality tools even for classical models.

## arXiv-2026-11-16 — Item 2

### Microcausality Bounds on Dispersion Laws
**Paolo Creminelli, Michael Mancini, Warin Patrick McBlain — arXiv:2611.17407**

A medium can break Lorentz boosts while the underlying theory remains relativistically causal. How much freedom then remains in the dispersion of a weakly coupled excitation, beyond requiring a subluminal sound speed?

For tree-level, nondissipative modes, microcausality constrains complex-frequency singularities through $\operatorname{Im}\omega\leq|\operatorname{Im}k|$. The authors turn this into coefficient bounds by showing that $k+\omega(k)$ and $k-\omega(k)$ belong to a restricted analytic class. Toeplitz positivity supplies a hierarchy of joint constraints on higher-derivative corrections. Crucially, the allowed corrections collapse as $c_s\to1$. Keeping a correction fixed therefore forces the dispersion expansion to fail at a parametrically lower scale. Several microscopic models test the bounds.

**Background.** Typically real functions preserve the sign of the imaginary part within their analytic domain. This converts causal propagation into positivity conditions on Taylor coefficients. The paper distinguishes singularities of the frequency from singularities of its square: taking a square root can obstruct the former expansion without introducing another physical mode, whereas singularities of the latter diagnose the need for additional degrees of freedom.

**Why it matters?** The result strengthens low-energy consistency tests beyond a speed bound. It offers a systematic way to constrain effective descriptions in states that break boosts, while making the assumptions about dissipation and analytic breakdown explicit.

## arXiv-2026-11-16 — Item 3

### What Comes Out Of Monopole-Fermion Scattering?
**David Tong — arXiv:2611.17416**

Monopole scattering presents a striking mismatch between symmetry and particle language: an incoming fermion can carry conserved quantum numbers that no finite collection of allowed outgoing particles reproduces. Calling the outgoing state a twisted sector resolves the formal bookkeeping, but leaves its detector interpretation obscure.

Tong constructs outgoing wave packets and computes particle occupations, charge correlations, and energy correlations. The state contains infinitely many low-energy particle–antiparticle excitations, with an infrared-divergent number but finite energy. Measurements fragment the outgoing spherical wave into ordinary hard particles accompanied by a soft cloud. The hard tracks have standard quantum numbers; the unusual accounting survives in the cloud and its large charge fluctuations.

**Background.** The lowest angular-momentum scattering channel reduces to a chiral theory on the radial half-line. Bosonization implements the monopole boundary condition, but the resulting outgoing excitation need not belong to the finite-particle Fock space. A twisted sector changes the fermionic boundary conditions. Here that abstract change is translated into a rearrangement of the filled sea and measurable correlations between angular detector regions.

**Why it matters?** The paper supplies an operational interpretation of a longstanding QFT puzzle. Its broader appeal is methodological: it shows how bosonized and sector-based descriptions can be connected to actual particle measurements when an asymptotic state contains essential infrared structure.

### Correlation functions of the Lieb--Liniger Bose gas in equilibrium states: a microscopic approach to space-time asymptotics
**Frank Göhmann, Karol K. Kozlowski, Mikhail D. Minin — arXiv:2611.18316**

Knowing the exact eigenstates of an interacting integrable model does not make its dynamical correlations easy to calculate. The thermodynamic limit requires resumming infinitely many microscopic contributions, and heuristic hydrodynamics does not address every observable.

The authors reorganize form-factor expansions to extract large-distance, long-time behavior at fixed $x/t$. For density and current correlations, their microscopic calculation reproduces generalized-hydrodynamic predictions. More significantly, they obtain the asymptotics of $\langle\Phi(x,t)\Phi^\dagger(0,0)\rangle$, governed by a space-time-dependent nonlinear integral equation. The stationary ensembles considered have analytic, rapidly decaying Bethe filling functions, encompassing thermal states and suitable generalized Gibbs ensembles.

**Background.** A form-factor series sums matrix elements over intermediate eigenstates. The difficulty is that thermodynamic resummation and asymptotic analysis need not commute. The multidimensional free-flow construction maps the interacting series to a resummable free-fermion structure through functional transformations, then carries its ordered asymptotic expansion back to the interacting theory. It is an analytic device, rather than an assertion that the physical particles stop interacting.

**Why it matters?** This provides a microscopic route beyond correlations of conserved densities. It is especially interesting for non-equilibrium stationary states, where exact benchmarks for coherence-sensitive observables can test the reach of hydrodynamic descriptions and numerical methods.

### Dissipation-induced integrability in a spin-1 chain and a Bose-Hubbard mixture
**Kaixiang Lu, Mingdi Xu, Xiang-Ping Jiang, Lei Pan — arXiv:2611.17928**

Can coupling a nonintegrable chain to an environment create exact solvability? The obstacle is that solvable evolution between quantum jumps does not automatically make the full dissipative dynamics solvable: jumps return population and coherence to the system.

The authors engineer a bond channel whose dissipative amplitude cancels one direction of coherent spin conversion. The surviving directed moves obey an algebra generating Yang–Baxter integrability and commuting open-chain charges. A compatible triangular structure also yields the entire Liouvillian spectrum. Exponentially many blocked configurations support stationary superpositions. Resolving previously indistinguishable environmental outputs preserves the conditional Hamiltonian but dephases those superpositions. A related construction produces counterpropagating bosons at a tuned interaction point.

**Background.** Nil-Coxeter relations mean that repeated local moves vanish and neighboring moves satisfy braid-type identities. Here a conversion leaves a blocking spin behind, making these relations physically transparent. Indistinguishable decay paths contribute amplitudes coherently to a common jump operator. Separating their outputs changes the information acquired by the environment, even when the total loss kernel—and thus no-jump evolution—stays fixed.

**Why it matters?** The useful advance is a construction principle connecting interference, integrability, and environmental distinguishability. It also sharply separates conditional solvability from full open-system dynamics, providing an exact setting for studying how monitoring changes stationary quantum coherence.

### Efficient representations by PEPS: critical free-fermion and chiral topological states
**Ignacio Cirac, Rahul Trivedi — arXiv:2611.17936**

Two-dimensional critical fermions and chiral topological states are difficult tests of tensor-network expressiveness. Numerical success and area-law intuition do not establish that a prescribed global approximation error can be reached with polynomial resources.

The authors construct projected entangled-pair states with bond dimension polynomial in system size and inverse global error. Their examples include a half-filled two-dimensional tight-binding ground state, uniformly gapped finite-range quadratic models, and specified projected chiral states. For the critical model, wavelet transformations separate scales into one-dimensional chains and local pieces. Replacing the chains by general matrix product approximations, then reversing the transformations, gives the desired representation. Gapped Gaussian models use a different filtering construction.

**Background.** Bond dimension measures the size of virtual indices joining neighboring tensors. Importantly, a free-fermion target need not be approximated most efficiently by a Gaussian tensor network: the critical construction escapes Gaussian restrictions when representing the extracted chains. Gutzwiller projection locally restricts occupancies of several copies; the authors control error amplification for their specified family of identical inputs.

**Why it matters?** This establishes expressiveness with global error control in physically important cases. It strengthens the theoretical basis of tensor-network simulation, while leaving contraction cost and the practical discovery of good tensors as separate questions. The projected-family restriction matters when extending the result to interacting chiral phases.

### Anyon Proliferation at Self-Triality: From a Lattice Model to Adjoint QCD$_3$
**Yuxuan Guo — arXiv:2611.17724**

When symmetry puts several anyon-condensation channels on equal footing, their mutual statistics can frustrate simultaneous condensation. Could this competition produce a continuous symmetry-breaking transition different from the usual local-order-parameter scenario?

Guo constructs a honeycomb color-code deformation with three equally weighted channels and proposes dual bosonic and fermionic continuum descriptions. Selecting one channel would break the permutation symmetry while reducing double-toric-code order to residual toric-code order. In the fermionic description, the transition corresponds to changing the mass of an adjoint Majorana fermion in a Chern–Simons gauge theory. Symmetry and phase matching support the proposal; continuity and emergent time reversal remain unestablished.

**Background.** Self-triality means that a symmetry permutes three competing channels. Their excitations are mutually nonlocal: braiding one around another changes the wavefunction, obstructing their treatment as simultaneously ordinary condensates. The Chern–Simons–Higgs theory encodes those statistics through gauge fields. Its fermionic dual uses an adjoint field, transforming by conjugation like a traceless matrix, with fermion parity gauged to obtain a bosonic theory compatible with the spin model.

**Why it matters?** This is a concrete bridge from a QEC-derived lattice Hamiltonian to strongly coupled continuum QFT. Establishing its proposed critical point would reveal a route to symmetry breaking controlled by anyon statistics. Numerical tests are essential to turn the matching construction into a demonstrated universality class.

### Magic of Dicke states: density, cost and yield
**Yves H. Kwan — arXiv:2611.17623**

Does a small many-body magic diagnostic imply little useful non-Clifford resource? Dicke states provide a controlled test because their permutation symmetry makes them tractable while their fixed excitation number creates global correlations.

The paper calculates their stabilizer Rényi entropy analytically. At fixed filling $f=n/L$, it is extensive except at $f=1/2$, where it grows logarithmically. Nevertheless, block protocols extract an extensive average number of CCZ resource states even at half-filling, and preparation cost remains extensive. The yield fluctuates macroscopically between runs, so the average rate does not describe a typical sharply concentrated outcome. Conversion to T states requires a catalyst in the construction studied.

**Background.** The stabilizer Rényi entropy diagnoses the distribution of Pauli expectation values; its scaling need not coincide with operational conversion rates. A Dicke state is a fixed-magnetization projection of tilted product spins. Small subsets behave approximately like a mixture over a common azimuthal phase. That shared phase correlates the successes of different extraction blocks, explaining why increasing system size does not eliminate relative yield fluctuations.

**Why it matters?** This gives an explicit separation between a popular diagnostic and usable computational resources. The connection between global projection, locally accessible magic, and non-self-averaging yield is also a useful many-body mechanism, beyond the immediate resource-theory application.

### Entanglement and information dynamics in charged dual-unitary circuits with impurities
**Shachar Fraenkel, Colin Rylands — arXiv:2611.18435**

Can a single symmetry-breaking impurity alter relaxation far away and leave persistent entanglement between separated regions? Conserved quantities offer a mechanism: scattering can create correlations that ballistic carriers transport through the bulk.

The authors place a non-dual-unitary impurity inside a charged dual-unitary circuit. They calculate early-time entropies, mutual information, and logarithmic negativity exactly, then examine later dynamics through quasiparticle and entanglement membrane descriptions. Impurity-generated charge fluctuations can make entropy nonmonotonic and change its stationary value. **Why it matters?** The impurity provides a localized source of extensive long-range correlations, making this a useful solvable laboratory for non-equilibrium information transport. The distinction between persistent mutual information and demonstrably quantum entanglement is particularly important when assessing relaxation in scrambling systems.

### Local Quantum Metrology: Precision Limits for Observers in QFT
**Markus Fröb, Albert Much — arXiv:2611.17948**

What estimation precision is available to an observer restricted to a spacetime region? Standard quantum-metrology formulas use density matrices and traces, whereas continuum local observable algebras generally lack that intrinsic structure.

The authors define the relevant quantum Fisher information through Uhlmann transition probability on an arbitrary von Neumann algebra. They prove a quantum Cramér–Rao bound, including collective measurements on independent copies, and establish sharpness under stated regularity assumptions. Thus the quantity equals the supremum of classical Fisher information available to measurements in the accessible algebra. Sharpness can mean arbitrarily close approximation rather than existence of one optimal measurement.

**Background.** Uhlmann transition probability compares states through the largest overlap of their vector representatives. It remains meaningful without a local density matrix. Its infinitesimal change defines the Bures statistical geometry. The construction projects state variation onto directions accessible to local observables; variation visible only outside the region cannot improve local precision. This supplies the operational bridge between algebraic localization and estimation theory.

**Why it matters?** The result makes information geometry quantitatively meaningful for continuum observers. Its value is more than formal generalization: comparisons between regions now compare attainable precision, allowing localized information loss to be studied without introducing an arbitrary ultraviolet tensor-factor cutoff.

### Real-time simulation of scattering in $\phi^4$ theory: localized excitation, scalable wave packet preparation, and peak-generating artifacts
**Boyu Gao, Natalie Klco — arXiv:2611.18552**

Reliable real-time scattering requires incoming particles prepared above the interacting vacuum. It also requires distinguishing physical outgoing structure from peaks and apparent particle production introduced by lattice dispersion or field digitization.

The authors jointly optimize a local interpolating operator and its single-particle momentum profile through eigenvalue problems. Translated, weighted copies prepare extended wave packets, with relative momentum-state phases fixed using a Lorentz-symmetry relation. Matrix product simulations then identify two distinct artifacts: Airy distortions caused by lattice propagation, and artificial inelasticity caused by inadequate local field sampling. After controlling these effects, they report emerging evidence for physical inelastic processes, rather than a completed continuum scattering determination.

**Background.** An interpolating operator creates a state with substantial overlap onto the desired particle sector. Here localization and particle fidelity are optimized together. Enlarging field range at fixed local dimension can therefore worsen accuracy.

**Why it matters?** This directly addresses preparation and validation bottlenecks in quantum simulation of QFT. The artifact analysis is especially useful: apparently interesting scattering signals can survive visual inspection while failing controlled digitization or continuum checks.

### Spectrum of a 2D QFT analogue of the SYK model
**Avik Chakraborty, Pratik K. Das, Manavendra Mahato — arXiv:2611.18063**

Extending the solvability of the Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev model to a field theory requires understanding more than its two-point saddle. The four-point function determines the exchanged spectrum and exposes soft modes or instabilities hidden by the saddle alone.

For a two-dimensional model with a topological kinetic term and random interactions, the authors sum conformal ladders at order $1/N$. A single-lightcone-coordinate exchange splits the problem into even and odd sectors. The resulting poles form a spectral curve in two conformal weights, rather than the isolated spectrum familiar from the one-dimensional model. Reparametrization modes at $(2,0)$ and $(0,2)$ are non-normalizable, while an odd Lorentz mode at $(1,1)$ is normalizable. The contour representation has a restricted parameter domain.

**Background.** A conformal ladder kernel adds one interaction rung to a four-point diagram. Diagonalizing it turns repeated rungs into a geometric resolvent; eigenvalue one produces a singular channel. The extra exchange symmetry must be resolved before constructing its complete eigenbasis. Normalizability distinguishes modes belonging to that spectral space from formal symmetry variations requiring separate treatment.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantial spectral calculation in a tractable interacting QFT. It also identifies obstacles to a simple gravitational interpretation: an Einstein-gravity dual and the fate of potentially unstable channels require analysis beyond the conformal limit.

### Invertible parafermionic topological phases in one dimension
**André L. G. Mudry, Ömer M. Aksoy — arXiv:2611.17429**

Combining two invertible phases usually suggests an order-independent operation. Parafermionic degrees of freedom challenge that intuition because the exchange algebra retains phases more general than fermionic signs.

Under stated assumptions about protected boundary algebras, the authors classify one-dimensional phases through boundary projective representations and derive their stacking rules. The classification data determine protected edge degeneracy, but also reveal that stacking can be non-Abelian. Explicit Hamiltonians with $\mathbb{Z}_N^3$ symmetry realize a group of order $2N^3$ for the odd-prime-power construction studied. Even the symmetry-protected subgroup can have noncommutative composition.

**Background.** The parafermionic exchange algebra multiplies reordered operators by an Nth root of unity. A boundary symmetry representation records both its parafermion charge and the phase acquired when symmetry operations compose. Stacking must respect the ordered, graded combination of these boundary algebras. Cross terms between their charges then modify the projective phase differently when the stacking order is reversed. This explains why simply counting phases misses the new structure.

**Why it matters?** The contribution is a composition law for topological resources, not merely another list of phases. It broadens the familiar edge-representation approach and offers a precise setting in which generalized exchange statistics change how independently invertible systems combine.

## arXiv-2026-11-16 — Item 14

### Second order and secondary functional renormalization group flow equations
**Stefan Floerchinger — arXiv:2611.18025**

A mass regulator is attractive for functional QFT calculations because it preserves locality and avoids problematic momentum-dependent analytic structure. Its drawback is ultraviolet convergence: the ordinary flow can remain divergent even though infrared modes are regulated.

Floerchinger develops two distinct extensions. Differentiating the usual flow with respect to its scale gives exact higher-order equations, useful for exposing two-loop structures and checking truncations, but does not generally remove ultraviolet subdivergences. A secondary flow instead differentiates with respect to effective mass-squared parameters. Sufficient differentiation makes this auxiliary flow finite; integration reconstructs the original flow with constants carrying ultraviolet information. An effective-potential application illustrates the construction.

**Background.** The functional renormalization group evolves a scale-dependent effective action. With $R_k=k^2$, arbitrarily high momenta still contribute to the flow. Mass differentiation introduces additional propagator powers, improving convergence. Differentiating the full scale-dependent action also differentiates its interacting vertices, which is why the first construction does not automatically obtain the same improvement. Integration constants restore the microscopic information removed by differentiation.

**Why it matters?** This offers a potentially useful route to nonperturbative calculations with a simpler causal analytic structure. Its practical impact will depend on stable truncations and numerical implementations; the additional exact equations already provide independent consistency checks on approximations.

### Transfer-Matrix Calculus for Infinite Matrix Product States
**Qi Yang, Philippe Corboz — arXiv:2611.18427**

Long-range interactions can change infrared physics, yet infinite matrix product calculations often replace them by finite sums of exponentials. Even an accurate fit over many distances can alter a nonanalytic long-wavelength feature.

The authors formulate transfer-matrix calculus, evaluating prescribed interactions directly as matrix functions. Differentiating those functions supplies variational gradients, effective Hamiltonians, excitation actions, and energy variance within one framework. For a solvable hopping model, the method reproduces a $|k|$ dispersion cusp that exponential fits round off. In the half-filled Coulomb chain at $V=2$, variance extrapolation resolves a small charge-density-wave amplitude consistent with the predicted weak order.

**Background.** Between two operator insertions, an MPS contraction propagates through powers of its transfer matrix. Summing over separation with the interaction coefficients therefore produces a function of that matrix. Varying a tensor inserts an open position into those powers. Block-matrix identities evaluate the sum over all insertion positions through derivatives of the same matrix function, avoiding a separate fitted interaction representation.

**Why it matters?** This removes a systematic model distortion from a standard many-body method. It should be valuable for classical benchmarks of long-range quantum simulators and for resolving weak order, although finite bond dimension and extrapolation still require independent control.

**Hendrik Timme, Benjamin F. Schiffer, Ohad Lib, Shijia Sun, Maximilian Ammenwerth, Dominik S. Wild, J. Ignacio Cirac, Immanuel Bloch, Johannes Zeiher — arXiv:2611.18122**

Analog simulators can implement interacting dynamics without providing the controlled evolution needed by standard phase estimation. Return probabilities are accessible, but their missing phase prevents direct reconstruction of absolute spectral energies.

This experiment reconstructs the complex Loschmidt amplitude, $G(t)=\langle\psi_0|e^{-iHt}|\psi_0\rangle$, using ordinary evolution and auxiliary state-preparation branches. **Background.** The Cauchy–Riemann equations connect the real-time phase derivative of an analytic amplitude to an imaginary-time derivative of its magnitude. This is statistical phase estimation from a reconstructed signal; finite evolution time, sampling, and probe overlap determine the accessible resolution and spectral weight.

**Why it matters?** This experiment earns inclusion by demonstrating an algorithmic principle: phase-sensitive many-body spectroscopy without an auxiliary control register. The combination of energies and filtered observables is a useful step toward investigating thermalization through spectral structure, rather than only through time-dependent populations.

### Quantum Preparation without Disturbance: Information Costs and Hypothesis Testing
**Pablo Costa Rico, Robert Salzmann, Mario Berta, Ángela Capel — arXiv:2611.18135**

A memory can instruct a device to prepare one of two quantum states, but using that information may disturb the memory. What extra information cost is imposed by requiring the instruction to remain reusable?

The authors minimize distinguishability between the two possible memory states, measured by Umegaki relative entropy. Consuming the memory costs the ordinary relative entropy of the targets. Preserving its marginal raises the minimum to Belavkin–Staszewski relative entropy. Finite reuse interpolates between these costs, and the latter quantity also emerges in asymptotic discrimination of preparation devices. Outputs may be correlated with the memory and one another; preservation does not promise independent copies.

**Background.** The operational meaning of Belavkin–Staszewski relative entropy here is a reverse preparation problem. Represent both targets as mixtures of the same collection of quantum states, with different classical mixing probabilities. Minimize the classical relative entropy between those probabilities. A reusable classical label prepares the corresponding mixture without changing its own marginal. The theorem shows that allowing a quantum memory cannot lower this optimum under exact preservation.

**Why it matters?** This gives an unfamiliar divergence a clear physical task and quantifies the distinction between consuming information and retaining a reusable record. It is relevant to quantum resource accounting and correlated preparation, without invoking an impossible nondisturbing copier of arbitrary states.

### Solution of the Qubit Marginal Problem from Fermionic Representability
**David A. Mazziotti — arXiv:2611.18503**

Local density matrices can each be physical while remaining incompatible with any common global state. Such incompatibility limits energy optimization and the certification of measurements from quantum simulators.

Mazziotti embeds each qubit as one fermion occupying two orbitals. Enforcing $\langle(n_s-1)^2\rangle=0$ at every site isolates precisely the qubit sector, allowing complete fermionic representability hierarchies to transfer to qubit marginals. Site-occupation symmetry splits the resulting optimization into smaller blocks. Low-order tests on twelve-qubit rings recover the exact Majumdar–Ghosh energy and reach roughly half-percent energy accuracy near the Ising critical point. Completeness of the hierarchy does not make the general problem efficiently solvable.

**Background.** An exposed face is a subset of a convex set selected by saturating a supporting linear constraint. Here a positive occupation-penalty operator has zero expectation only on singly occupied sites. This identifies the desired face of the fermionic marginal set. Representability conditions then test positivity of operator squares while cancelling dependence on unretained higher-body correlations, yielding progressively stronger constraints on the retained marginals.

**Why it matters?** The useful result is a structural transfer of certification methods between fermions and qubits. It offers rigorous energy bounds and a route to enforcing physical consistency in noisy data. Assessing accuracy at fixed hierarchy level remains essential for larger or more frustrated systems.

### All coupling Anderson localization in the quantum kicked rotor model
**Yunfeng Shi, Li Wen — arXiv:2611.18320**

The quantum kicked rotor illustrates how interference can suppress motion that is chaotic classically. Its connection to localization relies on a deterministic quadratic phase sequence behaving sufficiently like disorder—a physical intuition whose rigorous scope has remained difficult to establish.

The authors prove localization for a nearest-neighbor lattice operator with potential $V_n=\tan[\pi(\theta+n^2\alpha)]$. For every fixed positive hopping strength, almost every phase-frequency pair gives pure point spectrum with exponentially decaying eigenfunctions. The proof exploits the tangent potential through a Cayley transform, obtains nonperturbative Green-function estimates, and excludes persistent resonant coincidences. The theorem concerns this specified nearest-neighbor reduction, rather than every hopping operator generated by the conventional cosine-kicked rotor.

**Background.** Mapping a Floquet eigenvalue problem to a lattice equation turns angular momentum into a lattice coordinate. The quadratic phase supplies correlated deterministic onsite values. Localization requires controlling rare resonant intervals, not merely showing that these values look random. Equidistribution estimates constrain the relevant finite-volume determinants, while the resonance analysis prevents distant exceptional regions from sustaining extended eigenfunctions.

**Why it matters?** This strengthens a foundational link between deterministic driving and Anderson localization across all hopping strengths in a concrete model. It supplies a rigorous benchmark for quantum suppression of transport while preserving the distinction between spectral localization and stronger dynamical transport statements.

### Positivity Bounds, Negative Running, and the Weak Gravity Conjecture
**Jaime Fernandez — arXiv:2611.17408**

A Wilson coefficient that is positive at matching can run negative at low energy. Does this violate the causal and unitary assumptions behind positivity bounds, or reveal that a tree-level sign test is being applied outside its scope?

In a photon–scalar–fermion effective theory, Yukawa-induced loop effects can drive the coefficient of $(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})^2$ negative. The same low-energy physics modifies the dispersion-relation bound, allowing that running consistently. The paper then examines an apparent consequence for Einstein–Maxwell–dilaton black-hole extremality. The scalar background induces a fermion mass that shuts off the relevant running before the proposed subextremal shift can be inferred.

**Background.** Loop-level positivity constrains a combination of Wilson coefficients and explicitly calculable low-energy dispersive contributions. The positive ultraviolet integral alone does not require each renormalized coefficient to remain positive at every scale. Applying running couplings to a black-hole background also requires local threshold matching: particles light in the asymptotic vacuum can become heavy in the radial region that dominates an observable.

**Why it matters?** This is a useful controlled lesson in EFT consistency. It shows why negative running must be assessed together with infrared contributions and background-dependent decoupling. The black-hole analysis identifies an obstruction to this particular challenge, rather than establishing the Weak Gravity Conjecture generally.
