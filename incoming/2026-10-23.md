### Mixed State Deep Thermalization under Generic Quantum Errors
**Xie-Hang Yu, Pavel Kos — arXiv:2610.31580**

A subsystem can appear thermal while retaining much richer information in the states conditioned on measurements of its surroundings. Noise makes these conditional states mixed, raising a sharper question than ordinary thermalization: which features of an error survive scrambling in the entire distribution of measurement-conditioned states?

Yu and Kos identify a universal limiting ensemble selected by the error channel. For dilute local errors separated by sufficient scrambling, the surviving information is the spectrum of the joint normalized Choi state. Microscopic details of the intervening chaotic dynamics disappear from the limiting distribution. This gives a structured replacement for the pure-state Haar ensemble familiar from deep thermalization.

Analytical treatments cover dual-unitary circuits, global Haar dynamics, and local Haar circuits at large local dimension. Qubit-circuit and kicked Ising calculations support the prediction beyond those controlled settings. The dilute-error and scrambling assumptions are essential to the stated universality.

**Background.** A normalized Choi state records a channel by applying it to one half of a maximally entangled pair. Its eigenvalues describe how the channel mixes independent operator components. The reference ensemble uses this state, denoted by $M$, to correlate a complex Gaussian matrix $X$, producing conditional density matrices

$\rho(X)=XMX^\dagger/\operatorname{Tr}(XMX^\dagger)$.

Each sample additionally receives its Born weight, proportional to the unnormalized trace. This weighting is necessary because measurement outcomes occur with unequal probabilities. The resulting Choi–Wishart construction explains how universal random amplitudes can coexist with a channel-dependent distribution of mixed states.

**Why it matters?** This provides a useful organizing principle for noisy many-body dynamics: scrambling can erase microscopic history while preserving a precise fingerprint of the noise. For non-equilibrium quantum physics, it suggests observables beyond the average reduced state that can distinguish error processes. Extending the correspondence to persistent noise, conservation laws, and incompletely measured environments would determine how broadly this description applies.

### The limits of magic-state distillation
**Llorenç Balada Gaggioli, Jakub Mareček — arXiv:2610.31115**

Magic-state factories consume a large fraction of fault-tolerant computing resources. Improving an existing factory leaves a fundamental question unanswered: could a radically different adaptive or multistage protocol use fewer noisy inputs to achieve the same error suppression?

Balada Gaggioli and Mareček reduce this unrestricted protocol search to stabilizer-code projections. An accepted branch can preserve the tolerated input-fault patterns without preserving the original acceptance probability. With ideal stabilizer operations and independently faulty T-state inputs of error probability $p$, they use this reduction and exhaustive exact searches to establish that quadratic suppression for one T output requires exactly nine inputs. Eight inputs are optimal for quadratic CCZ-state production. For one T output with suppression order $d$ at least two, they also prove $n\geq 2d$, where the output error scales as $O(p^d)$.

The nine-input construction succeeds on ideal inputs with probability only one in 128. Its optimal input count therefore answers a different resource question from the expected cost of producing an accepted output.

**Background.** A fault pattern is harmless when projection either rejects it or maps it onto the same encoded target as the fault-free input. This geometric condition makes protocol-independent searches possible. A second reduction applies to targets such as CCZ whose Pauli expectation values are rational: algebraic conjugation that reverses the sign of the square root of two fixes the target while exchanging faulty and fault-free T inputs. Consequently, complementary fault patterns must be harmless together. The T target transforms differently, so its bound requires a separate argument.

**Why it matters?** The main conceptual advance is a lower-bound method that includes adaptation and arbitrarily rare postselection. It closes loopholes left by optimizations within particular code families. For QEC resource analysis, the distinction between input-count optimality and factory throughput is especially useful: future designs must improve acceptance, circuit cost, and suppression together, even after the minimum input count is settled.

### Onsager Algebras and Lattice Fermions
**Ioanna Kourkoulou, David Tong — arXiv:2610.31653**

Lattice fermions force compromises between chirality, locality, and the removal of unwanted low-energy species. A continuum symmetry that is obscure in the usual lattice variables might nevertheless have an exact microscopic origin, carried by a larger algebra rather than an immediately recognizable internal symmetry group.

Kourkoulou and Tong construct this origin for free staggered, Wilson, and overlap fermions in four spacetime dimensions. Their exact Onsager algebras reduce to the full $U(2N)$ symmetry of $N$ massless continuum Dirac fermions, with right-handed fields rewritten as charge-conjugate left-handed fields. They also identify formulation-dependent trade-offs between generator locality and quantized charge spectra.

The staggered construction makes the mechanism particularly transparent. Its two Majorana components decouple; translating one component independently and conjugating the number charge generates an infinite family of conserved charges. Their noncommuting closure contains the continuum symmetry in its low-energy action. The paper also analyzes the related Hamiltonian and Euclidean structures. Its results concern free fermions; survival under gauging remains open.

**Background.** An Onsager algebra is an infinite algebra of noncommuting generators, historically associated with the Ising model. Here its role is to organize exact conserved transformations whose distinct lattice actions collapse onto a finite-dimensional continuum symmetry. The authors call this an emanant symmetry: the infrared symmetry descends from an exact microscopic algebra. Locality must be specified carefully. An ultra-local generator has a fixed finite range, whereas an exponentially local generator permits tails whose strength decreases exponentially with separation. Enlarging the allowed tails can enlarge the exact compact symmetry available at finite lattice spacing.

**Why it matters?** This changes how one can search for chiral symmetry in a regulator: examining only familiar onsite transformations can miss its microscopic realization. The connection between exact algebraic structure and continuum QFT is valuable for both solvable models and quantum simulation. The next consequential step would be to identify interactions or gauge couplings compatible with enough of this structure to retain its advantages.

### Size-independent Logarithmic Sobolev Inequalities for Quantum Gibbs Samplers
**Hongrui Chen, Zhiyan Ding, Jianfeng Lu — arXiv:2610.31571**

A dissipative algorithm may have the correct thermal stationary state yet approach it too slowly to be useful. Controlling relaxation uniformly as the system grows requires ruling out bottlenecks for entire states, beyond establishing decay of a few observables.

Chen, Ding, and Lu prove size-independent entropy-contraction rates for a specific Gaussian KMS Gibbs sampler. The applications include finite-range one-dimensional spin systems at every fixed finite temperature and sufficiently high temperatures in any fixed dimension. Writing the target Gibbs state as $\sigma_\beta$ and the evolving state as $\rho_t$, the result includes

$D(\rho_t\|\sigma_\beta)\leq e^{-\kappa t}D(\rho_0\|\sigma_\beta)$,

with a positive rate $\kappa$ independent of volume. Its temperature dependence can still be unfavorable.

The proof transports an $L^2$ logarithmic Sobolev inequality from infinite temperature along a quasi-local path of square-root Gibbs vectors. A many-copy argument then converts uniform contraction of the sandwiched Rényi-2 divergence into relative-entropy contraction.

**Background.** A modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality bounds the remaining relative entropy by its instantaneous dissipation. It therefore controls relaxation from arbitrary initial states. Kubo–Martin–Schwinger detailed balance supplies the Gibbs-weighted symmetry used in the analysis. The transport acts on vectorized square roots of density matrices in a doubled Hilbert space, where thermal states become normalized vectors. Locality estimates keep the transported inequality from deteriorating with volume. The final conversion requires contraction even for inputs correlated across independent copies: the asymptotic equipartition property uses such correlated approximations to connect Rényi-2 control to ordinary entropy.

**Why it matters?** This is a substantial methodological contribution to dissipative state preparation and quantum thermalization. It offers a route from a simple product-state bound to interacting Gibbs states without accumulating a volume-dependent penalty. A constant relaxation rate still leaves the initial extensive entropy and the cost of implementing the generator to account for; translating the theorem into practical preparation costs is a separate, meaningful task.

### Sign-problem-free Bosonic Auxiliary Field Quantum Monte Carlo
**Yin-Kai Yu, Zhong-Chao Wei, Zi-Xiang Li — arXiv:2610.31541**

Frustrated hopping produces incompatible signs around lattice loops, obstructing conventional world-line sampling of interacting bosons. Whether that obstruction survives a change of representation is a physical-computational question: the sign of a local matrix element need not determine the sign of a globally resummed weight.

Yu, Wei, and Li develop bosonic auxiliary-field Monte Carlo with sufficient positivity criteria imposed on every decoupled imaginary-time slice. Suitable antiunitary symmetries protect the resulting determinant weights; reflection positivity supplies another route, including pairing models. Their finite-temperature benchmarks on a two-flavor triangular-lattice Bose-Hubbard model agree with exact diagonalization on a small lattice.

The supplement also treats a specifically bosonic complication: some fixed-field traces diverge. The authors prescribe a Gaussian-cutoff finite-part regularization for the determinant weights. This prescription belongs to the method and deserves scrutiny when transferring it to additional models.

**Background.** After the Hubbard–Stratonovich decoupling, the crucial operation is tracing out the bosons before sampling the auxiliary fields. For particle-number-conserving quadratic evolution, the weight is an inverse determinant of identity minus the single-particle propagation matrix. In the simplest protected case, an antiunitary symmetry squaring to minus identity pairs complex eigenvalues with their conjugates and gives real eigenvalues even degeneracy. Their determinant factors are nonnegative together. Reflection positivity instead relates two conjugate sectors with positive semidefinite couplings. These conditions concern the auxiliary-field representation throughout imaginary time; checking a symmetry of the original Hamiltonian alone is insufficient.

**Why it matters?** The useful advance is a set of design principles for finding sign-free bosonic formulations on geometries usually considered difficult. For quantum simulation, such classical methods can supply valuable benchmarks and reveal which apparent computational barriers depend on representation. Broader impact will require demonstrating stable, scalable sampling in new interacting regimes, with the trace prescription and decoupling conditions checked explicitly.

### The Critical 2D KPZ Process
**Quentin Berger, Francesco Caravenna, Jeremy Clark, Alexander Dunlap, Yu Gu, Ziyang Liu, Shuta Nakajima, Rongfeng Sun, Li-Cheng Tsai, Zuodi Xie, Nikos Zygouras — arXiv:2610.31614**

The Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation describes fluctuating interface growth, but white noise makes its nonlinear gradient term singular. In two spatial dimensions, even defining the critical continuum height field is difficult: the corresponding stochastic heat flow is a singular measure whose logarithm cannot be taken pointwise.

The authors construct a nontrivial critical process through two convergent descriptions. Centered logarithms of Gaussian directed-polymer partition functions converge in a critically tuned disorder window. The same limit comes from smoothing the critical stochastic heat flow, taking its logarithm, subtracting a divergent mean, and removing the smoothing. The convergence is formulated for spatial distributions, jointly at finitely many times and bounded continuous initial conditions.

The resulting field is non-Gaussian and cannot be represented locally by an ordinary function or even a finite signed measure. Its covariance has a double-logarithmic short-distance divergence, while suitably normalized averages over shrinking regions become Gaussian.

**Background.** The Cole–Hopf transformation $h=\log u$ replaces nonlinear height evolution by a heat equation with multiplicative noise. Its positive solution can also be represented as a sum over directed paths weighted by a random environment—the polymer partition function. At criticality, the continuum object is too singular for the usual pointwise transformation. Smoothing before taking the logarithm, together with centering, defines the transformation through its action on smooth spatial probes. The critical window simultaneously weakens the microscopic disorder as the cutoff is removed while retaining a nontrivial continuum effect.

**Why it matters?** Constructing the continuum object is a foundational advance for non-equilibrium universality: it makes precise a critical theory whose formal equation was already familiar. It also illustrates how renormalization can preserve nonlinear, non-Gaussian structure despite Gaussian behavior at very small averaging scales. This result concerns a particular critical scaling regime; determining the familiar strong-coupling growth exponents remains a different problem.

### Transversal syndrome extraction: A unifying construction for measuring the error syndromes of any stabilizer code
**Miles D. Gorman — arXiv:2610.30279**

Syndrome extraction can make an attractive code impractical: measuring checks sequentially costs time, while measuring many together requires a larger prepared ancilla. General stabilizer codes need more freedom to navigate this trade-off than choosing between two fixed extraction schemes.

Gorman constructs ancilla stabilizer states tailored to groups of checks, allowing transversal data–ancilla interactions for arbitrary stabilizer codes. Starting from generalized Steane extraction, the construction changes ancillary stabilizers and deletes unnecessary qubits while preserving the intended syndrome information. Different groupings trade interaction depth against peak ancilla width, including non-CSS codes. Shor extraction is recovered under specified conditions.

As one example, the $[[8,2,3]]$ code admits a four-layer interaction circuit using at most twelve ancillas. These counts exclude ancilla preparation and redundant syndrome measurements. Fault tolerance additionally requires sufficiently clean prepared ancillas and reliable syndrome repetition; transversality controls direct fault propagation but does not by itself guarantee a correct inferred syndrome.

**Background.** The specialized ingredient is the ancillary stabilizer structure. Its X components propagate through controlled-Pauli gates to collect data stabilizers, while its Z components cancel unwanted propagated terms. Measuring the ancilla then reveals syndrome parities without exposing the encoded logical state. For a selected group of checks, unused ancillary degrees of freedom can be fixed in simple states and removed. Overlap between checks constrains this reduction and can cause additional syndrome bits to be measured alongside the requested ones. The ancilla therefore carries the correlations needed to make grouped extraction compatible with general Pauli support.

**Why it matters?** This supplies a constructive way to optimize extraction separately from code selection, extending flexibility beyond CSS constructions. That is useful when evaluating higher-rate codes whose nominal encoding advantages may be offset by measurement circuitry. The contribution is a general design framework; establishing an end-to-end resource advantage requires including ancilla preparation, connectivity, repetition, and decoding in the comparison.

### Monodromy Defects of 3D Wilson-Fisher Criticality on the Fuzzy Sphere
**Yin Tang, W. Zhu — arXiv:2610.31269**

Knowing a bulk critical theory does not determine how it responds to an inserted symmetry flux. The line carrying that flux has its own interacting spectrum and couplings, which are difficult to obtain nonperturbatively across several universality classes.

Tang and Zhu implement these monodromy defects in fuzzy-sphere regularization by threading symmetry-selective flux through the poles. Fermion flavors odd under the chosen symmetry receive a half-unit shift in their angular-momentum orbitals, enforcing the required sign change around the line. They study the Ising twist, the $O(2)$ half-turn twist, and three inequivalent $O(3)$ twists.

Finite-size spectra and matrix elements yield defect scaling dimensions, bulk-to-defect couplings, and defect-defect operator product expansion coefficients. Among the resolved low-lying operators, none supplies a relevant scalar perturbation preserving every defect symmetry. This supports stability within the explored spectrum; some higher operators and couplings remain less well converged.

**Background.** A monodromy defect requires a field transported around a line to return transformed by a symmetry element. In radial quantization, a straight line intersects the spatial sphere at both poles, turning that winding condition into an orbital boundary condition. The fuzzy sphere realizes a finite angular resolution using fermions projected into the lowest Landau level of a monopole background. Energies on the resulting cylinder encode scaling dimensions, and matrix elements encode operator couplings. The selective orbital shift puts the twist directly into this regulator. For a one-dimensional defect, a scalar perturbation is relevant when its defect scaling dimension obeys $\widehat{\Delta}<1$; symmetry quantum numbers determine whether it is allowed.

**Why it matters?** The main gain is a reusable numerical construction that accesses several kinds of defect data together. It can complement bootstrap constraints and perturbative expansions with direct strongly coupled calculations. For QFT, this opens a concrete route to studying symmetry-flux responses and defect renormalization-group flows, where bulk universality alone leaves important physics undetermined.

### Finding CFTs from spatial positivity of partition functions
**Léo Buisine, Horacio Casini, Leandro Martinek — arXiv:2610.30234**

Modular invariance is powerful for organizing complete two-dimensional CFTs, but a consistent local subalgebra can omit sectors needed for an invariant torus partition function. Classifying such theories calls for a weaker consistency condition that still has physical content.

Buisine, Casini, and Martinek propose spatial positivity. For every finite collection of upper-half-plane parameters $\tau_i$, the matrix

$Z^S_{ij}=Z\!\left(-1/(\tau_i-\overline{\tau_j})\right)$

must be positive semidefinite. Modular invariance implies this property through ordinary thermal positivity, while the converse is unnecessary. The authors connect it to topological defect lines and positivity of replica-twist correlations.

In rational examples, the proposal produces tractable inequalities on sector multiplicities and successfully identifies consistent submodels. Locality, vacuum uniqueness, and appropriate treatment of degenerate sectors are also needed. The general characterization remains conjectural; positivity of an arbitrary proposed partition function alone is not a sufficient construction of a CFT.

**Background.** Spatial positivity is a matrix condition across different torus geometries, stronger than requiring each partition function to be a positive number. A useful physical picture starts with a complete theory and inserts a projector onto a smaller operator sector. For example, averaging symmetry actions projects onto neutral operators. Exchanging the torus cycles converts that insertion into defect-sector information. In the examples studied, projectors onto valid submodels can be expressed as positive combinations of simple topological defect lines. This connects the positivity test to an actual selection of local operators rather than an arbitrary removal of energy levels.

**Why it matters?** This is a promising extension of partition-function methods to operator algebras that a modular-invariant search would omit. Its appeal lies in replacing detailed operator-product checks with simpler spectral constraints where the conjecture applies. The connection between entanglement positivity and generalized symmetry is conceptually useful; proving the characterization and clarifying its nonrational scope would turn an effective diagnostic into a broader classification tool.

### Discrete time liquids
**Zuo Wang, Paul M. Schindler, Marin Bukov, Marios H. Michael — arXiv:2610.30266**

A noisy oscillator loses phase coherence, but an interacting system responding to it can retain local temporal structure for much longer. Understanding that possibility requires separating the persistence of period doubling from the persistence of one shared subharmonic phase.

Wang and collaborators simulate a two-dimensional classical spin lattice coupled to a thermally fluctuating cavity mode. The cavity filters thermal noise into an approximately periodic signal that modulates the interaction and transverse field oppositely. At intermediate temperatures, the spins develop a long-lived period-doubled response. Individual trajectories repeatedly switch between its two temporal configurations, destroying long-range phase coherence while rebuilding local period doubling.

The authors call these switches time-solitons and the resulting metastable regime a discrete time liquid. A minimal stochastic switching model reproduces the distribution of trajectory correlation times after matching two measured timescales. The large-system evidence uses classical spins and neglects their backaction on the cavity. The proposed extension to equilibrium temporal order remains an outlook.

**Background.** Sampling a period-doubled response once per oscillator cycle gives alternating signs. Removing this regular alternation leaves a slowly varying sign that distinguishes the two subharmonic phases. A time-soliton switches that sign: it is a domain wall in temporal phase, without requiring permanent destruction of the oscillation amplitude. The toy description is a random telegraph process multiplied by a slowly decaying envelope. Independent switches shorten ensemble phase coherence, while the envelope controls eventual loss of the ordered response. These mechanisms therefore produce different correlation and thermalization times.

**Why it matters?** The useful conceptual contribution is a defect-based diagnostic for temporal order with finite coherence. A sharp subharmonic signal can coexist with repeated phase rearrangements, so spectral peaks alone do not establish persistent time-crystalline order. For non-equilibrium physics, the mechanism suggests a productive way to study noise-induced collective motion. Quantum fluctuations, cavity backaction, and genuinely stationary realizations are the next tests needed to establish its broader status.
